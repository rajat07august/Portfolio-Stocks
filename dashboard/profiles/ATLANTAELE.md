## 1. Business in Plain English

**Atlanta Electricals makes the transformers that step electricity up and down as it crosses the country.**

Electricity leaves a power plant at a voltage too low to travel efficiently, so a transformer raises it — to 132,000 volts, 220,000, 400,000 or 765,000 — to be pushed down transmission lines with minimal loss. At the other end another transformer steps it back down. **A transformer is a very large, very heavy, very boring-looking steel box, and the grid does not function without one at every junction.**

Atlanta builds these to order for **state transmission utilities (RVPNL in Rajasthan, PSTCL in Punjab, GETCO in Gujarat, APTRANSCO in Andhra), Power Grid Corporation, renewable developers and industrial customers.** It does **not** make the small pole-mounted distribution transformers you see on a street — management was explicit that it is *"not present in [the] distribution market segment."* Its business is the big ones: **power transformers were 79% of Q1FY27 revenue.**

**Where it sits on the ladder — and this is the entire investment question.** A transformer maker's economics are set by the voltage class it is *approved* to build. Higher voltage means bigger units, fewer competitors, longer approval cycles, larger orders. Atlanta today is a **220 kV company**: 220 kV class was **56% of Q1FY27 revenue** and **over 55% of the order book**. It has just received Power Grid approval for **400 kV** (2-Apr-2026) and is negotiating a technology partner for **765 kV**. *Neither has yet produced a commercial rupee.*

**Five plants**: three legacy units at Anand and Bengaluru, **Unit 4 at Vadod** (commissioned July 2025, 30,000 MVA nameplate), and **Unit 5 at Ankhi, Jambusar** — the former **BTW-Atlanta Transformers India**, acquired and renamed **Atlanta Trafo**, which brings EHV design and testing capability up to 500 MVA / 765 kV. Installed capacity went from **~16,000 MVA to 63,060 MVA** inside about eighteen months.

**The analogy.** Atlanta is a **shipyard that has just finished building itself a much bigger dry dock**. The order book is full, the yard is busy, and the numbers are excellent. But the dock was built for a class of vessel the yard **has not yet launched a single one of** — and the whole valuation rests on the first one floating.

**Scale (FY26):** revenue **₹1,852cr** (+48.8%), EBITDA **₹344cr** (18.6%), PAT **₹202cr** (+70.1%), **22,943 MVA produced**, order book **₹3,117cr** at 30-Jun-2026.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

1. **India's grid is being rebuilt, not maintained.** Management's framing — *"not a cyclical upturn but a multi-year investment cycle"* — is supported by the actual driver list: interstate transmission expansion, renewable evacuation corridors, battery storage, railway electrification, industrial electrification.
2. **Renewables need more transformers per unit of power than thermal does.** Solar and wind are generated far from load centres and at low voltage. Every farm needs step-up transformers, and the grid needs evacuation capacity to carry it. **Renewables were ~19% of Q1FY27 revenue.**
3. **The approval barrier is the moat.** A utility will not buy a 220 kV transformer from a manufacturer whose plant it has not audited and whose prototype has not passed a short-circuit test at CPRI. Power Grid approval is plant-specific and voltage-class-specific. **This is genuinely hard to enter and genuinely slow to exit.**
4. **Supply is short across the range.** Atlanta's COO: *"Supply deficit is there across all ranges according to us, including IDT. And that is the reason we have put up a facility exclusively for the IDT."* Lead times have stretched industry-wide.
5. **Replacement demand is structural.** A large share of India's installed transformer base is at or past design life, and losses in old units are a direct cost to a discom.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

1. **The returns are exceptional and they are not an accounting artefact.** FY26 **ROCE 45.3%, ROE 31.7%** — and ROCE has been **45%, 50%, 45%, 66%, 28%** across FY22-FY26. This is a business that earns a very high return on the capital it employs.
2. **It built capacity ahead of the cycle and filled it immediately.** Unit 4 at Vadod: ~**₹200cr invested**, contributed **₹495cr of revenue in its first seven months**, ran at ~39% of nameplate. Management's payback claim is **3–4 years against an industry norm of 5–7** — and the arithmetic supports it. The COO's account of *why* is the most impressive operational detail in the file: *"orders were secured and structured in advance specifically to ensure there was no idle time at unit four commissioning."*
3. **PGCIL 400 kV approval in two years from groundbreaking.** Received 2-Apr-2026 for Vadod. Management calls this *"among the fastest construction to PGCIL approval timelines in the Indian transformer industry."* **Subject to completion of the short-circuit test** — the approval is real, the qualification is not yet complete.
4. **Prototype-first discipline, which is genuinely creditable.** On 400 kV tenders: *"we are deliberately participating in restricted quantities. Our approach is straightforward, manufacture and prove the first prototype, validate it fully, and only then open the doors to additional 400 kV orders."* **A company chasing a multiple would bid the book and worry later.**
5. **Debt commitment made and kept.** ₹340cr of term loans (₹130cr Vadod + ₹210cr BTW acquisition) → **nil at 31-Mar-2026, ahead of schedule.**
6. **Record order book and record inflow, with named customers.** ₹3,116.63cr at 30-Jun-2026; **Q1FY27 inflow ₹972.42cr, the highest ever**, including RVPNL ₹291.68cr and PSTCL ₹285.15cr. **₹2,400cr of it is executable within FY27.** A further **₹193.92cr from APTRANSCO landed 18-Aug-2026**, after the quarter.
7. **Utilisation leaves real headroom.** FY26 produced **22,943 MVA against 63,060 MVA of exit nameplate capacity.** Incremental volume drops through a largely fixed cost base — which is exactly what FY26's +300bps of margin was.
8. **Disclosure is above watchlist average.** 30+ page decks, four executives on every call, MVA volumes given when asked, quarterly monitoring-agency reports on IPO proceeds, and a **voluntary, detailed, self-damaging disclosure of the SBPDCL debarment** (§3.5) that most companies would have buried.

### Where the moat is weaker

- **The moat is at 220 kV, and 220 kV is where everyone is arriving.** An analyst put it directly on the Q1FY27 call: **~60,000 MVA of new industry capacity from CG Power and Transformers & Rectifiers within three to four months** — roughly Atlanta's entire installed base, added by two competitors in one quarter.
- **400 kV and 765 kV are the thesis, and neither has been built yet.** Management will not even estimate their margins: *"it is not right to comment as to what kind of margins would we be earning… since we are going to manufacture these products for the first time."*
- **Backward integration is thin.** The tank-and-radiator facility addresses components that are *"not more than 4 to 5% of the total transformer cost"* (CMD). This is not the vertical integration story it can read as.
- **The core input comes partly from a promoter-owned company.** See §3.5.
- **Realisation per MVA is a commodity, and management says so.** *"Revenue per MVA is directly related to the commodity pricing… Per MVA pricing will naturally not be a factor to highlight how the industry is performing."* — CMD, FY26 call.

**Three things follow, and they should be read in order of weight.**

1. **₹114.56cr of purchases from a promoter-controlled stampings company is 6.2% of FY26 revenue, and a materially higher share of raw-material cost.** Stampings and laminations are the *core* transformer input. **The related-party note discloses the amount and not the pricing basis.** Because gross margin expanded **300bps in FY26** and another **130bps in Q1FY27**, and because this is the largest single input line, **the transfer price at which Amod sells to Atlanta is a direct determinant of Atlanta's reported margin.** *This is a question, not an allegation — the transactions are fully disclosed, board-approved and audited, and no evidence of mispricing exists. But an investor paying 67x for margin expansion is entitled to ask what fraction of it is set inside the family.*
2. **There is two-way trade.** Atlanta buys ₹114.56cr from Amod and sells ₹39.52cr back to it. Both directions grew. **Two-way related-party trade is a pattern that deserves tracking**, even where each leg is individually ordinary.
3. **🔴 Atlanta UHV Transformers LLP — a promoter entity — has given a corporate guarantee of ₹1,320cr against the working-capital limit sanctioned by the SBI consortium, and *"has not charged anything for issuing this corporate guarantee."*** That figure matches, to the rupee, the *"bank facilities enhanced from ₹910 crores to ₹1,320 crores"* the CFO reported on the FY26 call. **The company's entire banking facility rests on a promoter LLP's guarantee.** *Read generously — and this is a fair reading — the promoters are backing the company for free, which is real alignment. Read carefully, a ₹1,320cr credit line depends on a related party's continued willingness, and that dependency appears nowhere except a footnote.*

### 🔴 The SBPDCL debarment — disclosed impeccably, and raised on zero earnings calls

**The full timeline, from the company's own two filings:**

| 13/14-May-2026 | Order received; **disclosed to both exchanges the next day**, in detail. |

**What should be said in the company's favour, clearly.** The 14-May disclosure is **better than most of this watchlist manages**: it names the customer, dates every step, quantifies the order (<₹3cr), states the technical facts including the unflattering ones, records its disagreement, and cites industry practice. It was **financially immaterial** — under ₹3cr against ₹1,852cr of revenue. And it was **withdrawn in full**, which supports the company's account.

**What still stands.** A two-year debarment by a state discom is **a bidding-eligibility matter, not only a financial one** — utilities routinely ask tenderers to declare debarments. **It was live across an entire earnings call at which nobody mentioned it.** And the underlying Show Cause Notice **dated 22-Aug-2024 pre-dates the IPO by thirteen months**. *I have not verified whether the SCN was disclosed in the RHP; that is a stated gap, not a finding.* **The FY26 Annual Report contains no reference to SBPDCL or to the debarment** — defensible, since the matter arose after the balance-sheet date and was resolved before the AR was signed, but worth recording.

> **🔴 45% of the revenue growth was realisation, not volume.**
> **What the CFO said in prepared remarks:** *"This strong growth was primarily volume-driven, supported by the commissioning and ramp-up of our new manufacturing facilities… **Importantly, there were no material changes in pricing or product mix**, with the growth largely attributable to increased production capacity."*
> **What the CFO said in Q&A, asked directly for the volume split:** *"this is directly attributable to the **price variation clause** which we have in our purchase orders and because of that, any incremental price can be passed on to customers. Because of this and the increase in raw material prices, that is the reason [for the increase] in per MVA realization."*
> **And what the CMD said one quarter earlier, unprompted:** *"Revenue per MVA is directly related to the commodity pricing… if the commodity pricing goes up — I'm talking about steel, copper, oil, etcetera — the prices per MVA will relatively also go up. **Per MVA pricing will naturally not be a factor to highlight how the industry is performing.**"*
> **The same remarks also assert "no material changes in… product mix" two sentences before attributing margin gains to "a gradual shift towards higher-value products, including increased production of the 220 kV class."**

**How this differs from DYCL, and it matters.** Dynamic Cables passes aluminium through **1:1 with no margin**, so a metal spike inflates revenue and dilutes margin. **Atlanta's price-variation clause is margin-protecting — gross margin *rose* 130bps in the same quarter.** *The pass-through is a feature of the contracts and a genuine strength. The problem is describing it as volume.*

**And management has flagged that realisation will now work against them.** The CMD, FY26 call: *"we will be seeing per MVA realizations getting dropped in coming times because more the MVA per unit, per MVA realizations drop. We are in the process of prototyping 400 and 765, which are typically 500 MVA products, which are higher than what we are making right now, typically 160 and 200 MVAs."* **The move up the voltage ladder — the entire bull case — mechanically lowers revenue per MVA. Future growth needs more volume to buy the same rupee.**

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**The virtuous loop is real and it is currently running.** Grid capex and renewable evacuation create transformer demand → demand exceeds industry supply → Atlanta builds capacity ahead of it and pre-books orders so the plant never idles → volumes rise against a fixed cost base → margin expands 300bps → cash funds the next plant without debt → higher capacity wins bigger orders → **record order book, record inflow, 45% ROCE.**

**Every link in that loop is a 220 kV link.** The plants, the approvals, the order book, the margin — all of it is the voltage class Atlanta already owns. And **220 kV is precisely where the industry is adding roughly 60,000 MVA of capacity in a single quarter.** Management's answer is that shortage persists and pricing has not corrected, and on this quarter's evidence they are right. **But the defence against that capacity is not the current business — it is 400 kV and 765 kV, and neither exists yet.**

**So the chain that actually has to hold is this:** 400 kV prototype built → short-circuit test passed → commercial orders from FY28 → 765 kV technology partner signed → Ankhi revalidated → prototype → type tests → orders. **Six sequential gates, the first of which has not yet closed, and the technology agreement that unlocks the back half has already slipped a quarter.**

**Meanwhile the reported growth rate is partly a commodity.** 45% of Q1's growth was realisation. **And the move up the voltage ladder mechanically *reduces* revenue per MVA**, on management's own account. **So the 40% CAGR must be delivered on volume, from a higher base, against more competition, in products the company has never made — at the same time as ~₹1,800cr of stock has to find new owners to satisfy minimum public shareholding.**

**None of that makes it a bad business. It is a very good one.** It makes the price the entire question.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

1. **🔴 The multiple is the position.** 67x and 15.7x book, with Shilchar at 33.3x on a *higher* ROCE and Indo Tech at 38.6x on 40.4%. **A de-rate to peer multiples alone, with earnings intact, is roughly −45%.**
2. **🔴 The growth rate is not what the headline says.** 21.5% volume, 21.8% realisation, described as *"primarily volume-driven"* with *"no material changes in pricing."* **If the commodity cycle turns, the reported growth rate halves before a single order is lost.**
3. **🔴 And the mix shift works against realisation from here.** The CMD: *"per MVA realizations [will get] dropped in coming times… 400 and 765, which are typically 500 MVA products."* **The 40% CAGR now needs volume it has never delivered.**
4. **🔴 Related-party purchase of the core input.** ₹114.56cr from promoter-owned Amod Stampings (6.2% of revenue), plus ₹39.52cr sold back to it, **pricing basis undisclosed**, in a year gross margin expanded. *Disclosed, board-approved, audited — and still the single largest unanswered question in the file.*
5. **🔴 The entire ₹1,320cr working-capital facility is guaranteed by a promoter LLP, free of charge.** Alignment on one reading; an off-balance-sheet dependency on another.
6. **🔴 Minimum public shareholding.** Promoters at 87.28%, public at 12.72%. **On SCRR Rule 19A, ~12.3% of the company — ~₹1,800cr — must reach the market by roughly September 2028.** *My inference; the AR does not discuss it.*
7. **🔴 ~60,000 MVA of industry capacity arriving in three to four months** from CG Power and TARIL — approximately Atlanta's entire installed base, added by two competitors in one quarter, into the 220 kV class that is Atlanta's whole order book.
8. **🔴 400 kV and 765 kV are the thesis and neither has produced a rupee.** Commercial 400 kV contribution guided to **FY28**. The 765 kV technology tie-up **has already slipped one quarter** and remains unsigned; the CMD's *"Certainly"* on end-Q2 PGCIL approval contradicts his own sequencing on the same call. Terms disclosed: **USD 3–5m one-time plus 2–4% royalty for three to four years.**
9. **🟡 Chinese manufacturers have been permitted into PSU tenders** through Indian manufacturing facilities. Management sees no impact yet and argues the one transformer maker among them is fully loaded — *"we do not see any logic"* in it chasing lower-margin PSU work. **Reasonable, and untested.**
10. **🟡 CRGO steel anti-dumping investigation.** DGTR verdict expected by end-March 2027 on the core raw material. Management will not speculate. **A duty would raise input cost; the price-variation clause passes it on with a lag, but not costlessly.**
11. **🟡 Cash conversion is the weakest number here.** CFO/OP above 100% once in six years; 5% in FY22; 77% in FY26. Cash conversion cycle ~83 days and lengthening with scale.
12. **🟡 Extreme seasonality invites misreading.** Q4FY26 was 40% of the year. **Any single quarter — good or bad — will overstate the trend.**
13. **🟡 Concentration in state discoms.** RVPNL, PSTCL, GETCO, APTRANSCO. State utilities are slow payers and, as SBPDCL demonstrated, capable of a two-year debarment over a sub-₹3cr order.
14. **🟡 Zero dividend, and a local Vadodara auditor** (PSCA & Co.) for a ₹14,624cr company carrying three Key Audit Matters — revenue cut-off, warranty/liquidated-damages provisions, and CWIP capitalisation. ***All three were tested and cleared with a clean opinion; they are named because they are precisely the three levers a newly-listed company under growth pressure would use, not because anything was found.***
15. **🟡 The risk that this section is wrong.** If the 400 kV prototype passes on schedule and 765 kV signs, Atlanta moves into a product class with a handful of qualified Indian suppliers, order sizes several times larger, and structurally better margins — **while still holding a 45% ROCE and 63,060 MVA of capacity.** In that world 67x will look cheap and this profile will read as having priced a step-change as though it were an extrapolation.
