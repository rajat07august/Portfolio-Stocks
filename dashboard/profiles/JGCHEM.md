## 1. Business in Plain English

J.G. Chemicals makes **zinc oxide** — a white powder that almost nobody has heard of and every tyre contains.

**Why a tyre needs it:** rubber does not become a tyre until it is vulcanised — cured with sulphur under heat. Zinc oxide is the **activator** that makes that cure happen efficiently and evenly. It makes up roughly **3.5-5% of a tyre compound by weight but only ~1-1.5% of the tyre's cost** *(MD & CEO, Q4FY26 call)*. It is also used in ceramics glazes, paints, pharmaceuticals and cosmetics (sunscreen, ointments), animal feed and agriculture, and electronics.

**What makes JG unusual is where the zinc comes from.** It does not buy refined zinc ingots. It buys **zinc dross and scrap** — the crusty residue skimmed off galvanising baths, bought from scrap yards and galvanisers across India, Europe, the US and the Middle East, **roughly 50:50 domestic and imported** *(Q2FY26 call)* — and turns it into more than **90 specified grades** of zinc oxide using the **French process**, plus **zinc sulphate** (an agricultural micronutrient) made from the zinc ash left over. Management calls it *"India's largest zinc recycler."*

**The analogy: a refinery for someone else's rubbish, selling into a factory that never changes its recipe.** The input is variable scrap; the output must be identical every batch, because a tyre maker who approves a supplier does so for years — *"Tire industry remains approximately 5 years. Approval process"* (Q1FY27 call).

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**1. Every tyre needs it, and India is becoming a tyre factory for the world.** Management cites **~₹20,000-25,000cr of announced tyre-industry capex** and ~25% of Indian tyres now exported. When tyre makers add lines, zinc-oxide demand follows mechanically — *"When the tire industry invests, JG Chemicals is a direct beneficiary."* GST cuts from September 2025 lifted auto volumes (ATMA Q1FY27: PV +26.6%, 2W +16.5%, CV +16.5%).

**2. The market is domestic and hard to import into.** India consumes ~**1,40,000 t** of zinc oxide a year, of which imports are *"hardly about 5,000 tons"* (Q1FY26). The top three or four producers hold ~60-67%, with a long tail of 30-40 small players (Q4FY26). JG claims ~30% share.

**3. Customers are consolidating their supplier base.** Large tyre makers — under pressure from their own OEM customers — increasingly want fewer, bigger, certified vendors. **Small sub-5,000 t producers *"do not qualify"*** (MD, Q2FY26). The same shift is described as beginning in ceramics.

**4. Zinc oxide is priced off the LME, so the commodity is passed through.** Both the scrap JG buys and the oxide it sells are referenced to the London Metal Exchange zinc price on an **"M minus 1"** basis. **The producer earns a conversion spread, not a zinc price.** The exposure that remains is the ~2 months of inventory on hand.

**5. Recycling has an ESG premium.** Tyre makers face mandates to raise recycled content; zinc oxide made from 100% scrap helps them meet it. This is the logic behind JG's second product line — recycled rubber (§5.5).

---

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**1. Scale in a fragmented market.** At ~70,000 t, JG says the gap to its nearest domestic competitor is *"significant"*, and it is the largest supplier to the tyre industry. **Scale is also part of the process moat**: to blend scrap of varying purity into 90 consistent grades, you must hold a wide variety of scrap at once — *"our IP is also internally protected by the size that we have"* (Q2FY26).

**2. Qualification is slow and sticky.** Five-year tyre approvals, 95%+ repeat orders (internal survey cited Q2FY26), and direct sales — *"close to 90%"* of new sales direct to the customer.

**3. Certifications at Naidupeta.** IATF 16949 (automotive quality) and WHO-GMP plus pharmacopoeia licences. **Management describes it as *"the only IATF certified zinc oxide facility anywhere in the world"* — a claim not independently verified here.**

**4. Capital-light growth.** ~₹57cr of gross block produced ~₹850cr of FY25 revenue (CFO, Q1FY26). **Dahej's ₹100cr, including land, is sized for ~₹900cr of revenue at full capacity** — the arithmetic depends on LME zinc, but the asset turnover is genuinely high.

**5. Near-zero debt.** Consolidated borrowings **₹6cr** at March 2026 against equity of ₹528cr and investments of ₹136cr. Payable days of **5** — it pays its scrap suppliers almost on delivery, which is part of why they sell to it.

**6. A pass-through pricing model** that has held through a war-driven cost spike: the March 2026 energy and freight shock was *"transparently passed on to all our customers with effect from 1st April 2026"*.

### Where the moat is weaker — honestly

- **It is a conversion business on a commodity.** EBITDA *excluding* other income has been **7-10%** for four years. Customers are the most procurement-disciplined buyers in Indian manufacturing. **The margin is a spread, and spreads get competed for.**
- **One end market.** ~85% rubber and tyre. Management has been talking about 30% non-rubber since at least August 2025; the share was flat through FY26.
- **Inventory is a real exposure, not a theoretical one.** When LME zinc fell ~$700/t in two months in 2023, **consolidated operating margin fell to 3.2% (Jun-2023 quarter)** and FY24 PAT halved to ₹32cr.
- **Disclosure is thin.** Volumes, realisations, segment margins and export geography are all withheld *"as a company policy"*.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**The core franchise is real and the growth path is logical.** Tyre makers are building capacity in India; they are consolidating suppliers; JG is the largest certified supplier; Dahej puts it next to the Gujarat tyre plants whose wallet share it currently holds at **"almost zero"** and next to the Morbi ceramics cluster, where it holds **"less than 1%"** of a 25-30,000 t market. **Every one of those statements is internally consistent, and the ₹100cr ask is small relative to the prize.**

**The margin story is where the threads come apart.** Management's route to 14-15% EBITDA is non-rubber mix (pharma and specialty grades earn 18-20%, per the FY25 deck as cited on ValuePickr), operating leverage, and new products. **But the non-rubber share did not move in FY26, the flagship new product (recycled rubber) is a year behind its first description, and the historical "10-11% core" margin was only reached with other income added.** Today's 11% ex-OI quarter coincides with rising LME zinc — the one condition under which inventory gains flatter margins.

**So the chain is: tyre capex → JG volume → Dahej capacity → ceramics and specialty mix → margin.** The first three links are strong. **The last two are promises, and the evidence so far is that they arrive late.** Meanwhile every link consumes working capital, so free cash stays thin while the plan runs — and the zinc cycle, not management, decides when the inventory line helps or hurts.

**The tension in one sentence: an 18%-ROCE market leader with a cheap, well-sited expansion, valued at 30x on a quarter that probably contains the best of the zinc cycle, by a management that reports margins on its most favourable basis.**

**Against the closest business models — the metal recyclers — JG is fully priced.** Pondy Oxides earns more (23.4% ROCE) at a lower multiple (24.2x); Gravita earns about the same at the same multiple with a far larger, more diversified footprint. **Against rubber chemicals (NOCIL) it looks cheap**, but NOCIL's multiple sits on cyclically collapsed earnings. **ROE of 13.3% below an 18.1% ROCE is the cash drag** — surplus IPO money and deposits earning deposit rates.

**Why the market pays 30x:** a certified market leader in a consolidating supply chain, a tyre capex cycle, an expansion that could raise capacity ~60% for ₹100cr, a debt-free balance sheet and a quarter that printed +63% PAT.

**Why it should not pay more:** operating profit fell in FY26; margins look better only with other income; volume data is withheld; the forward promises are late; and the current quarter sits on a rising zinc price that will one day fall.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**1. 🔴 The zinc cycle turns.** The pass-through model protects the spread, not the inventory. **A fast LME fall converts ~2 months of stock into losses** — Jun-2023 quarter OPM 3.2%, FY24 PAT −44%. Q1FY27 was helped by rising zinc; that tailwind reverses mechanically.

> 📈 **Factor check 19-Sep-2026:** Not yet: zinc is +11.3% over three months and at a 52-week high (World Bank, August). The risk grows the longer the rise runs. *(Numbers and sources: Input & factor check, at the top.)*

**2. 🔴 The margin is weaker than it is presented.** FY26 ex-OI margin 8.4% vs "10.1%"; operating profit −6% in a revenue +15% year; a Q4 margin spoken at 10.26% that computes to 9.36% from the same call's figures. **An investor using management's framing overstates operating margin by ~1.5-2 points.**

**3. 🔴 Promises arrive late.** Recycled rubber: "10% of revenue next year" → "commercial in 12 months". Dahej: H1 → November. Non-rubber mix: flat for a year while the target was brought forward. **The 14-15% margin target has been raised before any of its drivers have arrived.**

**4. 🔴 Customer concentration in a squeezed industry.** ~85% of revenue goes to rubber and tyres. The watchlist's own tyre name (TVSSRICHAK) calls FY27 raw-material inflation *"unprecedented."* **Tyre makers under margin pressure are the most aggressive procurement negotiators in India.**

**5. 🟡 Growth consumes cash.** ~100 days of working capital; FY25-26 free cash −₹11cr on ₹136cr of PAT. Dahej plus its working capital is fundable from the balance sheet — **but not if zinc stays high and growth stays fast at the same time for long.**

**6. 🟡 Disclosure is restrictive and narrowing.** Volumes, realisations, segment margins and export geography withheld; one volume figure given and then withdrawn. **The company has held only five calls.**

**7. 🟡 Governance details.** A ₹12.2cr investment in a promoter vehicle that owns ~10% of the company; ₹33cr of IPO money still undeployed after 30 months; an unidentified 5.87% minority in the main operating subsidiary; ~30x director pay ratios; an independent-director vacancy after a death in August 2026. **None is alarming alone; together they are a reason to read the RPT note every year.**

**8. 🟡 Dahej ramp risk.** New region, new customer segments (ceramics, specialty), and Morbi's ceramics cluster was partly shut by gas shortages in 2026. **Breakeven needs 60-65% utilisation; FY28 guidance is 50-60%.**

**9. 🟡 Raw-material access.** Half the scrap is imported; the March 2026 Gulf disruption froze Middle East supply and forced spot domestic buying. The duty on zinc dross was not removed when scrap duty was — *"it has been a slip."*

**10. 🟡 Institutional exit and a hot chart.** FIIs halved; shareholder count down 43% since listing; the stock more than doubled off its low and drew an NSE volume query. **Momentum-driven entry points carry a real drawdown risk.**

## 8. 30-Second Memory Hook

**J.G. Chemicals turns galvanisers' zinc dross into the white powder that makes rubber into a tyre** — India's largest zinc-oxide maker, ~30% of the market, certified supplier to every major Indian tyre company and nine of the world's top ten, debt-free, 18% ROCE, with a ₹100cr Gujarat plant that could add ~60% capacity next to the tyre and ceramics clusters where it sells almost nothing today. It is also a company whose FY26 "record year" had operating profit **down 6%**, whose EBITDA margin includes other income, whose only volume disclosure was quietly withdrawn, whose recycled-rubber line went from "10% of revenue next year" to "commercial in twelve months", and whose best-ever quarter arrived with LME zinc rising.

**The cricketer is Ajinkya Rahane, 2021.** Genuine class, a technique built for difficult pitches, captained India to a famous series win — and then a stretch where the reputation ran ahead of the scores, and every good innings was greeted as the return while the average said otherwise. **JG's franchise is genuinely class. The question is whether the numbers — ex-other-income, ex-inventory-gain — keep up with the story being told about them.**

**Thesis in a sentence:** *A certified, debt-free market leader in a consolidating tyre supply chain, with a cheap and well-sited expansion, priced at 30x on a quarter flattered by rising zinc — worth owning at a lower price, and worth watching closely at this one until Dahej runs and management reports margins without other income.*
