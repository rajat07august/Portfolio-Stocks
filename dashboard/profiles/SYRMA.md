## 1. Business in Plain English

Every electronic product you buy — a smart meter, a car dashboard, a hearing aid, a Wi-Fi router, an industrial sensor — starts as a **printed circuit board assembly (PCBA)** or a **fully-built product ("box build")** manufactured by an Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) company. Big brands (Honeywell, Bosch, Kaynes, TVS Electronics) design the product; EMS players like Syrma SGS assemble and ship it. **Syrma SGS is India's third-largest listed EMS player**, behind Dixon Technologies and Kaynes Technology.

The 30-second business snapshot:
- **HQ Chennai, Tamil Nadu** — formed by merger of Syrma Technology (electronic component manufacturer) and SGS Tekniks (PCB assembly) in 2022; both had 20+ year histories separately
- **FY26 revenue INR 4,857 cr** (+~32% YoY) — one of India's fastest-growing mid-large EMS players
- **FY26 EBITDA INR 545 cr (11.3% margin); PAT INR 346 cr (+87% YoY)**
- **Six revenue verticals**: Industrial (31%), Automotive (25.6%), Consumer (low-margin, deliberately shrinking), Healthcare (36% growth), IT/networking, Consumer Appliances
- **Elcome acquisition** (defence electronics, FY26) — added INR 55 cr EBITDA
- **ODM revenue** grew 80% YoY (INR 453 cr → INR 825 cr) — critical margin lever
- **Exports INR 1,200+ cr** (25% of revenue, +41% YoY)

**Big picture in one sentence:** Syrma is a **diversified mid-cap EMS + ODM platform** riding India's electronics manufacturing megatrend + PLI schemes + China+1 outsourcing, delivering revenue growth of 30–40% + PAT growth of 60–90% for 3+ years. Trades at 82.8x trailing P/E — expensive but reflects both the growth trajectory + the ODM margin expansion story.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

Indian EMS rides five durable forces:

**Force 1 — Electronics demand acceleration.** India's electronics consumption is projected to grow from ~$155B (FY24) to ~$500B by 2030, driven by smartphones, EVs, smart home, industrial automation, healthcare, and 5G. India historically imports most PCBAs; the domestic EMS opportunity to substitute imports is ~$100B by 2030.

**Force 2 — PLI schemes accelerating.** ~INR 30,000+ cr of PLI incentives across mobile, electronics, semiconductors. Every dollar of PLI unlocks 8–12 dollars of EMS revenue on average. Syrma is a beneficiary in multiple PLI segments.

**Force 3 — China+1 supply chain rebalancing.** Global brands (Apple, Bosch, Panasonic, HP) actively moving PCBAs out of China. India + Vietnam are the two dominant destinations. India's cost + English + IP protection edge attracts higher-value PCBAs; Vietnam gets lower-mix.

**Force 4 — Auto electronics content explosion.** Every new car has 3–5x the electronic content of a car a decade ago. Auto EMS TAM growing 25% CAGR. Syrma's auto exposure is 25.6% and rising.

**Force 5 — Defence + healthcare + industrial IoT.** Multi-year defence electronics indigenisation (INR 40,000+ cr TAM) + healthcare medical devices growth + industrial IoT sensors all create diversified EMS demand.

**Where Syrma sits in the map.** It's the **#3 listed pure-play EMS in India** by revenue:
- **Dixon Technologies** — largest, consumer-focused (mobiles, LED TVs)
- **Kaynes Technology** — #2, industrial + auto + medical, similar to Syrma
- **Syrma SGS** — #3, most diversified across 6 verticals
- **Avalon Technologies** — #4, US-heavy, higher box build mix
- **Amber Enterprises** — consumer durables focus, different niche
- **Cyient DLM, Rossell Techsys** — smaller / different niche

Syrma's positioning: **broadest vertical diversification** + rising ODM contribution + rising export mix. Trade-off: broad diversification means less category dominance in any single vertical.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Six-vertical diversification.** Industrial (31%) + Auto (25.6%) + Consumer + Healthcare (up 36% YoY) + IT/Networking + Appliances. No single vertical is more than 33%. Cyclical downturn in any one vertical doesn't cripple the company.

**Moat 2 — ODM shift (from INR 453 cr → INR 825 cr in FY26, +80%).** ODM (Original Design Manufacturer) is the higher-margin, higher-IP category vs EMS (contract manufacturing). Syrma's ODM growth is the single most important margin lever. FY26 ODM contribution ~17% of revenue; guidance is higher.

**Moat 3 — Export mix scaling (INR 1,200 cr, 25% of revenue, +41% YoY).** Exports command premium margins vs domestic. Syrma's export growth outpaces domestic + management guides continued acceleration.

**Moat 4 — Elcome acquisition (defence electronics, closed FY26).** Elcome brings defence-grade electronics manufacturing + certifications. FY26 contribution INR 55 cr EBITDA. Long-term margin uplift (defence EMS is higher-margin than commercial).

**Moat 5 — Manufacturing scale + geographic footprint.** Multiple factories across Chennai + Pune + Delhi NCR + Punjab. Total capacity ~5x Aimtron, ~10x Concord. Scale = better OEM negotiation + component sourcing + PLI incentives.

**Moat 6 — Diversified customer base (top 5 = 34%, top 10 = 47%, top 20 = 63%).** No single customer dominates. Historical customer relationships across 10+ year cycles.

**Moat 7 — Positive OCF (INR 290+ cr, 53% of operating EBITDA).** WC days improved from 69 to 63 (Elcome at 58). Cash conversion is strong — unusual for a fast-growing EMS company.

**Moat 8 — Merger execution track record.** Syrma + SGS Tekniks integration in 2022 delivered synergies + broadened customer base. Elcome integration in FY26 is progressing well. M&A execution capability is real.

**Where the moat is weaker.**
- Consumer segment (deliberately shrinking) hurts reported growth optics.
- Auto EMS margins compressed by aggressive OEM pricing; volume-heavy, margin-light.
- Competition intensifying — L&T entering EMS, Dixon adding capacity.
- Working capital rises with volume; if orders slip, WC gets stuck.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

Syrma's story is **diversified EMS + ODM shift**, with 5 threads:

**Thread A — Vertical growth (all 5 productive verticals firing).** Auto +39%, Industrial +30%, Healthcare +36%, Export +41%. This is broad-based growth, not a single-segment story.

**Thread B — ODM shift (INR 453 → INR 825 cr).** ODM is higher-margin. If ODM reaches INR 1,200+ cr by FY28 (feasible), margin expansion is ~100 bps.

**Thread C — Export expansion (INR 1,200 cr, 25% share).** Higher-margin export mix rising.

**Thread D — Elcome integration + defence electronics.** Multi-year TAM, higher-margin than commercial.

**Thread E — Consumer segment de-emphasis.** Deliberately shrinking low-margin consumer = margin optics improve.

**Where threads reinforce.** Auto + Industrial + Healthcare + Export all benefit from the same PCBA capability. ODM shift is orthogonal — happens across verticals simultaneously.

**Where they don't compound.** Consumer segment shrinkage optically drags reported revenue growth. Working capital may rise with ODM (ODM has more inventory + longer receivables).

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — Growth deceleration.** 35% growth on INR 4,857 cr base = INR 6,500 cr FY27. If actual is 25–28%, market re-rates.
**Risk 2 — Margin compression.** Auto EMS is margin-thin. If auto grows faster than higher-margin verticals, blended margins compress.
**Risk 3 — Fourth dilution.** Post 5% dilution over 3 years, another round would be a concern.
**Risk 4 — L&T + others entering EMS.** L&T's Coimbatore facility is direct competition.
**Risk 5 — Consumer segment losses.** Deliberate shrinkage might drag Q1-Q2FY27.
**Risk 6 — Working capital stretch as ODM grows.**
**Risk 7 — Elcome integration issues.**
**Risk 8 — PLI scheme changes / delays.**
**Risk 9 — Retail momentum unwind.** 178,986 shareholders is high; a correction could hurt.

Weighted expected return: ~10% over 2 years; ~5% CAGR.
