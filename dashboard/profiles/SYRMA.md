## 1. Business in Plain English

Every electronic product you buy — a smart meter, a car dashboard, a hearing aid, a Wi-Fi router, an industrial sensor — starts as a **printed circuit board assembly (PCBA)** or a fully-built product (**"box build"**) made by an **Electronics Manufacturing Services** company. Big brands design the product; EMS players like Syrma assemble and ship it.

**Syrma is India's third-largest listed EMS player**, behind Dixon Technologies and Kaynes Technology.

**The snapshot:**
- **HQ Chennai.** Formed by the **2022 merger of Syrma Technology** (electronic components) **and SGS Tekniks** (PCB assembly) — both with 20+ year histories.
- **FY26 revenue ₹4,857cr (+32%), EBITDA ₹545cr (11.3%), PAT ₹346cr (+87%).**
- **Q1FY27: revenue ₹1,604cr (+67.1%), EBITDA ₹176.6cr (+72.0%), PAT ₹105.7cr (+111.8%).**
- **Exports ₹387cr, +61%, 24% of revenue. ODM ₹270cr, +116%, 17% of revenue.**
- **Order book ₹6,770cr** at June-end, quantified with a full vertical split.
- **FY27 guidance: 35% revenue and 30-35% EBITDA growth — and management says it will be exceeded.** Multi-year: **30-35% for FY28 and FY29.**
- **🟢 FY26 operating cash flow ₹290cr at 69% conversion, positive free cash flow, a 48-day cash conversion cycle and an ₹800cr+ treasury.**

**Big picture in one sentence:** Syrma is a diversified EMS and ODM platform riding India's electronics build-out, **and the only company in this refresh batch funding its own growth from operations** — where the growth thesis has now been proven, the multiple *fell* from 82x to 76x while the stock rose 45% because earnings outran price, and **the residual risk is almost entirely the starting multiple.**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Electronics demand acceleration.** India's electronics consumption is projected to grow from **~$155bn (FY24) to ~$500bn by 2030**, across smartphones, EVs, smart home, industrial automation, healthcare and 5G. India historically imports most PCBAs; **the domestic import-substitution opportunity is ~$100bn by 2030.**

**Force 2 — PLI schemes.** **~₹30,000cr+ of incentives** across mobile, electronics and semiconductors. **Every rupee of PLI unlocks 8-12 rupees of EMS revenue on average**, and Syrma is a beneficiary in multiple segments.

**Force 3 — China+1.** Global brands are actively moving PCBAs out of China. **India and Vietnam are the two dominant destinations** — India's cost, English and IP-protection edge attracts the higher-value work; Vietnam gets lower-mix.

**Force 4 — Auto electronics content explosion.** **Every new car has 3-5x the electronic content of a decade ago**, and the auto EMS TAM is growing ~25% CAGR.

**Force 5 — Defence, healthcare and industrial IoT.** Multi-year defence electronics indigenisation (**₹40,000cr+ TAM**), medical devices, and industrial sensors.

**Where Syrma sits.** **#3 listed pure-play EMS by revenue.** **Dixon** is largest and consumer-focused; **Kaynes** is #2 across industrial, auto and medical; **Avalon** is #4 and US-heavy with a higher box-build mix; **Amber** sits in consumer durables; **Cyient DLM and Rossell Techsys** are smaller and different. **Syrma's positioning is the broadest vertical diversification, plus rising ODM and export mix.** *The trade-off: broad diversification means less category dominance in any single vertical.*

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Six-vertical diversification.** Industrial, Automotive, Consumer, Healthcare, IT/networking and Consumer Appliances, **with no single vertical above about a third.** A downturn in one does not cripple the company. **Q1FY27 mix: auto 29%, consumer 30%, industrial 24%, healthcare 7%, IT/railways 9%.**

**Moat 2 — The ODM shift, and it is accelerating.** ODM (Original Design Manufacturer) carries higher margin and higher IP than contract manufacturing. **FY26 ODM went ₹453cr → ₹825cr (+82%); Q1FY27 was ₹270cr against ₹125cr a year earlier (+116%), now 17% of revenue.** **This is the single most important margin lever.**

**Moat 3 — Export mix scaling.** **FY26 exports grew 40%; Q1FY27 grew 61% to ₹387cr, 24% of revenue.** Exports command premium margins over domestic. Gujral's framing: *"for a long-term, sustained, profitable growth, **integration of the Indian EMS companies with the global clients is a must**."*

**Moat 4 — The Elcome acquisition** (defence electronics, closed FY26) brings defence-grade manufacturing and certifications, and added **₹55cr of EBITDA** in FY26. **Defence EMS is structurally higher-margin than commercial.**

**Moat 5 — Manufacturing scale and footprint.** Factories across Chennai, Pune, Delhi NCR and Punjab. **Roughly 5x Aimtron and 10x Concord in capacity** — scale that translates into OEM negotiating power, component sourcing and PLI eligibility.

**Moat 6 — Customer diversification.** **Top 5 = 38%, top 10 = 51%, top 20 = 66%.** *Reasonable for EMS, and materially better than the 74% single-customer concentration at SSWL this cycle.* **18 new clients onboarded in Q1FY27** alone — 5 automotive, 3 industrial, 2 healthcare, the rest telecom/IT/railways.

**Moat 7 — 🟢 It funds its own growth.** **FY26 CFO ₹290cr at 69% conversion**, positive free cash flow, **cash conversion cycle halved from 99 days to 48** over four years, and a move **from ₹264cr of net debt at FY25-end to a net cash position.** **In a refresh cycle where AZAD posted CFO/OP of −31%, GPIL −118% at the operating line and SATHLOKHAR −₹163cr, this is the one company financing itself.**

**Moat 8 — Merger execution.** The 2022 Syrma + SGS Tekniks integration delivered synergies and a broader customer base; **Elcome is integrating well. M&A capability here is demonstrated, not asserted.**

**Where the moat is weaker — stated plainly:**
- **🔴 Order book coverage is thinning.** ~₹5,800cr (Sep-2025) → **₹6,770cr (Jun-2026)**, up ~17% while revenue grew far faster — **coverage down from ~1.9x annualised revenue to ~1.1x.** *The sharpest caveat on an otherwise excellent quarter.*
- **🔴 76x on a 14.0% ROE is a full price**, after +112% in a year.
- **🟡 Percentage margins face a mechanical headwind** from component-cost pass-through for as long as prices rise (Section 4).
- **🟡 Working capital is deliberately deteriorating** — 63 → 71 days on a stated inventory build.
- **Consumer is deliberately shrinking**, which hurts reported growth optics.
- **Auto EMS margins are compressed by aggressive OEM pricing** — volume-heavy, margin-light.
- **Competition is intensifying** — L&T entering EMS, Dixon adding capacity.

**🟡 The QoQ margin dip is not a warning, and the reason is worth understanding.** OPM fell 11.9% → 10.2% sequentially. Gujral, pressed on whether rising memory prices are a pass-through:

> *"Even if it is a complete pass-through, as an example, if my chip is Rs. 60 and I am selling it at Rs. 100… it is a 40% gross material margin. The chip goes from 60 to 100. I sell it at 140 to the customer. **140 is not 40%.** So, while the **absolute figures will remain intact**, broadly, **the percentage margin would undergo a change** because you don't get a markup, you don't get a value add on the increased cost of a component."*

**That is the clearest explanation of EMS margin optics I have read in a transcript.** Rising component prices **mechanically dilute percentage margins while leaving rupee margin per unit intact.** **It means OPM percentage is a poor way to judge this company while component prices are rising — watch absolute EBITDA and EBITDA growth against revenue growth instead.** *My inference: on that basis, EBITDA growing 72% against revenue growing 67% is the more informative pair, and it is positive.*

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Five threads, and what matters is that they are orthogonal rather than sequential.**

**A — Vertical growth, broad-based.** Auto +39%, Industrial +30%, Healthcare +36%, Exports +41% in FY26. **This is not a single-segment story**, and no vertical exceeds about a third.

**B — The ODM shift.** ₹453cr → ₹825cr in FY26, then **+116% in Q1FY27 to 17% of revenue.** ODM carries higher margin and higher IP. **If ODM reaches ₹1,200cr+ by FY28 — feasible on the current trajectory — that is roughly 100bps of margin.**

**C — Export expansion.** 24% of revenue and growing 61%. Higher-margin than domestic, and Gujral's stated strategic requirement rather than an opportunistic add.

**D — Elcome and defence electronics.** Multi-year TAM, structurally higher-margin than commercial, **early but expected to scale.**

**E — Consumer de-emphasis.** Deliberately shrinking the lowest-margin vertical. **It drags reported revenue growth and improves margin optics simultaneously.**

**Where the threads reinforce:** auto, industrial, healthcare and export all draw on the *same PCBA capability* — one capital base, four demand pools. **And the ODM shift is orthogonal: it happens across every vertical at once**, which is why it can lift blended margin without any change in vertical mix.

**Where they do not compound — and this is the part that matters now.** **ODM carries more inventory and longer receivables than contract manufacturing**, so the very shift that lifts margin also consumes working capital. **Q1FY27 shows exactly that: working capital days 63 → 71 on a deliberate inventory build.** Management frames it as time-boxed and strategic, and the framing is credible. **But the structural point stands: a faster ODM mix is a permanently heavier balance sheet, and the ₹800cr treasury is what makes that affordable.**

**And the constraint sitting above all five threads is the order book.** ₹6,770cr growing 17% against revenue growing 67% means **coverage has fallen from ~1.9x to ~1.1x**. **Every thread above converts demand into revenue efficiently. None of them creates demand.** *That is the question the next two quarters answer.*

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — 🔴 The starting multiple.** 76.1x on a 14.0% ROE, 9.9x book, after +112% in a year and 5% off an all-time high. **The base case requires a de-rating to 50x and still returns only 15% over two years.**

**Risk 2 — 🔴 Order book coverage is thinning.** ₹6,770cr growing ~17% since Sep-2025 against revenue growing far faster — **coverage ~1.9x → ~1.1x annualised.** *EMS books are rolling rather than backlog-like, but this cannot continue indefinitely.* **The single most important number to watch.**

**Risk 3 — 🟡 Percentage margins face a mechanical headwind** while component prices rise. **Judge on absolute EBITDA growth, not OPM** — but the market will judge on OPM.

**Risk 4 — 🟡 Working capital may not reverse.** 63 → 71 days, framed as time-boxed to *"a quarter or so."* **If it keeps climbing past Q2, the "strategic asset" framing becomes a symptom.** *And the ODM shift structurally carries more inventory and longer receivables, so some of this is permanent.*

**Risk 5 — 🟡 Supply chain.** Specialty chemicals for PCB manufacture and component lead times, with a **"war room"** in place and normalisation expected only by **end CY2026.**

**Risk 6 — 🟡 PCB commissioning slips** beyond April 2027, or the ECMS incentive does not land as assumed.

**Risk 7 — 🟡 Retail concentration.** **+25,163 shareholders (+14%) in a single quarter near a high.** *Institutions are holding rather than selling — better than the AZAD picture — but this reverses badly if growth stumbles.*

**Risk 8 — 🟡 The new CEO's remit.** A professional CEO layered under a continuing founder-MD. **Watch how responsibilities divide.**

**Risk 9 — 🟡 Competition intensifying** — L&T entering EMS, Dixon adding capacity, and auto EMS margins already compressed by aggressive OEM pricing.

**Risk 10 — ⚪ EMS peer-group correction.** At 76x, Syrma re-rates with the sector whether or not it deserves to.

**Risk 11 — ⚪ Consumer de-emphasis** continues to drag reported revenue optics even as it helps margin.
