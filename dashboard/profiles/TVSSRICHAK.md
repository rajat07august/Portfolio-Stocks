## 1. Business in Plain English

TVS Srichakra makes the tyres on India's scooters, motorcycles, auto-rickshaws and e-rickshaws, and sells them under the brand **TVS Eurogrip**. It is one of the two largest two-wheeler tyre makers in the country. A second, smaller business makes **off-highway tyres (OHT)** — the chunky ones on tractors, backhoes, graders, skid-steers and forklifts — which it exports to more than 85 countries.

The company runs two plant clusters: **Madurai, Tamil Nadu** (Vellaripatti and Therkutheru) and **Rudrapur, Uttarakhand** (Units I and II). Headcount at 31-Mar-2026 was **2,745 permanent employees**.

The revenue splits three ways, and the distinction matters more than anything else in this profile:

- **OEM supply** — selling to vehicle makers fitting tyres on new vehicles. High volume, low margin, brutal annual price negotiation. You win it on qualification and reliability, and you keep it on price.
- **Replacement market** — selling to the rider whose tyres have worn out, through depots and distributors. **This is where the money is.** Brand, distribution reach and shelf presence let you hold price.
- **Exports** — chiefly OHT, plus 2W/3W. Growing faster than domestic, per FY26 MD&A.

**The analogy: a razor-blade business where the company also has to sell the razors at cost.** The OEM channel is the razor — it is sold thin, sometimes barely above cost, and its real purpose is to put the brand under a rider who will later walk into a shop and buy a replacement. The replacement sale is the blade, and it is where the margin lives. A tyre maker's profitability is largely a question of how much of its output it can push through the blade channel rather than the razor channel.

Two structural features define the economics. First, **roughly two-thirds of the selling price is raw material** — natural rubber, synthetic rubber, carbon black, and crude-derived chemicals. The company says so itself in its own risk register: RM costs "represent a significant portion of our selling price." Second, tyre plants are **capital-hungry and must run hot** — a curing press that sits idle still depreciates, so utilisation drives margin far more than it does in an assembly business.

A third business, easy to miss, sits inside the subsidiary tree: **TVS Sensing Solutions** (₹178.39 Cr FY26 turnover) and **Fiber Optic Sensing Solutions** (₹30.04 Cr) make sensors, not tyres. As of **31-Aug-2026** this arm also owns 51% of **Weber Drivetrain**, a Pune EV motor-and-controller maker. This is a genuine, if small, diversification — see §5.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**1. Tyres are the only consumable on a vehicle that is also a safety component.** Wear is not optional and cannot be deferred indefinitely. This makes replacement demand a genuine annuity, tied to the *parc* — the installed base of vehicles on the road — not to new-vehicle sales. India's two-wheeler parc is enormous and still growing; every one of those vehicles needs two tyres every few years, regardless of whether anyone buys a new scooter this quarter.

**2. India's two-wheeler fleet is the largest on earth, and it is being replaced, not just expanded.** FY26 saw the 2W segment recover close to pre-COVID levels (FY26 MD&A), with a slow first half and a strong second half after GST cuts. A recovering new-vehicle market feeds the OEM channel now and the replacement channel in three years.

**3. Electrification changes the vehicle but not the tyre — and slightly favours the tyre maker.** EVs are heavier (battery mass) and deliver instant torque, both of which increase tyre wear. The company states it has been "assessed and cleared for supplies by a vast majority of the new clutch of OEMs manufacturing electric (EV) two-wheelers," and has "achieved significant share in the growing E-rickshaw segment." Unlike an exhaust or a gearbox supplier, a tyre maker faces no existential EV risk.

**4. India is becoming a tyre export base.** Tyre exports from India rose **9% in FY26 even against tariff restrictions**, outpacing the **6-8%** domestic demand growth (FY26 MD&A; FY25 domestic growth was ~4.4%). The PwC/ATMA *Viksit Bharat 2047* roadmap cited in the annual report projects India's share of global tyre trade value rising from **2.8% (FY24) to 11-12% (FY47)**.

**5. Off-highway is a genuinely large, genuinely global market.** The company sizes the OHT opportunity at **over US$20 billion**. Unlike 2W tyres — where India is the centre of gravity — OHT is a world market in which Indian producers are low-cost challengers to Michelin, Titan and Trelleborg. This is the segment where Balkrishna Industries built a ₹41,845 Cr business.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**The moats, honestly assessed:**

1. **The TVS name, and a real consumer brand in Eurogrip.** In a category where the buyer cannot evaluate the product before use, brand *is* the product. TVS is among the most trusted industrial names in southern India. Eurogrip was built deliberately to give the company a brand of its own rather than renting the group's.

2. **OEM qualification is a multi-year moat.** Getting onto a 2W maker's approved list requires years of testing, audits and consistent supply. Once there, you are rarely displaced — the switching risk for the OEM is a safety recall. The company supplies on a **just-in-time basis across the country**, which deepens the lock-in.

3. **Genuine R&D depth for a company this size.** Not just product development but "fundamental research on material science" (FY26 MD&A). The eight-year technical partnership with the **PETRONAS TVS India One Make Championship** is a real R&D feedback loop, not only sponsorship — the Aug-2026 launch of the **Protorq Racing** slick range came out of it.

4. **Distribution reach in the replacement channel** — a national network of depots and distributors, with FY26 spent on "structured programs to increase the Company's width and depth."

5. **An OHT export franchise with its own channel.** Exports to 85+ countries, and FY26 saw the company begin **placing its own personnel in global markets** rather than relying purely on distributors.

6. **A near-decarbonised cost base at the largest plant.** Renewable energy reached **~90% of electricity at the Madurai plants in FY26**, backed by group-captive solar SPV investments (Navia Two Power). In an energy-intensive process, this is a durable cost advantage and a hedge against grid tariff inflation.

7. **Financial credibility out of proportion to its returns.** India Ratings rates the company **IND AA-/Stable** with **IND A1+** on commercial paper — a high investment grade that a 7.85% ROCE business would not normally command, and which reflects the group's standing and the balance sheet's improvement.

**🔴 Where the moat is weaker — and this is the larger half of the section:**

- **The moat has not produced returns for a decade.** This is the single most important fact about the company, and it is the company's own disclosure. From the FY26 annual report's ten-year table (a *table*, not a chart — read directly):

  | | FY17 | FY20 | FY22 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | PBDIT / net sales | **14.5%** | 10.4% | 6.5% | 10.2% | 7.3% | **8.0%** |
  | PBIT / net sales | 11.7% | 5.7% | 3.3% | 6.7% | 3.4% | 4.1% |
  | Return on average net worth | **28.5%** | 11.2% | 6.1% | 9.6% | 5.3% | **7.3%** |

  Margins have roughly halved and return on net worth has fallen by three-quarters. Revenue grew from ₹1,970 Cr to ₹3,403 Cr (standalone) across the same ten years — **the company got much bigger and much less profitable at the same time.** That is the definition of growth without a moat holding.

- **It is a price-taker on two-thirds of its cost base.** No amount of brand protects against natural rubber and crude. Management's own FY27 comment is unambiguous, and appears twice in the MD&A: *"the start to FY27 has seen an unprecedented increase in raw material prices - largely driven by the Middle East conflict."*

- **Sub-scale against every listed peer.** At ₹3,942 Cr market cap it is roughly a **fourteenth of MRF** and a **tenth of Balkrishna**. In a business where scale buys procurement leverage and R&D amortisation, being the smallest listed player is a structural disadvantage, not a temporary one.

- **Concentration, conceded in the company's own risk register.** Risk #4, *Business Concentration Risk*: "Concentration has been in a few product and market segments and limited geographies."

- **🔴 No investor communication whatsoever.** No calls, no presentations, no guidance. For a public company of this size this is an outlier, and it is itself a governance signal — see §3.5.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Four threads, and they do not all pull the same way.**

**Thread 1 — a real, verified operating recovery.** Margins bottomed in FY25 (PBDIT/sales 7.3%, RONW 5.3%) and have turned: FY26 8.0% and 7.3%, Q1FY27 standalone pre-exceptional PBT six times the prior year. Three drivers, all documented: RM cost moderation through FY26, the company's own procurement and in-plant wastage programmes, and ~90% renewable power at Madurai. **This is not a paper recovery — CFO of ₹297 Cr at 111% of operating profit confirms it in cash.**

**Thread 2 — the recovery is being underwritten by an input cycle that has just reversed.** The same MD&A that reports the FY26 margin gain warns of "unprecedented" FY27 raw-material inflation. **Threads 1 and 2 are the same thread running in opposite directions**, and the sequencing matters: FY26's gain came substantially from input costs falling, and those costs are now rising. The mitigations management lists — strategic procurement, wastage reduction, finished-goods price increases — are the standard three, and only the third actually protects margin, at the cost of volume.

**Thread 3 — capacity is being built into the constraint, which is the right call.** At 80-85% utilisation the company is close to the ceiling, and ₹430 Cr is committed to lifting it, weighted toward Rudrapur (+40-45%) and OHT (+25%). **The OHT tilt is the strategically interesting part** — it is the higher-margin, larger-addressable-market, export-facing segment, and it is where Balkrishna demonstrated what scale in Indian OHT is worth. If the export franchise scales, the mix shifts toward the blade and away from the razor.

**Thread 4 — a quiet, cheap diversification out of rubber entirely.** The sensing arm now turns over **₹208 Cr combined and earns ₹13.3 Cr** — small, but profitable and growing (FOSSPL +317%), and it is the only part of the group earning a respectable return. Weber Drivetrain adds EV motors and controllers for **₹10 lakh**. **The stated object is "Electronics Capability Building."** *My inference: this is the beginning of a deliberate second leg, and it is being assembled at near-zero cost while nobody is watching — there has been no call, no presentation and no press release explaining it.*

**How they reinforce — or don't.** Threads 1, 3 and 4 describe a company that is fixing its cost base, expanding into its best segment and quietly buying optionality in electronics. That is a coherent turnaround. **Thread 2 is the one that decides whether any of it reaches the P&L in the next eight quarters** — and it is entirely outside management's control. The honest summary: **the operating story is better than it looks, the input cycle is worse than the last print implies, and the price already assumes the first and ignores the second.**

## 6. Why the Market Is Paying This Multiple

These are inconsistent with each other.** Screener's own trailing consolidated EPS is **₹120.63** (Q2FY26 ₹14.51 + Q3FY26 ₹14.56 + Q4FY26 ₹47.13 + Q1FY27 ₹44.43 — verified against the quarterly table and against the Q1FY27 filing). There is no peer in the set that is both more expensive and less profitable than any other; TVS Srichakra is the only name that sits at the extreme of both columns.

**So why is the market paying it?** Three defensible reasons and one undefensible one:

1. **The earnings are depressed, not the business.** At an 8% PBDIT margin against a 14.5% history, current EPS is cyclically low. Normalise the margin to even 11% and FY26 PAT would be roughly ₹180-200 Cr, putting the stock at ~20x — in line with peers. **The multiple is high because the denominator is small.** This is the strongest bull argument and it is a real one.
2. **Recovery momentum.** Two consecutive strong quarters (Q4FY26 EPS ₹47.13, Q1FY27 ₹44.43) against a weak FY25 make the trailing number look worse than the run-rate. Annualising the last two quarters alone gives EPS ~₹183 and a P/E of ~28x.
3. **Scarcity and float.** Only 76.6 lakh shares exist and promoters hold 45.7%. A small free float with rising DII interest amplifies moves in both directions — and BSE has queried the volume three times.
4. **The TVS name.** *My inference: the group multiple visible at TVS Supply Chain Solutions (81x on 10.1% ROCE) suggests a brand premium applied across this stable that the returns do not support.*

### Weighted return framework — 2 years, to FY28

**Base case (55%).** Revenue compounds ~10-12% to ~₹4,450 Cr by FY28 as Rudrapur lands. RM inflation caps OPM in the 8-9% band — better than FY25, short of the FY17 era. Lower interest as debt amortises, offset by higher depreciation on ₹430 Cr of capex. Super Grip narrows losses but does not turn. **FY28 PAT ~₹145 Cr at 22x → mcap ₹3,190 Cr → −19%.**

**Bull case (25%).** The Middle East input shock proves short. OHT exports scale into the +25% capacity, lifting mix and taking OPM toward 11%. Sensing/Weber contributes a visible second leg. Super Grip breaks even. **FY28 PAT ~₹210 Cr at 28x → mcap ₹5,880 Cr → +49%.**

**Bear case (20%).** RM inflation persists through FY27-28 and price increases cost volume. OPM slips back toward 6%. The ₹430 Cr capex arrives into a soft market, with depreciation and interest landing ahead of the revenue. Super Grip keeps bleeding. The multiple normalises to the peer group. **A return to the 52-week low of ₹2,815 → mcap ₹2,156 Cr → −45%.** *Note this is not a theoretical floor — the stock traded there within the last twelve months.*

**Weighted expected return = (0.55 × −19%) + (0.25 × +49%) + (0.20 × −45%) = −7.2% over two years.** Add ~1.5% of cumulative dividend → **≈ −5.7%, or roughly −3% CAGR.**

**The base case is negative on its own, and the distribution is wide (−45% to +49%). Per the §0A rule — when the honest answer is a negative expected return, write the negative number.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risks (the case against):**

1. **🔴 Raw materials, flagged by management as "unprecedented."** Two-thirds of the selling price. This is the dominant near-term variable and it is entirely exogenous.

   > 📈 **Factor check 19-Sep-2026:** Natural rubber has levelled off (+1.4–1.5% over three months after +27–31% over the year), but crude is +30.1%, which feeds synthetic rubber and carbon black. *(Numbers and sources: Input & factor check, at the top.)*

2. **🔴 The multiple.** 42.7x trailing on a 7.85% ROCE, against a peer group at 12-30x on 11-19% ROCE. Multiple compression alone can produce the bear case without a single operational miss.
3. **🔴 A decade of margin erosion that has not yet been explained.** PBDIT/sales 14.5% → 8.0%, RONW 28.5% → 7.3%. Two good quarters do not reverse a ten-year trend, and no management commentary exists to say what changed or what will change it.
4. **🔴 Super Grip USA:** −₹18.03 Cr on ₹91.82 Cr, funded by a ₹52.96 Cr parent loan, exposed to US tariffs. No disclosed path to breakeven.
5. **🔴 ₹430 Cr capex against ₹11.78 Cr cash and a 0.88 current ratio.** Debt will rise; depreciation will rise ahead of the revenue.
6. **🟡 Zero investor communication.** No calls, no presentations, no guidance. Every thesis here is reconstructed from filings. **Bad news would reach you late.**
7. **🟡 Disclosure control weakness** — the 14x Weber consideration error, corrected six weeks later at completion.
8. **🟡 Sub-scale.** Smallest listed peer by a wide margin in a scale business.
9. **🟡 Thin float, three BSE volume queries in fifteen months.** Volatility cuts both ways and liquidity is limited.
10. **🟡 FII holding at 0.92% and falling;** shareholder count down 10% in two years.

**What would prove the bear case wrong — the upgrade triggers:**

- **Q2FY27 (~Nov-2026) OPM holds above 7.5%** despite the RM shock. This is the single most important number and the cleanest test of whether the recovery is structural or cyclical.
- **Rudrapur capacity commissioned on schedule in H1 FY28** at the stated ₹210 Cr.
- **Super Grip reaches breakeven**, or is written down / exited.
- **The sensing + Weber arm crosses ~₹300 Cr of revenue**, making the second leg material rather than decorative.
- **The company holds its first-ever earnings call.** *This would be a genuine re-rating event in its own right, independent of the numbers — it would change the information asymmetry the current price embeds.*

**Explicit downgrade trigger:** to **Speculative** if Q2FY27 OPM falls below 6.0%, or if the ₹430 Cr capex is revised upward without an accompanying explanation.

## 8. 30-Second Memory Hook

**Yuvraj Singh, 2017.** A genuine former match-winner with a real record behind him, brought back into the side on the strength of a couple of good innings — and priced by the selectors as though the old strike rate had returned, when the five-year average said otherwise. The talent was never in question. The question was whether two good knocks were the comeback or the exception.

**Thesis in a sentence:** *India's #2 two-wheeler tyre maker is executing a real, cash-backed margin recovery — CFO at 111% of operating profit, debt down ₹119 Cr, underlying Q1FY27 profit six times the prior year — but it carries the highest multiple and the lowest returns in its entire peer group, its margins have halved over a decade with no public explanation, its US subsidiary is bleeding, and management has just warned of "unprecedented" raw-material inflation while never once holding an earnings call to discuss any of it.*

**The one number to remember: 42.7x earnings on a 7.85% ROCE.** MRF is 22x on 15.7%.

**The one thing to watch: Q2FY27 operating margin, due ~November 2026.**
