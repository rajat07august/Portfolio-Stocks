## 1. Business in Plain English

**63 moons is a cash pile with a court case attached, running a venture studio on the interest.**

Under its old name, Financial Technologies, this was one of India's great fintech stories: Jignesh Shah's company built **ODIN** (the trading terminal most Indian brokers used), and then the exchanges themselves — **MCX, IEX, MCX-SX, DGCX, SMX** — on a technology-licensing model. In July 2013 its subsidiary **National Spot Exchange (NSEL)** suffered a ~₹5,600cr payment default by its trading counterparties. Regulators declared the group not "fit and proper", the exchange stakes were sold (2014–15, *background — not from the filings read for this profile*), the company was renamed 63 moons in 2016, and thirteen years of litigation followed.

What is left in 2026:

1. **A treasury.** ~₹3,200cr of financial assets (bank deposits ₹1,610cr current + ₹785cr other non-current financial assets, investments ₹583cr, cash, loans) against ₹237cr of liabilities. It earned **₹147cr of interest in FY26** — more than the operating businesses' revenue for most of the last three years.
2. **A settlement to pay.** ₹1,950cr to NSEL's 5,682 larger traders, in exchange for their claims against the defaulters.
3. **A portfolio of new ventures**, announced in a May-2024 "strategic transformation" press release: **63SATS** (cybersecurity — a reseller of Morphisec, Zimperium, Blackberry and others, plus its own CYBX consumer app); **Ticker** (Web3 — the 3.0 Verse trading app, a Dubai arm, crypto/FIU-registered client technology, a new "63 Digital Asset Technologies"); **QiLegal** (legal-tech SaaS); **exchange technology** for the Metropolitan Stock Exchange; and a **regulatory data platform** used by the housing-finance regulator.

**The analogy:** *a retired fast bowler living on his savings while he funds his sons' start-ups — and with a large bill from an old lawsuit due before he can touch most of the savings.* Whether the shares are cheap depends almost entirely on how much the old lawsuit eventually pays back (the recovery rights) and whether any of the start-ups becomes a business.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

This is not one industry. The forces that matter, by asset:

1. **India's cybersecurity spend is growing fast and is now compliance-driven** — the DPDP Act's data-protection obligations (63SATS reports "65+ DPDP clients onboarded" in Q1FY27), RBI/SEBI cyber-resilience frameworks, and a consumer fraud epidemic. 63SATS's own framing cites a ₹1 lakh crore market by 2029 (press release, 28-May-2024 — *company's number, not verified*).
2. **Market-infrastructure technology is a niche with very few vendors** — exchanges, clearing corporations and regulators buy it rarely and stay for decades. 63 moons built MCX's platform and still runs MSE's (re-launched Jan-2026).
3. **Web3 / digital assets** — FIU-registered virtual-asset platforms in India and the UAE need trading and compliance technology. Ticker says its technology went live for "an FIU approved client" (May-2026). *Regulatory direction for crypto in India remains unsettled — my inference.*
4. **Asset recovery** — the NSEL decrees are enforced by a Supreme Court-appointed committee (Justice Nandrajog) that has "lifted the corporate veil" on some defaulters (press release, May-2024). After the OTS, those recoveries flow to 63 moons.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

1. **A balance sheet no start-up has.** ~₹3,200cr of financial assets; essentially no debt (borrowings ₹5cr, Screener). The ventures can be funded for years without external capital — though the subsidiaries have raised ₹252cr from outside investors in FY26 anyway (cash-flow statement).
2. **Exchange-grade technology lineage.** The team built and ran MCX's and MSE's trading systems; the regulatory data platform for the HFC regulator and a foreign bank's data warehouse are live (AR FY26 directors' report).
3. **The recovery option.** On payment of ₹1,950cr, 63 moons is assigned the claims of traders whose outstanding was ~₹4,610cr (FY26 note 4). Decrees of **₹4,294.77cr** against 21 defaulters are under execution; a ₹969cr decree against the 22nd (N.K. Proteins) is pending; **attached defaulter assets of "approx. ₹6,000cr"** sit under the MPID Act and PMLA, with overlap between the two (letter, 9-Nov-2024).
4. **63SATS has real revenue momentum** — ₹3.6cr (FY25) → ₹87cr (FY26) → ₹218cr billed in H1FY27 (*provisional, subsidiary's own update*), with 72 enterprise clients and 3.25 lakh paid CYBX subscribers.
5. **The NSEL overhang is closer to closure than at any time since 2013** — NCLT, NCLAT and the Supreme Court have all cleared the scheme; the MPID court released the first attachment on 29-Jul-2026.

### Where the moat is weaker, honestly

- **The old moat was sold.** ODIN (15.3% of FY24 revenue), MATCH and STP-Gate went to Synapsewave for ₹135cr, and the atom stake to NTT. What remains has **no demonstrated earning power**: segment results were −₹167cr in FY26 (rendered results).
- **63SATS is largely a reseller.** Group COGS was 61% of Q1FY27 revenue; "AI CyberOps" (own IP) was ₹13cr of the ₹288cr Q1 order book. *The "IP-led" description is the subsidiary's; the cost structure says mostly resale — my inference.* Its EBITDA is "negative by design" until FY28.
- **Ticker earned ₹27 lakh of standalone revenue in FY26, against a ₹35.8cr loss**, yet the group has bought its shares at a price valuing it at ~₹4,860cr (§3.5).
- **Exchange technology depends on one client, MSE**, a marginal exchange — and the one disclosure about it drew formal warnings from NSE and BSE (§3.5).
- **Recoveries are slow by construction.** Thirteen years after the default, NSEL has recovered a fraction; "around ₹179cr" was paid to small traders in 2013, the Competent Authority has paid more since. **The timing and size of future recoveries is not disclosed anywhere, and I have not estimated it from a primary source.**

## 5. Connecting the Dots

The threads do not yet reinforce each other; they compete for the same cash.

- **The settlement unlocks the balance sheet** — but consumes ~60% of it. After ₹1,950cr, ₹93cr of withheld dividends and the FY26 dividend, roughly **₹1,100–1,200cr of financial assets remain** (₹850–950cr if the Yes Bank AT-1 and NSEL receivables are excluded) — *my arithmetic.*
- **Interest income then falls.** At ~7% on the deployed balance, paying ₹1,950cr removes ~₹130cr a year of other income — *my estimate.* The group's operating loss before other income was **₹282cr** in FY26 (Screener). **Unless 63SATS turns profitable quickly, the post-settlement company burns ₹200–250cr a year against ~₹1,000cr — a four-to-five-year runway** (my inference).
- **Recoveries are the only thing that can refill it**, and their timing is set by courts.
- **Meanwhile capital is flowing into Ticker at ₹27 and to IGM-linked contracts.** Every rupee of treasury deployed at a valuation like Ticker's is a rupee that cannot later come back to shareholders — and the dividend history says shareholders have not received cash for a decade.

**What would make the dots connect:** settlement paid without dipping below ~₹1,000cr of treasury; a first visible recovery from the assigned claims; 63SATS reaching break-even on its own funding; and an end to purchases of Ticker stock.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

1. **🟢 Recoveries are faster and larger than assumed.** The Supreme Court committee has lifted the corporate veil on defaulters; attached assets are put at ~₹6,000cr. If even half converts within three years, the bull case is conservative. *This is the main way this profile is wrong.*
2. **🟢 63SATS is real.** ₹218cr billed in H1, 72 enterprise clients, 3.25 lakh paid consumer subscribers. A ₹600cr-revenue cybersecurity company could be worth more than the whole of 63 moons' market cap.
3. **🔴 The settlement costs more or takes longer.** The scheme is "63 moons and NSEL"; NSEL has little money. More MPID releases are needed; the timetable is not disclosed.
4. **🔴 Criminal and regulatory proceedings.** EOW charge-sheets, CBI (PEC/MMTC), ED prosecution under PMLA, SFIO complaint (stayed) — **not closed by the scheme**, which settles civil claims of the 5,682 specified creditors. The auditors cannot assess them.
5. **🔴 Board control.** MCA's petition to supersede the board and install three nominees is in abeyance, not withdrawn.
6. **🔴 Capital allocation.** ~₹100cr into Ticker at ₹27 in three months; ₹8cr lent to a promoter-influenced company; promoter-linked LLPs co-investing in subsidiaries. If the pattern continues, recoveries may not reach minority shareholders.
7. **🔴 Related-party revenue.** IGM's 33-year contract will be ~16% of revenue; IGM lost ₹31cr on ₹48cr of turnover.
8. **🔴 Disclosure quality.** Formal exchange warnings (Feb-2026); a restated segment table; subsidiary "investor updates" with provisional numbers released before consolidated audited figures.
9. **🟡 Yes Bank AT-1.** ~₹200cr still carried (my reading of Note 3); the Supreme Court has reserved judgment.
10. **🟡 Burn after interest income falls.** ~₹200–250cr a year against ~₹1,000cr (my estimate).
