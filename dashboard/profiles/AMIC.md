## 1. Business in Plain English

Amic Forging is a **specialty steel-forgings house serving India's capital-goods and process-industry customers**. Take a nuclear reactor pressure boundary, an oil refinery's coker unit, a cement plant's kiln drive shaft, a hydel turbine runner — the load-bearing components inside all of these need to be *forged* rather than machined from bar or cast, because forging aligns the metal's grain to survive extreme pressure, temperature and cyclic stress. Amic makes those parts: rounds, shafts, blanks, gear couplings, hubs, flanges and finished engineering spares in carbon, alloy, stainless, nickel and tool-alloy steel to AISI, BS, IS and DIN standards.

**Two things distinguish it from a plain forge shop:**

1. **Integrated single-facility workflow.** Forging, heat treatment and precision machining under one roof. The customer receives a finished, tested part, not a raw forging that must travel elsewhere to be finished.
2. **Custom-spec, small-to-mid batch.** Amic operates below the tonnage where Bharat Forge and Happy Forgings compete on unit cost, and above the level where jobbing shops compete on price. **Nuvama's Dec-2024 initiation put it starkly:** *"Given the high level of customisation, there is no direct competitor to AMIC and no new player is expected to enter in the near term… Previously, these customised products (~90% of domestic demand) were met by production in Ukraine and Europe."* **That import-substitution framing is the demand thesis.**

**Corporate history (verbatim from the RHP):** incorporated **19-Jun-2007** as *Kali Mata Forging Private Limited*; renamed *Amic Forging Private Limited* by EGM resolution of **2-Jan-2020**; converted to public and renamed *Amic Forging Limited* ahead of the **Aug-2024 BSE-SME IPO**. Registered office **3A, Garstin Place, 2nd Floor, Kolkata 700001**; head office Diamond Heritage, 16 Strand Road; **factory at Mahavir Complex, Baidyabati, Hooghly, West Bengal.** The name traces to the Chamaria family's predecessor partnership **Associated Manufacture & Industries Corporation**, a fifty-year Eastern-India steel-forging enterprise.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Grain flow.** A forged part is stronger than a cast or machined one because the metal's grain follows the component's contour. For anything that holds pressure or spins under load, forging is not a preference, it is a specification.

**Force 2 — Import substitution from a war zone.** Per Nuvama, roughly **90% of Indian demand for these customised forgings was historically met from Ukraine and Europe.** That supply base has been disrupted since 2022 and Indian buyers have been qualifying domestic alternatives ever since.

**Force 3 — India's process-industry capex cycle.** Amic's end markets — steel, cement, sugar, power, oil & gas, hydel — are all in a domestic capacity-addition phase, and each new plant needs forged spares for life.

**Force 4 — The aftermarket is the annuity.** A kiln drive shaft or coker unit component is a replacement item on a defined cycle. **Qualification is slow and the reorder is sticky.**

**Force 5 — Qualification into regulated sectors is where the margin is.** Aerospace, nuclear and defence pay materially more per tonne, and take years to enter. **Phase 2's 5,000-tonne press is explicitly aimed at that door.**

**Where AMIC sits.** ₹2,260cr in **Sector 3 (Auto Components & Forgings)**, alongside HAPPY, SSWL, SUNITA and NRBBEARING. **🔴 But note the sector label is a poor fit and always has been: Amic sells to refineries, cement kilns, power and hydel — not to vehicle makers.** *(This is the same misfit recorded in the Shared Sector Analysis rules in CLAUDE.md §1 — the placement is a taxonomy artefact, not a description of the business.)*

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — 🟢 A margin structure that is genuinely peer-best.** **FY26 OPM 30%, H2FY26 33%.** Against Happy Forgings' high-20s and the broader forging pack in the mid-teens, this is the number that justifies the attention.

**Moat 2 — 🟢 No direct competitor, per an independent initiation.** Nuvama's finding that the products are too customised for a new entrant is the clearest external validation on file.

**Moat 3 — Integration is being taken to its logical end.** Phase 1 adds **48,000 MTPA of in-house ingot from a standing start** — Amic will melt its own steel. **Management's stated rationale is working-capital as much as margin:** *"optimise the working-capital cycle as ingot integration reduces raw-material dependency."*

**Moat 4 — Capacity discipline, historically.** The RHP's own utilisation history:

| Year | Installed capacity (MT) | Production (MT) | Utilisation |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY21 | 6,300 | 3,961 | 62.87% |
| FY22 | 12,600 | 8,991 | 71.36% |
| **FY23** | 12,600 | 10,695 | **84.88%** |
| Q1FY24 | 12,600 | 2,640 | 83.06% |

**Capacity doubled 6,300 → 12,600 MT between FY21 and FY22 as demand grew; utilisation reached 84.88% in FY23, which is what triggered the IPO and Phase 1.** Management says existing assets ran at *"near-full utilisation"* through FY26. ***Capex has followed demand, not preceded it — and that is the single best reason to believe Phase 1 will fill.***

**Moat 5 — 🟢 Promoters hold at ₹5–12 a share against a ₹1,957 price and have not sold.** From the RHP's cost-of-acquisition table, the four Chamaria promoters carry an average cost of **₹4.74–₹12.17**. **That is a 160–410x paper gain, and promoter holding has moved 57.74% → 57.96% across six half-yearly snapshots with zero net selling.** ***This remains the single most persuasive fact about the company.***

**Moat 6 — Promoter money went in alongside, at market.** Three of the 28 allottees in the ₹96.88cr warrant issue were promoters subscribing at **₹1,211**, and **Rashmi Chamaria took 39,000 of the 65,000 warrants issued in Dec-2025 at ₹1,536.** *Promoters buying at 100–300x their own cost basis is not a costless signal.*

**Moat 7 — Zero net debt, historically.** Borrowings ₹4cr (FY24) → ₹0cr (FY25) → **₹6cr (FY26)**. The build has been equity-funded.

### Where the moat is weaker — and this is where the refresh lands

- **🔴 Scale.** ₹142cr of revenue against Happy Forgings' ~₹1,700cr and Bharat Forge's ~₹13,000cr. **On commodity tonnage Amic will never win on cost.**
- **🔴 The 30% margin has not been tested at 3x the volume.** Every historical margin datapoint comes from a small, hand-picked order book at near-full utilisation. **Filling 40,000 MT of forging and 33,000 MT of machining will require accepting work that the 18,000-MT book could refuse.**
- **🔴 The cash never showed up.** **FY26: ₹42.76cr of EBITDA, ₹9.85cr of operating cash flow, −₹54cr of free cash flow.** *(§4.)*
- **🔴 There is no CFO**, and there has not been one since 1-Apr-2026. *(§3.5.)*
- **🔴 The audited consolidated results contain an arithmetic error that a BSE notice and a re-filing did not fix.** *(§3.5.)*
- **⚠️ Growth has been bought with equity.** ~**₹151cr** raised across four preferential tranches since Oct-2024; share count up **10.4%**, and **11.0% fully diluted.**
- **⚠️ Effectively no institutional ownership.** FII **0.15%**, DII **0.56%**, **2,686 shareholders — down from 2,888.** *There is no institutional holder doing diligence alongside you.*
- **⚠️ Zero dividend since listing**, stated policy *"to conserve resources for future development."*

### 🔴 Finding 2 — The audited consolidated results contain an arithmetic error that survived a BSE notice and a re-filing

**What happened, in order:**

1. **30-May-2026** — the FY26 audited standalone and consolidated results are filed, with the auditor's *unmodified* opinion.
2. **30-Jun-2026** — **BSE issues a notice** *"regarding the discrepancies observed in the Consolidated Audit Report submitted by the Company for the financial year ended March 31, 2026."*
3. **3-Jul-2026** — Amic files a **revised Consolidated Audit Report**, stating the observations *"have been duly addressed"* and enclosing the report *"after incorporating the necessary rectifications."*

**🔎 I read both versions and rendered the relevant pages as images. What actually changed, and what did not:**

**What BSE was objecting to — the form of the report, not the numbers.** The **original** consolidated auditor's report is addressed *"To the Members of AMIC Forging Limited"* and audits *"the Consolidated financial statements… which comprise the consolidated Balance Sheet, Statement of Profit and Loss, Cash Flow Statement"* — **a Companies Act s.143 statutory audit report on financial *statements*.** The **revised** report is addressed *"To the Board of Directors of AMIC FORGING LIMITED (Holding Company)"*, is titled *"Report on the Audit of Consolidated Financial **Results**"*, and states it is submitted *"pursuant to the requirement of Regulation 33 of the SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015."* ***The company had filed the wrong instrument with its results. That is the discrepancy.***

**🔴 What did not change: the results table itself is identical in both filings — and it does not add up.** Rendering page 12 of the original and page 13 of the revision gives the same figures. *(₹ lakh):*

| | H2FY26 *(audited)* | H1FY26 *(un-audited)* | FY26 *(audited)* | FY25 *(audited)* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **VII — PAT before minority interest** | **1,586.81** | **1,240.11** | **2,826.92** | **3,555.70** |
| VIII — Minority interest | (0.24) | – | (0.24) | – |
| **IX — Profit after tax and minority interest** | **2,826.92** ❌ | **3,555.94** ❌ | **2,827.16** ✅ | **3,555.71** ✅ |
| **EPS basic (₹)** | 26.30 ❌ | **33.90** ❌ | 26.77 | 33.90 |

**Row IX must equal row VII less row VIII. For the two full-year columns it does. For both half-year columns it does not:**
- **H2FY26** should read **1,587.05**. It reads **2,826.92** — *the full-year figure.*
- **H1FY26** should read **1,240.11**. It reads **3,555.94** — *approximately the prior full year's figure.*
- **And the EPS line follows the error**: H1FY26 basic EPS is shown as **₹33.90**, identical to FY25's full-year EPS. **On ₹12.40cr of half-year profit and 1,04,88,400 shares the correct figure is about ₹11.8.**

> **⚠️ I checked whether this was an extraction artefact before writing it.** The filings are scanned images — 11.5 MB and 10.7 MB yielding 68 KB and 55 KB of text — which is exactly the signature §4A warns produces false column misalignment. **So I rendered both pages at 130–150 dpi and read them visually. The error is on the printed page in both versions.**
>
> ***Stated precisely, because precision matters here: the full-year numbers are internally consistent and are the ones every other source uses. The defect is confined to the derived half-year column and the EPS line beneath it.*** **It is a disclosure-quality failure, not evidence of anything about the underlying business.** **But it was signed by the Managing Director, carried an unmodified audit opinion, was flagged by the exchange, was re-filed — and is still wrong.**

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — 🟢 The margin transformation is real, and it is not an accounting artefact.** **OPM 3.1% → 12% → 13% → 23% → 30%, with H2FY26 at 33%** — achieved while absorbing a **197% increase in employee cost.** *Mix shift into finished, machined parts is doing exactly what management said it would.*

**Thread D — 🔴 The growth has consumed cash, not generated it.** **FCF −₹13cr → −₹26cr → −₹54cr; CFO/OP 49%; cash conversion cycle −25 → 184 days.** **Every rupee of the ₹150cr build has come from issuing equity.** *At 8–10x book that is a cheap way to fund it — but it is funding, not self-financing, and Phase 2's ₹165cr will need the same again.*

**Thread E — 🟢 The promoters are unambiguously aligned.** **₹5–12 cost basis, 160–410x paper gain, not a share sold across six half-yearly snapshots, and fresh promoter money in at ₹1,211 and ₹1,536.** *This is not a promoter cashing out into a re-rating.*

**Thread F — 🔴 But the finance and compliance function has fallen behind the company.** **Three CFOs in seven months and none for five; two exchange queries in three months; a conceded 24-hour disclosure breach; and audited results that still do not add up after being sent back.** *A ₹142cr family forge can run on trust and a Company Secretary. **Thread G — There is no forum at which any of this can be tested.** **No earnings call, no transcript, no analyst Q&A, no order-book disclosure, no management guidance.** The only channels are a half-yearly press release written by the company, an annual report, and — new this year — **a sell-side conference appearance.** **FII 0.15%, DII 0.56%, 2,686 shareholders.** *Nobody with a research budget is checking this alongside you.*

**How they interact.** ***A genuinely excellent small manufacturer — no direct competitor, a 900-basis-point margin re-rating, promoters who have never sold and keep buying, and a plant delivered on the day it was promised — has outgrown its own back office at precisely the moment it started consuming cash and issuing shares.*** **The operating case is intact and arguably strengthening. The evidentiary base under it has weakened, and the price has gone up anyway.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — 🔴 The multiple.** **79.9x on earnings that fell 20%, 9.9x book, zero dividend, on BSE-SME.** A de-rate to Happy Forgings' 66.5x on unchanged FY26 earnings is **−17% with nothing having happened.** **A de-rate to M M Forgings' 27x is −66%.**

**Risk 2 — 🔴 Phase 1 ramp risk, which is the whole thesis.** **Machining capacity is up 3.9x and ingot goes from zero to 48,000 MTPA.** ⚠️ **Melting steel is a different manufacturing discipline from forging it** — new yield curves, new energy management, new quality control, new working capital. **Management has never operated a melt shop.** *No order book, no ramp guidance and no utilisation target has been disclosed, and there is no call at which to ask.*
**⚠️ ACCUMULATING:** **~₹15cr of new annual depreciation lands in FY27 whether the revenue arrives or not.**
