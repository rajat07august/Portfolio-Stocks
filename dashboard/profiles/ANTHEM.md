## 1. Business in Plain English

**Anthem is a contract laboratory and factory for other people's drugs.** A biotech in Boston or Basel invents a molecule. It does not own a plant, does not employ 1,100 scientists, and does not want to. It hands the molecule to Anthem, which works out how to make it at gram scale, then kilo scale, then tonne scale — and if the drug is approved, manufactures the active ingredient commercially for as long as the drug sells.

The industry calls this **CRDMO** — Contract Research, Development and Manufacturing Organisation. The "R" matters: Anthem gets in at the discovery stage, years before anyone knows whether the molecule works. Most of those programmes die. The few that survive become annuities.

**Two things make Anthem's version of this unusual.**

First, it runs **both chemistry and biology** — traditional small-molecule synthesis (425 kilolitres of custom synthesis capacity) *and* fermentation-based biology (142 KL). Most Indian peers are one or the other. That lets it take enzymes, peptides, ADCs and oligonucleotides as well as ordinary chemistry, which is where the industry is going.

Second, it has a **second business** — **Specialty Ingredients (16.6% of FY26 revenue)**: probiotics, enzymes, nutraceutical actives, sold largely B2B into India. It is lower-margin, lower-glamour, and it turns over monthly rather than in six-month project cycles. Management treats it as ballast.

**The analogy.** Anthem is a **bespoke process-engineering workshop attached to a pilot plant**. A designer arrives with a sketch that has never been built. Anthem works out the tooling, proves it at small scale, then builds the production line — and once that line is qualified by a regulator, moving it elsewhere costs the customer years and a re-filing. The revenue is lumpy because a workshop ships when the customer says ship, not when the quarter ends. The margins are high because almost nobody else can do the first part.

**Scale today (FY26):** revenue **₹2,124cr**, PAT **₹592cr**, **2,282 employees** of whom **1,117 are scientific staff and 32 hold PhDs**. **14 commercial molecules** in production and **10 in late-stage (Phase III)**, on top of **100+ early-stage programmes**.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

1. **Innovators stopped owning factories.** Emerging biotech — venture-funded, 20 people, one molecule — never had plants. Big pharma has spent two decades shedding them. Someone has to make the drug; that someone is a CRDMO.
2. **The molecules got harder.** Peptides, ADCs, oligonucleotides and complex fermentation need capability that a generic API maker does not have. This is why Anthem's chemistry-*and*-biology combination is worth something.
3. **China+1 is real and it is regulatory, not rhetorical.** Anthem's own FY26 evidence: it **discontinued Chinese intermediate procurement entirely** for a key customer product and now makes the intermediate in-house. Material margin went **57.8% (FY24) → 59.7% (FY25) → 61.8% (FY26)** on the back of it. That is China+1 showing up in a P&L rather than in a slide.
4. **Switching costs are regulatory.** A commercial molecule's manufacturing site is written into the drug's filing. Changing it means re-validation, re-filing, and often a fresh inspection. Customers do not move for a 5% price cut.
5. **The funnel compounds.** 100+ early-stage programmes → 10 in Phase III → 14 already commercial. Each cohort that graduates adds an annuity. Four molecules commercialised in FY26 alone.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

1. **The best returns profile in the listed Indian CRDMO set.** FY26 **post-tax ROCE 30.8%** and **ROE 21.7%** — against Divi's 22.0%/16.5%, Neuland 26.5%/21.2%, Sai Life 19.6%/15.4%, Syngene 10.0%/7.8%, Cohance 5.8%/4.9%. Anthem is first on both measures.
2. **Margins that are genuinely structural, not mix luck.** EBITDA margin **39–43%** depending on basis, against a sector that mostly lives in the 20s. The driver is **material margin**, which has risen three years running on backward integration and yield work — a mechanism management can name and evidence, not an assertion.
3. **Chemistry plus biology under one roof.** 425 KL custom synthesis and 142 KL fermentation. Peers pick a lane.
4. **Early entry into the customer's programme.** Anthem takes molecules at discovery. That is expensive and slow, and it is why the relationships last: *"These relationships are a timeframe of maturing over 3 or 4 years… every time we onboard a new customer, it gives us a lot of hope that this is now going to span over many years."* (Ajay Bhardwaj, Q1FY27)
5. **A balance sheet with no arguments in it.** Net cash **₹1,720cr** at 30-Jun-2026 (from ₹785cr a year earlier), borrowings down **₹113cr → ₹54cr**, **zero promoter pledge** (see §3.5), FY26 **CFO ₹844cr = 126% of operating profit**, FCF **₹637cr**. **ICRA upgraded to AA (Stable)/A1+ on 2-Jul-2026**, from AA− (Positive).
6. **Europe-weighted, not US-weighted.** FY26: **Europe 53.9%, North America 27.3%, India 14.9%.** In a year when US tariff exposure repriced half this watchlist, Anthem's largest market is not America.
7. **Technology as a selling argument, not a slogan.** Flow chemistry and biocatalysis are used to cut the customer's cost of goods while holding Anthem's margin — the MD's framing: *"you're not just a pair of hands, you're actually contributing to innovation."*
8. **Anthem's own capital built the plants.** 100% OFS IPO. No dilution, no fresh-issue overhang.

### Where the moat is weaker

- **Three customers are 56.71% of revenue.** Whatever else is true, this is the business.
- **Anthem does not control its own quarter.** Revenue lands when the customer takes delivery. Q3FY26 −15%, Q4FY26 +27%, Q1FY27 −23%. Management is explicit and honest about it — but it means the P&L is not a control variable.
- **The MD's own warning about the moat.** *"What you might consider specialty today will become fine chemicals much faster. A fine chemical will go towards commoditization much faster than before."* That is the CEO describing the decay rate of his own advantage.
- **No order book is disclosed in rupees.** Only a percentage — *"60% of the order book for the full year"* — with no base, no ageing, no comparative. It cannot be tracked quarter to quarter.
- **Unit 3 (Neoanthem) is not yet a moat, it is a cost.** See §4.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**The threads reinforce each other on the way up, and they will reinforce each other on the way down. That symmetry is the whole investment question.**

**Upward:** early entry at discovery → a 100-programme funnel → 10 in Phase III → commercial annuities (14 today, 4 added in FY26) → those annuities need capacity → Units 3 and 4 → capacity attracts Big Pharma, who audit before they commit → more programmes at discovery. Backward integration out of China raises material margin, which funds the capex from internal cash, which is why the company has ₹1,720cr of net cash without ever having raised a rupee from the market.

**Downward, and it is the same chain:** the annuities are concentrated in three customers. Those customers set the delivery schedule, so they set Anthem's quarter. Capacity is being built for molecules that have not yet been approved, so an empty Unit 3 costs ₹79cr a year while it waits. Depreciation from Unit 4 arrives in FY28 whether or not the volumes do. And a fixed cost base — the CFO's own words, *"employee costs are more fixed in nature"* — turns a revenue miss into a bigger margin miss.

**The load-bearing question is therefore not "is this a good business."** It obviously is: 30.8% ROCE, 126% cash conversion, no debt, no pledge, best-in-class margins, and a moat with a regulatory lock on it. **The question is whether the FY26 deceleration and the two shrinking quarters are the timing noise management says they are, or the first evidence that a three-customer revenue base has stopped compounding at 20%.** Management has staked its credibility on the former and put a testable claim on the record: *"We clearly see this Q1 as the softest quarter for the year… you will see the numbers coming back as a recovery in Q2, Q3 and Q4."*

**H2FY27 settles it.** Not the thesis — the direction of the multiple.

### Weighted return framework (2-year, to FY28)

**Base case — 55%.** The Q1 shortfall is timing, as described. H2FY27 recovers; FY27 revenue ~₹2,350cr (+11%), FY28 ~₹2,800cr (+19%) as Unit 3 fills and 2–3 late-stage molecules commercialise. PAT margin holds ~26.5% → **FY28 PAT ~₹740cr**. The multiple de-rates to **65x** — still a large premium, but reflecting growth actually delivered rather than promised → **market cap ₹48,100cr = −9%.** With dividends, **≈ −8%.**

**Bull case — 25%.** H2FY27 lands hard, as it did in Q4FY26. FY27 +20% and FY28 +22%; Neoanthem turns profitable, removing the tax drag and a ₹79cr loss; CDSCO clears Semaglutide and GLP-1 becomes a real line; two of the ten Phase III molecules go commercial; the newly-onboarded Big Pharma relationships start contributing. **FY28 PAT ~₹880cr at 85x → ₹74,800cr = +41%.**

**Bear case — 20%.** One of the three top customers in-sources, loses share, or reformulates. Revenue does not recover: FY27 ₹1,950cr, FY28 ₹2,050cr. Operating deleverage against a fixed cost base plus Unit 4 depreciation from FY28 compresses PAT margin to ~22% → **FY28 PAT ~₹460cr**. The multiple breaks to **40x** on a business the market no longer treats as a compounder → **₹18,400cr = −65%.**

> **The bear case is severe by construction, and it is meant to be.** At 89x, a de-rating and an earnings miss multiply. *Per the §0A rule: when the honest answer is a −65% bear case, write the −65%.*

**Weighted expected return ≈ (0.55 × −8%) + (0.25 × +41%) + (0.20 × −65%) = −7.7% over two years.** Roughly **−4% CAGR**, dividend yield of 0.21% immaterial.

### Conviction: **MEDIUM-LOW**

**Not a quality judgement — a price judgement, and the two should not be confused.** On operating economics Anthem would rank in the top three names on this watchlist: 30.8% ROCE, 126% cash conversion, no debt, no pledge, honest management, and a moat with regulatory switching costs. **There is nothing wrong with the business.**

**What is wrong is the arithmetic of buying it here.** The base case does not make money. The bull case needs the funnel, GLP-1, Neoanthem and a sustained multiple all to work together. The bear case is a two-thirds loss and it only requires one of three customers to change its mind. **A stock 1.4% off its 52-week high, on 89x trailing earnings that are currently shrinking, with 57% of revenue in three unnamed accounts, is not offering a margin of safety.**

**Margin of safety.** At **60x FY27E PAT of ~₹640cr** the market cap is ~₹38,400cr, or **≈ ₹680 per share** — a level the stock traded at within the last twelve months (52-week low ₹579). **Interesting again in the ₹680–720 range**, or sooner if H2FY27 delivers the recovery management has promised *and* the multiple has not already re-rated to price it.

**Upgrade trigger:** two consecutive quarters of YoY CRDMO growth **plus** Neoanthem at or above break-even. **Downgrade trigger:** Q2FY27 fails to recover, or top-3 concentration rises again, or any disclosure that a top-3 customer is reducing scope.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

1. **🔴 Customer concentration — the risk that contains all the others.** Top 3 = 56.71%. Anthem does not name them, does not disclose molecule-level concentration, and does not publish a rupee order book. **A single account moving to dual-source could take 10–20% off revenue in a year.** Falling from 61.18% is genuine progress and does not change the order of magnitude.
2. **🔴 The multiple is the position.** 89x with TTM revenue below the last full year. Any quarter that disappoints costs twice — earnings and rating.
3. **🔴 Two of the last three quarters shrank.** Q3FY26 −15.1%, Q1FY27 −22.6%. Management's "lumpiness" explanation is coherent and consistent, and FY26 as a whole supports it. **But the same evidence is consistent with a growth base that has plateaued, and there is no guidance on record to distinguish them.**
4. **🔴 No FY27 guidance exists.** After missing FY26's revenue number, management declined to give one twice. **There is nothing to hold them to.**
5. **🔴 Neoanthem must turn.** ~₹570cr of parent capital, ₹71cr of revenue, −₹79cr PAT, −₹141.7cr accumulated reserves, ₹49cr of parent guarantees. **Break-even was promised for FY27.**
6. **🟡 Capacity is running ahead of demand.** Gross fixed asset turnover 1.51x → 1.10x. Unit 2's custom-synthesis utilisation *fell* 65% → 50% after its expansion. ₹1,200cr more is going into Unit 4, ₹700cr of it in FY27 — with FY28 depreciation arriving regardless of volumes.
7. **🟡 GLP-1 has been a story for four calls and zero rupees.** Flagged from Aug-2025; still awaiting CDSCO. **Nothing in the current numbers depends on it — which also means nothing in the current numbers would be hurt if it never arrives. Do not underwrite it.**
8. **🟡 The Upside Sharing Arrangement, and what the institutions did about it.** ₹127.68cr to three founders keyed to a shareholder's exit price. Disclosed impeccably; **21.67% of institutional votes against, versus ≤4.8% on every other resolution.** The arrangement is now discharged, but it establishes what this board will recommend.
9. **🟡 The promoter-group exit's mode is unreconciled.** 1.71cr shares recorded as an "Open Market" sale on a day NSE traded 12.53 lakh. **Stated as an open question, not a finding.**
10. **🟡 Three EBITDA bases and a thin quarterly deck.** The Q1FY27 deck runs four substantive slides — no pipeline detail, no geography, no order book, no customer split. Peers publish 30–40 pages. **Disclosure is compliant; it is not generous.**
11. **🟡 Europe is 53.9% of revenue.** Usually a tariff advantage against US-exposed peers. It is also a single-region dependency, and European pharma pricing is under its own political pressure.
12. **🟡 PhD headcount fell 35 → 32** while total headcount rose 2,062 → 2,282. Trivial in isolation; worth watching in a company whose pitch is *"science-led."*
13. **🟡 Chairman, MD and CEO are one person**, with an investor nominee on the board and 4 of 8 independent. Founder-normal, and a single point of both judgement and key-man risk.

## 8. 30-Second Memory Hook

**Anthem is VVS Laxman.**

Technically the most gifted player in the room — a touch nobody else in the Indian CRDMO set has, worth watching for its own sake. **Best-in-peer ROCE at 30.8%, margins in the 40s, cash conversion at 126%, no debt, no pledge, and a plant that was paid for out of profits rather than out of the market.** But he does not score evenly. **And Laxman's greatest work came in a handful of partnerships.** So does Anthem's: **three customers, 56.71% of revenue, none of them named.**

**Thesis in a sentence:** *An outstanding contract-manufacturing franchise with the best returns in its sector and the least control over its own quarter, priced at 89x trailing earnings that are currently shrinking — the business deserves the premium, the entry price does not offer one, and H2FY27 will decide which.*
