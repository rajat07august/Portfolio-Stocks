**Read this quarter honestly and it says two things at once.**

**Against last year it looks excellent: PAT +31.6%.** **Against last quarter it is a sharp step down: revenue −12.9%, PAT −26%, margin 28% → 24%.** Q4FY26 was the best quarter in the company's history; Q1FY27 gave back most of it. Both statements are true, and a refresh that reported only the +31.6% would be doing what v1 did with Garware's "highest ever."

**And the growth is not revenue growth. Revenue rose 1.5%.** The entire PAT increase came from cost:

> Cost of materials consumed ₹63.60cr, *less* the ₹4.30cr inventory build = **₹59.29cr of net material cost, against ₹64.70cr in Q1FY26 — down 8.3% on revenue that was up 1.5%.**

**Why material cost fell is the most important sentence in this refresh, and it comes from the FY26 Annual Report:**

> *"Owing to the emergence of **Hide as a competitive raw material** for Gelatin manufacturing globally, **the price of Gelbone (premium quality Crushed Bone) reduced significantly.** Your Company has utilized this opportunity and has **imported substantial quantity of Gelbone** during the year."*

**The same force is cutting both ways.** Cheap gel bone is expanding Nitta's margin. But hide-based gelatin is *also* the reason Nitta's selling price is falling — the AR states plainly: *"There was a decrease in sales realisation per unit of Gelatin in line with the global prices. This downward trend is due to the **emergence of Hide as a cheaper alternative** for Gelatin preparation and the impact of US tariff measures."*

***This is my inference, not management's stated position:*** the margin expansion of the last three quarters is a **spread trade on a raw-material dislocation, not a structural repricing of the product.** Bone prices fell faster than gelatin prices. When bone normalises — or when hide-based capacity forces gelatin prices down further without a matching bone move — the spread compresses. **Nothing in the disclosure tells you how long the dislocation lasts, and there is no concall at which to ask.**

**FY26 volume vs price, verbatim from the AR — the clearest single disclosure the company makes:**

### 🔴 The finding v1 could not have had: the SION revision

**On 5-Jun-2026 the DGFT revised the Standard Input Output Norms for Gelatin and Ossein.** From the company's own Reg-30 letter:

> *"the permissible input-output ratio for the manufacture of 1 MT of Gelatine has been **reduced from 7.35 MT to 3.92 MT of gel bones**. Further, the norm for the manufacture of 1 MT of Ossein has been prescribed at 2.24 MT of gel bones."*

**That is a 47% cut in the duty-free gel-bone import entitlement per tonne of gelatin.** Nitta imports degreased crushed bone as its key raw material, under the **Advance Authorisation scheme**.

**On 31-Jul-2026 the company issued an update:**

> *"the Company has made representations to the DGFT through the industry body for restatement of the SION based on technical assessment. Further, the Company has evaluated the matter legally and understood that **there is no financial Impact** due to this revision. The customers for Ossein and Limed Ossein have **agreed to absorb the obligation** if any… Further **from July 2026, the Company has started to import based on the Duty Drawback Scheme.** Based on the current position, the change in the SION has **no significant financial bearing** on the Company."*

**🔴 That assertion is in direct tension with what the company said in its own annual report three weeks earlier:**

> *"All these imports of bone are under **advance authorization scheme** as envisaged in the EXIM policy of the Government of India. This has helped your Company to reduce its dependency on domestic raw materials and **has cost advantage as well.**"*
> — FY26 Annual Report, Directors' Report, filed 8-Jul-2026

**Both statements cannot be fully true.** If Advance Authorisation carries a stated cost advantage, moving to Duty Drawback — which refunds a notified percentage rather than exempting the actual duty, and is typically the less generous of the two — is not cost-neutral. **I am not asserting the impact is large; I am asserting that "no significant financial bearing" is a management claim that has not yet been tested against a reported number.** *(This tension is my finding, not something either document acknowledges.)*

**The critical timing point: Q1FY27 does not test it.** The quarter ran April–June. **The switch to Duty Drawback began in July 2026 — Q2FY27.** So the first quarter carrying the new import regime reports in **late October / early November 2026**, and **the margin line in that quarter is the single most important number for this thesis.** Anyone reading Q1FY27's 24% margin as evidence that SION was harmless is reading a quarter that predates it.

**Two mitigants, both real:** Ossein/Limed Ossein customers have contractually agreed to absorb any export-obligation shortfall, and a representation for restatement is live with DGFT through the industry body. **Neither is resolved.**

### ⚖️ Second regulatory item: the Supreme Court hears the customs matter next month

From Note 5 to the Q1FY27 consolidated results — a matter running since FY2018-19:

- Commissioner of Customs raised a duty demand of **₹8.77cr plus a penalty of ₹10.91cr** for import of fish protein under Advance Authorisation, alleging misclassification.
- CESTAT gave a partly favourable order; **the Kerala High Court ruled for the company on 26-Jun-2025**, finding *"there had been no breach in any of the conditions of advance authorisation issued to the Company with respect to its imports."*
- **The customs department filed a Special Leave Petition. The Supreme Court has admitted the matter, scheduled the final hearing for September 2026, and granted an interim stay on the operation of the High Court's judgment.**
- **Contingent liability carried: ₹18.20cr.** Provision created: ₹1.49cr.

**₹18.20cr against a company earning ~₹25cr a quarter and carrying a ₹1,461cr market cap.** Not existential — but the hearing is **next month**, it is unprovided for beyond ₹1.49cr, and note that the underlying subject matter is *the same Advance Authorisation scheme* that the SION revision has just curtailed. **Two independent regulatory threads, both attached to the same import mechanism.**

### 🏭 Third item v1 missed entirely: the subsidiary is not a going concern

**Bamni Proteins Limited** (100%-held, Chandrapur district, Maharashtra) — from Note 4 to the consolidated results and the FY26 AR:

- **13-Mar-2024:** Maharashtra State Pollution Control Board issued a **closure order** for failure to comply with conditions on discharge of treated effluent. Manufacturing stopped.
- MPCB issued a restart order on 2-Aug-2024. **The subsidiary's board decided on 25-Jul-2024 to permanently close the unit anyway.**
- The Board **"concluded that the subsidiary ceased to be a going concern"**; its financial statements are prepared **on other-than-going-concern basis**, assets carried at the lower of cost or estimated net realisable value.
- **16-Jun-2025:** the holding company's board decided to dispose of the assets and authorised the MD to invite expressions of interest for sale of the property, plant and equipment.

**Two things follow.** First, the "discontinued operations" line in the P&L is Bamni, and it is small (₹0.10cr loss in Q1FY27) — so this is a clean-up, not a bleed. Second, and more important: **the closure was caused by effluent-discharge non-compliance.** ValuePickr post #66 (4-Jun-2026) names *"pollution norms"* as the key risk on this business, and Bamni is the worked example of what that risk looks like. Which leads directly to:

> **⚠️ The FY26 AR records that Consent to Operate has been renewed to 30-Jun-2028 for the main divisions, but that the consent to operate "for the Reva Division… is in process."** Reva (Jhagadia, Bharuch, Gujarat) was also fined **₹2 lakh by Narmada Clean Tech** (a GIDC subsidiary) in June 2025 on an effluent matter. **The fine is trivial. ## 1. Business in Plain English

**Gelatin** is a protein extracted from animal bones and hides — used to make **pharmaceutical capsule shells** (every hard-shell capsule you swallow), **gummy candies**, **desserts** (jelly, marshmallows), **premium food thickeners**, and **collagen peptide supplements** (the wellness/nutrition category exploding globally). **Nitta Gelatin India** is the Indian arm of the Japan-based **Nitta Gelatin Inc.** — a 100+ year-old Japanese specialty ingredients company. This is a **50-year-old joint venture between the Kerala family (Kapadia/Vijayan lineage) + Nitta Gelatin Japan**, producing gelatin, collagen peptide, and ossein (the bone-derived intermediate) from a manufacturing base in Kochi, Kerala.

The 30-second business snapshot:
- **HQ Kochi, Kerala** — founded 1975 (50-year heritage)
