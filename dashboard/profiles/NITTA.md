## 1. Business in Plain English

Nitta Gelatin India takes **crushed animal bone** and turns it into three products that end up inside things you swallow.

**Ossein** is the protein scaffold left when bone is stripped of its minerals. **Gelatin** is ossein cooked into the substance that makes capsule shells, tablets and confectionery work. **Collagen peptide** is gelatin broken down further into a nutraceutical powder. The mineral by-product — **dicalcium phosphate (DCP)** — is sold into animal feed.

**The useful analogy is an oil refinery running on bone.** One input stream, several output grades, and **the entire economics live in the spread between what the input costs and what the outputs fetch.** *That framing is the key to this profile.*

**Who owns it is unusual and matters:**

> The company's own letterhead, on every filing: *"Joint venture of **Kerala State Industrial Development Corporation Ltd.** and **Nitta Gelatin Inc.**"*

**KSIDC is a Government of Kerala public-sector undertaking. Nitta Gelatin Inc. is a Japanese industrial parent.** **There is no promoter family.** The Chairman is **APM Mohammed Hanish IAS**, and KSIDC's board seat is held by a serving IAS officer that rotates by government order — on **14-Jul-2026** KSIDC replaced **Shri Vishnuraj P IAS** with **Shri Arun K Vijayan IAS**.

**The 30-second snapshot:**
- **Kochi, Kerala** · **50th AGM** held 2026 · **JV of KSIDC (Kerala PSU) and Nitta Gelatin Inc. (Japan)**
- **FY26 revenue ₹588 cr (+11.5%)** · operating profit ₹137cr (23%) · **PAT ₹111 cr (+35%)** · EPS **₹121.81**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Every capsule needs a shell.** Gelatin is the default hard- and soft-capsule material for the global pharmaceutical industry, and demand tracks drug volumes rather than economic cycles.

**Force 2 — India has the bone and the cost position.** Domestic supply plus duty-advantaged imports have historically made Indian ossein and gelatin globally competitive.

**Force 3 — Collagen peptide is the higher-value adjacency.** Nutraceutical demand — joint health, skin, sports nutrition — carries better economics than bulk gelatin and is where the growth leg sits.

**Force 4 — 🔴 But hide-based gelatin is changing the industry's cost curve, and it cuts both ways.** From the FY26 Annual Report:
> *"Owing to the emergence of **Hide as a competitive raw material** for Gelatin manufacturing globally, the price of **Gelbone (premium quality Crushed Bone) reduced significantly.** Your Company has utilized this opportunity and has **imported substantial quantity of Gelbone** during the year."*

**And the same document, on the other side of the trade:**
> *"There was a **decrease in sales realisation per unit of Gelatin** in line with the global prices. This downward trend is due to the **emergence of Hide as a cheaper alternative** for Gelatin preparation and the impact of **US tariff measures**."*

**Force 5 — DCP is a genuine second revenue line**, not a waste stream, selling into animal feed at pricing that has held up.

**Where NITTA sits.** ₹1,428cr in **Sector 12 (Pharma & Excipients)** alongside HALEOS. **The natural comparable is Narmada Gelatines**, which the refresh used as the de-rating anchor in its bear case.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — A 50-year Japanese technical parent.** Nitta Gelatin Inc. supplies process technology and global market access that a standalone Indian processor could not assemble.

**Moat 2 — Returns that are genuinely elite for a commodity processor.** **ROCE 31.4%, ROE 24.9%** — on a business that turns bone into powder.

**Moat 3 — 🟢 Cash conversion that is better than the P&L suggests.** **CFO/OP: 26% → 109% → 97% → 83% → 115%.** **FY26 CFO of ₹125cr against ₹137cr of operating profit, with ₹73cr of free cash flow.** *Four of the last five years above 80%.*

**Moat 4 — A near-debt-free balance sheet.** Interest has fallen ₹4cr → ₹3cr → ₹3cr → ₹2cr → **₹1cr TTM**, and is now **₹0cr in the last two quarters.**

**Moat 5 — Vertical breadth across the bone stream.** Ossein, gelatin, collagen peptide and DCP from one input, which lets the company sell into whichever output is priced best.

**Moat 6 — Duty-advantaged imported raw material**, historically under the **Advance Authorisation** scheme — *though this is precisely what the SION revision has just disturbed (§4)*.

**Moat 7 — An ownership structure that will not sell.** **74.49% unmoved across every quarter on record.** *Genuinely stable — for a reason worth understanding properly (below).*

**Where the moat is weaker — and the ownership belongs here, not above.**
- **🔴 The margin expansion is a spread, not a repricing.** *Cheap bone widened it; falling gelatin realisation is narrowing it from the other side.*
- **🔴 There is no concall.** **No management Q&A exists at which to test any of this.**
- **🔴 The SION revision cut the duty-free gel-bone entitlement 47%**, and the company's "no financial impact" claim sits in tension with its own annual report (§4).
- **⚠️ Revenue growth has stalled.** Q1FY27 revenue **+1.4%**; the FY26 growth was volume, offset by falling price on the two main products.
- **⚠️ The stable 74.49% is a state government and a Japanese parent, not a family compounding alongside you.** **Board continuity follows IAS transfer cycles**, and capital allocation is a negotiation between a Japanese industrial parent and a state development corporation. ***That is a different governance animal and should be read as such.***
- **⚠️ Institutional ownership is effectively nil** — **DII 0.95%, FII 0.05%.** *There is no institutional bid to re-rate this.*
- **⚠️ The Gelatin Expansion has no disclosed capacity or commissioning date.**

**From the FY26 Annual Report:**

### 🔴 The SION revision — the single most important pending item

**On 5-Jun-2026 the DGFT revised the Standard Input Output Norms for Gelatin and Ossein.** From the company's own Reg-30 letter:
> *"the permissible input-output ratio for the manufacture of 1 MT of Gelatine has been **reduced from 7.35 MT to 3.92 MT of gel bones.** Further, the norm for the manufacture of 1 MT of Ossein has been prescribed at 2.24 MT of gel bones."*

**That is a 47% cut in the duty-free gel-bone import entitlement per tonne of gelatin.** **Nitta imports degreased crushed bone as its key raw material, under the Advance Authorisation scheme.**

**On 31-Jul-2026 the company issued an update:**
> *"the Company has made representations to the DGFT through the industry body for restatement of the SION… the Company has evaluated the matter legally and understood that **there is no financial Impact** due to this revision. The customers for Ossein and Limed Ossein have **agreed to absorb the obligation** if any… Further **from July 2026, the Company has started to import based on the Duty Drawback Scheme.** Based on the current position, the change in the SION has **no significant financial bearing** on the Company."*

**🔴 That sits in direct tension with the Annual Report three weeks earlier:**
> *"All these imports of bone are under **advance authorization scheme**… This has helped your Company to reduce its dependency on domestic raw materials and **has cost advantage as well.**"*
> — FY26 Annual Report, Directors' Report, filed 8-Jul-2026

**Both statements cannot be fully true.** If Advance Authorisation carries a stated cost advantage, moving to **Duty Drawback** — which refunds a notified percentage rather than exempting the actual duty, and is typically the less generous — **is not cost-neutral.** ***I am not asserting the impact is large; I am asserting that "no significant financial bearing" is a management claim that has not been tested against a reported number.*** *(This tension is my finding, not something either document acknowledges.)*

**🔴 THE CRITICAL TIMING POINT, and it is easy to get wrong.** **Q1FY27 ran April–June. The switch to Duty Drawback began in July 2026 — Q2FY27.** **The first quarter carrying the new import regime reports in late October / early November 2026, and the margin line in that quarter is the single most important number for this thesis.** ***Anyone reading Q1FY27's 24% margin as evidence that SION was harmless is reading a quarter that predates it.***

**Two mitigants, both real and both unresolved:** Ossein/Limed Ossein customers have **contractually agreed to absorb any export-obligation shortfall**, and a **representation for restatement is live with DGFT** through the industry body.

### ⚖️ The second regulatory thread — and note what it attaches to

**₹18.20cr against a company earning ~₹25cr a quarter and carrying a ₹1,428cr market cap is not existential.** ***But note the subject matter: it concerns the same Advance Authorisation scheme that the SION revision has just curtailed.*** **Two independent regulatory threads, both attached to the same import mechanism.**

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — This is a spread business, and the spread is currently wide.** Cheap gel bone (because hide substitution collapsed bone prices) against gelatin realisation that is falling for the same reason. **Margin went 20% → 24% on that gap, not on volume.**

**Thread B — 🔴 And the same force is closing it from the other side.** Hide-based capacity is what is pushing gelatin prices down. **When bone normalises, or gelatin falls further without a matching bone move, the spread compresses.** *Nobody has told you the duration, and there is no call at which to ask.*

**Thread C — Volume is growing everywhere; price is falling where it matters.** **Gelatin +6.6% volume with realisation down; Collagen Peptide +57% volume with realisation −14.5%; DCP +9% volume and +13.7% price.** *The by-product is the only line pricing up.*

**Thread D — 🔴 Two regulatory threads hang off the same import mechanism.** **The SION revision cut the duty-free gel-bone entitlement 47%**, and **the ₹18.20cr Supreme Court customs matter concerns the same Advance Authorisation scheme.** *Concentration of regulatory exposure on one dependency.*

**Thread E — Environmental compliance has already cost a subsidiary.** **Bamni is closed on effluent non-compliance; Reva's consent to operate is "in process."** *The precedent exists inside the company.*

**Thread F — 🟢 The cash is real and the balance sheet is clean.** **CFO/OP 115%, FCF ₹73cr, interest down to zero in the last two quarters.** *This is a genuinely well-run processor.*

**Thread G — But the growth leg has no date.** **The Gelatin Expansion has no disclosed capacity and no commissioning date**, against **₹176cr of FY26 investing outflow.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**🔴 Risk 1 — The SION / Duty Drawback margin hit, untested until Q2FY27.** **A 47% cut to the duty-free gel-bone entitlement per tonne of gelatin**, with the switch to Duty Drawback starting **July 2026**. **Management declared *"no significant financial bearing"* on 31-Jul — three weeks after its own Annual Report said Advance Authorisation *"has cost advantage as well."***
> **🕐 THE FIRST TESTABLE QUARTER REPORTS LATE OCTOBER / EARLY NOVEMBER 2026.** ***Q1FY27's 24% margin is not evidence — that quarter predates the change.*** **Signal to watch: Q2FY27 operating margin against 22%.**

**⚖️ Risk 2 — The Supreme Court customs matter.** **₹18.20cr, hearing September 2026, provided for only to ₹1.49cr.** *Not existential against a ₹1,428cr market cap — but note it concerns **the same Advance Authorisation scheme** the SION revision just curtailed.* **Two regulatory threads, one dependency.**

**🏭 Risk 3 — Pollution-control consent, with a worked precedent inside the company.** **Subsidiary Bamni Proteins is no longer a going concern, closed on effluent-discharge non-compliance.** **Reva Division's Consent to Operate is *"in process"*** (main divisions renewed to 30-Jun-2028), after a **₹2 lakh Narmada Clean Tech fine in June 2025.** **The fine is trivial; the precedent is not** — the AR notes a Reva disruption would *"result in lower capacity utilisation."*

**🔴 Risk 4 — The growth leg is missing, not just slow.** **Revenue has compounded ~1% over three years** (₹487cr FY23 → ₹588cr FY26 is +6.5% CAGR, but ₹473cr in FY24 makes the path flat), and **Q1FY27 grew 1.4%.** **The Gelatin Expansion has no disclosed capacity and no commissioning date**, against **₹176cr of FY26 investing outflow.** **Signal to watch: any disclosure putting a number and a date on it.**

**🔴 Risk 5 — The margin is a spread, and spreads close.** *My inference, not management's:* the profit expansion came from **gel-bone prices falling faster than gelatin prices**, both caused by hide substitution. **When bone normalises, or gelatin falls further without a matching bone move, the spread compresses.** **Nothing in the disclosure gives the duration, and there is no call at which to ask.**

**⚠️ Risk 6 — Every product is growing volume and losing price.** **Gelatin +6.6% volume, realisation down. Collagen peptide +57% volume, realisation −14.5%.** *Only DCP, the by-product, is pricing up (+13.7%).* **A business that must run faster to stand still on revenue.**

**⚠️ Risk 7 — Raw material sourcing.** Bone and hide availability is the input, and the company is now **importing substantial quantities** rather than relying on domestic supply.

**⚠️ Risk 8 — Plant-based collagen substitution.** A structural, slow-moving threat to the entire gelatin category.

**⚠️ Risk 9 — Currency and export concentration.** **Ossein/Limed Ossein is 100% exported; collagen peptide 69%; gelatin 33%.** **US tariff measures are already named in the AR as a cause of falling gelatin realisation.**

**⚠️ Risk 10 — Competitive supply.** Sterling Biotech restructuring or Pioma expansion could add capacity into a category already losing price.

**🔴 Risk 11 — Disclosure is structurally thin, and that is the real risk multiplier.** **No earnings calls — verified across 150 filings from January 2024 to August 2026.** **No investor presentations. No analyst meets. No guidance. Single-segment reporting.** ***Every risk above is harder to monitor than it would be at any other company on this watchlist, and the only channel is an annual report published once a year.***

**⚠️ Risk 12 — Ownership is stable but not aligned in the usual sense.** **74.49% held by a Government of Kerala PSU and a Japanese industrial parent.** **Board continuity follows IAS transfer cycles** — the KSIDC seat changed by government letter on 14-Jul-2026. **Capital allocation is a negotiation between two institutions, neither of which is a founder.**

**⚠️ Risk 13 — There is no institutional bid.** **DII 0.95%, FII 0.05%.** *A cheap stock with no institutional ownership can stay cheap indefinitely.*

**🟢 Risk 14 — the risk of being too cautious.** **This is a 31.4% ROCE, 24.9% ROE business with 115% cash conversion, ₹73cr of free cash flow, effectively zero interest cost and a 35% profit year — at 12.2x earnings and 2.89x book.** **Volume is growing across every product line. The collagen peptide plant is complete and running near capacity. The Japanese parent has published a 2033 ambition to double group sales and named India as a contributor.** **And the two mitigants on SION are real: Ossein customers have contractually agreed to absorb the export obligation, and a DGFT restatement request is live.** ***If Q2FY27 prints a 23%+ margin under Duty Drawback, the single biggest objection to this stock disappears in one line of a results filing.***
