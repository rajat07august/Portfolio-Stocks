## The one-paragraph version

**v1's central claim was a "momentum turn." It did not hold — but not for the reason a steel bear would guess.** Q1FY27 revenue was **₹1,750cr, +32% YoY and +9% QoQ**, with **realisations up across every single product** both YoY and QoQ. And yet **EBITDA fell 24% QoQ to ₹334cr and margin collapsed from 27.3% to 19.1%**, leaving PAT at **₹222cr — up just 3% YoY and down 21% QoQ**. The cause is specific, disclosed and internal: **iron-ore mining volume fell 16% YoY and 20% QoQ because overburden-dumping space ran out pending tree-cutting permissions on newly allotted land**, forcing GPIL to buy ore in the market at roughly **₹6,200/tonne against a captive landed cost of ~₹2,900/tonne**. That single substitution is most of the margin gap. **Total expenses rose 42% on revenue up 32%.** The read-across is unflattering: in the same quarter, in the same state, **Sarda Energy posted a 41% operating margin against GPIL's 19%** — so this was company-specific execution, not the cycle. **Worse, Q2FY27 is pre-announced as weaker still:** the 2.0 MTPA pellet plant was **suspended from 14-Jul-2026** and management expects it to stay shut through the quarter. The call also *corrected the filing*: gas supply is **100% secured** — the problem is a **40-45% price increase** under new PNGRB guidelines, so the plant is idled by economics, not unavailability, with **no visibility on when it ends** (*"he has no guidelines. It's an everyday situation… we just hope for the best"*). Against that, the growth pipeline is far larger than v1 knew and largely funded: **pellet capacity already expanded 74% to 4.7 MnT**, a **5.4 MnT beneficiation plant due Q3FY27** that structurally fixes the ore problem, and a **₹1,425cr / 20 GWh battery-storage plant** in Maharashtra with cells contracted from EVE Power and state incentives approved — **₹501cr already spent**. Balance sheet is **net cash ₹837cr**. **Conviction HELD at Medium-High, moved to Under Watch.**

**Volumes rose too** — pellet sales +36% YoY, sponge iron sales **+291%**, rolled structural sales **+333%**, billets +13%.

**The one number that went the wrong way was captive mining.** *(deck slide 10)*

| | Q1FY26 | Q4FY26 | **Q1FY27** | YoY | QoQ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Iron ore mined (tonnes)** | 6,42,243 | 6,73,452 | **5,37,832** | **−16%** | **−20%** |

The deck's own explanation: *"Fall in Iron Ore Mining volumes was primarily due to **space constraints for overburden dumping in view of the delay in obtaining tree-cutting permissions in the additional allotted land**, resulting in higher market procurement of iron ore for pellet production and, consequently, higher input costs."*

**And the cost gap is enormous.** The deck's own resource slide puts the **market price of iron ore at ~₹6,200/tonne in Q1FY27 against GPIL's captive landed cost of ~₹2,900/tonne** — a ~₹3,300/tonne penalty on every substituted tonne. Pellet production was 9.04 lakh tonnes against 5.38 lakh tonnes mined, so a large share of feed was bought in.

Management's second cause: **higher coal prices "amid the West Asia crisis."**

> **My read, labelled as mine:** this is a **self-inflicted, permission-bound bottleneck, not a demand or price problem** — which makes it more fixable than a cycle turn, and less excusable. The fix is already capitalised: the **5.4 MnT beneficiation plant lands Q3FY27** and **Ari Dongri's capacity has been approved to go from 2.35 to 6 MnT**. Management guides margin recovery **from Q4FY27** — i.e. **three more quarters of this**.

## 🔴 Q2FY27 is pre-announced as weaker — and the call corrects the filing

**The 14-Jul-2026 filing said this:**

> *"…has **temporarily suspended the operations at 2.0 MnT Iron Ore Pellet Plant** … **w.e.f. 14.07.2026**, due to **curtailment of contracted gas supplies by GAIL**, pursuant to withdrawal of Natural Gas (Supply Regulation) Order, 2026 … continuing **Force Majeure** situation … resulting in **increased gas pricing, effective from 09.07.2026**, and also on account of **lower production of iron ore from Ari Dongri Mines in view of rainy season**… making production of pellets **economically unviable**."*
>
> *"The shutdown … will result into lower production and sales volumes of Iron Ore Pellets, **impacting profitability of the Company in Q2FY27**, however there will be **no long-term impact on profitability**."*

**🔴 On the call, management corrected the framing — and this matters.** Asked how big the gas *supply* issue was, the answer was:

> *"See, I think there was a **little misunderstanding**. So we are **able to secure the 100% gas from the supplier**. But the issue is, as per the new guidelines of PNGRB, the **purchase value of the gas has gone up drastically. It's almost up by 40-45%**. So purchasing iron ore from the market and also getting gas at a higher price, that makes pellet plant operation **commercially unviable**."*

**So it is not a curtailment — it is a ~40-45% price shock**, and the plant is idled by economics rather than by unavailability. The filing's word "curtailment" pointed at the wrong thing. *(Machine-transcript quote; the substance is corroborated by the filing's own reference to "increased gas pricing effective from 09.07.2026", but **re-verify the 40-45% figure against the official transcript**.)*

**Compounding it, pellet prices hit a low:** *"…where [steel] is down and **pellet prices touch[ed] all-time low of about 80-100** … in the starting of July."* *(Unit ambiguous in the machine transcript — almost certainly US$/tonne. **Flagged, not relied upon.**)*

**And there is no visibility on when it ends:**

> *"We have been [in] touch with our supplier, **Gail**, but he has **no guidelines. It's an everyday situation.** You know, how the war unfolds every day… things are very irrational right now. So we just **hope for the best**."*
>
> *"at the moment, it is under shutdown and **we feel August will also be a shutdown**"* · *"it will **continue to remain shut in this quarter**"*

**Q2FY27 is therefore guided down on both variables at once** — the pellet plant idled and mining flat: *"So **Q2, you can expect the same numbers in terms of mining production**."*

### The recovery path management laid out — dated and specific

| When | What management said |
|---|---|
| **End-Sep 2026** | *"We are **very hopeful we should get the desired approvals by end of September**"* — the pending item is *"the final approval from the state government for **entry to the land and tree cutting**. Because it is **government land, revenue land**… a process we have to follow which includes the **forest department**."* |
| **Oct 2026** | *"from October onwards, we will get the land to start dumping… ramp up will start happening from October onwards"* |
| **Q3FY27** | Pellet plant back at *"full production"*; market ore purchase still **25-30%** |
| **Q4FY27** | Market purchase *"gradually… should come down **below 10%**"*; margin recovery begins |
| **Q1FY28** | *"finally in FY28, it should be **100% captive**. That is the target."* Full mining capacity |

**Useful nuance:** the mine has two land parcels. On the **private land** *"all the formalities are over and we have started [levelling] the land for dumping."* It is only the **government/revenue land** that is stuck behind the forest department.

> ⚠️ **The one claim I would push back on.** Management said flatly: *"The **3.4 million guidance** given for the whole year for the mining production… **That is still on track**… **We are not revising our mining guidance for the full year.**"* **The arithmetic is hard to reconcile.** Q1 delivered **0.54 MnT**; Q2 is guided at *"the same numbers"* (~0.54). That is **~1.08 MnT in H1, or 32% of the year** — leaving **~2.32 MnT for H2, roughly 1.16 MnT per quarter, more than double the H1 run-rate**, and the ramp only starts in October. **This is my arithmetic, not a management statement — but "very much on track" and the disclosed quarterly path do not sit easily together.** Treat 3.4 MnT as an aspiration, not a forecast.

**🟢 20 GWh BESS — a genuine second business, and further along than a plan.** ₹1,425cr at 1:1 debt:equity, **₹501cr already incurred**; 112 acres acquired at **AURIC Industrial Area, Maharashtra**; **long-term cell supply agreement finalised with EVE Power Co. Ltd.**; assembly equipment from Lead Intelligent Wuxi with factory acceptance testing due end-August; BOS from ROCHE Energy; PCS from FIMER India and Hopewind. **Maharashtra has approved electricity- and stamp-duty exemptions, power tariff subsidy, interest and capital subsidies, SGST-linked incentives and ESI/EPF reimbursements.** Commissioning **Q1FY28**. GPIL's equity in GNEPL is now **₹600cr** after a ₹150cr rights allotment on 3-Jul-2026.

**🟢 And it is already going to market — a full year before commissioning.** From the call: *"we have already **tied up with domestic EMS**. We have already tied up [with] **domestic PCS** as per the Government of India, where currently **20% of your entire system has to be made in India. So we have already achieved that**."* On commercialisation: *"in terms of commercial sales, **from August onwards, we have started [quoting] our containers in the market. And we will start participating in tenders for future supplies**"* — *"mainly with a **back-to-back guarantee to the developer**."* **Meeting the 20% domestic-content threshold matters: it is the qualification gate for most Indian BESS tenders.**

**🔴 And two things were shelved — the call names the specific blocker.** The deck says only *"delays in approvals and on-the-ground challenges."* The transcript is far more precise:

> *"the milestone which we expect[ed] was the **water allocation**. So earlier we were given a **LOI by the state government for the water allocation**. But once we got back to them for the confirmation… there were challenges and because of which the entire water allocation of **9.4 [MCM]** has been delayed. **It's been almost six, eight months now.** So that is the reason the project has been kept on hold."*
>
> Asked whether the steel plant should still be treated as committed capex: *"from an [investment] angle, I think you should **keep the steel plant as an optional now for the medium-term growth**… **till the time we don't get the water approval… we really don't know what is going to happen**."*

**A pending water allocation of ~9.4 MCM, stuck six to eight months after an LOI was already granted, is the single point of failure** for the 1 MnT integrated steel plant *and* was the reason the CRM had to move — *"CRM was coming on the **same land as the steel plant**."*

**🔴 And the Vision 2030 target has been quietly rebased.** Asked directly about the 4x roadmap, management confirmed: *"…**but now we have removed the steel part from that investor presentation. Yes, we have.**"* **The ₹24,000cr / ₹2,500cr PAT 2030 goal no longer includes the integrated steel plant** — so either the target was cut, or BESS and CRM are now expected to carry it. **The deck does not say this anywhere; only the call does.**

**The CRM relocation, in management's words:** a *"**plug-and-play**"* industrial belt like the BESS site; land allotment *"by the end of this month"*; ground activity from October; *"**CRM, there is a delay of almost six months**, but CRM will be on track."* The strategic rationale is genuine — *"**Maharashtra being an automobile hub**… a lot of our products will be consum[ed by] automobile[s], so we expect demand growth local in Maharashtra"* — with cold-rolled feed available from *"JSW, [JSPL]… [ArcelorMittal] in Gujarat. So we don't see a challenge."*

> **This is a real strategic pivot and v1 has none of it.** GPIL is redirecting capital **out of** a Chhattisgarh integrated steel plant and **into** Maharashtra energy storage plus value-added flat steel. Whether that is disciplined capital allocation reacting to permission risk, or a metals company drifting into an unrelated industry at scale, is the central open question in this name. **The ₹1,425cr BESS is 9% of market cap and larger than the CRM.**

**Force 1 — India's steel consumption growth.** India steel demand growing 8-10% CAGR through 2030, driven by infrastructure + construction + auto + capital goods. India will be world's 2nd largest steel consumer.

**Force 3 — Structural steel demand (rebars + TMT).** Infrastructure + housing + real estate drive rebar demand.

**Force 4 — Regional advantage (Chhattisgarh).** Raw material proximity, transport savings, energy availability.
