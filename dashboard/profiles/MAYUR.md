## 1. Business in Plain English

The seat cover in a Mahindra Thar, the dashboard trim in a Ford, the strap on a schoolbag, the sofa fabric in a hotel lobby — a surprising number of "leather-like" surfaces are **synthetic leather**: a woven textile base coated with PVC, polyurethane or semi-PU resin, embossed to look like leather, at roughly a fifth the cost of the animal product and easier to clean and sew. **Mayur Uniquoters is India's largest organised manufacturer of it.**

**The analogy is a specialist mill that sells by the metre and gets paid by the certificate.** The physical product is a coated fabric — unglamorous, priced per linear metre. **What Mayur actually sells is qualification.** A US auto OEM takes five to seven years of testing to approve a synthetic-leather supplier for a vehicle platform. Once approved, the supplier is on that platform for its life. **That is the whole moat, and it is why a Jaipur company with three Rajasthan plants can charge dollar prices to Ford.**

The 30-second snapshot:

- **HQ Jaipur (Sitapura), Rajasthan** — founded **1974 by Suresh Kumar Poddar**, still Chairman & MD after **51 years**
- **Three plants in Rajasthan** plus **Mayur USA**, a subsidiary for OEM distribution
- **FY26:** consolidated revenue **₹967cr** · operating profit **₹235cr (24%)** · **PAT ₹192cr (+28.9%)**. *(On the company's own standalone basis: revenue ~₹1,010cr, PAT ~₹190cr at a ~19% net margin — extraordinary for a materials manufacturer. Consolidated ~₹1,030cr.)*
- **TTM:** revenue **₹1,020cr — through ₹1,000cr for the first time** · operating profit ₹251cr (25%) · **PAT ₹207cr**
- **Q1FY27:** revenue **₹269.23cr (+24.5% YoY)** · PBT **₹74.12cr** · **PAT ₹56.12cr (+36.6%)**
- **Six verticals:** Export OEM (US auto) · Auto OEM Domestic · Footwear · Replacement · Export General · Furnishing
- **Export mix ~42.5% of standalone revenue**, guided to 40–45% in FY27
- **Capacity utilisation 75–78%**; installed PVC capacity ~3.5–4.2 million metres/month depending on thickness
- **Promoter 58.77% and rising** — creeping accumulation, not dilution. **FII 3.18% → 4.78% in eighteen months**
- **🆕 On record for the first time: top-line growth of 10–12% for the next three years**

**Big picture in one sentence:** a 51-year-old founder-run specialty materials compounder whose profit has grown three times faster than revenue on an export mix shift into dollar-priced US auto OEM contracts — **at 15.8x on 24.5% ROCE, with the founder now unable to speak on his own earnings calls.**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Animal leather is structurally losing share.** ESG pressure, regulatory action against tanneries, animal-welfare sentiment and rising hide costs are all pushing auto, footwear and furniture OEMs toward synthetics. **Auto leather-look interiors are now 60%+ synthetic globally, from ~30% in 2010.** This is a one-way trend.

**Force 2 — Interior premiumisation across every price point.** Entry-level cars now ship with leather-look seats and trim. **Every 1% shift in Indian auto interior premiumisation adds roughly ₹50cr to the domestic TAM.**

**Force 3 — China+1 in automotive supply chains, and Mayur is the only qualified Indian option at scale.** US OEMs are diversifying away from China; **India and Mexico are the two alternatives.** Mayur is the only large Indian synthetic-leather player holding US OEM approvals. **FY26 exports grew 35% by value.**

**Force 4 — Footwear and furniture.** India's furniture market compounds at 10–12% and footwear at 8%+, both intensive users. Bata and Liberty are core customers.

**⚠️ And the counter-force that runs through this entire profile: the same export concentration that produced the margin gains is exactly what a tariff regime would hit.** Management's answer is to build inside the wall — and after two years they still have not decided where. See §5.

**Where Mayur sits.** **Most Indian synthetic-leather production is unorganised** — small-scale converters buying base fabric. Mayur has genuine vertical integration and scale. The real competition is **Chinese and Vietnamese imports**, partly offset by India's basic customs duty. **There is no clean listed domestic comparable** — Manaksia Coated Metals, Uniparts and Genus are adjacent at best, which is why §6 has no peer table worth the name.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — US auto OEM approvals, which take five to seven years to earn.** Ford is a major customer and added new platforms in FY26; Chrysler, GM and Mercedes are approved. **New entrants cannot threaten this in the short or medium term** — the certification clock is the barrier.

**Moat 2 — All three resin chemistries: PVC, PU and semi-PU.** Most competitors do one. PVC is the volume workhorse, PU the premium product, semi-PU the middle. **Breadth means Mayur can quote any OEM's spec.**

**Moat 3 — Genuine vertical integration** — base fabric through resin blending, coating and embossing. Most Indian competitors are converters who buy base fabric in. **Integration is margin capture, quality control and speed.**

**Moat 4 — The export mix shift, which is an automatic margin lever.** **42.5%** of standalone revenue, from ~35% two years ago. **Export business earns roughly 5–8 percentage points more than domestic**, and is invoiced in dollars.

**Moat 5 — A structurally currency-favoured model.** Exports are invoiced in USD, so INR depreciation lifts margin directly — **FY26 carried a ₹30cr forex gain.** *(The same mechanism produced Q4FY26's anomalous 31% margin, which is why it did not repeat — see §4.)*

**Moat 6 — 51 years of customer relationships.** M&M, Tata, Ashok Leyland, Maruti, Bata, Liberty — multi-decade, and they stayed through COVID and the 2019 auto slowdown.

**Moat 7 — Cash conversion that actually works.** FY26 **CFO ₹133cr, FCF ₹110cr, CFO/operating profit 86%**; five-year record **114% / 94% / 108% / 86%**. **ROE has sat at 16–18% across ten years, five years, three years and the last year** — remarkably stable through cycles.

**Moat 8 — A promoter who is buying.** 58.77% and rising from 58.59%; **zero dilution in three years**; the family's only listed entity, so a fully concentrated bet.

**Moat 9 — Capacity headroom with a costed expansion path.** Running at **75–78%**, with a new line already ordered for the existing premises.

**Where the moat is weaker — and the first two are the ones that matter.**

- **🔴 Export concentration cuts both ways.** ~42% of revenue in dollar-priced exports drove the margin gains **and is precisely what a US tariff regime would hit.** Top five export OEMs are likely 60%+ of export revenue.
- **🔴 The overseas plant decision has been open for two years.** Management: *"We definitely need to put more plants somewhere outside India… **we have not taken a final call**."* **This is the answer to the tariff risk and it is unresolved.**
- **🔴 Key-man risk is now concrete, not theoretical.** The 51-year founder-CMD **had an infection and is "having a little difficulty in talking on con-call"** — management's own words, §5.5(b). A question about a full-time MD was asked on the same call and cut off before it could be answered.
- **🟡 Roughly a fifth of PBT is other income.** ₹23cr of Q1FY27's ₹74cr PBT (31%); FY26 ₹55cr of ₹259cr (21%). **Mayur runs a substantial investment book alongside the operating business, so the operating business is smaller than headline profit implies.**
- **🟡 PU remains the visible gap.** FY26 PU revenue was just **₹27cr** — and PU is where global growth is fastest. A new US customer audit passed but no commercial deal has followed.
- **🟡 Petrochemical input volatility.** Management on the current quarter: *"the prices went up very sharply. Then there was a fall… Then again the prices went up again."*
- **🟡 Volume growth is thin.** **Q1FY27 volume growth was ~2%**; the rest of the +24.5% came from price and mix. **The value/volume gap is deliberate and management says so — but it is not the same thing as demand growth.**

### 🔴 Why margin didn't expand despite export OEM growing 40%

**Export OEM went ₹53cr (Q1FY26) → ₹73.56cr (Q1FY27), roughly +40%.** An analyst asked why that didn't reach gross margin. Three reasons, all given plainly:

1. **Shipping costs up 4x.** *"shipping cost increased in this last quarter, **4x**, okay, for the export."* Other expenses rose **₹9cr QoQ** on shipping plus travel, legal and professional costs. Management would not call it permanent: *"we cannot say that this will be continued. But since the situation -- war situation will be improved, then definitely the freight charges may come down."*
2. **Raw material volatility since March.** *"the prices went up very sharply. Then there was a fall in prices of some raw materials. Then again the prices went up again."* Still *"more expensive than what it was in the month of March."*
3. **Price increases requested but deliberately not pushed.** On US export OEM: *"We have already sent them mails and requested for a price hike, **but we haven't pushed it yet**"* — because prices began softening fast and they already had *"an advantage of US dollar appreciation."* Note the correction management volunteered: *"I think so, **we are taking a price hike for every customer.** We were specifically talking about the US Export OEM market."*

**Read: the margin shortfall is cost-side and self-limited pricing, not a demand or mix failure.** Recoverable if raw materials and freight stabilise — **but it means the export mix shift alone does not automatically translate into margin.**

**Also worth naming: volume growth was only ~2%.** *"The growth is driven by the volume is around 2% and remaining growth has come from price"* and mix. **Footwear was muted** because sole prices *"went up to almost like two times, three times"* — a customer-side cost shock, not a Mayur problem.

> **⚠️ Two margin bases coexist in the discussion and should not be blended.** Analysts and management refer to a **"25% plus"** margin on the Q1FY27 call while **Screener's consolidated OPM reads 22%.** The company's own basis appears to include other income and/or run on standalone. **Both are stated here; the 22% consolidated figure is used in all tables.** *(Same discipline as the NRB precedent in §4A — do not mix bases.)*

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — The export mix shift is the engine, and it is dollar-priced.** ~42% of standalone revenue from ~35% two years ago, earning 5–8 percentage points more than domestic. **This is why FY26 profit grew three times faster than revenue.**

**Thread B — US OEM approvals are what make Thread A possible**, and they take five to seven years to earn. Ford added new platforms in FY26.

**Thread C — Capacity is being added, and it is now costed and sized.** 75–78% utilisation; a new line ordered for Feb–Mar 2027 worth ~₹150cr of annual revenue; a second facility that would take the total uplift to **₹250–400cr** and cost **₹250cr**.

**Thread D — Cash conversion funds all of it.** 86% CFO/OP, ₹110cr of FCF, no dilution in three years, promoter buying.

**Thread E — PU is the unfilled adjacency, and it is quantifiable.** ₹27cr of FY26 revenue in the resin chemistry where global growth is fastest, at higher realisations than PVC. **If PU reached ₹100cr by FY28 — feasible if the new US customer commercialises — that alone is worth roughly 150bps of blended margin.** It has not moved in several years.

**Two sensitivities worth carrying, because they make the mix arithmetic concrete:** **every 1 percentage point of shift from domestic to export adds ~30bps to blended EBITDA margin**, and on the current trajectory **FY28 export revenue lands at ₹550–650cr against FY26's ~₹440cr.** *(The initiation also sketched a South India plant at ~₹200cr adding ~6 million metres and ~₹150cr of FY28 revenue — since superseded by the overseas-plant framing, but a useful scale reference for what a domestic expansion costs and returns.)*

**🎯 Where the threads meet — and where they are exposed to a single decision.** Threads A through D are one coherent, well-executed machine: qualify with US OEMs, sell dollar-priced product, convert the profit to cash, fund the next line from it. **On its own terms it is working, and 15.8x on 24.5% ROCE is a reasonable price for it.**

**The exposure is that Thread A and the tariff risk are the same fact viewed from two sides.** ~42% of revenue is dollar-priced exports into the US auto chain. **That is the source of the margin expansion and the entire target surface for a tariff regime.** Management knows it — the answer is a plant inside the wall, in Mexico, the US or NAFTA. **They have been considering it for about two years and have not decided.**

> **So the honest structure of this investment is: a very good compounder, priced reasonably, with one large binary sitting outside management's control and one large decision sitting inside it that has not been taken.** **The ₹250cr figure is what the decision costs. The 10–12% three-year guidance is what happens if they never take it.**

**And a second, quieter dependency.** Threads A and B rest on relationships and judgement built over 51 years by one man — **who, on the company's own account, is now having difficulty speaking on calls.** Arun Bagaria has led the last two and been re-appointed for five years from Aug-2027. **The transition appears to be happening in an orderly way. It is happening.**
