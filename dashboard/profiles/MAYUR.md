### 🔴 Why margin didn't expand despite export OEM growing 40%

**Export OEM: Q1FY26 ₹53cr → Q1FY27 ₹74cr, roughly +40%.** An analyst asked why that didn't show up in gross margin. Three reasons, all given plainly:

1. **Gulf War shipping costs — up "four times higher."**
2. **Raw material volatility since March.** *"the prices went up very sharply. Then there was a fall in prices of some raw materials. **Read: the margin shortfall is cost-side and self-limited pricing, not a demand or mix failure.** Recoverable if raw materials stabilise — but it means the export mix shift alone does not automatically translate into margin.

## 1. Business in Plain English

The seat cover in your Mahindra Thar, the dashboard trim in your Ford Escape, the strap on your kid's schoolbag, the sofa fabric in a hotel lobby — a surprising number of "leather-like" surfaces in daily life are actually **synthetic leather** (also called coated fabric, PVC vinyl, or PU leather). It's a woven textile base coated with layers of vinyl (PVC), polyurethane (PU), or semi-PU resins, embossed to look like leather, priced at 1/5th the cost of animal leather, and easier to clean and sew. **Mayur Uniquoters is India's largest organized manufacturer of synthetic leather** — supplying to global auto OEMs, footwear brands, and furniture makers.

The 30-second business snapshot:
- **HQ Jaipur (Sitapura Industrial Area), Rajasthan** — founded 1974 by Suresh Kumar Poddar, currently Chairman & MD (51+ year founder tenure)
- **Three manufacturing plants** in Rajasthan; a US subsidiary (Mayur USA) for OEM distribution; capex evaluation for a South India plant + potential Mexico plant (currently postponed due to Trump tariffs)
- **FY26 revenue INR ~1,010 cr** (standalone); **~INR 1,030 cr** (consolidated) — one of India's mid-large specialty coated-fabric players
- **FY26 PAT (standalone) ~INR 190 cr; PAT margin ~19%** — extraordinary margins for a materials manufacturer
- **Six revenue verticals**: Export General (open market exports), Export OEM (US auto), Auto OEM Domestic (Indian auto makers), Replacement Market, Footwear (Bata + others), Furnishing (hotels + retail)

**How the six verticals break down (Q4FY26 standalone segment mix):**
- Export OEM: INR 88 cr (34% of Q4 revenue) — highest-margin segment
- Auto OEM Domestic: INR 55 cr (21%)
- Footwear: INR 48 cr (18%)
- Replacement: INR 41 cr (16%)
- Export General: INR 21 cr (8%)
- Furnishing + Others: INR 8 cr (3%)

**Big picture in one sentence:** Mayur is a **51-year-old founder-run specialty materials compounder** that quietly delivered 43% PAT growth in FY26 through export mix shift (42.5% exports now, targeting 45–50%) + currency tailwind + product-mix upgrade. Trading at 20x P/E on 24.7% ROCE with rock-stable promoter (58.77% for 12+ quarters) — one of the highest-quality specialty materials businesses in Indian mid-cap space.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

Global synthetic leather demand rides four durable forces:

**Force 1 — Animal leather is losing share.** ESG concerns, regulatory pushback on tanneries, animal welfare movement, and rising leather costs are all driving OEMs (auto, footwear, furniture) toward synthetic alternatives. Auto OEM leather-look interiors now use 60%+ synthetic leather globally (up from 30% in 2010). This is a one-way structural trend.

**Force 2 — Auto interior upgrades.** Even entry-level cars now come with leather-look seats and trim. Interior premiumisation is universal across price points — Maruti's Baleno + Hyundai's Creta + Tata's Nexon all use significant synthetic leather content. Every 1% shift in auto interior premiumisation adds ~INR 50 cr to India's synthetic leather TAM.

**Force 3 — Global "China +1" in automotive supply chains.** US auto OEMs (Ford, GM, Chrysler) are aggressively diversifying supply chains away from China. India + Mexico are the two dominant alternatives. Mayur is the only large Indian synthetic leather player with US OEM approvals — creating a structural export tailwind. In FY26, exports grew 35% by value.

**Force 4 — Furniture + footwear demand.** India's furniture market growing at 10–12% CAGR; footwear at 8%+. Both use synthetic leather intensively. Bata, Liberty, and other footwear majors are Mayur's core customers. Furniture is a smaller but growing vertical.

**Where Mayur sits in the map.** It's the **largest organised Indian player** in synthetic leather. Peers:
- **Manaksia Coated Metals** — larger metal-coated fabric focus, different economics
- **Uniparts India** — auto components, adjacent but different
- **Genus Paper & Boards** — paper coating, different segment
- **Chinese + Vietnamese imports** — the primary competitive threat; India's basic customs duty on synthetic leather partly offsets this
- Most Indian synthetic leather production is **unorganised** (small-scale converters); Mayur has genuine scale advantage

The trade-off: **quality + scale (Mayur) vs low-cost (unorganized / Chinese)**. Mayur has been steadily winning share from unorganised in India + winning US OEM contracts on quality + certifications. This is a slow-but-durable share-gain story.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Only large Indian synthetic leather player with US auto OEM approvals.** Ford is a major customer (added new platforms in FY26), Chrysler, GM, Mercedes are approved. US OEM approvals take 5–7 years of quality testing + certifications. New entrants can't threaten Mayur in the short-medium term.

**Moat 2 — Product portfolio breadth (PVC + PU + Semi-PU).** Most synthetic leather manufacturers focus on one resin type. Mayur makes all three — with PVC being the workhorse (higher volume, lower margin), PU being premium (higher margin, lower volume), and Semi-PU as the middle ground. Product breadth = ability to serve any auto OEM's spec.

**Moat 3 — 51-year founder-run heritage.** Suresh Kumar Poddar has been running Mayur since 1974. Half a century of relationships across Indian auto OEMs (Tata, M&M, Maruti, Ashok Leyland), Bata, Liberty, and international brands. This tenure creates deep customer trust + credit standing.

**Moat 4 — 42.5% export mix (rising to 45–50%).** FY26 exports contributed 42.5% of standalone revenue, up from ~35% two years ago. Target: 45–50% by FY28. Export business commands ~5–8% higher margin than domestic. Mix shift = automatic margin expansion.

**Moat 5 — Scale + integration (base fabric + PVC/PU resin blending + coating + embossing).** Mayur's plants integrate the full production stack — from base fabric weaving to final coated product. Most Indian competitors are converters who buy base fabric. Vertical integration = margin capture + quality control + speed.

**Moat 6 — Currency-hedged export business model.** Export invoicing in USD; INR depreciation = margin uplift. FY26 forex gain INR 30 cr. Structural INR weakness = ongoing tailwind.

**Moat 7 — Rock-stable promoter + healthy dividend track record.** 58.77% promoter holding, stable for 12+ quarters. Zero dilution history. Regular dividend (0.57% yield) + no aggressive capex funding via equity. Signals disciplined capital allocation.

**Moat 8 — Warm customer relationships in Indian auto + footwear.** M&M, Tata, Ashok Leyland, Bata are multi-decade relationships. Even in tough years (COVID, 2019 auto slowdown), these customers stayed with Mayur. Relationship stickiness = downside protection.

**Where the moat is weaker.**
- Trump tariff situation creates uncertainty on Mexico plant + US export routes (some exports flow through Mexico).
- PU segment remains muted — Mayur has invested in PU capacity but revenue only INR 27 cr in FY26; PU is where the highest global growth is happening, and Mayur is under-penetrated here.
- Petrochemical input (PVC + PU resin) price volatility can compress margins in short-term.
- Chinese + Vietnamese imports are a persistent competitive threat.
- Auto OEM concentration — top 5 export OEMs likely 60%+ of export revenue.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

Mayur's story is **quiet compounding with four independent threads**:

**Thread A — US auto OEM export ramp.** Ford new platform additions ongoing. Chrysler, GM, Mercedes existing volume expansion. Export general (open market) contributing INR 20–25 cr/quarter. Realistic FY28 export revenue: INR 550–650 cr (vs FY26 INR ~440 cr).

**Thread B — Product-mix upgrade to premium.** Semi-PU and PU commanding higher realisations than PVC. FY26 PU only INR 27 cr but ramping. If PU reaches INR 100 cr by FY28 (feasible if new US customer commercialises), margin expansion is ~150 bps.

**Thread C — Currency-hedged margin uplift.** Export contribution rising to 45–50% + INR structurally weak = automatic margin uplift as export mix grows. Every 1% shift from domestic to export adds ~30 bps to blended EBITDA margin.

**Thread D — South India / Global capex.** INR 200–300 cr plant decision pending. If South India (INR 200 cr), incremental capacity ~6 million meters, potential FY28 revenue add INR 150 cr. If Global-scale (INR 300 cr), potential Mayur becomes a genuinely large international player.

**Where threads reinforce.** Export OEM (Thread A) + Product-mix upgrade (Thread B) + Currency benefit (Thread C) all compound in the same direction — margins expand as export + premium mix grows. Capacity expansion (Thread D) removes the capacity constraint that would otherwise cap Thread A growth.

**Where they don't compound.** PU business remains stubbornly muted despite multi-year investment — INR 27 cr FY26 is small relative to potential. If PU doesn't scale to INR 100+ cr by FY28, Thread B underdelivers. Also, Mexico plant postponement means near-term Trump tariff exposure isn't structurally solved.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — Trump tariff escalation on Indian exports.** Q4FY26 concall: "prices have gone up… very difficult to predict what Trump does tomorrow." If US tariffs on Indian-origin synthetic leather escalate, export margin gets hit. Signal: US Trade Representative decisions + India trade deal announcements.

**Risk 2 — PU business never scales.** Mayur has been trying to ramp PU for 5+ years; still only INR 27 cr revenue in FY26. If FY27 PU stays below INR 50 cr, the "premium mix upgrade" thesis stagnates. Signal: quarterly PU revenue disclosure (management gives this).

**Risk 3 — INR appreciation.** ~42.5% export revenue = high FX exposure. Any INR appreciation to 78–80 (from current ~83) removes forex gain tailwind + directly compresses reported revenue. Signal: RBI + broader USD/INR trajectory.

**Risk 4 — Petrochemical raw material spike.** Paste PVC + PU resin are petroleum by-products. Q4FY26 mention: "prices have gone up significantly" in March. If input costs stay elevated + Mayur can't fully pass through, margins compress. Signal: quarterly raw material cost trajectory.

**Risk 5 — Auto OEM concentration.** Top 3–5 auto OEMs (Ford + Chrysler + Mercedes + Tata + M&M) likely 60%+ of revenue. Loss of any single OEM allocation would hurt materially. Auto industry consolidation is a slow risk.

**Risk 6 — Capex decision delay.** South (INR 200 cr) vs Global (INR 300 cr) plant decision still pending after multiple concalls. If pushed to FY28+, growth capacity constraints appear.

**Risk 7 — Chinese + Vietnamese competition.** Cheaper imports could pressure domestic pricing. India's basic customs duty helps but not fully.

**Risk 8 — Founder transition risk.** Suresh Poddar handed over Q4FY26 concall to Arun Bagaria. Generational transition ongoing. If Bagaria is not the natural successor (family member) or if senior transition creates strategy shifts, execution rhythm can disrupt.

**Risk 9 — Auto cycle downturn.** Auto OEMs are cyclical. FY26 was strong for both domestic + US auto. If FY27 has a global auto slowdown, Mayur's Q3-Q4 momentum reverses. Signal: SIAM Indian auto data + US Auto sales.

**Bear case scenario (15% probability):** Trump tariffs escalate + PU never scales + auto slowdown. FY28 revenue INR 1,180 cr, PAT INR 195 cr, multiple stays at 20x = INR 3,900 cr. **~2% upside (flat).**

**Bull case (30%):** Export ramp + PU breakthrough + margin expansion. FY28 revenue INR 1,450 cr, PAT INR 275 cr, multiple 25x = INR 6,875 cr. **~80% upside.**
