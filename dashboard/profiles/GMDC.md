## 🔴 The FY26 exceptional item — what v1's "all-time" numbers actually contained

**The company's own Note 5 and Note 6 to the FY26 results, verbatim:**

> *"With the recent changes in **GST rate with effect from 22nd September 2025 on the supply of Lignite (from earlier 5% to now 18% supplemented with removal of compensation cess)**, the Group is eligible to utilise **GST Input Credit of ₹492.63 Crores**, which was **expensed out in earlier financial periods considering remote possibilities of utilisation** of input tax credit against output tax liabilities **when the supply of lignite was under inverted duty structure**. Accordingly, the Group has recognized the input tax credit asset of ₹492.63 Crore with consequential credit as **an exceptional item** in the Statement of Profit and Loss considering revised realisation estimate owing to **cessation of inverted duty structure**."*
>
> *"ITC pertaining to earlier years which were under litigation and was written off in books of accounts, has now been **restored following withdrawal of appeal approved by the Joint Commissioner of State Tax**. Accordingly, **₹30.02 crore** has been recognised as ITC asset with a corresponding exceptional item."*

**₹492.63cr + ₹30.02cr = ₹522.65cr of exceptional income in FY26.**

**The reported "+39.5% profit growth" becomes a ~14% decline once the write-back is removed — and the operating business shrank 30.5%.** Revenue itself fell 6.9%.

**Three-year compounding confirms this is a trend, not a one-year dip: sales CAGR −9%, profit CAGR −22%.**

> **A fair word for the item itself.** This is **real economic value**, not a paper trick — credits previously written off as unrecoverable have genuinely become usable, because the GST rate change on lignite (5% → 18%, cess removed) ended the inverted duty structure that stranded them. **It is also non-recurring, non-cash in the period, and irrelevant to the earning power of the mines.** The company disclosed it properly as an exceptional item; **the problem is that it was never separated in v1, which reported FY26 as a growth year.**

**🟢 And there is a forward-looking positive buried in the same note:** with the inverted duty structure gone, **GMDC can now utilise input credits against output liability on an ongoing basis** rather than expensing them. That is a structural margin benefit from FY27 onward, though its size is not quantified anywhere in the filings.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

Lignite + coal mining rides four durable forces:

**Force 1 — India's power sector demand.** India's electricity demand growing 6-7% CAGR through 2030. Even with renewables ramp, coal remains 55-60% of generation mix.

**Force 2 — Gujarat power capacity buildout.** Gujarat is India's largest state power market. Lignite from GMDC feeds state PSUs + private power plants.

**Force 3 — Coal blocks award to non-Coal India entities.** Government policy shift enabling private + state PSUs to develop coal blocks.

**Force 4 — Cement + industrial demand for lignite.** Non-power lignite consumption growing.

**Where GMDC sits.** #1 specialised Indian lignite producer. Peers:
- **Coal India** (CIL) — much larger, focused on thermal coal
- **NLC India** — focused on lignite, primarily Tamil Nadu
- **Singareni Collieries** — Andhra + Telangana coal
- GMDC positioning: **Gujarat-focused lignite specialist + emerging coal presence**
