### 🧩 The Conversion Model — v1 OMISSION, corrected

**v1 never mentioned this, and it is the single most important thing about how parts of this company earn money.** Correcting it here.

In the **windmill machining / large-castings machining** business — and parts of fabrication — **the customer supplies the raw material.** Wheels India never buys the casting; it bills only the machining and engineering value-add. Management calls this a "conversion business." Three verbatim confirmations across three separate calls:

> **Q2FY26 (Oct-2025), on segment-level return on invested capital:** *"We have some businesses which are actually what I would call as conversion businesses **where there's no RM**, notably the windmill machining business, which is not insignificant. So there, I would say the returns will be **clean double-digit or even higher** than that."*

> **Q4FY25 (May-2025), on the ₹100cr windmill investment:** *"There is at least Rs. 66 crores which is in a long lead time asset acquisition and that Rs. 66 crores will have more of an **asset turn of 1x**. But it is a very profitable business because **it is a conversion business, it is not a material-based business.**"*

> **Q4FY26 (May-2026), on the co-located caster scaling 3,500 → 10,000 tons/month:** *"**It's a conversion business. So the profit is much better than the Wheels India profit profile because it's on conversion.**"* … *"**Sales value will be less because it's conversion, not material.**"*

**What this does to the reported numbers — three distortions:**

1. **Revenue understates activity.** No metal in the top line. Comparing Wheels India's revenue growth against a material-inclusive peer is a false comparison for this slice of the book.
2. **Asset turnover collapses to ~1:1** — an artefact, not inefficiency. ₹90cr of windmill machining capex adds roughly ₹90cr of revenue, versus 3–4x on a material-inclusive wheel line.
3. **The blended 7.5% OPM is two businesses averaged together** — a low-margin, material-heavy wheel book and a high-margin, no-material conversion book. Management describes conversion margins as *"clean double-digit type of **healthy IT type of margins**."*

**This resolves the flat-revenue puzzle.** Q2FY26, an analyst pressed on 14 quarters of revenue stuck at ₹1,050–1,150cr despite heavy capex:

> *"Significant amount of capex has gone into conversion type of business, where basically **there's no material cost**… your asset turnover ratio on conversion type of business tends to be more 1:1. At the same time, the EBITDA margins are clean double-digit… **So, while the top line has not grown, the bottom line has grown faster than the top line, largely due to the fact that there's a lot of conversion business that's happened.**"*

**Two honest qualifiers v1 would have needed anyway:**

- **⚠️ The wheel business is NOT conversion — it is raw-material pass-through.** Steel and aluminium are bought, billed inside revenue, and repriced with a lag. Management concedes in Q4FY26: *"we don't think that over the longer term, we are able to get a complete pass-through on material cost."* These are two different economic models and must not be conflated. Conversion applies to windmill machining and parts of fabrication only.
- **⚠️ The conversion benefit is mostly still ahead, not in the numbers.** Conversion sits inside the **Industrial Components** segment, which is only ~20% of revenue (Q3FY26: ₹258cr of ₹1,280cr) — and that segment's **EBIT actually FELL 30% YoY in Q3FY26 and 18% YTD-Dec25.** Why: the ₹90cr machining capex commissioned in FY26 carries depreciation now but earns revenue only from FY27, because *"it takes about 12 months really for the equipment to come and with commissioning maybe 12 to 15 months."* So today the segment bears the cost without the revenue. **The conversion story is an FY27–FY28 call option, not a current earnings driver.**

**Net read:** the conversion model is a genuine, real, and market-under-appreciated feature — it means Wheels India's headline revenue growth and asset turns systematically *understate* its economics, and it is precisely the kind of thing that screens badly and reads well. But it is ~20% of the book at most, its P&L contribution is still in front of us, and it does not currently rescue a 7.5% blended margin.

## 1. Business in Plain English

Where SSWL is India's largest **independent** wheel maker, **Wheels India is the TVS Group's captive-ish wheel + component diversified company** — but with a broader product portfolio than just wheels: air suspension systems, wind turbine components (fabricated + machined), hydraulic cylinders, and steel/aluminium wheels across passenger + commercial + construction + agriculture segments. Founded in 1960 (India's oldest wheel maker), Wheels India is a **65-year TVS Group heritage company** with multi-decade OEM relationships.

The 30-second business snapshot:
- **HQ Chennai, Tamil Nadu** — part of **TSF Group** ($3.2B, TVS family offshoot)
- Chairman & MD: **Mr. Srivats Ram** (2nd generation TVS)
- **FY26 revenue INR 5,124 cr** — first time crossing INR 5,000 cr (+16% YoY)
- **Q4FY26 revenue INR 1,471 cr (+23% YoY)**
- **FY26 exports INR 1,342 cr** — 26% of revenue
- **Q4FY26 exports INR 400 cr**
- **FY26 PAT +31% YoY; Q4 PAT +45%**
- **10 manufacturing plants, 8,400 people**
- **20% global market share** in construction + agriculture tractor wheels

**Business divisions:**
1. **Wheels** (car/truck/tractor/construction): largest segment, 3 sub-verticals
2. **Air Suspension**: trucks + buses (growing)
3. **Windmill Components**: fabricated + machined + large castings machining
4. **Hydraulic Cylinders**: industrial + construction
5. **WIL Car Wheels** (subsidiary): passenger car steel wheels
6. **Axles India** (associate): axle housings for CV
7. **TOPY JV**: passenger car steel wheels for MNCs

**Big picture in one sentence:** Wheels India is the **TVS Group's diversified auto components conglomerate** — 65-year heritage, 10 manufacturing plants, 20% global market share in construction/agri wheels, growing air suspension + wind + hydraulic diversification — trading at **23.6x P/E** (cheapest in the sector) with 18.8% ROCE, 15.8% ROE, and stable 58.31% promoter holding.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — Auto cycle turn.**

> **⚠️ ACCUMULATING (28-Jul-2026).** Named by management as *the* FY27 swing factor. Q4FY26: *"Now with this **West Asia crisis**, how much growth will actually happen this year in India and how will it be next year, will actually determine how we grow, because **we are very much a derived demand**."* And on customers: *"if you look at Tata's or Mahindra's… they've been **very guarded** about the growth in the coming year. So we'll be kind of governed by what they do."* Q1FY27's −4.7% QoQ revenue is the first data point consistent with this.

**Risk 2 — Wind energy capex slowdown.**

> **⚠️ ACCUMULATING.** Wheels India has now put ~₹90–100cr into windmill machining and stepped up fabricated structures including offshore. Warranty provisions for the European windmill business appeared in Q4FY26 other expenses. Concentration into a single cyclical end-market is rising just as the capex lands.

**Risk 3 — Aluminium price volatility.**

> **✅ PARTLY MITIGATED — and this is where the conversion model helps.** On the wheel book, metal is pass-through (imperfectly: *"we don't think that over the longer term we are able to get a complete pass-through on material cost"*). On the **conversion book there is no RM at all**, so that revenue is structurally immune to metal price swings. Growing the conversion share reduces this risk.

**Risk 4 — Currency exposure on exports.**

> **⚠️ MATERIALIZED, and the upside is smaller than assumed.** Q4FY26: *"while you may get immediate benefit of any sudden devaluation of the rupee, there is also a **process of sharing currency gains with customers** over a longer term… **we don't actually get any sustainable advantage from devaluation.**"* FY26 forex gain was ₹10cr total (₹4cr in Q4). **🆕 New sub-risk: CBAM.** From 1-Jan-2026 a Europe-exported product carries a CBAM tax liability booked in other expenses — CFO confirmed it *"will continue."* Exports are ~25% of revenue.

**Risk 5 — TSF Group inter-company complexity.**

> 🟡 **UNRESOLVED, now with a live catalyst.** Wheels India holds ~12.5% of Axles India. Q4FY26 on whether group entities might combine: *"it's not unreasonable to say that at some point or the other, there may be a way of making the companies closer together."* An undisclosed-purpose ₹400cr raise plus open talk of bringing group companies together is a combination that deserves watching — a related-party acquisition funded by minority dilution would be the adverse version.

**Risk 6 — WIL Car Wheels regression.**

> **✅ TRACKING WELL.** Subsidiary was loss-making two years ago, turned around, and Q4FY26 delivered the first consolidated quarterly PAT above ₹50cr (₹58.81cr on ₹1,573cr). No sign of regression.

**Risk 7 — Slower relative growth vs SSWL.**

> **⚠️ MATERIALIZED — and the gap widened.** Q1FY27 head-to-head: Wheels India revenue **+17.8%** / PAT **+25.8%** (−33.9% QoQ) at **7.5% OPM**; SSWL revenue **+27.2%** / PAT **+43.3%** at **11.0% OPM**. 3-year sales CAGR: Wheels India **6%**. Both now trade at ~21x, so the valuation compensation for the slower growth has disappeared. This has moved from a theoretical risk to the central argument against the stock.

**Risk 8 — Multi-vertical distraction.**

> 🟡 **UNRESOLVED but being managed.** Management is consolidating plants (air suspension went from two plants to one, moving out of rented facilities) and explicitly rejecting new sites: *"No new plant… no new site."* Verticals that were losing money (hydraulic cylinders, fabrication) turned positive in Q4FY26. Counter-signal: the double-digit EBITDA target depends on exactly these sub-scale verticals continuing to fix themselves.

**🆕 Risk 9 — Dilution with undisclosed use of proceeds (NEW, 28-Jul-2026).** ₹400cr enabling resolution approved 10-Jul-2026, postal ballot dispatched 13-Jul — equity, convertibles, warrants, QIP, rights or preferential, at the board's discretion. ~11.8% dilution, +38% to net worth, ROE from 16.0% to ~11.6% on unchanged earnings. **No use of proceeds disclosed.** It directly reverses the Q3FY26 PPT commitment to *"fund capex predominantly thro accruals and holding the debt at current levels"* — on a balance sheet generating ₹477cr CFO with D/E already down to 0.74x. Signal to watch: postal ballot outcome, instrument chosen, pricing, whether promoters participate pro-rata, and any use-of-proceeds statement at the Q2FY27 call.

**🆕 Risk 10 — Institutional distribution into retail (NEW, 28-Jul-2026).** DII holding **18.81% (Sep-23) → 6.39% (Jun-26)**, including **−3.25ppt in the June quarter alone**, while shareholder count rose **+29% in that same quarter** to 29,271. Professionals have been selling this name for three years into a stock that rose 95% in the last one. Promoter holding is untouched at 58.31%, which is the mitigating fact. Signal to watch: DII line in the Sep-2026 shareholding pattern — below 5% would confirm a full institutional exit.

**🆕 Risk 11 — The conversion ramp is a call option that may not pay in time (NEW, 28-Jul-2026).** The conversion thesis rests on windmill machining scaling — and the co-located caster's own plan is 3,500 → 10,000 tons/month *"over a 3- to 4-year period."* Meanwhile Industrial Components EBIT fell **30% YoY in Q3FY26** and **18% YTD-Dec25** while carrying the new depreciation. Equipment lead times are *"12 months… with commissioning maybe 12 to 15 months."* If the ramp slips a year, the segment carries cost without revenue for another four quarters and the double-digit EBITDA target moves to FY29. Signal to watch: Industrial Components segment EBIT in the Q2FY27 PPT — **this is the single most informative number in the whole disclosure pack.**

**🆕 Risk 12 — The Q4 spike is mistaken for a run-rate (NEW, 28-Jul-2026).** OPM has been range-bound 6.8–8.1% for eight quarters with a Q4 seasonal peak in both FY25 and FY26 that reverses in Q1 both times. Q4FY26's ₹59cr PAT was GST-2.0-assisted (*"a year of 2 halves"*); Q1FY27 gave back a third of it. Any model anchored on the Q4FY26 exit rate overstates FY27 by a wide margin. Signal to watch: whether Q2FY27 OPM breaks above 8.1% — it has not done so in eight quarters.
