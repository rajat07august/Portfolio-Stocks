## 1. Business in Plain English

Wheels India makes **the wheels that big things roll on** — and, increasingly, something rather different alongside them.

The original business is steel and aluminium wheels for trucks, buses, tractors and earthmovers, made in ten plants for a customer list that reads like the Indian and global CV industry. It is a low-margin, high-volume, raw-material-heavy business: buy steel, form it, sell it, and try to pass the metal price through.

**The interesting half is the part that isn't wheels.** Wheels India also machines large castings for wind turbines, fabricates structural parts, and builds hydraulic cylinders and air suspension systems. **In much of this work the customer supplies the raw material** — Wheels India never buys the casting and bills only its engineering value-add. Management calls this a *"conversion business"*, and it earns *"clean double-digit or even higher"* returns against a ~7.8% blended margin.

**The useful analogy is a precision machine shop that also happens to own a wheel factory.** The wheel factory is the revenue; the machine shop is increasingly the profit.

**The 30-second snapshot:**
- **Chennai, established 1960** · part of the **TSF Group** (T S Santhanam family, lineage from the erstwhile TVS Group) — **~$3.2bn group turnover, 36+ plants, 15 business entities, since 1936**
- **Sister companies:** Brakes India, Turbo Energy, Axles India, Sundaram Dynacast, **Sundaram Finance**, Royal Sundaram
- **~8,900 workforce · 10 plants · $540m revenue, $141m of it exports**
- **FY26 revenue ₹5,465 cr (+15%)** · operating profit **₹413 cr (8.0%)** · **PAT ₹158 cr (+41%)**
- **Q1FY27 revenue ₹1,491 cr (+17.8% YoY)** but **PAT ₹39 cr, −33.9% QoQ** off a GST-flattered Q4
- **🆕 Industrial Components EBIT +256% YoY** — the conversion capex has started earning
- **CFO ₹477 cr, CFO/OP 124%, FCF ₹203 cr** — cash conversion is genuinely excellent
- **Promoters 58.31% for twelve quarters, going to ~60.4%** on a ₹180cr all-promoter preferential issue
- **22.6x earnings, 3.55x book, 0.95% yield, ROCE 18.8% / ROE 15.8%**

**Big picture in one sentence.** Wheels India is a **six-decade, family-controlled auto-component business quietly rebuilding itself into a higher-margin precision engineering company** — the shift is real, it has just shown up in the segment numbers for the first time, and **the promoters are putting ₹180 crore of their own money in to fund the next stage of it.**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Commercial vehicles are a replacement business before they are a growth business.** Every truck, bus and tractor needs wheels, and they wear out. The base demand is durable even when the cycle is not.

**Force 2 — Wind energy build-out needs machined castings, and very few shops can do them.** Large-casting machining for wind turbine gearboxes and hubs — including **offshore WEGs** — is capital-intensive, quality-critical and slow to qualify into. **This is where the conversion economics live.**

**Force 3 — Global OEMs are sourcing components from India.** Exports are $141m of a $540m business, into North America and Europe, where the TSF group's decades of relationships matter.

**Force 4 — Premiumisation within the wheel itself.** Cast aluminium wheels carry better economics than steel, and the mix is shifting.

**Force 5 — Adjacent systems, not just parts.** Air suspension for buses, lift axles and hydraulic cylinders are systems sold into the same customers — higher value per vehicle without a new customer relationship.

**Where Wheels India sits.** ₹3,696cr market cap, roughly a third of Steel Strips Wheels' revenue-scale peer group. **The direct comparison is SSWL** — also on this watchlist — and the two are genuinely different: **SSWL earns a better margin (~11% vs 7.8%); Wheels India earns a better return on capital (18.8% ROCE vs SSWL's 14.8%)**, because a chunk of its book carries no raw material.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — The conversion model, which is the most under-appreciated fact about this company** (§4). In windmill machining and parts of fabrication the customer supplies the metal. **Revenue understates activity, asset turns look artificially poor, and margins on that slice are "healthy IT type of margins."**

**Moat 2 — TSF Group parentage.** A 1936-founded, ~$3.2bn group including Sundaram Finance and Brakes India. **Governance pedigree and customer access that a standalone ₹3,700cr company could not buy.**

**Moat 3 — Qualification barriers in large-casting machining.** Long lead times on equipment (*"12 to 15 months"* from order to commissioning), heavy capital, and customer qualification. **Slow to enter is slow to be displaced.**

**Moat 4 — Cash conversion that most auto-component businesses do not have.** **CFO/OP has been 117%, 134%, 173% and 124% across four of the last five years.** FY26 CFO ₹477cr against ₹413cr of operating profit.

**Moat 5 — Deleveraging while investing.** Standalone debt ₹755cr (FY23) → **₹675cr (FY26)**, D/E **1.26 → 0.72**, debt/EBITDA 1.55x. **Capex went up every year through that.**

**Moat 6 — 🆕 Promoters buying, not selling.** 58.31% unmoved for twelve quarters, and now a **₹180cr preferential issue subscribed entirely by the promoter group** at ₹1,418/share, lifting them to ~60.4%.

**Moat 7 — Genuine product breadth into one customer set.** Wheels, wind components, hydraulic cylinders, air suspension, lift axles, fabrication — sold largely to the same OEMs.

**Where the moat is weaker.**
- **🔴 Blended margin is 7.8%**, and management's own double-digit-EBITDA ambition is *"1 or 2 years away"* and depends on **loss-making divisions turning positive**, not on growth.
- **🔴 The wheel business is raw-material pass-through, not conversion** — and management concedes *"we don't think that over the longer term, we are able to get a complete pass-through on material cost."*
- **⚠️ 3-year sales CAGR is 6%.** This is not a fast-growing company.
- **⚠️ Domestic institutions have been leaving for three years** — DII 18.81% (Sep-2023) → **6.39%** (Jun-2026).
- **⚠️ Capex is stepping up ~60% to ₹400–450cr in FY27**, and the returns on it arrive with a 12–15 month lag.
- **⚠️ Dilution is coming**: ₹180cr approved, with **₹270cr of headroom still unused** under the enhanced ₹450cr limit.
- **⚠️ Only two Q&A opportunities a year.** Q1 and Q3 are decks with no questions.

### 🧩 The Conversion Model — why this company's revenue understates its economics

**This is the most important structural fact about Wheels India.** In **windmill machining, large-castings machining and parts of fabrication, the customer supplies the raw material.** Wheels India never buys the casting; it bills only machining and engineering value-add. Three verbatim confirmations across three separate calls:

> **Q2FY26 (Oct-2025):** *"We have some businesses which are actually what I would call as conversion businesses **where there's no RM**, notably the windmill machining business, which is not insignificant. So there, I would say the returns will be **clean double-digit or even higher**."*

> **Q4FY25 (May-2025):** *"There is at least Rs. 66 crores which is in a long lead time asset acquisition and that Rs. 66 crores will have more of an **asset turn of 1x**. But it is a very profitable business because **it is a conversion business, it is not a material-based business.**"*

> **Q4FY26 (May-2026), on the co-located caster scaling 3,500 → 10,000 tons/month:** *"**It's a conversion business. So the profit is much better than the Wheels India profit profile because it's on conversion.**"* … *"**Sales value will be less because it's conversion, not material.**"*

**Three distortions this creates in the reported numbers:**
1. **Revenue understates activity.** No metal in the top line for this slice. **Comparing Wheels India's revenue growth to a material-inclusive peer is a false comparison.**
2. **Asset turnover collapses to ~1:1** — an artefact, not inefficiency. ₹90cr of machining capex adds roughly ₹90cr of revenue, against 3–4x on a material-inclusive wheel line.
3. **The blended 7.8% OPM is two businesses averaged.** A low-margin material-heavy wheel book, and a no-material conversion book that management describes as *"clean double-digit type of **healthy IT type of margins**."*

**This resolves the flat-revenue puzzle.** An analyst pressed in Q2FY26 on 14 quarters of revenue stuck near ₹1,050–1,150cr despite heavy capex, and got: *"…your asset turnover ratio on conversion type of business tends to be more 1:1. At the same time, the EBITDA margins are clean double-digit… **So, while the top line has not grown, the bottom line has grown faster than the top line, largely due to the fact that there's a lot of conversion business that's happened.**"*

**Two honest qualifiers.**
- **⚠️ The wheel business is NOT conversion — it is raw-material pass-through**, and an imperfect one. Management, Q4FY26: *"we don't think that over the longer term, we are able to get a complete pass-through on material cost."* **Two different economic models; do not conflate them.**

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — Wheels are the revenue; conversion is the profit.** Automotive components is 84% of revenue at a 6.0% EBIT margin; Industrial components is 16% at 3.3% and rising fast off a depressed base. **The mix shift is the entire investment case.**

**Thread B — Windmill machining is the sharp end of Thread A**, and it has just started earning after a 12–15 month commissioning lag. **Offshore WEG components are the next leg** — the deck lists *"Step up the capacity and volumes of the fabricated, structural parts for windmills, including off-shore WEGs."*

**Thread C — The capex step-up to ₹400–450cr is Threads A and B being funded.** ~60% above FY26. **It arrives with the same 12–15 month lag, which means FY27 carries the depreciation and FY28 gets the revenue** — exactly the pattern that made Industrial Components EBIT fall 30% before it rose 256%.

**Thread D — Promoters are funding it themselves.** ₹180cr of promoter cash at ₹1,418. **A family that has held 58.31% unmoved for twelve quarters choosing to increase rather than dilute, in the same quarter the thesis first shows in the numbers, is the strongest read-through available.**

**Thread E — The wheel book is the ballast and the drag.** Diversified across seven end markets with none above 22%, cash-generative, and structurally low-margin with imperfect raw-material pass-through. **It funds Threads A–C and caps the blended margin.**

**Thread F — Deleveraging continues through all of it.** D/E 1.26 → 0.72, debt held flat, CFO/OP above 100%. **This is what lets a 7.8%-margin business earn an 18.8% ROCE.**

**How they interact.** **The company is converting a commodity wheel business into a precision engineering business using its own cash flow, at a pace set by equipment lead times.** Q1FY27 is the first quarter where the conversion showed up in segment EBIT. **The ₹400–450cr capex and the ₹180cr promoter cheque are a bet that it keeps showing up.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — The CV/tractor cycle turns.** Management names it as *the* FY27 swing factor. > **🟡 MIXED (22-Aug-2026)** — Q1FY27 revenue **+17.8% YoY but −4.7% QoQ**. The YoY is fine; the sequential is the first data point consistent with the risk. **Signal to watch: Q2FY27 sequential revenue.**

**Risk 2 — Wind energy capex slowdown.**
> **⚠️ RISING EXPOSURE, IMPROVING RETURNS (22-Aug-2026)** — ~₹90–100cr already in windmill machining, and the deck now guides *"Step up the capacity and volumes of the fabricated, structural parts for windmills, **including off-shore WEGs**."* **Concentration into a single cyclical end-market is increasing exactly as the capex lands** — and warranty provisions for the European windmill business already appeared in Q4FY26 other expenses. **The +256% EBIT is the reward for that concentration; a wind capex pause is the price.**
