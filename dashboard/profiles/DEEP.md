## 1. Business in Plain English

Deep Industries is **India's largest post-exploration oil and gas services specialist.**

**The analogy is the plumber and power-tool partner to India's oil producers.** Once a well has been drilled by someone else — ONGC, Oil India, Reliance, Cairn — and exploration is finished, **Deep steps in to keep it producing**: gas compression to move product through pipelines, workover services to unclog and revive slowing wells, gas dehydration to clean gas before it enters the grid, drilling services, and integrated project management.

**The economic point is what Deep does *not* carry.** In its production enhancement contracts, management is explicit: ***"we do not carry any success risk."*** **Deep is paid to raise output from wells that already exist.** *That is a fundamentally different risk profile from an explorer, and it is why a company this small can run 40%+ EBITDA margins.*

The 30-second snapshot:

- **Founded 1991 by the Savla family (Ahmedabad).** **Paras Savla, CMD** — founder-generation, still running it 35 years in
- **Q1FY27 was an all-time-high quarter:** revenue **₹278.9cr (+39.8%)** · EBITDA **₹131.8cr (43.6% margin)** · **PAT ₹89.1cr (+44.5%)**
- **FY26:** revenue **₹891cr** · operating profit **₹355cr (40%)** · PAT ₹197cr *(after a ₹183cr write-off)*
- **🎯 Explicit guidance on the record: FY27 PAT ~₹350cr; FY28 PAT ~₹500cr**
- **Order book ₹3,047cr** — ~3.8 years of cover against ~₹800cr of guided FY27 execution — including a **₹1,402cr / 15-year ONGC production enhancement contract**
- **20 onshore rigs at 100% utilisation** · **80+ gas compression units, the largest fleet in India, ~85% of the outsourced market**
- **Net-debt-free.** Borrowings ₹203cr against ₹1,999cr of net worth; **debt/EBITDA 0.48**; **no equity raise contemplated**
- **Promoter 63.49% — unchanged across 12 quarters**, including through a reported loss year

**Big picture in one sentence:** a founder-run, net-debt-free, 40%-margin services monopoly in Indian post-exploration work, **guiding to more than double profit in two years** — **with two live and legitimate questions: two consecutive years of Q4 write-offs after an assurance there would be none, and a core standalone business that has been flat for five quarters while subsidiaries carried all the growth.**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — India is in one of its strongest multi-year energy investment cycles.** The policy shift is from *"energy security to energy independence"*, with a roadmap to mobilise **US$100bn+ in oil and gas by 2030** and **US$500bn+ across the energy value chain.**

**Force 2 — Gas consumption is set to grow ~60% by 2030** — from 188 MMSCMD in FY24 to ~297 MMSCMD on the government's "good-to-go" scenario. **Deep's compression and dehydration fleet sits directly in that flow.**

**Force 3 — The May-2026 royalty overhaul improves upstream economics.** Rates were *"slashed"* and the regime moved to wellhead pricing with a fixed 15–20% deduction, *"removing disputes over post-production cost deductions."* **Better producer economics means more activity, especially in difficult fields — which is precisely where post-exploration services are needed.**

**Force 4 — Acreage is opening at scale.** *"Nearly 1 million square kilometres"* released for seismic and drilling as no-go areas shrink, and a pipeline network *"crossing 25,000 kilometres in 2026."* **🆕 Plus Samudra Manthan, a ₹80,000cr National Deep Water Exploration Mission with the government bearing up to 50% of exploratory well drilling cost.**

**Force 5 — Structural outsourcing.** Management sizes the onshore rig market at **215–220 rigs, of which ~120–125 are outsourced, with the outsourced share rising.** In gas compression, outsourcing is only ~30% of the total market — **and Deep already holds ~85% of that outsourced slice.** *The runway is the outsourcing ratio, not the market size.*

**⚠️ And a macro caveat management gave honestly.** Asked whether the surge in enquiries is cyclical or crisis-driven following the **Strait of Hormuz closure** in early 2026 — now settled into *"structural rebalancing"* with the strait *"still operating under restricted capacity"* — Savla did not oversell it: ***"it is a mixture of both."***

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Dominant share of Indian post-exploration services.** Screener's characterisation is 70%+; **management's own numbers are more specific and more striking: ~85% of the outsourced gas-compression market with 80+ units, the largest fleet in India.**

**Moat 2 — Long-tenor contracts that competitors cannot displace.** The order book contains a **15-year ONGC production enhancement contract worth ₹1,402cr**, with others averaging ~2.5 years. **More than 60% of the book executes over two to two and a half years.**

**Moat 3 — 40%+ EBITDA margins, sustained.** Q1FY27 **43.6%**; Q3FY26 47.6%; guided at **44–45% (±1–2%)**. **A 43.6% EBITDA margin on ~₹280cr of quarterly revenue is exceptional for an oilfield-services business**, and it comes from the integrated PMC positioning — *"a margin-assertive opportunity."*

**Moat 4 — Mobilisation time creates real switching costs.** A community analyst put it well: gas compression carries **4–6 month mobilisation**, which is modest but genuine friction for a customer considering a switch.

**Moat 5 — Capital discipline, and a QIP that was shelved.** **Debt/EBITDA 0.48**, CFO **₹270cr** in FY26 against ₹210cr, **CFO/operating profit 81%**, EBITDA-to-cash conversion **75–80%**. Strong enough that the planned QIP was cancelled — *"we have decided not to go ahead with QIP"* — and reaffirmed on the Q1FY27 call: *"as of now we are not foreseeing any equity raise."*

**Moat 6 — A 35-year founder still running it, with holding unchanged through a loss year.** **63.49% across twelve quarters**, including through the FY25 reported loss and the QIP cancellation — **both natural exit points for a promoter who had doubts.**

**Moat 7 — 🆕 A cheap, under-modelled margin lever at Kandla.** The plan is to manufacture a critical drilling/IPM chemical in-house, worth *"one, one and a half percent"* of **EBITDA margin**, on capex of only *"10, 15 crores"* with **no debt.** **100–150bps on a 43.6% base, for ₹15cr.**

**Where the moat is weaker — and two of these are the whole debate.**

- **🔴 Two consecutive years of large Q4 write-offs, the second after an assurance there would be no more.** Other income of **−₹245cr in Mar-2025** (PAT −₹207cr) and **−₹183cr in Mar-2026** (PAT −₹7.2cr). **The base rate on management's "nothing else left" assurance is one broken promise out of one.**
- **🔴 The core standalone business has been flat for five quarters at ~₹175cr a quarter.** **All of the 40% consolidated growth is coming from subsidiaries** — Dolphin Offshore (~₹43cr) and the Dubai and Indian subsidiaries (>₹50cr).
- **🟡 Rigs are at 100% utilisation.** Incremental onshore orders require newbuild, and that is capital-intensive.
- **🟡 Customer concentration.** ONGC dominates; contract timing moves earnings.
- **🟡 PEC execution risk is demonstrated, not theoretical.** The **Mori-5 gas leak (January 2026)** cost **5–6 months** of ramp.
- **🟡 Free market gas pricing on the PEC** — currently **US$8–15/MMBTU**. Deep takes price exposure even though it takes no exploration risk.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — Post-exploration is structurally the right place to sit.** Deep is paid to raise output from wells that already exist, and *"we do not carry any success risk."* **An explorer's upside without an explorer's risk.**

**Thread B — The outsourcing ratio is the runway, not the market size.** ~120–125 of 215–220 onshore rigs are outsourced and the share is rising; in gas compression outsourcing is only ~30% of the market and **Deep already has ~85% of that slice.**

**Thread C — The PEC model is the highest-value version of Thread A**, and it is now dated: incremental production from **September-end/October 2026**, **₹150cr+** of FY28 revenue from one field, with two much larger ONGC tenders (Gamij, Geleki) live.

**Thread D — Offshore is a second leg with better margins**, built strictly against firm orders.

**Thread E — Kandla is a margin lever disguised as an acquisition.** *"one, one and a half percent"* of EBITDA margin for *"10, 15 crores"* of capex.

**Thread F — The balance sheet funds all of it without dilution.** Debt/EBITDA 0.48, CFO/OP 81%, QIP shelved, no equity raise contemplated.

**🎯 Where the threads meet — and where the file's real tension sits.** Threads A through F describe a genuinely good business: **a structurally advantaged services monopoly with 40%+ margins, self-funding growth, dated catalysts and a founder who has not sold a share in twelve quarters.** *On the strategy, nothing here is in doubt.*

**The tension is that two independent observers arrived at the same two doubts, and neither is about strategy.**
