## 1. Business in Plain English

When an Indian bank, research lab, defence agency or AI startup needs serious compute — a supercomputer, an AI training cluster, a private-cloud stack — it faces a choice: buy an imported system from Dell, HPE or Supermicro, or buy one designed and built in India. **Netweb is the credible Indian answer.**

It designs and manufactures **high-end computing systems** at Faridabad: supercomputers (its Tyrone brand), AI training and inference clusters, private cloud, high-performance storage and data-centre servers. It is not an assembler badging someone else's box — it owns the design, holds the Make-in-India certification that matters for government and PSU procurement, and increasingly writes its own software stack on top.

**The useful analogy is a bespoke racing-car builder in a market that has just discovered motor racing.** The chassis and engine parts come from outside (GPUs are Nvidia's), but the design, integration, tuning and service are the product — and when demand for the sport explodes, the builder with the local factory and the government's approved-vendor stamp is in an extraordinary position.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — India is building sovereign AI compute from close to zero.** The **IndiaAI Mission — $1.2bn+ of initial funding, targeting ₹10,000+ cr over five years** — government GPU tenders, PSU and defence procurement, and university supercomputing all require compute that is *procured in India*. This is a policy-driven demand curve, not a cyclical one.

**Force 2 — Make-in-India certification is a procurement moat, not a slogan.** Government and PSU tenders carry local-content requirements that imported systems cannot satisfy. **Netweb is one of very few Indian companies that can bid and win on that basis.**

**Force 3 — AI inference is moving on-premise.** Data residency, cost per token at scale, and latency are pushing workloads out of hyperscaler clouds and back into private data centres. That is Netweb's exact product.

**Force 4 — Enterprise HPC is the ballast.** Research computing, weather modelling, genomics, engineering simulation — steady, less spectacular, and the base the company was built on before AI arrived.

**Force 5 — GPU supply has stopped being the binding constraint, and capital has become one.** Management's own framing on the Q1FY27 call: *"we do not want a situation where a very large growth looking at the pipeline is available for us to grab and we don't grab it because of the lack of capital."* **The QIP is the answer to that sentence.**

**Where Netweb sits.** No listed Indian pure-play comparable exists. Global analogues (Supermicro, Penguin Solutions) are larger and trade very differently. **Within the watchlist it has no natural peer either** — AURIONPRO, DATAMATICS and MPSLTD share a sector label and nothing else.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Owned design plus domestic manufacture, in a market where that combination is a legal requirement for a large slice of demand.** This is the whole moat and it is genuinely narrow to replicate.

**Moat 2 — Extraordinary asset efficiency.** **Gross fixed asset turnover of 43.9x** at 30-Jun-2026. This is a design-and-integrate business, not a capital-intensive one — which is why ROCE is 48.2% on management's Q1 basis while revenue triples.

**Moat 3 — The order book grew 19% in the quarter revenue grew 172%.** ₹2,100cr entering FY27 → **₹2,507cr firm** at 30-Jun, with **L1 up 183% to ₹848cr**. **Backlog rising while shipments triple is the signature of real demand rather than drawdown.**

**Moat 4 — A ₹10,400 cr pipeline with a stated conversion rate.** Management guides **~60% conversion over 18–24 months**. That is an unusually specific and falsifiable disclosure, and it should be held to.

**Moat 5 — Margin held through a violent mix shift.** AI Systems went from 43.4% of FY26 revenue to **62% of Q1FY27**, and EBITDA margin *expanded* 222bps sequentially to 14.7% — above the guided 13–14%. **Mix-shifting into a new product category without margin loss is the hard version of this.**

**Moat 6 — 🆕 A QIP book of genuine quality.** ICICI Prudential across five funds (16.67%), **Nomura (13.10%), Goldman Sachs (11.21%), Think India (8.33%), Edelweiss (8.33%)** — and priced at just a **1.96% discount to floor**, which is what a heavily oversubscribed book looks like.

**Moat 7 — Working capital improved in the fastest-growing quarter.** Receivable days **86 → 78** on *"stronger collections"*, cash conversion cycle **96 days**. **Most hardware businesses growing 172% do the opposite.**

**Moat 8 — Optionality being built quietly.** R&D underway in **physical AI and quantum computing**, disclosed but deliberately not sized: *"we are at the liberty to disclose this much only."*

**Where the moat is weaker — and the list is short but sharp.**
- **🔴 Valuation is the risk, not the business.** 122x trailing, 127x post-QIP.
- **🔴 GPU dependence sits upstream.** Nvidia sets availability, allocation and cost. Netweb integrates; it does not control the scarce input.
- **⚠️ Contracts are large and lumpy.** ₹430cr of a single ₹1,600cr strategic order was executed in one quarter.
- **⚠️ Execution cycles have lengthened** — management moved its own guidance from *"8 to 12 weeks"* to **"16 to 20 weeks."**
- **⚠️ Inventory days rose 86 → 110**, deliberately, to secure GPU supply. Sensible, and it is still capital tied up.
- **⚠️ Prior-period balance sheet was restated** on an inventory-valuation policy change (§4).
- **⚠️ Promoter holding fell 71.03% → 66.98%** in the March-2026 quarter, and the QIP takes it to **~64.2%**.

The 28-Jul refresh set the 29-Jul call as its highest-priority watch item. Three analysts on that call asked management about a ₹1,200 crore fundraise. **Here is the complete sequence, every item from a filing or a filed transcript:**

**What was said on 29-Jul, verbatim:**

> **Akshay (AK Investment):** *"recently, we had the exchange billing of INR1,200 crores QIP. So what is the rationale for INR1,200 crores QIP?"*
> **Sanjeev Sancheti:** *"**I don't know where you got this news for the QIP. I haven't seen anything like that.** There was an enabling resolution which we took to raise capital if need be. It has a valid of 12 months. So having said that, for the growth, if at some stage, we think we need to raise capital, we'll look at it."*

> **Mansimer Singh Sethi (Citi Capital):** *"We are fundraising INR1,200 crores, right? So will there be such more fundings going on?"*
> **Sanjeev Sancheti:** *"No, no. So first of all, **we haven't raised.** So there's no question of further more. We've taken an enabling resolution… **Which form of capital, whether it will be debt or equity, we have not decided.**"*

> **Omkar (Shree Investments):** *"you said that you don't have anything to say on the enabling resolution… at least you can tell us like at first place, why did you take that call and what it is for exactly?"*
> **Sanjeev Sancheti:** *"you've seen the pipeline and the order book that we have, it is possible that in order to pursue that both, we may at some stage need capital… we cannot suddenly raise the resolution and take the capital. So we have taken an enabling resolution for this."*

**🟢 The fair reading first, because it carries real weight.** ***Nothing here is an allegation of impropriety.*** **SEBI's ICDR regulations restrict publicity around a prospective issue** — management genuinely cannot market or pre-announce a QIP that has not opened, and saying so plainly on a public call would itself be a problem. **And the substance of what Sancheti said was true on the day: they had not raised, and an enabling resolution is exactly what the 1-July board approval was.** The refusal to be drawn was, in substance, correct behaviour.

**🔴 What is still worth recording.** The analysts were not working from a rumour. **The ₹1,200 crore figure was in the company's own board-meeting outcome, filed with both exchanges 28 days earlier.** *"I don't know where you got this news… I haven't seen anything like that"* is not an accurate description of the company's own disclosure, and *"whether it will be debt or equity, we have not decided"* was followed by an all-equity QIP opening **19 days later.**

***My assessment, stated as mine:*** **the decision was defensible and the wording was not.** A single sentence — *"the ₹1,200 crore is the cap in our 1 July board approval; we can't say more while a resolution is live"* — would have been accurate, compliant and complete. **For a holder, the lesson is narrow and practical: on this company, read the filings rather than the call for anything to do with capital.** It does not extend to the operating disclosures, which have been detailed, specific and reliable throughout.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — AI Systems is the whole story now, and it arrived faster than anyone modelled.** 12% of FY25 → 43.4% of FY26 → **62.29% of Q1FY27**, growing 484%. Everything else is context.

**Thread B — Make-in-India certification converts a policy tailwind into an order book.** Government, PSU and defence tenders carry local-content rules that imports fail. **This is why the order book grows while revenue triples: Netweb is bidding in a field with few qualified competitors.**

**Thread C — Capital was the binding constraint, and it has just been removed.** Interest ₹1cr → ₹12cr, inventory days 86 → 110, net debt zero → ₹200cr. Management named the problem precisely: *"we do not want a situation where a very large growth… is available for us to grab and we don't grab it because of the lack of capital."* **The QIP is Thread C resolving.**

**Thread D — Execution cycles are lengthening, which is what scale does.** *"8 to 12 weeks"* became *"16 to 20 weeks."* **Bigger contracts take longer, which stretches working capital further — and is another reason the QIP matters.**

**Thread E — GPU supply sits upstream and outside the company's control.** The inventory build is a hedge against it. **Netweb's margin is an integration-and-service margin, so a GPU price move affects revenue more than profit — but availability affects everything.**

**Thread F — physical AI and quantum are being built quietly.** Disclosed, deliberately unsized: *"we are at the liberty to disclose this much only."* **Optionality, not thesis.**

**How the threads interact.** A and B are the engine; C was the brake and has been released; D and E determine how smoothly the engine runs. **The business risk in this name is genuinely low relative to its growth rate. The risk is entirely in what you pay for it.**

### What 122x is paying for, and whether it can be earned

**Take the bull case entirely at face value.** Order book ₹3,355cr including L1, pipeline ₹10,400cr, management's own conversion guide of ~60% over 18-24 months. **That funnel alone implies ~₹6,200cr of eventual revenue from what is visible today.** At a 13-14% EBITDA margin and ~10% PAT margin that is ~₹620cr of PAT — **against a ₹33,295cr market cap, roughly 54x on the fully-converted pipeline.**

***That is the honest framing of the price: even if you grant management every number it has disclosed and assume the entire visible funnel converts, the stock is at 54x that outcome.*** **The multiple does not just require the current pipeline to convert — it requires the pipeline to keep being replaced at this rate for years.**

**The counter-argument deserves its weight.** Netweb has beaten every guide it has given, by wide margins, for two years running: a 50% guide met with 90%, a 60% guide met with 87%, a 13-13.5% margin guide met with 14.70%. **ROCE is 37.5% trailing and 48.2% on the current quarter, with a gross fixed asset turn of 43.9x.** **A business that compounds at these rates with almost no capital intensity genuinely deserves a multiple most businesses do not.** The question is whether it deserves this one.

### Weighted return framework (2-year, to FY28)

*Scenarios run **per share on the post-QIP count of 5.9446cr**, not on market cap, because the share count has just changed.*

**Base case — 50%.** AI Systems demand persists through FY27-28, the order book and a good share of the funnel convert, margin holds at 13-14%, and the QIP capital does its job. **FY28 revenue ~₹4,500cr, PAT ~₹470cr → EPS ~₹79. The multiple compresses to 65x — still a very rich multiple for a hardware integrator. → ₹5,142/share. ≈ −8%.**

**Bull case — 30%** *(raised from 25% at the last refresh, because the capital constraint that would have throttled this outcome has been removed).* The India AI-infrastructure cycle runs hard, Netweb holds share as the Make-in-India incumbent, the ₹10,400cr funnel converts above 60%, and physical AI or quantum starts contributing. **FY28 revenue ~₹6,000cr, PAT ~₹660cr → EPS ~₹111 at 75x → ₹8,325/share. ≈ +49%.**

**Bear case — 20%.** A large contract slips, GPU allocation tightens, IndiaAI budgets are deferred, or a hyperscaler or Dell/HPE India in-sources. **Growth need not stop — it need only normalise to ~30%, at which point the multiple resets to where hardware businesses actually trade.** **FY28 revenue ~₹3,400cr, PAT ~₹320cr → EPS ~₹53.8 at 35x → ₹1,883/share. ≈ −66%.**

**🔴 Weighted 2-year expected return ≈ −2.5%** *(0.50 × −8 + 0.30 × 49 + 0.20 × −66)*, with a negligible dividend.

**Compare to 28-Jul: +21% → ≈0%.** **Nothing in the business caused that. The price moved 28% in 25 days and the arithmetic followed.**

**The bear case is the thing to sit with.** It does not require Netweb to fail, or management to disappoint, or the AI thesis to be wrong. **It requires only that growth normalises and the market stops paying 122x** — and it produces −66%. **That asymmetry is what a 122x multiple does to an excellent business.**

### Where this becomes attractive again

**At ~₹4,000 (roughly 50x FY28 base EPS) the weighted return turns clearly positive.** The QIP was priced at **₹4,790**, which is a useful anchor: **institutions with full access to management and the order book paid ₹4,790 three days ago, and the market is asking ₹5,601 today — a 17% premium to the price at which ICICI, Nomura and Goldman were willing to commit ₹1,200cr.** *That is not a valuation model, but it is a real data point about informed opinion, and it points the same way as the arithmetic.*

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**🔴 Risk 1 — Valuation is the dominant risk, and it is not the same as business risk.** 122x trailing, 127x on the post-QIP count, ~54x on the *fully-converted* visible pipeline. **The bear case requires nothing to go wrong operationally — only for growth to normalise.** **Signal to watch: nothing. This one is a decision, not an observation.**

**🔴 Risk 2 — GPU supply and allocation sit upstream and outside the company's control.** Nvidia determines availability, allocation and cost. The 86 → 110 day inventory build is an explicit hedge against exactly this — management cited *"surging global demand for AI compute infrastructure."* **Netweb's margin is an integration margin, so a GPU *price* move passes through; a GPU *availability* problem does not.** **Signal to watch: inventory days, and any commentary on allocation.**

**🔴 Risk 3 — Contract lumpiness, now with a longer cycle.** ₹430cr of a single ₹1,600cr order executed in one quarter. **Management has itself lengthened the execution guide from "8 to 12 weeks" to "16 to 20 weeks."** **A single large slip moves a quarter materially, and quarters at 122x are not forgiven.** **Signal to watch: sequential revenue against the ₹3,355cr book.**

**⚠️ Risk 4 — IndiaAI and PSU budget timing.** The demand is policy-driven, which cuts both ways: it is durable and it is also subject to fiscal decisions and tender timelines nobody outside government controls. **Signal to watch: IndiaAI Mission allocations; order inflow from government versus enterprise.**

**⚠️ Risk 5 — Competition from Dell/HPE India entities establishing local manufacture.** The Make-in-India certification moat is regulatory, and regulatory moats can be entered by anyone willing to build a plant. **Signal to watch: any global OEM announcing Indian manufacturing for server/AI systems.**

**⚠️ Risk 6 — Dilution has been continuous.** Promoter **75.45% (Sep-2023) → 66.98% (Jun-2026) → ~64.2% post-QIP**. The QIP is issuance, not promoter selling, and it was raised at a 1.96% discount into a top-tier book — **which is close to the best possible version of dilution.** **It is still ~11 percentage points of control in three years.** **Signal to watch: whether the 12-month enabling resolution is used again.**

**⚠️ Risk 7 — 2.47 lakh shareholders and a stock 156% off its low.** Retail base has grown 3.3x since listing. **ValuePickr's own last substantive post (18-Jun-2026) made this exact point** (§9). **Momentum unwinds are self-reinforcing in names with this holder profile.**

**⚠️ Risk 8 — Customer concentration is undisclosed.** AI Systems is 62% of revenue growing 484%, and **no customer-concentration data is published.** An analyst asked about order mix on the Q1FY27 call and received a qualitative answer. **Term-swept the transcript: the word "concentration" does not appear once.** **Recorded as a disclosure gap, not as evidence of a problem. Signal to watch: any segment or customer disclosure in the FY27 annual report.**

**🆕 Risk 9 — Capital-markets communication (NEW — 22-Aug-2026).** Three analysts were told on 29-Jul that a ₹1,200cr figure was something management *"hadn't seen anything like"*, when it was in the company's own board filing of 1-Jul; a ₹1,200cr all-equity QIP closed 22 days later (§3.5). **The substance was compliant and the wording was not.** **This is a narrow risk and should be held narrowly: it says read the filings for capital-structure news, not that the operating disclosure is unreliable — that has been exemplary.** **Signal to watch: how the next capital event is communicated.**

**🆕 Risk 10 — The inventory-accounting restatement (NEW — 22-Aug-2026).** Prior-period balance sheet restated on a shift to moving-weighted-average inventory valuation. Routine and plausibly correct. **Unverified against the annual report.** **Signal to watch: the FY27 accounting-policy note, and whether gross margin behaves consistently across the change.**

**🟢 Risk 11 — the risk that this profile is too cautious, and it deserves stating.** **Every single watch item set in July resolved favourably.** Working capital improved in the fastest-growing quarter a company has ever had. The order book grew while revenue tripled. Margin beat guidance during a 19-point mix shift. ICICI, Nomura and Goldman committed ₹1,200cr at a 1.96% discount. **If India's sovereign-AI build-out is a decade-long capex cycle rather than a two-year one, then FY28 earnings are the wrong denominator entirely and 122x on trailing will look like a rounding error.** **Being right about the price and wrong about the duration of the cycle is a real way to lose money in this name.**
