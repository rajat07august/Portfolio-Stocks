## 1. Business in Plain English

Every port needs three services continuously: **dredging** — clearing silt from harbour channels so ships can enter safely (a bulldozer under water, moving sediment 24×7); **tug and vessel charter** — the harbour tugs that push and pull ships in and out of berths, plus specialised offshore vessels; and **shipbuilding** — constructing new vessels for the ecosystem.

**KMEW is a rare Indian company that does all three**, and it is pivoting into **green tugs** — battery-electric replacements for diesel harbour tugs — a segment the Ministry of Ports is actively pushing through the Green Tug Transition Programme.

**The snapshot:**
- **HQ Mumbai (Chembur), founded 2015 by the Kewalramani family** — a **ten-year-old company**, and that youth matters: there is no through-cycle record.
- **FY26 revenue ₹256cr (+27% from ₹201cr), EBITDA ~₹97cr (38%), PAT ₹79cr (31%)** — elite margins for marine services.
- **Q1FY27 revenue ₹115.41cr (+138%), PBT ₹65.33cr (+387%), PAT ₹62.75cr (+466%)**, blended EBITDA margin **63.6%** — with **no exceptional item**.
- **Order book ₹1,400cr** at FY26 exit — 5.5x revenue — after **₹1,075cr of order wins, the highest ever.**
- **Green tug entry:** two BOOT contracts (VOC Port + Vishakhapatnam) worth **₹650cr over 15-year tenures.**
- **45+ vessels owned.**

**Big picture in one sentence:** KMEW is a young, high-margin marine-services operator turning itself into an integrated maritime platform — dredging, chartering, shipbuilding and green-tug annuities — with a **ten-year tonnage-tax shield now confirmed**, a **shrinking order book**, and **promoter holding that has just crossed below 50%**, priced at 53x.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — India's port expansion (Sagarmala, Vadhvan, Green Ports).** Port capacity is projected to grow from **~2,600 million tonnes a year (2024) to 4,000+ mtpa by 2030**. **Vadhvan Port (Palghar)** — being developed as India's largest deepwater port — sits near KMEW's Saphale shipyard site. Every expansion needs dredging, tugs and specialised vessels. **India's dredging and related marine-services market was ~₹8,000cr in FY24 and is projected to double by 2030.**

**Force 2 — The Green Tug Transition Programme.** A Ministry of Ports mandate to progressively replace diesel harbour tugs with battery and hybrid tugs — **40+ green tugs across six major ports by 2030**. The contract structure is a **15-year BOOT concession**: KMEW builds, owns and operates the tug, creating multi-year annuity revenue. **Only ~4–5 Indian companies can bid and execute.**

**Force 3 — Inland waterways.** The Inland Waterways Authority of India is developing **111 National Waterways**. KMEW executes IWAI orders for cutter suction dredgers and ancillary craft — smaller, but growing, and now extending into **hybrid-electric passenger ferries**.

**Force 4 — Shipbuilding indigenisation.** India's shipbuilding capacity is **~15% of what it needs**, and the government offers a **10–15% construction subsidy per vessel**, applied before build and decided within ~60 days of application.

**Where KMEW sits.** A mid-cap integrated marine-services player. **Dredging Corporation of India (DCI)** is the PSU incumbent in maintenance dredging — 74% owned by four government ports, domestic-only, limited contract range — and is also a **customer**, subcontracting work to KMEW. **Cochin Shipyard, Mazagon Dock and Garden Reach** are PSU shipbuilders focused on defence. **Great Eastern Shipping and SCI** are bulk shipping with different economics. **Global Offshore Services and Seamec** occupy a different offshore niche.

**No listed Indian peer combines dredging + chartering + shipbuilding + green tugs.** The trade-off is **breadth versus depth**: if every segment scales, KMEW becomes a full-stack maritime company like **Boskalis** in the Netherlands; if only one or two do, it is a mid-cap contractor at a full-stack multiple.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — The only listed Indian combination of dredging + chartering + shipbuilding.** Creates genuine cross-selling: build the vessel in-house, operate it, maintain it.

**Moat 2 — Green tug BOOT contracts.** ₹650cr of contract value over 15 years ≈ **₹43cr a year of recurring revenue per contract** at steady state. **Long-tenure annuity revenue is unusual in this sector** and is the single most important differentiator. Both tugs are being built at Saphale by KMEW's own team; components and thrusters ordered, physical construction post-monsoon 2026, completion guided **mid-monsoon 2027**.

**Moat 3 — River Pearl 47, India's largest and deepest self-propelled backhoe dredger.** Built in-house. **Rock dredging** — underwater controlled drilling and blasting — is a capability many peers lack; the **₹50cr JNPA contract** was executed with it. Management guides **240+ days a year of utilisation for the next 2–3 years**, targeting rock-dredging bids at **New Mangalore, Bombay Port, JNPA, Vishakhapatnam and Tuticorin** for jobs of 10,000–500,000 cubic metres.

**Moat 4 — The Saphale shipyard, and it is now real.** ~**15 acres acquired (plus a 5-acre option)** on the Vaitarna river through subsidiary **Knowledge Shipyard Private Limited**: **~188m waterfront, natural slipway, ~6m high-tide draft**, near Vadhvan Port. Design envelope **10–120m vessels** (mooring boats, tugs, survey boats, oil tankers, barges); target **14 vessels a year** when fully functional. Management is *"doing marketing right now, in Europe"* and expects **export orders in Q3 or Q4 FY27**. Shipbuilding EBITDA guided at **25–30% pre-subsidy, 35–40% including the government subsidy.**
> 🟡 **But KMEW owns only 51% of Knowledge Shipyard** (guided to rise to 75%, the balance held by *"a few of the other past owners and upcoming shareholders"*), and the yard is a ₹100cr build inside a ₹400–500cr capex programme on a ₹256cr revenue base.

**Moat 5 — Diversified port customer base.** JNPA, Paradip, Pondicherry, VOC Port, Vishakhapatnam, IWAI, and DCI as a subcontracting channel. **No single customer above ~25% of revenue** — a genuinely favourable contrast with most of this watchlist.

**Moat 6 — Selective bidding, and the margins show it.** Kanak Kewalramani, Q4FY26: *"We continue to remain highly selective in project bidding with a strategic focus on contracts that meet our return thresholds and profitability expectations."* A 38% FY26 EBITDA margin is the evidence.

**Moat 7 — 🟢 A ten-year tonnage-tax shield, confirmed at source.** Approval under **s.115VP(3) of the Income Tax Act, effective ten years from AY2026-27**. Income from qualifying ships is computed on net tonnage, not actual profits. Management: *"the percentage will not go beyond 0.1% of our revenue"* from chartering or dredging. **Q1FY27's effective tax rate was 3.96%.** This is the most durable single advantage in the case, and it runs to AY2035-36.

**Where the moat is weaker — stated plainly:**
- **🔴 The order book went backwards** — ₹1,500cr (Feb-2026) → ₹1,400cr (Jun-2026), with the bid pipeline down a third.
- **Small base.** ₹256cr of FY26 revenue means a single lost contract or a vessel out of service hurts materially.
- **Asset-heavy.** Every new tug or dredger is ₹30–80cr of capital; FY27 capex is guided at ₹400–500cr.
- **Long working-capital cycle** — ports pay slowly; debtor days 109 against a 45–60 day target.
- **Geopolitical lumpiness.** Bahrain and Myanmar both went to zero revenue.
- **Ten years old.** No observed behaviour through a downturn.

### 💰 The tax story — verified at source, and larger than v1 knew

**Note 7 of the FY26 results (29-May-2026):**
> *"The Company have received an approval from the Joint Commissioner of Income Tax, Mumbai under section 115VP(3) of the Income Tax Act for exercising option for Tonnage Tax Scheme. According to the order, **the said approval is effective for a period of 10 years beginning from A.Y. 2026-27.**"*
> *"…the **deferred tax liability recognised in earlier years has been reversed / written back**…"*

**Two distinct effects — do not conflate them:**
1. **Structural and durable.** Income from qualifying ships is computed presumptively on net tonnage. Sujay: *"the percentage will not go beyond **0.1% of our revenue**"* from chartering or dredging. Shipbuilding stays on corporate tax; FY27 mix guided at **80% dredging/chartering, 20% shipbuilding.** Going forward the split is structural: **KMEW Ltd carries chartering and dredging (tonnage tax); Knowledge Shipyard carries shipbuilding (corporate tax).** **This runs to AY2035-36.**
2. **One-off.** The transition reversed prior-year deferred tax liabilities — a **₹6.66cr credit in FY26** (₹9.09cr in Q4FY26 alone), which is why FY26's rate was *negative* and reported PAT (₹79.11cr) slightly exceeded PBT (₹78.75cr). **Strip it and FY26 PAT is ~₹72cr, not ₹79cr.** *(My computation.)*

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Build → own → operate → maintain.** The Saphale yard is what turns three separate businesses into one. KMEW builds its own green tugs there rather than buying them, which converts a supplier's margin into its own and shortens the path from contract award to revenue. The same yard then sells vessels to third parties — target **14 a year**, with Europe marketing underway.

**Green tugs are the annuity layer.** ₹650cr over 15 years is not a project; it is a bond with an operating business attached. **Two contracts are ~₹43cr a year each at steady state — roughly a third of FY26 revenue, recurring**, before any of the ₹600cr of green-tug bids in the pipeline convert.

**Tonnage tax multiplies everything above it.** A 38% EBITDA margin taxed at 0.1% of revenue converts to cash at a rate almost no Indian industrial can match — **and it is locked for ten years.** This is why the ROE of 20% coexists with heavy asset intensity.

**But the three threads pull against one constraint: capital.** Every green tug, every dredger and the yard itself must be built before it earns. FY27 capex of ₹400–500cr on a ₹256cr revenue base is funded by ₹300cr of free cash, then debt, then equity — and equity has now been used twice in ten months. **The faster the strategy works, the more capital it consumes.**

**And the leading indicator is pointing the wrong way.** A ₹1,400cr order book that fell ₹100cr while the pipeline fell a third does not fit a company guiding 30% revenue growth for two years. **Either the ₹2,000cr converts by September as management said, or the growth has to come from a book that is shrinking.** That is the tension the whole thesis now rests on.
