## 💰 The tax story — verified at source, and genuinely large

**Note 7 of the FY26 results (29-May-2026):**

> *"The Company have received an approval from the Joint Commissioner of Income Tax, Mumbai under section 115VP(3) of the Income Tax Act for exercising option for Tonnage Tax Scheme. According to the order, **the said approval is effective for a period of 10 years beginning from A.Y. 2026-27.**"*

> *"…the **deferred tax liability recognised in earlier years has been reversed / written back**…"*

Two distinct effects, and they should not be conflated:
1. **Structural and durable.** Income from qualifying ships is computed presumptively on net tonnage, not on actual profits. Sujay: *"the percentage will not go beyond **0.1% of our revenue**, which is from chartering or dredging services."* Shipbuilding stays on corporate tax — and management guides FY27 mix at **80% dredging/chartering, 20% shipbuilding**. **This runs to AY2035-36.**
2. **One-off.** The transition reversed prior-year deferred tax liabilities — a **₹6.66cr credit in FY26** (₹9.09cr in Q4FY26 alone), which is why FY26's effective tax rate was *negative* and reported PAT (₹79.11cr) slightly exceeded PBT (₹78.75cr). **Strip it and FY26 PAT is ~₹72cr, not ₹79cr.** *(My computation.)*

**This is the single best thing about the investment case, and v1 identified it correctly.** It is now confirmed from the company's own note rather than from concall commentary.

## 💵 Cash, capex and the depreciation problem

| | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | **FY26** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cash from operations (₹ cr) | 35 | 21 | 38 | 58 | **74** |
| **CFO / Operating profit** | 126% | 53% | 95% | 87% | **89%** |
| Cash from investing | −31 | −23 | −72 | −127 | **−379** |
| Cash from financing | 7 | 35 | 28 | 64 | **+347** |
| **Free cash flow** | 8 | 4 | **−20** | **−43** | **−45** |
| **Debtor days** | 24 | 101 | 86 | **131** | **109** |
| Borrowings (₹ cr) | 26 | 25 | 60 | 133 | **222** |

**CFO conversion is good — 89% of operating profit, better than most names in this watchlist.** The cash problem is not collection quality, it is that **every rupee earned plus every rupee raised is going into vessels and a shipyard.** FCF has been negative three years running and will be far more negative in FY27.

**FY27 capex is guided at ₹400-500cr** — Sujay: *"close to ₹100 crores is on the shipyard. Balance will be acquisition of dredgers and tugs."* Against **FY26 revenue of ₹256cr** and a gross block of ₹211cr plus ₹99cr CWIP. Funding: *"cash and debt first before looking at the equity option"* — cash ~₹350cr of which ~₹300cr free — **and the equity option was exercised nine weeks later.**

**🔴 This is where I think the market is under-modelling.** Depreciation is already **+82% YoY in Q1FY27** and +70% in FY26. A ₹400-500cr asset addition on a ₹310cr gross block roughly doubles it again. **Tonnage tax protects the tax line but does nothing for depreciation** — so EBITDA growth of 30% can arrive alongside flat or falling PAT in FY28. *(This is my inference; management has not addressed the depreciation trajectory on any call.)*

**⚠️ Debtor days 109 against guidance.** On the Q3 call management targeted **45-60 days by year-end**. Pankaj Sachdeva pressed on it in June; Sujay attributed the gap to a **₹16cr one-off claim** on the Mangrol DCI contract — *"that's the claim that we have preferred that you are seeing, which is one-off, not for each and every billing"* — and expects to collect it in FY27. **Even excluding ₹16cr, days would be ~96 — still well outside the guided band.** 🔴 **Missed.**

## 1. Business in Plain English

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

Indian marine services + port infrastructure is riding four durable forces:

**Force 1 — India's port expansion (Sagarmala + Vadhvan + Green Ports).** India's port capacity is projected to grow from ~2,600 million tonnes/year (2024) to ~4,000+ mtpa by 2030. Vadhvan Port (Palghar, near KMEW's Saphale shipyard site) is being developed as India's largest deepwater port. Every port expansion requires dredging (capital + maintenance), tug fleets, and specialised vessels. India's dredging + related marine services market: ~INR 8,000 cr in FY24, projected to double by 2030.

**Force 2 — Green Tug Transition Program.** The Ministry of Ports issued a mandate to progressively replace diesel harbour tugs with green (battery + hybrid) tugs across major Indian ports. Initial rollout: 40+ green tugs across 6 major ports by 2030. Contract structure: 15-year concessions where KMEW builds, owns, and operates the tug (BOOT model) — creating multi-year annuity revenue. Only ~4–5 Indian companies have the capability to bid and execute.

**Force 3 — Inland waterways expansion.** Inland Waterways Authority of India (IWAI) has plans to develop 111 National Waterways. KMEW is executing IWAI orders for cutter suction dredgers + ancillary craft. This is a smaller but growing segment.

**Force 4 — Shipbuilding indigenisation.** India's shipbuilding capacity is ~15% of what it needs. Government has announced multiple incentive schemes to grow indigenous shipbuilding (Make in India for ships). KMEW's Saphale (Palghar) shipyard, strategically located near upcoming Vadhvan Port, positions them for both group demand + external orders.

**Where KMEW sits in the map.** It's a **mid-cap integrated marine services player**. Peers:
- **Dredging Corporation of India (DCI)** — PSU, dominant in maintenance dredging, but bureaucratic
- **Cochin Shipyard, Mazagon Dock, Garden Reach Shipbuilders** — PSU shipbuilders, focused on defence
- **Great Eastern Shipping, Shipping Corporation of India** — bulk shipping, different economics
- **Global Offshore Services, Seamec** — offshore support, different niche
- **KMEW is unusual** in combining dredging + chartering + shipbuilding + green tugs in one platform. No listed Indian peer has this combination.

The trade-off: **breadth (four segments) vs depth**. If each segment scales, KMEW becomes a full-stack maritime services company (like Boskalis in Netherlands). If only one or two scale, it's just a mid-cap contractor.
