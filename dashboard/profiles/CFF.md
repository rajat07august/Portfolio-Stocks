## 1. Business in Plain English

CFF makes the **plumbing and sensory equipment inside Indian Navy submarines and warships** — the valves, pumps, fluid-control and platform systems that let a submarine dive, surface, trim and stay silent, plus, increasingly, **sonar**.

**Three kinds of work:**
1. **Submarine systems and spares.** The largest steady stream is the **P75 Kalvari (Scorpene) programme** — the six French-designed submarines built by Mazagon Dock. CFF holds **Naval Group quality certification** and supplies equipment and spares through the Navy's **Material Organisation** and Mazagon Dock. It also works on the older HDW (German) SSK class and on refits at the Naval Dockyards in Mumbai and Karwar.
2. **Surface-ship systems.** Equipment for P17A Nilgiri-class frigates, P15B destroyers and ASW corvettes.
3. **Sonar — the step-change.** **Low Frequency Variable Depth Sonar (LFVDS)**, built with **Atlas Elektronik (Germany, now TKMS)**, for the Navy's Anti-Submarine Warfare Shallow Water Craft. Two orders: **₹266.94cr from GRSE (1-Jul-2024)** and **₹267.60cr from Cochin Shipyard (25-Jul-2024)** — together more than CFF's entire FY25 revenue.

**Scale:** FY26 revenue **₹208.7cr**; 263 permanent employees across **Khopoli** (Raigad) and **Chakan** (Pune); ~8,000 sq m of shop floor. Order book **₹551cr** at 3-Jul-2026.

**The analogy: a specialist cabinetmaker fitting out yachts for one owner.** Every piece is custom, certified and fitted to someone else's hull; the owner is the only customer; payment comes when the yacht passes its sea trials, not when the cabinet leaves the workshop. **Excellent margins, total customer concentration, and a long wait to be paid.**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**1. India is building submarines at home, and the next programme is enormous.** The FY26 AR cites **P75I — six air-independent-propulsion submarines, ~₹90,000cr, expected to be contracted in FY2027**, led by TKMS with Mazagon Dock. Behind it: P75(AS) (Naval Group), the indigenous P76, P17B frigates and next-generation corvettes. *The programme values and timings are the company's characterisation.*

**2. Indigenisation is policy.** Positive-indigenisation lists and the "Aatmanirbhar" push force foreign OEMs (Naval Group, TKMS) to source critical subsystems from **qualified Indian manufacturers.** CFF's value is that it is already qualified.

**3. Qualification is the barrier.** Submarine systems require OEM certification (Naval Group, Atlas), Navy dockyard authorisation and factory/sea acceptance trials. The AR: *"The Indian Navy does not grant second chances."* That barrier protects margin — CFF's operating margin has been **26-30% for five years.**

**4. Refits are recurring.** Each submarine and ship returns for scheduled refits; spares flow for decades. The P75 spares stream (a dozen Material Organisation orders of ₹4-28cr since 2024) is the annuity underneath the lumpy systems orders.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**1. Qualified on the programmes that matter.** Naval Group-certified for Scorpene; Atlas Elektronik's designated Indian partner for LFVDS after a full Atlas audit; authorised across Naval Dockyards (Mumbai, Karwar, Visakhapatnam). Customers include Mazagon Dock, GRSE, Cochin Shipyard, Naval Dockyards, Naval HQ and the Material Organisation.

**2. Exceptional margins for its size.** OPM **28%** in FY26, **26-30% every year since FY22**; PAT margin **18.8%**.

**3. Order cover.** ₹551cr against ₹208.7cr of FY26 revenue — **~2.6x** *(the AR says "approximately three times")*.

**4. Near debt-free.** Borrowings ₹19.8cr against equity ₹266cr (D/E 0.07). **No promoter pledge** (Mar-2026 shareholding pattern).

**5. Two technology partners** — Naval Group and Atlas Elektronik — that a newcomer would take years to replicate.

### Where the moat is weaker — honestly

- **One customer, in effect.** Every rupee ultimately comes from the Indian Navy. The AR's own risk register lists *"Programme Concentration"* first.
- **The margin is not turning into cash.** See §4 — this is the central issue, not a footnote.
- **The technology is partly licensed.** Atlas designs the sonar; Naval Group designs the submarine. CFF's moat is manufacturing qualification, not intellectual property.
- **A sister company with the same business.** Flash Forge Pvt Ltd (§3.5) is authorised to do what CFF does, with **no non-compete** — the FPO prospectus itself calls it a potential conflict of interest.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**The business case is real.** India is building submarines and warships at home; foreign OEMs must source from qualified Indian suppliers; CFF is qualified on the two platforms (Scorpene and LFVDS) that matter now, and on the OEM relationships (Naval Group, TKMS/Atlas) that lead the next ones. Revenue has compounded 43% with a stable 28% margin. **On the P&L, this is one of the best small defence manufacturers in the country.**

**The balance sheet tells a different story, and it is the same story every year.** Naval contracts pay on milestones and acceptance trials; foreign components must be prepaid; GST is paid before it is recovered. **Every rupee of growth therefore absorbs more than a rupee of working capital.** The company has closed that gap twice with equity (₹85.8cr in 2023, ₹87.75cr in 2025), not with operating cash.

**So the chain is: qualification → orders → revenue → receivables and advances → equity raise.** The first three links are excellent. **The last two mean each shareholder funds the growth twice — once by paying 57x earnings, and again through dilution when the next programme needs working capital.** P75I, if it comes, is a much larger programme than LFVDS; the ₹1,000cr non-fund-based limit approved at the AGM shows management knows the scale it will require.

**The main-board migration is the hinge.** It brings full LODR governance (independent-director and committee rules, RPT approvals), wider investor access — and makes a larger follow-on raise easier. **The tension in one sentence: a genuinely qualified naval supplier with excellent margins, whose cash cycle means the shareholder, not the customer, finances the growth.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**1. 🔴 The cash never arrives.** Four consecutive years of negative CFO totalling −₹65cr on ₹90cr of PAT; working-capital days 140 → 319. **If the model cannot fund itself at ₹200cr of revenue, it will not at ₹400cr** — and the gap will be filled by dilution.

**2. 🔴 Dilution is the funding model.** ₹173.6cr raised in two years. The AGM has approved up to ₹1,000cr of non-fund-based limits; main-board status makes a larger QIP straightforward. **Base and bull cases assume no further dilution; history says otherwise.**

**3. 🔴 Undisclosed supplier advances.** ₹44.0cr — 21% of revenue, +76% in a year — with no counterparty named. **Almost certainly foreign OEM prepayments; but "almost certainly" is my inference, and it is the balance-sheet line with the least disclosure.**

**4. 🔴 A same-business sister company.** Flash Forge Pvt Ltd has the same objects, no non-compete (per CFF's own prospectus), sold CFF its factory land, lent it ₹21.5cr pre-IPO, and receives ₹2.6-4.9cr a year of unexplained "reimbursements". **Its financial statements are posted on CFF's website; they were not reviewed for this profile.**

**5. 🟡 One customer.** The Indian Navy, through DPSU shipyards and Navy procurement. Programme timing is set by Delhi, not by CFF.

**CFF builds the valves, fluid systems and — now — the sonar inside the Indian Navy's Scorpene submarines and anti-submarine craft**, qualified by Naval Group and Atlas Elektronik, growing 43% a year at a 28% margin with ₹551cr of orders and almost no debt, at 58x — the cheapest multiple and best ROCE in the Indian defence small-cap set. Main-board migration on 16-Sep-2026 brings full governance rules — and an easier path to the next raise.

**The cricketer is Washington Sundar.** Genuinely high-quality, selected for the hardest conditions, delivers when picked — but the returns come in bursts on specific surfaces, the selectors (the Navy) decide when he plays, and he has never been a banker you can bank on game after game. **CFF's talent is not in doubt; whether the team can afford to keep carrying him until the big series (P75I) arrives is.**

**Thesis in a sentence:** *A qualified Indian submarine and sonar supplier with superb margins and the cheapest multiple in its peer set — whose working-capital cycle has so far been funded by shareholders rather than customers, and which must prove on the main board that growth can come with cash.*
