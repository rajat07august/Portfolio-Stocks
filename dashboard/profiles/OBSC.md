### 2. Diluted EPS exceeds basic EPS — arithmetically impossible

From the 12-Aug-2026 filing's own P&L:

| | Q1FY27 | Q4FY26 | Q1FY26 | **FY26** | **FY25** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basic EPS (₹) | 3.67 | 3.35 | 2.06 | **10.45** | **6.85** |
| Diluted EPS (₹) | 3.67 | **4.05** | 2.06 | **12.62** | **8.12** |

**Diluted EPS can never exceed basic EPS** under AS 20 / Ind AS 33 — dilution can only increase the share count. The quarterly columns are internally consistent; **every annual and Q4 column is not.** Basic reconciles cleanly (FY26 PAT ₹2,701.46 lakh ÷ 258.456 lakh shares = ₹10.45 ✓); the "diluted" figure implies **fewer** shares than basic, which no dilution calculation produces. Most likely a weighted-average-versus-closing share count mislabelled as basic/diluted. **Not a valuation issue — TTM EPS of ₹12.46 on Screener appears to inherit the erroneous figure, so the true trailing P/E on basic EPS is higher than the 64.9x screened.** *(On FY26 basic EPS of ₹10.45, CMP ₹790 is **75.6x**.)* **It is a reporting-quality issue, and it is the third such item.**
