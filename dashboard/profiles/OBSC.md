## 1. Business in Plain English

Where Happy Forgings and Sansera do precision forging and machining, **OBSC Perfection is a multi-process precision manufacturer** — CNC machining, turning, grinding, specialty coatings, heat treatment and assembly — for automotive and non-automotive customers. **Younger and much smaller than the big players, growing far faster, and deliberately shifting from single-process (CNC only) to multi-process capability.**

**The snapshot:**
- **HQ Delhi/NCR (Jasola). Founded ~2017 by the Leekha family.** **NSE-SME listed, 2,770 shareholders.**
- **FY26 revenue ₹220cr (+53.9%), operating profit ₹40cr (18%), PAT ₹27cr.**
- **Q1FY27: revenue ₹77.31cr (+84.7%), PAT ₹9.47cr (+88.3%)** — **highest-ever income, EBITDA and PAT.**
- **Exports a record 25.6% of revenue; non-auto a record 20.5%; marine tripled to 14.0%.**
- **Registered with 3 of India's 7 DPSUs**, with first defence prototype orders named.
- **Order book ₹1,200cr+**, running 6-7 years.
- **🔴 FY26 operating cash flow −₹2cr against ₹40cr of operating profit — CFO/OP of 6%. Free cash flow −₹79cr.**
- **🔴 A second preferential issue: ₹86.5cr at ₹601 against a ₹790 market price**, taking promoter holding 73.50% → **~66%** in about eighteen months.

**Big picture in one sentence:** a genuinely fast-compounding precision manufacturer — **seven straight quarters of sequential growth, exports and non-auto at records, Tenneco and DPSU credentials** — trading at **64.9x after a +157% year**, where **the business is real and the cash, dilution and disclosure picture is materially worse than it first appeared.**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — The domestic auto capex cycle**, and the content growth that comes with it.

**Force 2 — Non-auto diversification.** Defence, aerospace, medical and industrial applications carry better margins and longer product cycles than automotive, and **OBSC's non-auto share went 8.9% → 14.4% → 20.5% across FY25, FY26 and Q1FY27.**

**Force 3 — India as a China+1 precision manufacturing hub.** **Exports reached a record 25.6% of revenue in Q1FY27, across 17 countries**, and the Tenneco relationship explicitly rides this: management notes Tenneco *"intends to export a lot of their applications from India… A lot of the plants in the U.S. are being brought to India."*

**Force 4 — The move from single-process to multi-process.** A machining job-shop competes on price; a supplier that casts, forges, machines, coats, heat-treats and assembles sells a finished component at a structurally better margin and is far harder to displace. **This is the whole strategic direction of the company.**

**Where OBSC sits.** Small and fast-growing against **Sansera and Happy Forgings** (both mainboard and an order of magnitude larger) and **Uniparts.** *The peer comparison in §6 is uncomfortable and is stated there in full.*

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Growth that is genuinely exceptional.** **FY26 +53.9%; Q1FY27 +84.7%; seven consecutive quarters of sequential revenue growth.** **3-year sales CAGR 32%, profit CAGR 81%.**

**Moat 2 — The multi-process capability build is real, not a slide.** From CNC-only toward **casting, forging, stamping and assembly under one roof** — with the **Faridabad acquisition lifting forging capacity 5-6x** and stamping revenue beginning in Q1FY27.

**Moat 3 — 🟢 The mix shift is the best operating news in this file.** **Marine went 4.2% → 14.0% of revenue in five quarters** — ₹6.0cr (FY25) → ₹13.8cr (FY26) → **₹10.8cr in Q1FY27 alone.** Exports **25.6%** and non-auto **20.5%**, both records.

**Moat 4 — Order book of ₹1,200cr+ on a 6-7 year execution horizon.** *Visibility rather than backlog in the EPC sense — management's own conversion guide is **"₹100 crores to ₹200 crores per year."***

**Moat 5 — 🟢 Tenneco, and specifically the DaVinci DCx nomination.** A **dedicated Sanand plant** for shock-absorber rods — currently *"around 8%, 9% of our total revenue"* — which management expects the new line to *"almost double"*, at *"₹40 crores sort of revenue."* **Being written into a global tier-1's next-generation suspension platform is a different order of relationship from being a job-shop vendor.**

**Moat 6 — Defence and aerospace credentials, small but real.** **Registered with 3 of India's 7 DPSUs** (two added in Q1FY27); first prototype orders for **MK-84 bomb fins**, **case heads for 0.5 million 1.9 bullets**, and **lifting plugs for 155mm shells**; **AS 9100 audit completed by TÜV Nord**, certification awaited — the gate to aerospace work. *Scale check: the defence order book was **₹130cr over 10 years ≈ ₹13cr a year** at the Q4FY25 disclosure — **visibility, not scale.***

**Moat 7 — Founder-family focus.** The Leekha family runs **no other listed entity.** *Saksham Leekha answers directly, declines to guide when he cannot, and does not oversell — his comments on Supa's phasing and on stamping being a low-margin "means to an end" are notably candid.*

**Where the moat is weaker — and this half is the reason for the rating:**
- **🔴 Cash conversion has deteriorated for four straight years to 6%.** FCF **−₹79cr in FY26**, and **−₹119cr cumulative over five years.**
- **🔴 Working capital days have gone 5 → 110 in three years**, with **debtor days 69 → 110.**
- **🔴 Equity is being sold at a discount roughly annually**, with **promoters not participating.**
- **🔴 Margin is not moving.** OPM has oscillated **15.8%–20.1% for two years with no trend.** **This is a company adding scale, not margin.**
- **🔴 Three separate reporting-quality items** (§4) — NSE format queries in consecutive years, an arithmetically impossible EPS table, and an unanswered public question on cost structure.
- **BBB+ is the first rating it has ever carried**, two notches above speculative grade.
- **An extremely narrow register** — 2,770 shareholders, NSE-SME, **and FII holding has fallen 9.46% → 0.64%.**

**2. Diluted EPS exceeds basic EPS — arithmetically impossible.**

| | Q1FY27 | Q4FY26 | Q1FY26 | **FY26** | **FY25** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basic EPS (₹) | 3.67 | 3.35 | 2.06 | **10.45** | **6.85** |
| Diluted EPS (₹) | 3.67 | **4.05** | 2.06 | **12.62** | **8.12** |

**Diluted EPS can never exceed basic EPS** under AS 20 / Ind AS 33 — dilution can only increase the share count. **The quarterly columns are internally consistent; every annual and Q4 column is not.** Basic reconciles cleanly (FY26 PAT ₹2,701.46 lakh ÷ 258.456 lakh shares = ₹10.45 ✓); **the "diluted" figure implies fewer shares than basic, which no dilution calculation produces.** Most likely a weighted-average-versus-closing share count mislabelled.
> **⚠️ This one has a valuation consequence.** **Screener's TTM EPS of ₹12.46 appears to inherit the erroneous figure, so the true trailing P/E is higher than the 64.9x screened. On FY26 basic EPS of ₹10.45, CMP ₹790 is 75.6x.**

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**The strategic logic is coherent and it is working on the top line.** Single-process CNC → **multi-process (casting, forging, stamping, assembly)** → higher value per part → **access to customers who buy finished components rather than machining hours** → Tenneco's DaVinci DCx nomination, DPSU registrations, marine at 14% of revenue, exports at 25.6%. **Every link in that chain has evidence behind it, and the mix shift is the proof.**

**Capability build requires plants, and plants require capital.** Faridabad lifted forging capacity 5-6x; **Sanand** is a dedicated Tenneco line completing in ~6 months; **Supa** is 10.8 acres of integrated casting, forging and stamping that management believes could eventually do **₹700-800cr on its own** — against a company doing ₹220cr today.

**And here the chain breaks, precisely.** **The capability build is being funded by working capital expansion and equity dilution, not by operations.** FY26: **₹40cr of operating profit, −₹2cr of operating cash, −₹79cr of free cash flow, ₹78cr of financing inflow.** Q1FY27 repeats it in miniature. **Working capital days went 5 → 110 in three years, and receivables plus inventories plus short-term advances now equal the entire net worth.**

**So the honest construction is:** *a genuinely good manufacturing business, executing a sensible strategy, growing 85%, whose growth model requires an external capital injection roughly every twelve months — at a price the company cannot set — and whose management has said on the record it cannot forecast when that stops.*

**The margin question is the one that would resolve it.** **If multi-process capability is real, margin should rise as mix shifts.** **Exports hit a record, non-auto hit a record, marine tripled — and operating margin moved 5 basis points.** *One quarter proves nothing. But the mechanism management named as the margin driver fired, and nothing happened. That is the number that decides whether this is a compounding manufacturer or a fast-growing job shop.*
