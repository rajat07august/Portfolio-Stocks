## 1. Business in Plain English

Universal Cables makes the **insulated copper and aluminium cables that carry electricity underground** — from the low-voltage cable that feeds a building, through the 11–33 kV cables a distribution utility buries along a road, up to **220 kV and 400 kV "extra-high-voltage" (EHV) cables** that bring a transmission line into a city substation where an overhead tower cannot go. It also makes **power capacitors and capacitor banks** (devices that correct power factor and stabilise voltage on the grid), and it will **lay, joint and commission** an EHV cable link end-to-end as a turnkey EPC contractor.

Two plants: **Satna, Madhya Pradesh** (power cables of every voltage, PVC compounds, capacitors; the EHV line is built around two **vertical continuous vulcanisation (VCV) towers**, installed under a technical collaboration with **Furukawa Electric, Japan** (CARE) and upgraded in FY24, with **400 kV know-how licensed from NKT GmbH & Co. KG, Germany** (AR)), and **Verna, Goa** (winding wires, building wires and flexible cables). Brand: **UNISTAR**. Founded 1962 under the late M.P. Birla; part of the **MP Birla Group** alongside Birla Corporation (cement), Vindhya Telelinks (telecom cables, EPC) and Birla Cable.

**The analogy: a bespoke tailor who also owns shares in the mill across the road.** The tailoring business — high-voltage cable made to a utility's specification, sometimes delivered and stitched into place — is real, growing 25%+ a year and earning a 10% margin. But about 40% of the profit you see in the headline comes from the stake in the mill (Vindhya Telelinks) and the fibre JV, and the mill owns a quarter of the tailor.

**How the money moves** (FY26, standalone, AR note 42):
- **Products — ₹2,286cr (76%)**: cables and capacitors sold at a point in time, credit terms "upto 90 days".
- **EPC / works contracts — ₹710cr (24%, +40% YoY)**: turnkey EHV cable projects, revenue recognised over time against milestones; CARE notes EPC execution takes **18–24 months** and is funded with **bank borrowings rather than customer advances**.
- **Exports — ₹169cr (5.6%)** in FY26, **₹120cr (12.7%) in Q1FY27 alone**.
- Rough product split (FY26 growth from the Directors' Report): **EHV +~33%**, HV/MV +~9%, LV & conductors +~16%, Goa +23%, **capacitors +~78%**. *The rupee split between product lines is not disclosed — the company reports a single segment.*

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

1. **Cities cannot take more overhead lines.** As load grows inside dense urban areas and new capitals (the profile's one named domestic EHV project is **Amravati, Andhra Pradesh**, per the Nov-2025 press release), transmission has to go underground at 220–400 kV. Only a handful of Indian plants can make EHV cable at all (my inference from the qualification barrier, not a disclosed market share).
2. **The distribution grid is being rebuilt.** Reducing AT&C losses, RDSS-style schemes and metro/railway electrification all consume MV and LV cable (AR FY26 MD&A).
3. **New loads.** Data centres, EV charging, renewables evacuation and C&I capex (AR FY26 MD&A; the Aug-2026 press release leans hard on AI/data-centre power demand).
4. **Global shortage of EHV cable capacity.** European and Middle-East utilities are buying from India: Universal's **US$38m 400 kV order from the Middle East** (Nov-2025) was described as *"the first by any Indian cable company"* (press release, 12-Nov-2025 — company claim, not independently verified).
5. **Capacitors ride grid stability.** Renewables and non-linear loads raise demand for reactive-power compensation and harmonic filters (AR FY26 MD&A projects capacitor banks at ~28.7% of the power-quality market by 2028 — an industry estimate quoted by the company).

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

1. **EHV credentials, which take years to earn.** VCV towers (described by the company as *"India's first of its kind"*), a 400 kV underground HVAC project commissioned in Tamil Nadu (11.34 route-km, FY25), 400 kV supplies to Europe, the Middle-East 400 kV order, and NABL-accredited EHV test labs at Satna and Goa (valid to Nov-2029 / Aug-2029). The Directors' Report claims *"an industry leading market share in the Extra High Voltage (EHV) Power Cable business in India"* — **a claim, unquantified.**
2. **Licensed technology from the global majors.** **NKT GmbH & Co. KG (Germany)** for 400 kV XLPE underground HVAC cable and system design, under a Manufacturing Technical Collaboration Agreement signed **9-Aug-2018 and extended to 8-Aug-2028** (AR FY26); Furukawa (the VCV lines, per CARE, and the fibre JV); Viscas (Furukawa–Fujikura JV) for 220 kV+ jointing accessories (CARE, Feb-2026); and a 2026 manufacturing agreement with **TS Conductor Corp, USA** for HTLS conductors (Feb-2026 press release). A **220 kV, 2,500 sq mm cable system passed type and prequalification (PQ) tests to IEC 62067-2022 at CPRI** (AR FY24–FY26).
3. **Turnkey capability.** Being able to supply *and* lay/joint/commission an EHV link is a differentiator against pure manufacturers — and also the source of the working-capital problem (§4).
4. **A capacitor business growing faster than cables** (+78% FY26, +63% Q1FY27) with what the company calls "stable margins".
5. **Group support that a standalone mid-cap would not get.** CARE's rating rests on it: Birla Corporation injected **₹100cr in FY25 and ₹25cr in 9MFY26** for capex; group subordinated loans and ICDs were **₹273cr at Dec-2025**; Hindustan Gum (~₹2,000cr of liquidity, per CARE) has lent before.
6. **An optical-fibre option.** The 26.14% JV with Furukawa (BFFO) swung from a **₹37.5cr loss in FY25 to ₹76.3cr profit in FY26** on revenue **₹160cr → ₹491cr** (AR note 52), and posted its highest-ever profit in Q1FY27.

### Where the moat is weaker, honestly
- **Returns are mediocre for a cable company.** Standalone ROCE has been **9–12% for six years**; KEI earns 20%, Polycab 33%, RR Kabel 28%, Dynamic Cables 26% (Screener, 13-Oct-2026). Interest consumes **~40% of EBITDA** (FY26 ₹115cr of ₹289cr; Q1FY27 ₹36.5cr of ₹94.6cr).
- **Most of the volume is not EHV.** HV/MV grew only ~9% in FY26; the LV business is the most competitive segment in Indian cables.
- **Execution leans on EPC, and EPC leans on the balance sheet.** Receivables were **₹1,220cr at Mar-2026 (~147 days of revenue)** plus ₹95cr of contract assets.
- **No disclosure culture.** No calls, one segment, no product-wise revenue, no EHV share of sales — the market cannot check the claims the press releases make.

### Promoter group and the estate dispute

Universal is one of the **MP Birla Group** companies. The promoter group holds **61.89%** through **16 bodies corporate and no individuals** (NSE shareholding XBRL, Jun-2026): Vindhya Telelinks 23.85%, The Punjab Produce & Trading Co 14.77%, Gwalior Webbing 8.32%, Birla Corporation 2.31%, August Agents 2.31%, Birla Cable 2.27%, Insilco Agents 2.24%, Laneseda Agents 1.95%, and eight smaller holders. A further 6.8% sits with "persons acting in concert" classified as public — chiefly Eastern India Educational Institution (3.18%) and Hindustan Medical Institution (3.14%), and **63,004 shares held by the Administrators Pendente Lite of the estate of the late Priyamvada Devi Birla**.

**What the filings say about the dispute** (AR FY26, Corporate Governance Report, Harsh Lodha's own footnote): two of the court-appointed Administrators Pendente Lite (APLs) of the Priyamvada Devi Birla estate caused group companies to file Form DIR-12 recording that he had *"ceased"* to be a director, relying on a 18-Sep-2020 single-judge order of the Calcutta High Court. A Division Bench **modified that order on 14-Dec-2023** and, in his account, nullified the APLs' actions. **The defendants in the probate suit (T.S. No. 6 of 2004) have filed Special Leave Petitions; the Supreme Court declined interim relief on 22-Mar-2024. The matter remains open.**

**Why this reaches Universal's own accounts:** Vindhya Telelinks' three wholly owned NBFC subsidiaries — **August Agents, Insilco Agents and Laneseda Agents**, each also a direct Universal promoter — **could not be consolidated by Vindhya for FY22–FY26**, because *"certain delinquent and ex-directors of the Subsidiaries… are having unauthorized and illegal possession of the books of account"* (AR FY26, note 54; auditor's Emphasis of Matter). **By 7-Aug-2026 Vindhya had received approved and audited accounts for all five years and restated retrospectively**; Universal restated with it (+₹7.5cr to opening equity; FY26 consolidated PAT ₹163.1cr → ₹164.7cr). **How control of the books was regained is not stated in anything I read.** Universal itself owes **₹103cr of unsecured loans** to those same three companies (₹33.75cr + ₹34.00cr + ₹35.25cr, ~₹9cr a year of interest; AR FY26 RPT note).

## 5. Connecting the Dots

The cable story and the holding-company story pull in different directions.

**The cable business is in the best phase of its recent history** — a 25%+ growth year delivered, a 57% quarter, EHV and exports finally scaling, capacitors compounding, and a capacity build that roughly doubles physical output by mid-2027. If EHV and exports keep rising as a share of sales, the 10% margin can drift up, and as capex stops the interest line should flatten.

**But every rupee of that growth is currently borrowed.** EPC receivables, a capex budget that has more than doubled and a falling CFO mean debt rose ₹328cr in a year; interest is ~40% of EBITDA. **The thesis needs FY27–FY28 to be the years when the plant is finished, the receivables cycle turns, and operating cash finally covers both** — the same "show me the cash" test as RAJESH and ORIANA on this watchlist.

**On top sits a second, independent bet: optical fibre.** BFFO's turnaround (₹491cr revenue, ₹76cr profit) is the sharpest earnings change in the group, and the AI/hyperscale fibre super-cycle the AR describes is real enough that the JV wants ₹4,800cr of capacity. It may be the most valuable thing Universal owns — or the thing that consumes its balance sheet. **Today nobody outside the company knows how it will be funded.**

**And around both sits a contested promoter group** whose 27% bloc votes against the board, inside an estate dispute now 22 years old, with circular cross-holdings that make the consolidated book partly self-referential. **None of it has harmed the operating business so far** — but it caps the multiple, it explains the absence of calls better than any other reason I can find (*my inference*), and it means a special resolution — needed for a QIP or a large issue to fund growth — may be unpassable.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

1. **🔴 Cash never catches up with growth.** CFO was 36% of operating profit in FY26 and free cash flow −₹206cr. EPC is funded with bank debt by design. If receivables keep pace with revenue, every year of 25% growth adds ~₹150–200cr of borrowings.
2. **🔴 Interest eats the operating leverage.** Finance cost +51% YoY in Q1FY27. Debt/EBITDA ~4.1x on simple arithmetic — at CARE's negative-sensitivity level, though not on CARE's definition.
3. **🔴 The BFFO bill arrives.** ~₹4,800cr of JV capex on a ₹237cr net-worth JV, funding split undisclosed. A pro-rata equity call or further guarantees could exceed Universal's annual EBITDA.
4. **🔴 Capex keeps slipping and growing.** Four revisions in 27 months (₹277cr → ₹617cr + ₹73cr); two timetable slips. A fifth would make it a pattern, not a scope change.
5. **🔴 The promoter dispute escalates.** A standing 27% bloc against the board; the estate probate unresolved after 22 years; SLPs in the Supreme Court. A court outcome could change who controls ~a quarter of the votes, or the board.
6. **🔴 Finance-function churn.** Three CFOs in thirteen months, a CS gap, and the Q2FY27 results signed by a CFO who joins on 21-Oct. Errors and restatements become more likely; the Vindhya restatement has already happened once.
7. **🟡 Circular holdings flatter the book.** Vindhya ↔ Universal cross-holding and the ₹249cr Baroda Agents line mean consolidated net worth and ROE are partly self-referential.
8. **🟡 Associate quality.** Vindhya: revenue −11% in FY26, CARE A+ **Negative**, JJM receivable delays. Its profit is ~28% of Universal's consolidated PAT.
9. **🟡 Commodity and trade exposure.** Copper, aluminium and polyethylene dominate input cost (CARE); mitigated by escalation clauses on EPC and a hedging desk since Jan-2022 (*the cost-pass-through share is not disclosed*). Exports face trade measures (EU anti-dumping on optical-fibre cable, Jun-2024).
10. **🟡 Order-book coverage is thinning.** ₹3,025cr (Mar-26) → ₹2,860cr (Jul-26) against a revenue run-rate approaching ₹3,800cr.
11. **🟡 The 400 kV licence has an end date.** The NKT technical-collaboration agreement runs to **8-Aug-2028** (extended once, May-2023). Whether it is renewed, or whether the company can stand alone at 400 kV, is not addressed in any filing.
