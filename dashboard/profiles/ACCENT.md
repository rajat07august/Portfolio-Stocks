## 1. Business in Plain English

Every tablet you swallow is mostly not drug. A paracetamol or blood-pressure pill is typically 60–80% **excipients**: inert ingredients that bind the powder, help it compress into a tablet and make it break apart in the stomach. The active ingredient is often only 5–20%. The most widely used excipient is **microcrystalline cellulose (MCC)**, purified wood-pulp cellulose that acts as binder, filler and disintegrant at once. Accent Microcell makes MCC and the higher-value products built on it. It sells them to pharma, nutraceutical, food, cosmetics and industrial buyers (welding electrodes, for one) in **75+ countries, 200+ customers, mostly through distributors** (85% distributor / 15% direct, May-2026 call).

**A simple way to picture it:** Accent is a **flour mill for pill-makers**. Wood pulp goes in, gets broken down with acid, washed, dried and milled into a fine white powder graded to pharmacopoeia standards. The plain powder (MCC) is the bread-and-butter product, sold in 15+ grades. Some of it is then re-processed into premium forms that sell for two to three times as much: spheres (MCC Spheres), silicified MCC (SMCC), and in the coming plant croscarmellose sodium (CCS), carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) and sodium starch glycolate (SSG). It also sells magnesium stearate and powdered cellulose.

**The plants (AR FY26):**
| Unit | Where | Capacity | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unit I** | Pirana, Ahmedabad | 2,000 MTPA, "100%" utilised | Domestic market; non-spray-dried MCC; **almost all trading is booked here** |
| **Unit II** | Dahej SEZ, Bharuch | 7,200 MTPA, "95%" utilised | Export; spray-dried MCC, SMCC, Spheres; US-DMF; **no trading allowed in the SEZ** |
| **Unit III** | Nayka (Navagam), Kheda | 2,800 MTPA Phase I planned (CCS/CMC/SSG; management also cites 2,400 MTPA fungible across the three) + 12,000 MTPA MCC Phase II | **Not producing at 30-Sep-2026.** Land bought for six phases |

**FY26: revenue ₹349.0cr, PAT ₹43.9cr, 15,000 MT sold against ~9,200 MTPA of installed capacity.** The gap is made up of captive conversion into premium grades and of **trading**: buying semi-processed or finished MCC and selling it on (70% manufactured / 25–30% traded by volume, May-2026 call). **Exports were 63.28% of FY26 revenue (53.49% in FY25). Premium products were 13.12% of revenue (3.74% in FY25)** (FY26 investor deck). Domestic realisation is ₹170–190/kg; the blended average is ~₹260/kg.

**History:** a partnership (Accent Microcell Industries) from **2002**, a private company from April 2012, public limited from December 2022, and **listed on NSE Emerge (SME) on 15-Dec-2023**, raising ₹78.40cr at ₹140 a share. A **₹39.77cr rights issue followed in June 2025** at about ₹135 a share. First-generation promoters: **Vasant, Ghanshyam, Nitin and Vinodbhai Patel**, each with ~20 years in cellulose powders (CARE).

**Big picture in one sentence:** a well-run small MCC maker whose two existing plants are full and earning very well, whose growth plan rests on a third plant that is **a year late and counting**, and whose governance signals have weakened while the operating numbers improved.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Every solid dose needs a carrier.** Tablet and capsule volumes grow with generic drug consumption. Management cites MCC demand growth of **6.9% in pharma/nutra, 5.1% in food and beverages, 5.6% in cosmetics, 4.6% in pet and 5.1% in industrial** (May-2026 call). These are the company's market figures, not audited data. The deck puts the market CAGR at ~6–8%.

**Force 2 — Premiumisation inside the excipient bill.** Direct-compression and modified-release formulations need functional grades: spheres for pellet coating, SMCC for flow, CCS and SSG as super-disintegrants. These sell for **₹600–700/kg, against ₹170–190/kg domestic and ~₹260/kg blended MCC** (calls, May-2025 and May-2026). Pulp-based CMC: *"almost Rs 400/Kg"* domestically and *"Rs 800 to 900/Kg"* for export (Nitin Patel, May-2026).

**Force 3 — China+1 and regulated-market sourcing.** Buyers in Europe, the US, Australia, Japan and Korea are qualifying second sources. Qualification is slow (stability data, audits), which makes an approved supplier sticky once it is in.

**Force 4 — Raw material is global and dollar-priced.** Wood pulp is imported (SAPPI, AR FY26). Exports are about **3x imports**, so a weaker rupee helps margins. CARE notes there is **no active hedging policy**: the hedge is natural, not contractual.

**Where Accent sits.** One of the two listed Indian MCC makers. **Sigachi Industries** is the larger by revenue (₹471cr TTM) but has been badly hit: an FY26 loss of ₹83cr, operating margin down from 20% to 11%, and **a market cap now below Accent's** (₹1,067cr vs ₹1,649cr, Screener 15-Oct-2026). Sigachi has also announced an 18,000 MT Dahej expansion. The global leaders are private or inside conglomerates (JRS Pharma, IFF/DuPont/FMC, Asahi Kasei, Mingtai).

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Two full plants earning ~24% ROCE on old assets.** Net fixed assets were only ₹56cr at Sep-2026, against ~₹350cr of revenue. The existing business is capital-light and highly cash-generative when working capital is controlled. Management's FY26 gross margin figure is **~38%**; the H1FY27 figure computed from the P&L is **48%** (§4).

**Moat 2 — Premium grades that use MCC as their raw material.** Spheres and SMCC are made from Accent's own MCC, so a shift to premium grades needs no new feedstock. Premium share rose **3.74% → 13.12% of revenue in one year**; management guides **16–17% for FY27** (May-2026 call). MCC Spheres capacity is **~100 MT a month**, and **90% of H2FY26 Spheres were exported**. On competition, management said: *"we don't think so that, MCC Spheres capacity and the technology what we have is there with most of the competitors"*. It does *not* claim to be the only Indian maker: *"For Spheres, we are not claiming that we are"* (May-2026).

**Moat 3 — Export-led, regulated-market customer base.** US-DMF on the Dahej unit, EXCiPACT, GMP, FSSC 22000, HACCP, ISO, Kosher, Halal. **Exports 63.28% of FY26 revenue.** *(CARE puts FY25 exports at ~51% of total operating income against the company's 53.49% of revenue. The bases differ.)*

**Moat 4 — Natural FX hedge.** Exports ~3x imports. A weaker rupee is a tailwind; a stronger one is not hedged.

**Moat 5 — Moderate customer concentration.** Top 10 customers were **44.1% of FY25 operating income** (CARE). Customers are approved and retained. Management repeatedly describes trading as a way *"to ensure that the customer remains with us"* while capacity is short.

**Moat 6 — A long runway on owned land.** Land for six phases at Unit III. Phases 1 and 2 would take total capacity from **9,200 to ~24,000 MTPA** (Aug-2025 meet). Phase 3 is *"tentative… 2028"* (May-2026).

**Moat 7 — Pulp-based CMC → CCS, if it works.** Management says it spent **~8 years on R&D** on a route from wood pulp to CMC to CCS, and that it would be *"India's first plant that will make CMC from pulp"* (Nitin Patel, May-2026). 40–50 trial samples were made at Dahej. **This is a claim to be proven at commercial scale, not yet a moat.**

### Where the moat is weaker, honestly
- **The growth project does not exist yet in the P&L.** Unit III had absorbed **₹134.7cr of CWIP** at Sep-2026 and earns nothing. **₹77.8cr of it is classed as overdue in the company's own CWIP note** (AR FY26, note 10.2).
- **Trading has been a large and low-margin part of revenue.** FY26 stock-in-trade purchases were **₹95.6cr**, ~90% of it MCC. Management puts trading margins at *"4–5%"*, later *"2 to 3 percentage"* for some customers (May-2026).
- **Related-party flows are material** (§3.5).
- **Disclosure is thin and late:** half-yearly results, no H1 calls, CRISIL non-cooperation. Investors learned of Unit III delays from rating rationales and plant visits rather than exchange filings (an investor said so on the May-2026 call).
- **Extreme illiquidity:** 3,371 shareholders (Sep-2026), and normal daily volume of 25,000–60,000 shares.
- **SME regulation:** Regulations 17–27 of LODR (board composition, committees, related-party approvals) **do not apply** to SME-listed companies. The company says so in its own filings.
- **Chinese MCC pricing pressure** on the commodity grades, and the Sigachi Dahej expansion.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — The existing plants are a better business than they looked.** H1FY27 shows what Units I and II earn without heavy trading: ~48% gross margin, ~24% EBITDA, receivables back to ~47 days annualised. **If that holds in H2, the base business alone justifies most of the current price** (§6).

**Thread B — Unit III is the whole growth case, and it is stuck at the approval stage.** Consent to establish was in place; consent to operate, environment clearance and a power-line approval were not (13-May-2026 filing). Management blames two abnormal monsoons and an election-time pause in officials' work. **Those are plausible. But four dates have now passed, the last one silently.**

**Thread C — Trading was the bridge, and it has just been removed.** Management's own logic was to keep customers with traded volume until Phase 2 MCC arrived. Traded purchases have collapsed with no Phase 2. Either demand was met from premium-grade conversion and customers accepted less volume, or the accounting changed. **This is the question to put first if a call is held.**

**Thread D — The family's businesses sit on both sides of the trade.** Related-party purchases tripled to ₹46cr in the year trading peaked. The trading collapse in H1FY27 may coincide with those flows shrinking. That would be good for cleanliness, but it would also mean part of the FY26 revenue base was arranged volume.

**Thread E — Governance has moved the wrong way while the numbers improved.** CRISIL non-cooperation (Feb), pay ceilings +67% (Jul), CFO sales (Jun–Jul), an ICD to an undisclosed party (H1), no call (Oct). **Each item has an innocent explanation; together they are a pattern worth pricing.**

**Thread F — Diversification and certifications are intact.** Pharma, nutraceuticals, food, cosmetics and industrial demand, with US-DMF and EXCiPACT, still give the base business its resilience.

**How the threads interact:** A pays for the stock today. B is the upside. C and D decide whether A is as clean as it looks. E decides what multiple a careful buyer should pay for A + B.
