## 1. Business in Plain English

**EIL designs oil refineries.**

When Indian Oil decides to build a refinery expansion at Paradip, someone has to do the process design, the detailed engineering, the equipment specification, the tendering, and then supervise construction for four years. **That is EIL's core business, and it is sold as consultancy — it consumes almost no capital and earns 24–25% segment margins.**

It also takes **turnkey (LSTK) work**, where it takes responsibility for procurement and construction as well. **That business is four times larger in ticket size and earns about 7.5%.** The mix between the two is the single most important variable in EIL's P&L, and management is explicit about which way it wants it to go:

> *"Definitely, it is the strategy of the company to keep consultancy business on higher side since we are having strong margin in the consultancy segment. So management efforts are always there to grab the consultancy business first."* — **Sanjay Jindal, Director (Finance), Q1FY27 call**

**Who owns it:** the **President of India, through the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas, holds 51.32%** — unmoved for a decade. **Board appointments, including the Chairman and functional directors, are made by the ministry, not by the board.** *That is a governance fact with practical consequences, and §3.5 shows one live right now.*

**Who it sells to:** the Indian public-sector hydrocarbon complex — **IOCL, BPCL, HPCL, ONGC, GAIL** — plus a Middle East export practice run out of Abu Dhabi, and one large African contract (**Dangote, Nigeria**).

**The 30-second snapshot:**
- **New Delhi** · **PSU, 51.32% Government of India** · listed 1997 · **56.3cr shares**
- **Q1FY27 standalone: turnover ₹801cr (−6.5%), PBT ₹145cr (+55%), PAT ₹109cr (+55%)**
- **Consolidated PAT ₹157.94cr vs ₹65.4cr — +141%**, of which **₹42.51cr is the RFCL joint venture** *(against a ₹7.37cr loss last year)*
- **🟢 Standalone operating margin 14% vs 7%; EBITDA margin 18.55% vs 11.72% — and management confirmed no write-backs**
- **🟢 Consultancy segment margin 17% → 24%**, revenue **+22%**, mix now **~60%** of turnover
- **🔴 Order book ₹14,424cr (30-Jun) — DOWN from ₹15,109cr.** Consultancy ₹10,498cr, turnkey ₹3,926cr
- **🔴 Q1FY27 order inflow ₹514cr against an ₹8,000cr full-year target**
- **18.8x reported earnings · ~23.6x normalised · 4.7x book · ROCE 30.4% · ROE 23.4% · dividend yield 1.88%**
- **52-week range ₹164–₹267. CMP is 1.9% below the high**

**Big picture in one sentence.** EIL is **a genuinely high-quality, net-cash, 30%-return government consultancy whose operating margins are improving exactly as management said they would** — and whose **order intake has effectively stopped**, at a price that has run 17.5% in five weeks to the top of its range.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — India keeps building refining and petrochemical capacity.** Every expansion needs process licensing, front-end design, detailed engineering and project management before a single rupee of steel is bought. **EIL has done this for the Indian public-sector oil complex for sixty years and is the default choice.**

**Force 1b — The sizing, carried forward from v1.** **India's refining capacity is planned to go from ~250 MMTPA (FY24) to 400+ MMTPA by 2030, and every refinery expansion is a ₹5,000–15,000cr consultancy-plus-EPC opportunity.** *That is the structural backdrop; the problem in this profile is timing, not the existence of the pipeline.*

**Force 2 — 🟢 Nuclear has genuinely opened up, and EIL is already inside it.** Vivek Midha, Q1FY27: *"this government has started pushing on the nuclear and a lot of impetus has come after this Hormuz crisis… we have seen a lot of inquiries coming from the nuclear side."* **Three environmental studies for a private investor, one for government, engagement with NPCIL on engineering consultancy, and SMR projects secured from NPCIL last year.**

**Force 3 — 🟢 Coal gasification is a new, policy-funded segment.** *"as soon as the government has revised this policy and they have said that they will be giving **gap funding of INR34,000 crores**, many inquiries are there."* **Individual projects are ₹10,000–30,000cr in size**; EIL is executing a coal-to-SNG project for NTPC and bidding several feasibility studies.

**Force 4 — Infrastructure adjacency, including data centres.** *"We have just got a major assignment in the data center from PowerTel."*

**Force 5 — 🔴 And the counter-force, which is currently winning.** **The Middle East, historically EIL's growth market, has stopped placing new work.** Asked directly whether clients had put projects on hold, the answer was: *"New mega projects are under hold. Yes."* **Existing projects continue; new tenders do not come.**

**Where EIL sits.** ₹14,717cr in **Sector 6 (Engineering / EPC)**. ***It is the only pure consultancy on the watchlist — a design house rather than a builder, which is why its return on capital is 30% on almost no fixed assets.***

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — 🟢 Sixty years of incumbency with the Indian oil PSUs.** Refinery design is a licensing-and-reference business: you win work because you have done the last one. **EIL is embedded in IOCL, BPCL, HPCL, ONGC and GAIL in a way a new entrant cannot replicate.**

**Moat 2 — 🟢 Consultancy economics, and they just improved sharply.** **Segment margin 17% (Q1FY26) → 24% (Q1FY27)**, with management guiding *"we are sure to keep 24%, 25% segment profit in the consultancy segment."* **On essentially no capital employed.**

**Moat 3 — 🟢 A balance sheet with no debt and a large cash pile.** Interest expense of **₹1cr a quarter**; other income of **₹81cr in Q1FY27** is what the cash earns. **ROCE 30.4%, ROE 23.4%.**

**Moat 4 — 🟢 A real dividend.** **1.88% yield on a 41% payout**, with the **record date 24-Sep-2026** and the AGM on 18-Sep. *One of the few names on this watchlist where the dividend is a material part of the return.*

**Moat 5 — 🟢 RFCL is now a recurring earnings stream, not an oddity.** The Ramagundam fertiliser JV contributed **₹42.51cr in Q1FY27 against a ₹7.37cr loss a year ago**, and management says *"RFCL project is running well. And now we are expecting this kind of profit on a regular basis. We are expecting some kind of dividend in the current financial year also."* **Running at 100% capacity, with the planned shutdown absorbed inside the normal 30–35 day window.**

**Moat 6 — Diversification that is starting to look real.** Nuclear, coal gasification, infrastructure and data centres are all now named pipelines rather than aspirations — **and each is a consultancy-first entry, which is the high-margin end.**

**Moat 7 — Institutions are buying.** **FII 6.75% → 9.17% and DII 10.76% → 14.22% over five quarters**, while the retail shareholder count fell from 4.85 lakh to 3.90 lakh. *The opposite of the pattern on most small caps in this book.*

### Where the moat is weaker

- **🔴 Order intake has stalled.** **₹514cr in Q1 against an ₹8,000cr target.** *That is 6.4% of a year's target in 25% of the year.*
- **🔴 The order book is shrinking.** **₹15,109cr → ₹14,424cr.** *Execution is outrunning replacement.*
- **🔴 Revenue is going backwards.** **Turnover ₹801cr vs ₹857cr, −6.5%**, because turnkey fell 33% on *"tapering of certain major projects."*
- **🔴 The Middle East, the designated growth market, is frozen at the mega-project level**, and Saudi/Aramco is *"still in the initial stages of discussion."*
- **⚠️ Growth is structurally slow.** **Ten-year sales CAGR 10%, five-year 5%, three-year 6%.** *This is not, and has never been, a compounder.*
- **⚠️ Other income is a third of pre-tax profit.** ₹81cr of ₹197cr in Q1FY27; ₹242cr of FY26.
- **⚠️ It is a PSU.** Board seats are filled by ministry order, capital allocation is constrained, and the customer base is the same government that owns it.

**🟢 And the write-back question, which is the reason this refresh exists, was settled on the call.** Asked twice by Deep Sanghavi of Dalal & Broacha — including specifically about the HPCL Barmer project commercialised in June — the Director (Finance) said: ***"In the current quarter, there is no exceptional change order, which is included in the term now. And we are not expecting any write-off as such"*** and ***"we are not expecting any write-back of provisions. So all the provisions kept for guarantee and warranty are write-off as per accounting standard and as completion of projects only."*** *He did note that reversals follow the defect liability period, so Barmer-type reversals remain a future possibility — but not this quarter and not guided.*

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — 🟢 The earnings-quality question from July has been answered, and answered well.** **No write-backs, no exceptional change orders, confirmed twice on the record — and the standalone operating margin doubled anyway, 7% → 14%.** ***That is the single most important thing in this refresh and it is a genuine positive.***

**Thread B — 🟢 The mix shift is doing the work, exactly as management said it would.** **Consultancy revenue +22% and now ~60% of turnover; segment margin 17% → 24%.** *Turnkey is a ₹302cr business earning 7.5%; consultancy is a ₹499cr business earning 24%. Every point of mix shift is worth roughly 17 points of margin on that revenue.*

**Thread C — 🔴 But total revenue went backwards, because the mix shift is happening by subtraction.** **Turnkey fell 33% on *"tapering of certain major projects"*, and consultancy's +22% was not enough to cover it.** ***A margin improvement achieved by the low-margin half shrinking is not the same as a margin improvement achieved by the high-margin half growing — and this quarter was mostly the former.***

**Thread D — 🔴 The order book is now shrinking, and the inflow is the reason.** **₹15,109cr → ₹14,424cr, on ₹514cr of Q1 intake against an ₹8,000cr target.** *Execution is running ahead of replacement for the first time in this cycle.*

**Thread E — 🔴 And the two engines that were supposed to fix that are both stalled.** **The Middle East at the mega-project level — *"New mega projects are under hold"* — and the three named domestic awards, all of which slipped on the call.**

**Thread F — 🟢 The replacement engines are credible but early.** **Nuclear (SMRs already won from NPCIL), coal gasification (₹34,000cr of gap funding), data centres.** *All are consultancy-first, which is the right end. None contributed to Q1.*

**Thread G — 🟡 A third of pre-tax profit is not operating profit.** **₹81cr of other income and ₹42.51cr of JV equity income against ₹197cr of consolidated PBT.** *Both are defensible — one is interest on a genuine cash pile, the other is a fertiliser plant running at capacity that management says is now recurring — but neither is refinery design.*

**Thread H — 🔴 And the price went the other way.** **₹223 → ₹262, +17.5%, to within 1.9% of the 52-week high**, while the order book shrank and revenue fell. ***The 28-Jul note said "upgrade back to High at ₹180–190." The market moved 40% away from that level, not toward it.***

**How they interact.** ***A high-quality consultancy has fixed its earnings-quality problem, improved its margins exactly as guided, and simultaneously stopped filling its order book — while its share price rose 17.5%.*** **The business is better than it was in July. The investment is worse.**

### What the multiple is being asked to carry

**🟢 The case for it.** **30.4% return on capital, 23.4% on equity, no debt, ₹252cr of free cash flow, a 41% payout and a 1.88% yield, sixty years of incumbency with the Indian oil PSUs, a consultancy margin that just went from 17% to 24%, and institutional ownership rising from 17.5% to 23.4%.** *For a PSU that requires almost no capital to grow, this is a genuinely good franchise.*

**🔴 The case against it, and it is arithmetic rather than opinion.**
1. **The reported 18.8x is on a TTM that still contains the December-2025 windfall.** **Normalised it is ~23.6x** — and that is the number to use until January 2027, when the quarter laps.
2. **FY27 reported profit is likely to be roughly flat on FY26** despite 10%+ revenue growth, because FY26 contained a one-off that FY27 will not. ***The optics of FY27 will look like a company that stopped growing, and the market may treat them that way.***
3. **The growth record does not support a growth multiple.** **Sales CAGR: 10-year 10%, 5-year 5%, 3-year 6%.**
4. **Order inflow is 6.4% of target at 25% of the year, and the order book is shrinking.**

### Weighted return framework (2-year, to FY28)

*Anchored on ₹14,717cr and 56.3cr shares. FY27 turnover guided ~₹4,200cr standalone.*

**Base case — 55%.** **Guidance is broadly met: FY27 turnover ~₹4,200cr, consultancy 55% of it at 24% segment margin, order inflow lands somewhere between ₹6,000cr and ₹8,000cr — short of target but not collapsing.** FY28 revenue **~₹4,700cr**, consolidated operating margin ~16%, other income ~₹300cr, RFCL contributing ~₹150cr a year → **PAT ~₹800cr.** **Multiple holds at 18x** — no re-rating, because there is no growth to re-rate → **₹14,400cr, −2%. Plus ~3.8% of dividends over two years ≈ +2%.**

**Bull case — 25%.** **The ₹8,000cr inflow target is actually met, the Middle East unfreezes, and one of nuclear or coal gasification converts into a large consultancy mandate.** FY28 revenue **~₹5,500cr** at a richer consultancy mix, **PAT ~₹950cr**, and the market pays **22x** for a re-accelerating franchise → **₹20,900cr, +42%, plus dividends ≈ +46%.**

**Bear case — 20%.** **The ₹514cr quarter is the signal.** Middle East stays frozen through FY28, Paradip Phase 2 and the ONGC petchem stay unawarded, the order book falls below ₹12,000cr, revenue stalls, and **the market re-rates a no-growth PSU consultancy toward the low end of its historical band.** FY28 revenue **~₹3,800cr**, **PAT ~₹550cr**, multiple **14x** → **₹7,700cr, −48%, plus dividends ≈ −44%.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — 🔴 Order inflow, which is now the whole question.** **₹514cr in Q1 against an ₹8,000cr full-year target — 6.4% of the target in 25% of the year.** *And the ₹8,000cr was already flat on FY26's ₹7,979cr, which was itself down 2.9% on FY25. **This is a third consecutive year of no growth in intake, and the first in which the order book has actually fallen.***
**⚠️ MATERIALISED.**

**Risk 2 — 🔴 The Middle East is frozen at exactly the level that matters.** ***"New mega projects are under hold. Yes."*** Existing PMC work under long-term agreements continues (~₹400–500cr this quarter), but **Saudi/Aramco is *"still in the initial stages of discussion"* and *"new inquiries are a little bit slow."*** *The Middle East was the designated growth market in every prior version of this thesis.*

**Risk 3 — 🔴 The three big domestic awards are all slipping.** **BPCL Andhra** execution tender at the end of FY27 *"otherwise, it's early next financial year"*; **IOCL Paradip Phase 2** stalled on *"certain land issues"* awaiting an IOCL management decision; **ONGC petrochemical** *"going to take time."* *Management's fallback is projects *"which cannot be disclosed at this point of time"* — which is not something a shareholder can underwrite.*

**Risk 4 — 🔴 The multiple, on the normalised number.** **~23.6x after adjusting the Dec-2025 windfall out of the TTM**, on a business with a **6% three-year sales CAGR**. *A de-rate to 16x on FY28E PAT of ₹800cr is ₹12,800cr — **−13% with nothing going wrong operationally.***

**Risk 5 — 🟡 FY27 will optically look like a profit decline.** **FY26's ₹692cr contained a one-off; FY27 on guidance lands near ₹700cr on a much cleaner base.** *A flat headline after a 47% profit year reads badly, and the market may not do the normalising.*

**Risk 6 — 🟡 A third of pre-tax profit is not operations.** **Other income ₹81cr and JV equity ₹42.51cr against ₹197cr of consolidated PBT.** *Both are defensible and RFCL is confirmed recurring — but if interest rates fall or RFCL has an outage, the reported number moves without the business changing.*

**Risk 7 — 🟡 Revenue is shrinking while margin improves.** **Turnover −6.5%, turnkey −33%.** *The margin gain is partly composition — the low-margin half got smaller. **If turnkey does not recover in Q3–Q4 as guided, the 10% revenue growth target fails outright.***

**Risk 8 — 🟡 It is a PSU, and the board is the ministry's to fill.** **The C&MD has held additional charge of Director (Commercial) since 30 June on a three-month authorisation.** *At a company whose problem is order intake, that is the seat you would least want vacant.*

**Risk 9 — 🟢 RESOLVED, and it was the reason for the last downgrade.** v1's thesis rested on *"19.3x P/E on 30.6% ROCE"*; 28-Jul showed the P/E was optical because of provision reversals. ***Q1FY27 was clean of write-backs, confirmed twice on the record, and the operating margin doubled without them. The mechanism that caused the July downgrade did not repeat.***

**Risk 10 — 🟢 TRACKING WELL.** **Consultancy segment margin 17% → 24% with a commitment to hold 24–25%; mix at 60% against a 55% floor.** *The one part of the plan that is fully working.*

**Risk 11 — 🆕 Concentration in a single JV.** **RFCL contributed ₹42.51cr of ₹157.94cr consolidated profit — 27% — from one fertiliser plant.** *Management says it is recurring and running at capacity, and the annual shutdown is absorbed in the normal 30–35 day window. **But a 27% profit contribution from a single non-core asset is a dependency, and it did not exist a year ago.***
