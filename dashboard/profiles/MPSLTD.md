## 1. Business in Plain English

When a global academic publisher — Elsevier, Wiley, Springer, a large university press — receives a research manuscript, a long chain of unglamorous work has to happen before it becomes a published, citable, searchable article. Someone must check the paper isn't fraudulent. Someone must run peer review. Someone must typeset it, tag it for accessibility, and host it on a platform that never goes down. **MPS Limited does that work, for a large share of the world's serious knowledge organisations.**

The plain-English version: **MPS is the outsourced production and platform engine sitting behind global academic publishing, higher education, and corporate training.** You have never heard of it and you have almost certainly read something it processed.

It runs three segments:

1. **Research Solutions (55% of revenue)** — content production, peer review management, research-integrity checking, and the hosting platforms publishers run on (HighWire, DigiCore Pro, THINK365). Includes **AJE**, an author-services business serving researchers directly.
2. **Education Solutions (33%)** — content and courseware production for large US K-12 and higher-ed publishers, accessibility conversion at scale, and now **Unbound Medicine**, a subscription platform for medical and nursing schools, hospitals and academic libraries.
3. **Corporate Learning (12%)** — enterprise workforce training under the **Liberate Global** brand.

**The analogy: MPS is a bespoke tailor that quietly became the mill.** It began as a services shop billing for hours and pages. Over a decade it bought the looms — platforms, tooling, and now AI — so that the same garment takes less labour and commands a better price. The strategic shift management is now executing is the last step: **being paid for the finished suit rather than for the hours spent stitching.**

**The 30-second snapshot (Q1FY27, consolidated):** revenue **₹224.24cr (+20.4%)**, EBITDA **₹76.96cr (+53.0%)** at a **34.3%** margin, PAT **₹50.39cr (+43.0%)**, EPS ₹29.70 — *"the strongest first quarter in the Company's history, on every line that matters"* (CFO Prarthana Agarwal). Headcount rose **under 3%**.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Publishers must publish, but they do not want to run factories.** Academic publishing has high gross margins and enormous fixed process complexity. Every publisher faces the same build-vs-buy question on typesetting, XML tagging, peer-review administration and hosting, and most have concluded that these are not where their edge lies. That outsourcing decision, once made, is rarely reversed — the switching cost is operational, not contractual.

**Force 2 — Research volume keeps growing, and so does research fraud.** Global article output rises every year. So do paper mills, image manipulation and identity fraud. This creates a genuinely new category — **research integrity** — that did not meaningfully exist a decade ago and that publishers cannot staff internally at scale.

**Force 3 — Accessibility is becoming a legal requirement, not a courtesy.** Regulations in the US and EU increasingly mandate that educational and scholarly content be accessible. Retrofitting decades of back-catalogue plus all new output is a large, recurring, compliance-driven workload.

**Force 4 — Corporate skills obsolescence.** Enterprises must continuously retrain workforces as job content changes. This is a structurally growing market (management sizes it at 12–13% growth) though it is competitive and fragmented.

**Force 5 — AI raises the value of verification.** This is the contested one, and it is the whole debate on this stock. Management's position, in Rahul Arora's words: *"As models get stronger, the constraint moves away from generating content and toward verifying it, toward domain context, toward the last mile inside a real workflow. That last mile is exactly where MPS sits, and it is a tailwind for us, not a threat."*

**Where MPS sits:** it is the largest India-listed pure-play in this niche, competing globally against private peers and publishers' in-house teams. It is now the **second-largest peer-review provider in the industry by volume**, targeting first place by the end of FY27.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Mission-critical workflow embedding.** HighWire, DigiCore Pro and THINK365 are the hosting and publishing backbone for MPS's largest clients on multi-year contracts. Sukhwant Singh (Chief Delivery Officer, Research), Q1FY27: these *"run mission-critical workflows and are not easily replaced."* A publisher migrating off these platforms risks its own production pipeline — that is a real switching cost, not a stated one.

**Moat 2 — Occupying the verification control point.** *"The bottleneck in research today is no longer generating content; it is verifying it. That is precisely the control point we occupy."* Research Integrity Check sits on top of DigiCore, detecting paper mills, identity fraud and image manipulation. **This is a defensible position specifically because AI made content cheap and trust expensive.**

**Moat 3 — Measurable outcome superiority.** MPS's controlled author-services work shows a **manuscript acceptance rate of ~52% against an industry baseline near 32%**. That is a quantified, sellable result — and it is what makes outcome-based pricing possible at all.

**Moat 4 — MPS Labs, an AI engine amortised across three segments.** Arora: *"whose work compounds across every segment at very little marginal cost. That is not a feature. It is a structural moat."* AI author workflows have processed **over a million manuscripts**.

**Moat 5 — A demonstrated capability to buy underperforming assets and fix their margins.** The acquisition filter is explicit: targets growing revenue ≥10% CAGR over three years with **EBITDA of 5–15%**, which MPS then takes **to 30%**. This is the single most repeatable thing the company does.

**Moat 6 — Capital allocation discipline with a seven-year record.** *"Capital earns its keep within 12 months or gets returned to shareholders."* More than **₹650cr returned to shareholders FY19–FY25**, with a payout ratio among the highest in the industry (FY24 108%, FY25 95%).

**Moat 7 — Genuine operating leverage, now demonstrated.** Q1FY27: revenue +20%, EBITDA +53%, headcount +<3%. Research margin **45.1%**. This is not a one-quarter artefact — it is the second year of a deliberate restructuring flowing through.

**Moat 8 — Recurring and outcome-linked revenue mix.** **45% of revenue is recurring; 25% is outcome-based partnerships** (Arora, Q1FY27). Both proportions are rising by design.

**Moat 9 — Pricing power inside acquired assets.** On Unbound: *"every renewal with a customer, whether it's a 10-year-old customer or a 3-year-old customer, there is a price increase. Every year within a term of a contract, there is a price increase."*

**Where the moat is weaker.**
- **Research Solutions grows at ~15% in a market growing 8–9%** — outperformance, but the underlying market is slow.
- **Corporate Learning is losing to its market**: MPS grew 6–7% against a market at 12–13%. Arora concedes: *"that's the area where a lot of the catch-up has to happen."*
- The production layer of the business — typesetting, composition — **is exactly what AI compresses**. MPS is racing to move up-stack faster than the bottom erodes.
- **Client concentration** remains real, though Unbound has improved it (client count 404 → 841 YoY).
- No control over publisher end-markets; ~two-thirds of revenue is USD-denominated.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — Outcome-based pricing is the margin engine.** Arora: *"Our revenue is steadily shifting from being paid for effort to being paid for outcomes… Outcomes carry better economics than effort, because the value sits in the result rather than the input, and they are far harder for anyone to commoditize. That shift is a large part of why revenue grew 20 percent this quarter while EBITDA grew 53 percent."* Currently 25% of revenue.

**Thread B — AI converts labour cost into margin.** DigiCore, Research Integrity Check and AI composition workflows let MPS absorb materially more volume on a flat cost line. Research margin went to 45.1% on 13.2% revenue growth. **This is the same thread as A, viewed from the cost side.**

**Thread C — Recurring revenue via subscription assets.** Unbound Medicine brings 97% retention subscriptions, annual contractual price escalators, and a long tail of institutional accounts that dilutes client concentration. 45% of group revenue is now recurring.

**Thread D — Acquire, then re-margin.** The repeatable playbook: buy at 5–15% EBITDA, take to 30%, unlock Rule of 40. Funded by cash plus debt, explicitly not equity.

**Thread E — Corporate Learning turnaround.** From −16.5% revenue in FY26 to +6.9% with margin from 16.9% → 25.3%. Soma Bhaduri guides steady state at **30% EBITDA margin and ~12% organic growth**.

**Where the threads reinforce:** MPS Labs builds AI once and deploys it across all three segments. Unbound's medical content plus Liberate's immersive-learning capability is a genuine cross-sell that management discovered *after* the deal (a US roadshow) rather than underwriting it — the good kind of surprise. Higher margins fund M&A, which adds recurring revenue, which raises the multiple the market will pay.

**Where they might not:** every thread depends on AI helping rather than displacing. If publishers decide the "last mile" can be automated in-house, Threads A and B invert simultaneously — the same technology that expands margin becomes the thing that removes the work.

>

>
> **Stated rationale:** *"simplification, greater operational synergies and rationalization of the shareholding structure"* and *"reducing the number of legal entities in the group structure."* **Read plainly: one fewer layer between the family and the listed company, no economic change to anyone. Mildly positive, and exactly the kind of housekeeping a promoter with this capital-allocation record would do.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — AI disintermediates the production layer.** The central bear case, and it is not frivolous. Typesetting, composition and XML tagging are precisely the tasks large language models compress fastest. MPS's answer is to move up-stack into verification, integrity and outcomes — but that is a race, not a moat, until it is finished. *Signal to watch: revenue per employee, and whether Research Solutions growth holds above 13%.*

**Risk 2 — Legacy-data lock-in may be weaker than assumed.** ValuePickr @visitkumaresh (7-Jun-2026) argues MPS's position rests on privileged access to legacy publisher data, and that *"as long as the data remains in that old legacy without upgrading — if that happens 75 percentage of revenue is negatable."* I think this overstates it, given the platform contracts are live production systems rather than archives — **but it is the sharpest bear formulation available and it deserves testing, not dismissal.**

**Risk 3 — The dividend is suspended and the yield is illusory.** FY26 payout 0% against FY24's 108%. Anyone anchoring on the 3.07% screen yield is mispricing the security. Resumption is contingent on the M&A cycle closing, with no stated date.

**Risk 4 — FY28's ₹1,500cr target requires acquisitions that have not been announced.** From FY26's ₹768cr, that is ~40% annual growth against organic growth in the early-to-mid teens. **Management is explicit that the ₹300cr FY27 EBITDA is organic and acquisitions come "over and above"** — but the FY28 revenue number cannot be reached without deals. *Signal: whether tighter FY28 guidance at Q3/Q4FY27 retains the ₹1,500cr figure.*

**Risk 5 — Organic growth is unremarkable ex-acquisitions.** ValuePickr's @Vikas Mhatre put it directly on the Q1FY27 call: *"If you just strip off the Unbound revenue… the growth is in the early teens… The only thing that is missing is the organic growth."* Arora did not dispute the arithmetic.

**Risk 6 — Corporate Learning is losing share.** Growing 6–7% into a market growing 12–13%. Margin recovery has been real; growth recovery has not. It is only 12% of revenue, which limits the damage but also the payoff.

**Risk 7 — Acquisition integration and the size step-up.** MPS historically bought distressed assets cheaply (*"at ~0.5 times sales"* per community history). It has deliberately pivoted to healthier, larger, more expensive targets. **The playbook that produced 30–50% ROCE on distressed assets is not the playbook now being run.**

**Risk 8 — Management churn.** Raised on the Q1FY27 call. Arora's answer — *"as the talent machine expands in a quick span of time, sometimes you make mistakes"* — is candid but not reassuring on its own.

**Risk 9 — Client concentration.** Improving (404 → 841 clients) but the largest publishers still carry disproportionate weight, and a single lost anchor relationship would be material.

**Risk 10 — Currency.** The large majority of revenue is USD/GBP-denominated. Rupee strength compresses reported growth and margin.

**Risk 11 — Thin institutional ownership and float.** FII 1.38% + DII 2.14% = **3.5% institutional**. Promoter at 68.34% leaves a modest float. Price discovery is thin and the stock can move violently on sentiment — as the 2025–26 AI-fear drawdown and subsequent recovery both demonstrated.

**Risk 12 — Valuation is no longer distressed.** At ₹2,721 the stock is within 3% of its 52-week high, having recovered the entire AI-fear drawdown. **The easy money in this name has been made; from here it requires delivery.**
