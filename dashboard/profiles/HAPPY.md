## 1. Business in Plain English

Every commercial truck engine, tractor axle, or industrial turbine has **precision-forged steel components** — crankshafts, connecting rods, front axle beams, gears, transmission parts — all made through the process of taking a red-hot steel billet, hammering it into shape between dies, and then machining it to micron-level tolerance. **Happy Forgings is India's largest precision forgings + machining company** for commercial vehicles, tractors, and industrial applications.

The 30-second business snapshot:
- **HQ Ludhiana, Punjab** — founded 1974 by the Garg family (Ashish Garg, MD, second generation)
- **FY26 revenue INR 1,546 cr** — highest ever
- **FY26 EBITDA margin 30.4% (+160 bps YoY)** — best-in-class for auto components
- **FY26 PAT margin 19.5% (+90 bps YoY)** — extraordinary for forgings
- **Three end-markets**: Commercial Vehicles (37%), Farm Equipment/Tractors (32%), Industrials (14%), balance is exports and other
- **Volume grew 11% in FY26; realization stable at INR 245/kg** despite softening RM prices
- **Higher machining mix** (value-added, higher-margin) is the key strategic lever

**Big picture in one sentence:** Happy Forgings is a **precision engineering compounder** with elite margins (30%+ EBITDA), riding India's CV+tractor+industrials trifecta, rock-stable 78.48% promoter holding across 12 quarters (zero dilution), trading at 46.9x P/E. The best-in-class metrics justify the premium — this is the **quality anchor of the auto components space**.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

Precision forgings + machining rides four durable forces:

**Force 1 — India's CV megatrend.** MHCV (medium & heavy commercial vehicle) production grew mid-teens % in FY26 supported by GST rationalisation, infrastructure spending, and freight activity. Every truck = 40+ forged components at INR 30k-80k per set.

**Force 2 — Tractor mechanisation.** Domestic tractor industry grew on favorable monsoons + reservoir levels + rural cash flow. India's farm mechanization is still low (~50% vs 90% in developed markets) — multi-decade tailwind.

**Force 3 — Industrials capex cycle.** Power generation, renewable energy, wind, railways, oil & gas, digital infrastructure — all driving precision component demand.

**Force 4 — Machining value-addition.** Global OEMs increasingly outsource machining alongside forging = higher-margin work for suppliers who can do both.

**Where Happy Forgings sits.** It's the **quality leader** in Indian precision forgings — smaller than Bharat Forge, but with materially higher margins (30% EBITDA vs BF's ~18%). Peers: Bharat Forge (larger, diversified), MM Forgings (comparable size), Ramkrishna Forgings.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — CV cycle downturn.** 37% CV exposure.
> **🔄 EXPOSURE REDUCED, and deliberately.** CV is **33% of Q1FY27 revenue**, down from 37%, against a management target of **~27%**. **Domestic CV grew 18% against industry production growth of ~10% — a share gain, not cycle luck.** The risk is migrating away rather than materialising.
**Risk 2 — Tractor cycle downturn.** 32% farm exposure.
> **⚠️ ACCUMULATING IN EXPORT MARKETS, holding domestically.** Farm is 32% of revenue and grew in the **mid-20s**, with domestic **+20%+**. But management flagged the export backdrop explicitly: *"Tractor demand in these markets remains subdued due to **low farm incomes, high interest rates, weak commodity prices and cautious farmer spending**, resulting in deferred equipment purchases."* **Happy is still growing exports into that** — but the underlying market is weak and this is the largest single unhedged end-market risk.
**Risk 3 — Export volume continued decline.** NA + EU markets fragile.
> **🔴 MATERIALIZED in CV, with a specific and testable cause.** **CV exports fell ~12% in Q1FY27.** Management: European and Turkey contracts are **DDP (delivered duty paid)** and *"because of the geopolitical condition… almost a month of deliveries were pending. Because of the longer route and also because of the delays in transit."* **If this is transit, it reverses in Q2. If it does not reverse, it was demand.** Offsetting: PV exports **more than doubled**, and industrial and off-highway exports grew ~50% and ~40%.

**🆕 Risk 3b — Container freight.** Cost has gone **US$2,000 → ~US$6,000**. Contracts are *"around 75% pass-through"*; management expects to recover **~US$4,500 of US$6,000** and take a **15–20% hit** on the incremental cost, with full recovery from some customers. **A gross-margin item to watch in Q2.**
**Risk 4 — Raw material inflation not fully passed through.** OEMs push back.
> **✅ RESOLVED — and it is a tailwind, not a risk, for the next two quarters.** Management flagged input-cost inflation at Q4FY26 and has now *"successfully negotiated price revisions with OEMs"*. **Only ~30% flowed into Q1FY27; the balance lands from Q2.** Notably, *"the last increase was paid almost three years back"* — so this resets a three-year-old base. **Q1's 31.3% margin is not yet the fully-priced number.**
**Risk 5 — L&T + others entering forgings.** Competition intensifies.
**Risk 6 — Machining mix expansion slows.**
> **✅ TRACKING WELL.** Machining is **90% of the mix in Q1FY27** — 88% in Q1FY26, 89% in FY26, **73% in FY21**. Gross margin at **60.7% (+276bps YoY)** is the direct read-through. The next leg is the data-centre and heavy-engine line at **₹800–1,000/kg realisation against ₹253/kg blended**.
**Risk 7 — Capex-heavy expansion timing.**
> **⚠️ VISIBLE IN ROCE, which is the honest cost of the growth.** **ROCE has fallen 23% (FY24) → 19% (FY25) → 18% (FY26)** while capex ran ~₹460cr in FY26 with ₹450–500cr guided for FY27. Investing outflow was **−₹497cr in FY26** against CFO of ₹445cr. **Mitigating:** borrowings are only ₹330cr against ₹2,128cr of net worth, **CFO is 114% of operating profit**, ICRA AA is reaffirmed, and **forging utilisation is 59%** — so the assets to absorb growth already exist. **The question is when ROCE turns, not whether the balance sheet can carry it.**
**Risk 8 — Second-gen transition risks (long-term).**
> **No change.** Ashish Garg (MD) and Pankaj Kumar Goyal (CFO) both present and fluent on the Q1FY27 call. Promoter holding 78.46%, with the only movement being ESOP allotment (Reg-30 filing, 17-Jun-2026).

**🆕 Risk 9 — The multiple is now the main risk on this name.** **55.0x trailing and 8.4x book, 3% off the 52-week high**, on a weighted two-year return of **~12% (≈5.8% CAGR)** with a **−34% bear case**. The stock rose **27.3%** since v1 while TTM PAT rose 8% — **the re-rating from 46.9x to 55.0x did the work.** At this multiple the business has to keep beating its own guidance simply to stand still.

**🆕 Risk 10 — A 4.12% public float, with domestic institutions selling into it.** Promoter 78.46% + DII 15.54% + FII 1.88% = **95.88% of the register**. **DII holding has fallen every quarter for a year: 17.07% → 15.54%**, while retail shareholder count rose 11% to 64,341. *My inference, not a disclosed fact:* institutions have been trimming into strength and retail absorbing it. **On a float this thin, both directions are violent, and there is no ValuePickr or Substack coverage at all to provide a second opinion.**

**🆕 Risk 11 — The FY29 story is doing work in a FY27 multiple.** The data-centre and heavy-engine line — **₹250cr of the ₹950cr order book, at 3–4x blended realisation** — completes capex end-FY27, trials early FY28, and management is explicit that it *"will start contributing meaningfully from FY29 onwards."* **That is two full years away, and a 55x multiple is discounting it now.** Any slippage in the trials or the customer approvals is a direct hit to the rating.
