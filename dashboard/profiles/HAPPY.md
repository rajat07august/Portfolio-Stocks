## 1. Business in Plain English

Happy Forgings makes **the heaviest moving parts inside an engine** — crankshafts, front axle beams, steering knuckles and connecting rods — by heating steel billets to near-melting and hammering them into shape under presses up to **14,000 tonnes**.

The economics turn on two things. **First, tonnage capability:** very few Indian forgers handle components from 250 kg to 3 tonnes, and the customers who need them have almost nowhere else to go. **Second, machining:** a forged crankshaft sells for one price; **a fully machined one sells for three to four times as much**, and Happy does both under one roof.

**The useful analogy is a heavy-metal precision workshop that keeps climbing the value ladder** — forging to machining, commercial vehicles to industrials, India to export.

**End markets, and the shift is the story:**

| Segment | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Commercial vehicles** | The historic base; still largest, deliberately shrinking as a share |
| **Farm equipment** | Cyclical, subdued in export markets |
| **Industrials** | **11% → 16% of revenue in a single quarter** — wind, railways, oil & gas, digital infrastructure |
| **Passenger vehicles** | **7–8% of mix and rising**, predominantly export |
| **Off-highway** | 11% of revenue, **+40% growth** across domestic and export |

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Heavy forging is a capital and capability barrier, not a price game.** A 14,000-tonne press line and the metallurgy to run it are not replicable at will. **Utilisation on that line is only 65–70%**, so incremental volume comes at very high contribution margin.

**Force 2 — Machining is where the value is.** Management: on full-machined crankshafts *"the gross margins are in a range of… kind of 80%"*. **Forged → machined is a 3–4x realisation step on the same physical part.**

**Force 3 — India is becoming a supply base for European industrial forging.** An analyst put it directly: *"what we hear from other manufacturers is Europe is having a tough time in terms of their supplier base. So that large part of the business could move here."* **60% of the ₹950cr order book is export.**

**Force 4 — Industrial end-markets are broadening.** Management names *"renewable energy, power transmission and grid infrastructure, railway modernization, and the rapidly expanding data centre ecosystems."*

**Force 5 — Passenger vehicles are a new, structurally larger pool.** From near-zero to **7–8% of mix**, with management discussing a path toward *"10%, 12%."*

**Where Happy sits.** ₹20,875cr — **the most expensive name in Sector 3** against SSWL, WHEELS, NRBBEARING and AMIC. **The peer set trades at a fraction of this multiple on lower but not trivially lower returns.**

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Tonnage capability few competitors have.** 14,000-tonne press at 65–70% utilisation, plus a **10,000-tonne line added last quarter** — and parts can be shifted down to free heavy capacity. **Forging utilisation overall is only 59%: growth is available without new presses.**

**Moat 2 — Forging plus machining under one roof.** ~80% gross margin on full-machined components; **3–4x realisation per kg versus forged-only.**

**Moat 3 — Margin consistency that is genuinely rare.** **Four consecutive quarters above 30% EBITDA margin**, at 31% in each of the last four. OPM by year: 28% → 29% → 29% → 30% → **31% TTM**.

**Moat 4 — Cash conversion above 100%.** **FY26 CFO ₹445cr against ₹471cr of operating profit — 114%.** Five-year CFO/OP: 53%, 80%, 71%, 92%, **114%**.

**Moat 5 — A ₹950cr order book of the right kind.** *"Predominantly export-oriented"*, with a *"high proportion of complex precision engineering, value-added products that command superior realizations and are expected to support further margin improvement."* **60% export / 40% domestic.**

**Moat 6 — Diversification that is measurable, not aspirational.** Industrials **11% → 16% in one quarter**; PV to **7–8%**; off-highway **+40%**.

**Moat 7 — Capital-allocation discipline, stated against its own interest.** Asked about inorganic growth, Ashish Garg said they are open to it **in energy and aerospace** where approvals take years — but on the core: *"on the farm, CV, and other areas, we are already there. And plus, **the valuations are very expensive, which would be a drain on the return on capital employed. So we are being little cautious.**"* ***A management team declining to buy growth because it would dilute ROCE is a real signal.***

**Moat 8 — Promoter at 78.46%**, the only movement being ESOP dilution of 2bps a quarter.

**Where the moat is weaker.**
- **🔴 63.8x and 9.8x book.** The single largest risk in this profile is the price.
- **🔴 ROCE has fallen 23% → 18% across the capex cycle and has not yet turned.**
- **🔴 Free cash flow is negative** — FY26 FCF −₹16cr on ₹497cr of investing outflow.
- **⚠️ A ₹650cr capex programme** whose revenue is **FY28–FY29**, carrying depreciation now.
- **⚠️ Public float is 4.12%.** A ₹20,875cr company with roughly ₹860cr of genuinely free stock.
- **⚠️ DII holding fell 16.98% → 15.54%** over four quarters.
- **⚠️ FY26 revenue grew 10%** — the acceleration is recent, not established.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — Machining is the margin, and it is nearly maxed as a mix lever.** 73% of revenue in FY21 → **90% in Q1FY27**. It drove the 60.7% gross margin. **There is not much runway left in this particular lever.**

**Thread B — So the next margin leg has to come from mix and price, and both are in flight.** Industrials 11% → 16% in a quarter toward a 30–31% target; PV 8% and growing >70%; **~70% of the negotiated OEM price revision still to land from Q2.**

**Thread C — Capacity is not the constraint; it is the investment.** **Forging utilisation 59%, machining 78%**, the 14,000-ton line **65–70%**, machining capacity expanded to **75,200 MT**. **Growth is available without new presses — yet ₹497cr went out the door in FY26 and ₹450–500cr is guided for FY27.**

**Thread D — Because the capex is buying a different business, not more of the same.** The data-centre and heavy-engine line is *"largely the machined businesses with a realization of almost **Rs.800 to Rs.1,000**"* per kg, against a **₹253 blended** — a 3–4x step. Complete end-FY27, trials early FY28, **meaningful FY29**.

**Thread E — Solar is a quiet 1–1.5% of margin, and it has one named dependency.** 80 acres leased, +10 AC MW approved, clearances obtained; **on stream January, generation Q4FY27 — pending right-of-way work.**

**Thread F — Exports are the growth engine and the near-term drag simultaneously.** 60% of the ₹950cr order book is export; **CV exports fell 12% on DDP transit delays and freight went US$2,000 → US$6,000.**

**How they interact.** **Every operating thread is working, and the cost is that ROCE is falling while the capex lands.** **FY29 is when the ₹800–1,000/kg business shows up.** ***The multiple is currently discounting FY29 at FY27 prices — that is the entire investment question on this name.***

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — CV cycle downturn.** *(v1: 37% CV exposure.)*
> **🔄 EXPOSURE REDUCED, AND DELIBERATELY.** CV is **33% of Q1FY27 revenue**, down from 37%, against a management target of **~27%**. **Domestic CV grew 18% against industry production growth of ~10% — a share gain, not cycle luck.** **The risk is migrating away rather than materialising.**

**Risk 2 — Tractor cycle downturn.** *(32% farm exposure.)*
> **⚠️ ACCUMULATING IN EXPORT MARKETS, HOLDING DOMESTICALLY.** Farm is 32% of revenue and grew in the **mid-20s**, with domestic **+20%+**. But management flagged the backdrop explicitly: *"Tractor demand in these markets remains subdued due to **low farm incomes, high interest rates, weak commodity prices and cautious farmer spending**."* **Happy is still growing exports into that — but the underlying market is weak, and this is the largest single unhedged end-market risk.**

**🔴 Risk 3 — Export volume decline.**
> **🔴 MATERIALIZED IN CV, WITH A SPECIFIC AND TESTABLE CAUSE.** **CV exports fell ~12% in Q1FY27.** Management: European and Turkey contracts are **DDP (delivered duty paid)** and *"because of the geopolitical condition… almost a month of deliveries were pending. Because of the longer route and also because of the delays in transit."* ***If this is transit, it reverses in Q2. If it does not reverse, it was demand.*** **Offsetting: PV exports more than doubled, and industrial and off-highway exports grew ~50% and ~40%.**

**⚠️ Risk 3b — Container freight.** Cost has gone **US$2,000 → ~US$6,000**. Contracts are *"around 75% pass-through"*; management expects to recover **~US$4,500 of US$6,000** and take a **15–20% hit** on the incremental cost. **A gross-margin item to watch in Q2.**

**Risk 4 — Raw material inflation not fully passed through.**
> **✅ RESOLVED — and it is a tailwind, not a risk, for the next two quarters.** Management flagged input-cost inflation at Q4FY26 and has now *"successfully negotiated price revisions with OEMs."* **Only ~30% flowed into Q1FY27; the balance lands from Q2.** *"The last increase was paid almost three years back"* — **this resets a three-year-old base. Q1's 31.3% margin is not yet the fully-priced number.**

**Risk 5 — L&T and others entering forgings.** *(No update. Competition in heavy tonnage remains structurally limited by press capability and approvals.)*

**Risk 6 — Machining mix expansion slows.**
> **✅ TRACKING WELL, AND NEARING ITS CEILING.** Machining is **90% of the mix in Q1FY27** — 88% in Q1FY26, **73% in FY21**. Gross margin at **60.7% (+276bps YoY)** is the read-through. **But at 90% there is limited runway left in this lever** — the next leg is the data-centre and heavy-engine line at **₹800–1,000/kg against ₹253/kg blended**.

**🔴 Risk 7 — Capex-heavy expansion timing.**
> **⚠️ VISIBLE IN ROCE, WHICH IS THE HONEST COST OF THE GROWTH.** **ROCE has fallen 23% (FY24) → 19% (FY25) → 18% (FY26)** while capex ran ~₹460cr in FY26 with **₹450–500cr guided for FY27** and a separate **₹650cr programme** for the new industrial plant. **Investing outflow was −₹497cr in FY26 against ₹445cr of CFO — free cash flow was −₹16cr.** **Mitigating:** borrowings are only ₹330cr against ₹2,128cr of net worth, **CFO is 114% of operating profit**, ICRA AA is reaffirmed, and **forging utilisation is 59%**. ***The question is when ROCE turns, not whether the balance sheet can carry it.***

**Risk 8 — Second-generation transition risk.**
> **No change.** Ashish Garg (MD) and Pankaj Kumar Goyal (CFO) both present and fluent on the Q1FY27 call. Promoter holding 78.46%, the only movement being ESOP allotment.

**🔴 Risk 9 — The multiple is now the main risk on this name, and it has got worse.**
> **⚠️ ESCALATED (22-Aug-2026).** The 12-Aug refresh recorded **55.0x and 8.4x book, 3% off the high, on a ~12% weighted return with a −34% bear case.** **It is now 63.8x and 9.8x book, 5.6% off the high, on a ~−4% weighted return with a −43% bear case.** **The stock rose 27.3% between v1 and the refresh while TTM PAT rose 8%; it has risen a further 12.5% in the ten days since.** ***At this multiple the business has to keep beating its own guidance simply to stand still.***

**🔴 Risk 10 — A 4.12% public float, with domestic institutions selling into it.** Promoter 78.46% + DII 15.54% + FII 1.88% = **95.88% of the register.** **DII holding has fallen every quarter for a year: 17.07% (Mar-2025) → 15.54%, a 153bps reduction**, while retail shareholder count rose 11% to **64,341**. *My inference, not a disclosed fact:* **institutions have been trimming into strength and retail absorbing it.** **On a float this thin both directions are violent — and there is no ValuePickr or Substack coverage at all to provide a second opinion.**

**🔴 Risk 11 — The FY29 story is doing work in an FY27 multiple.** The data-centre and heavy-engine line — **₹250cr of the ₹950cr order book, at 3–4x blended realisation** — completes capex end-FY27, trials early FY28, and management is explicit it *"will start contributing meaningfully from **FY29** onwards."* **🆕 The heavy 250 kg–3 tonne range carries the same date:** *"will start contributing from third or fourth quarter of next financial year… meaningfully from FY29 onwards."* **That is two full years away, and 63.8x is discounting it now.** **Any slippage in trials or customer approvals is a direct hit to the rating.**

**🆕 Risk 12 — Solar has one named dependency and it is unglamorous (NEW — 22-Aug-2026).** The captive solar plant is worth **1–1.5% of margin**, has all clearances, and *"a large part of the capex is already done"* — but **power-up waits on right-of-way work**: *"Just some work on ROW, right of way, is going on. Once the ROW work is completed, we'll be in a position to power up. So I think January is the time."* **Generation from Q4FY27.** *A small, specific, checkable dependency — and exactly the kind that slips.*

**🟢 Risk 13 — the risk of being too cautious, and on this name it deserves real weight (NEW — 22-Aug-2026).** ***This is the best-executing company on the watchlist and it is not close.*** **Every guided number has been beaten**: late-teen volumes came in at 23.1%, ~30.5% margin came in at 31.3%, the H1FY27 press was commissioned in Q1. **FY21–FY26: volumes 1.8x, revenue 2.6x, EBITDA 3x, PAT 3.5x, funded almost entirely internally.** **Four straight quarters above 30% margin; 114% cash conversion; ICRA AA; zero dilution in three years.** **~70% of a three-year price revision still to land. Forging at 59% utilisation. A ₹950cr order book, 60% export, in PV and industrials.** ***If FY29 arrives on schedule at ₹800–1,000/kg, the FY27 multiple will look like the wrong denominator entirely — and this profile will have been early and wrong for two years.***
