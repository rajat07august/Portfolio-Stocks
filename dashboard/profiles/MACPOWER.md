## 1. Business in Plain English

Macpower makes the machines that make parts.

If you want to turn a bar of steel into a gear, a valve body, an engine block or a submarine fitting, you need a CNC machine tool — a computer-controlled lathe or milling machine that holds the metal, spins it or spins a cutter against it, and removes material to a tolerance measured in microns. Macpower builds those machines in Metoda GIDC, Rajkot, and sells them to the roughly 40,000 small and mid-sized engineering workshops across India that actually produce the country's metal parts.

**The cleanest way to place it: almost every company in Sector 3 of this watchlist is Macpower's customer, not its competitor.** Amic Forging appears by name on Macpower's own FY26 customer list, alongside BHEL, HAL, Ordnance Factory, Polycab, Bhagat Forge, GNA Gears, Emerald Tyre, NIPHA and Kirloskar *(Q4FY26 call, 28-May-2026)*. Macpower sells the capital good; they sell what it produces.

**The analogy: a bespoke kitchen-appliance maker who stocks every part.** Macpower offers **364 machine variants** *(Q1FY27 call; "more than 400" on the Q4FY26 call — the two numbers are used loosely and interchangeably by management)*. Each machine contains roughly **990 components**. To be able to build any of those variants on short notice, the company carries an inventory of **19,000-plus distinct components**. A customer specifies a configuration, and Macpower assembles it — because assembly is fast, but procurement is not.

That single operating choice explains almost everything about the financial statements, and Section 4 is built around it.

**Three further facts frame the business:**
- **Average machine realisation is ~₹20 lakh** — FY26 ₹20.0 lakh, new order bookings running at ₹20.9-21.9 lakh *(Q1FY27 call)*. The range is ₹12 lakh at the low end to ₹2cr at the high end, so this is a volume business, not a marquee-order business.
- **It is almost entirely domestic and almost entirely cash-on-delivery.** Product sales were ₹331.99cr of ₹333.18cr FY26 revenue; services ₹1.14cr. There is no geographic revenue split in the Annual Report because exports are immaterial. The CMD on exports: *"we are not focusing on any export."*
- **The customer pays before the machine leaves.** *"I do not give the machine without advance, or I do not even make it."* — Rupesh Mehta, Q1FY27 call. Trade receivables are ₹46.27cr on ₹333cr of revenue.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**1. Machine tools are the root of the industrial tree.** You cannot make a part to tolerance without a machine that holds tolerance. Every manufacturing policy — PLI, Make in India, defence indigenisation — eventually converts into demand for metal-cutting capacity, because that is the physical bottleneck.

**2. India consumes far more than it makes, and the gap is the opportunity.** Per IMTMA data presented at the Goa summit and quoted by management on the Q4FY26 call: India's domestic machine-tool **consumption is ~₹35,000cr** while India **produced ~₹16,000cr** in 2026. India ranks **9th globally in production and 4th in consumption** — the mismatch between those two ranks is the import bill, and it is the addressable prize for a domestic builder.

**3. The forecast growth is structural, not cyclical.** IMTMA projects consumption at **₹54,000cr by 2030** and ₹2,30,000cr by 2049. Management's read: *"averagely, we are predicting 25% growth in this segment."* Treat the 2049 number as directional; the 2030 number implies ~11% CAGR, which is the honest baseline.

**4. Import substitution has a price ceiling, and that ceiling is where Macpower lives.** In the sub-₹20 lakh turning segment, Indian builders compete effectively — management sizes it at **32,000 machines made in India**, of which **12,000-14,000 are below ₹15 lakh**. Above ₹1cr, volumes collapse to ~1,000-1,500 machines; above ₹2cr, 50-100; above ₹3cr, fewer than ten, and those are overwhelmingly imported. **The volume is at the bottom and the glamour is at the top.**

**5. The customer base is the long tail of Indian engineering.** Macpower's orders come predominantly from **tier-3 and tier-4 suppliers** — job-work shops, general engineering, die-and-mould, plastics, and increasingly EMS. These buyers finance machines with bank loans under ₹1cr and government subsidy schemes, which is why their purchasing is seasonal and paperwork-bound. That seasonality is visible in every quarter of this company's P&L.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**1. It holds inventory as a weapon, and says so openly.** The import lead time on critical components is brutal — the CMD: *"If I import something from Taiwan, then their delivery time is three months, and logistic time is now two months. So, I will not get it before six months."* Macpower carries **a full year of imported-component inventory**. When a customer wants one of 364 variants, Macpower can assemble it now; a competitor without the shelf cannot. *"Inventory is the game changer in our line."*

**2. It sells for cash and refuses credit — deliberately.** With a 2% share of domestic production, management's position is that chasing large institutional accounts means accepting their payment terms, and there is no need: *"as long as I am taking 100% money in the market... Why should I give credit now?"* The ₹46.27cr of receivables is characterised as PSU and bank letters settling in 3-4 days, not trade credit.

**3. Zero debt, zero dilution, zero pledge — over the entire listed record.** Total borrowings at 31-Mar-2026 were **₹0.39cr**. Equity share capital has been ₹10.00cr since FY18. Promoter holding has not fallen in twenty quarters. The FY26 Annual Report's CARO note confirms **no loans, no guarantees, no investments given to any party**, and no borrowing against pledged securities.

**4. Backward integration is real but only half-built.** Macpower makes roughly **40-45% of its machine's components in-house, by component count**, against a stated ceiling of **75-80%** (the rest — ball screws, LM guideways, sheet metal, fasteners, belts — nobody makes in-house; the CMD's analogy is that car companies don't make tyres). **The near-term bottleneck is specific and disclosed:** against a 2,500-machine assembly capacity, in-house **spindle capacity is 1,700-1,800** and **powder-coating capacity is 2,000**.

**5. A distribution network built ahead of the revenue.** **10 branch offices and 8 technology centres**, 220 people in sales and service, covering ~39 cities, with new branches in Baroda, Gurgaon, Kolkata and Nashik and technology centres in Pune, Kolhapur and Mumbai. This is the cost that has held margin flat, and it is the asset that produced the order-inflow inflection.

**6. Captive solar.** Roughly **90% of daytime power cost** eliminated by an installed captive solar plant.

**7. Genuine R&D throughput for a company this size.** **37 new variants developed in FY25**; in FY26-27, the DCM double-column series, LX 1000, LX 3000 (a 20-tonne machine), V-855 Super, Turn-Mill with Y-axis, and high-speed drill-tap centres with 20,000 RPM spindles aimed at the EMS sector.

### Where the moat is weaker — honestly

- **There is no technology moat.** The control system — the single most valuable component in a CNC machine — is bought from FANUC, Siemens or Mitsubishi. Macpower is an integrator and a fabricator, not a controls company.
- **The high-end is closed to it, by its own choice and by export control.** On 5-axis machines: *"it will not be my business model to sell 5-axis... we will sell 1-2 machines a year."* The stated reasons are real — FANUC and Mitsubishi won't supply 5-axis controls into India, Siemens requires a 300-page BAFA end-use agreement, and a diversion to a defence end-user is a compliance risk. **But the effect is that the fastest-growing, highest-margin end of the market is not addressable.**
- **Scale is against it.** Jyoti CNC is twelve times Macpower's market capitalisation. Global OEMs own the premium tier outright.
- **The 2% share cuts both ways.** It is a long runway, and it is also evidence that after 23 years the company has not yet built a decisive position.
- **Customer quality is thin.** Tier-3 and tier-4 buyers financing machines with sub-₹1cr bank loans are the first to stop buying in a downturn — and their purchase decisions are hostage to loan sanctions and subsidy paperwork, which is exactly what stalled 80 finished machines at the end of FY26.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

The threads reinforce each other, and one of them is load-bearing.

**The inventory shelf is what lets the branch network convert.** Macpower spent FY25 and FY26 building 10 branches, 8 technology centres and 220 sales people — the cost that held EBITDA flat at 16% through a period of 27% revenue growth. That network only produces orders if Macpower can *deliver* what it wins, across 364 variants, faster than a competitor waiting six months for a Taiwanese ball screw. **The 19,000-SKU shelf is the fulfilment engine behind the order-book inflection from ₹331cr to ₹456cr.** Inventory days and order growth are not two separate facts; they are the same fact.

**Operating leverage is the whole margin story, and management is straight about it.** The CMD names three margin levers in order: volume against a largely fixed cost base, backward integration, and defence mix. Only the first is currently working — Q1FY27 put +321bps of margin on +56% revenue with realisation flat. Backward integration is stuck at 40-45% of components pending the new plant's machinery, and defence is ₹20cr of a ₹456cr book with tenders frozen for two years. **The 20-21% margin target therefore depends almost entirely on volume, which depends on capacity.**

**And capacity is where everything converges.** The existing plant is good for ~₹500cr. FY27 guidance takes it to ~₹450cr. **FY28 growth requires the 13-acre plant to open roughly on time, and the 10,000-machine ambition requires the 60-acre government land that has now been pending for about two years.** The 13-acre lease is explicitly the workaround for that delay — *"we took it because we did not wait for 60 acre."*

**So the chain is: shelf → delivery → orders → volume → margin → cash to fund the next shelf.** It works, and it has been working. **But it produces almost no free cash while it runs** — ₹3.06cr in FY26 — because each turn of the wheel needs a bigger shelf. The company is financing 30% growth out of supplier credit and retained earnings, with ₹6.6cr of cash on the balance sheet and a ₹50cr plant to build.

**That is the tension in one sentence: a business with 29% ROCE and no debt that nonetheless has no spare cash, at the exact moment it needs to build.** The subsidy makes the debt nearly free, so the funding is solvable. **But it will be debt, not the ₹20-25cr of cash management described.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**1. 🔴 The FY28 capacity cliff.** The existing plant is good for ~₹500cr on management's own arithmetic and FY27 guidance takes it to ~₹450cr. **There is no room for FY28 without the new plant.** The company's record on delivering land-based capacity on time is the 60-acre plan: ~two years late, four separate timetables, still pending.

**2. 🔴 Capital goods is a cycle, and this customer base is its most fragile segment.** Tier-3 and tier-4 job shops buying ₹20 lakh machines on sub-₹1cr bank loans with subsidy support are the first to stop. The FY25 revenue print — **+8.7% in a year guided at 20-25%** — is what that looks like. It happened twenty-four months ago.

**3. 🔴 Inventory is 83% of net worth, verified lightly.** The CARO note discloses that only **A and B category inventory, ~80% of value, is physically verified — by the Store Manager**, internally, at "reasonable intervals". For the asset that *is* this balance sheet, that is thin. The same note discloses two quarters where the bank stock statement disagreed with the books (Jun-25 +₹0.67cr, Mar-26 −₹0.60cr), attributed to provisional figures under a compressed schedule. **Small, properly disclosed — but it is the one number that would hurt most if it were wrong.**

**4. 🔴 The gap between what is said on calls and what is filed.** Receivables understated by ₹12cr and a year, cash overstated by roughly 3-4x, market share doubled. **If an investor cannot rely on spoken figures, the only usable data arrives twice a year with the audited accounts — and Macpower publishes no quarterly balance sheet.**

**5. 🔴 The margin thesis rests on assets that do not exist.** 25% was conditional on the 60-acre land; 20-21% is conditional on the 13-acre plant's backward-integration machinery. Today's 16.2% is a volume-leverage number. If volume stalls, margin goes backwards fast — Q1FY26 at 12.98% shows exactly how fast.

**6. 🟡 Single-source import dependency.** Ball screws and LM guideways come from **one company in Taiwan**. Mitigated by a year of inventory — which is itself the reason inventory days are 259. A tariff, an export control, or a Taiwan Strait event converts a working-capital choice into an existential one.

**7. 🟡 No technology of its own at the value-dense point.** Controls come from FANUC, Siemens or Mitsubishi. The 5-axis segment is closed by export control and by choice. **The company cannot move up the value chain on its own; it needs the JV, and the JV is unsigned after multiple quarters of "discussions".**

**8. 🟡 Funding the ₹50cr plant.** With ₹6.62cr of cash and bank at year-end and ₹3.06cr of annual free cash, this will be debt-funded — cheap debt at ~1.25% effective, but debt, and the first on this balance sheet in years. The undrawn working-capital facility (>₹5cr, secured on current assets) is the other call on the same assets.

**9. 🟡 Liquidity and float.** 26.78% float, under 1% institutional ownership, and a security that has moved 2.7x in a year with four exchange surveillance letters. **Position sizing is a real constraint here, and exit in a downtick will be worse than the chart suggests.**

**10. 🟡 Key-man risk is total.** Rupesh Mehta answers essentially every question on every call. There is no disclosed second operating voice. The named successor is a recently-endowed family member with no disclosed role.

**11. 🟡 All three independent directors expire together on 16-Nov-2027** — a complete simultaneous turnover of the independent bench inside this thesis's window, on a board where the other three seats are one family.

**12. 🟡 The scrutiny gap.** One ValuePickr thread (142 posts), **zero Substack coverage**, under 1% institutional holding, no sell-side. **Nobody is checking this company's arithmetic except its own auditors and a handful of retail investors** — which is precisely why the three unreconciled call figures went unchallenged.

### Data quality notes

1. **All figures standalone.** No subsidiaries exist. Not a limitation, but stated because Screener's blank consolidated page invites the DYCL-style error.
2. **No quarterly balance sheet is published.** Receivables, inventory and cash can only be verified at half-year and full-year. **This is what makes the ₹33cr receivables claim and the ₹20-25cr cash claim unresolvable until the H1FY27 filing.**
3. **Machine volumes are not disclosed.** Management declines on competitive grounds: *"Number of machines we are not disclosing."* Unit volumes in this profile are derived from revenue ÷ stated average realisation and are labelled as calculations where used.
4. **The "80 machines" finished-goods count is my calculation** from the ₹12.89cr audited finished-goods balance and the ~₹20 lakh realisation, not a company disclosure. The audited rupee figure is the fact; the machine count is an inference.
