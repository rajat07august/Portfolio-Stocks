## 1. Business in Plain English

When Safran, GE or Rolls-Royce assemble a jet engine — or when an MRO shop tears one down for overhaul — they cannot do it by hand. Each engine needs **hundreds of bespoke steel fixtures, cradles, slings, stands and calibration jigs**, each machined to aerospace tolerance, each specific to one engine programme, each requiring a paper trail that satisfies a regulator.

**Unimech makes those tools.** It does not make the engine or the aircraft. It makes the equipment without which neither can be built or serviced — and once its tool is written into an OEM's assembly manual, changing supplier means requalifying the tool, which nobody does casually.

**The useful analogy is the surgical instrument maker, not the surgeon.** Small, unglamorous, high-margin, and impossible to substitute mid-procedure.

Three businesses, in order of size:

| Segment | What it is | Share of Q1FY27 revenue |
|---|---|---|
| **Aero tooling** | Engine and airframe tooling, ground support equipment, MRO tooling | **~76%** |
| **Precision components & assemblies (PCA)** | Machined parts and sub-assemblies for nuclear, semiconductor, defence and — newly — flying aerospace parts | balance |
| **Hobel Bellows** *(acquired Apr-2026)* | Metallic bellows, flexible tubing, precision pipe assemblies for engines 900–9,000 BHP | **21%** *(only 2 months consolidated)* |

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Narrowbody production rates are being rebuilt, and every new engine needs a new toolset.** Airbus is pushing toward **75 A320-family aircraft a month by 2027** from ~60; **LEAP engine deliveries ran 500 in a quarter, +40% YoY** (management's own Nov-2025 framing). Tooling demand tracks *programme* volume, not fleet size.

**Force 2 — MRO is the annuity behind the cycle.** Every engine that flies comes back for overhaul, and every overhaul shop needs the same tooling as the assembly line. **India's MRO market is projected from ~$1.7bn (2021) to $4bn by 2031, an 8.9% CAGR against a 5.6% global average** (NITI Aayog, cited by management).

**Force 3 — Western OEMs are actively de-risking supply chains into India.** This is the structural bet: a qualified, AS9100-certified Indian supplier at Indian cost is exactly what a Tier-1 wants on its approved vendor list.

**Force 4 — Nuclear and energy is a genuine second leg, domestically driven.** India targets 100 GW nuclear by 2047 with a ₹20,000cr SMR allocation; management sizes the opportunity at **₹400–500cr of addressable content per reactor**. Unimech has **₹87cr of cumulative nuclear orders** with execution planned for H2FY27.

**Force 5 — Qualification is the barrier, and it is measured in years.** First Article Inspections, OEM audits, programme-specific approvals. **Unimech completed 165 FAIs in Q1FY27 alone.** This is the moat and also the cost.

**Where Unimech sits.** A ₹7,600cr micro-cap in a niche with no direct listed pure-play comparable. Within the watchlist it sits alongside **PARAS, AZAD, AXISCADES and DYNAMATECH** — but it is closest in character to **AZAD** (precision aero components, elite margins, premium multiple) and least like AXISCADES (services).

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Gross margin of 65% and EBITDA of 36.5% in a machining business.** That is not a metal-bashing economic profile. It is what bespoke, qualified, low-volume, high-consequence tooling earns.

**Moat 2 — Qualification lock-in with a multi-year clock.** Long qualification cycles and high switching costs, in management's words — *"we will continue to remain deeply embedded within the customer ecosystem, supported by strong technical credentials, high switching costs, and long qualification cycles."*

**Moat 3 — In-house capability rather than outsourced capacity.** **Subcontracting is ~3% of revenue.** Management runs this deliberately, and it is why the margin holds.

**Moat 4 — A Free Trade Warehousing Zone that structurally blunts the tariff problem.** Now fully operational (Q1FY27). It lets US customers hold duty-free inventory of Indian-made tooling — **which directly addresses the mechanism that destroyed the December-2025 quarter.**

**Moat 5 — 🆕 A first long-term supply agreement for flying parts.** **FACC Austria, a Tier-1 airframe supplier: USD 7.5m over five years.** Small in money (~₹13cr a year) but strategically the first recurring, programme-linked component contract — a different and better revenue type than one-off tooling. **A leading engine Tier-1 is described as "at an advanced stage."**

**Moat 6 — Hobel Bellows adds a >50% EBITDA margin business with >50% pre-acquisition ROCE.** Debt-free, asset-light, niche, mission-critical. **On the disclosed numbers this is a better business than Unimech itself.**

**Moat 7 — Capacity is already built and only 58% utilised.** Management claims peak-capacity revenue potential of **~3.5x current in aero tooling and ~3x in precision**. If demand comes, the incremental margin is very high.

**Moat 8 — Promoters own 79.82% and have not sold a share.** Flat across six quarters. No pledging disclosed.

**Where the moat is weaker — and this list is long enough to matter.**
- **🔴 The customer is one step removed and holds all the timing power.** Unimech does not control when OEMs restock. In Dec-2025 they stopped, and revenue halved in a quarter.
- **🔴 Roughly half of pre-tax profit is treasury income, not operations** (§4). That is an artefact of the IPO, and it is now being spent.
- **🔴 The order book is thin and shrinking.** **₹280cr consolidated at 30-Jun-2026, down from ₹314cr in May** — roughly one year of revenue, in a business with multi-quarter production cycles.
- **Revenue is small and lumpy.** Quarterly revenue over eight quarters: ₹59 / 61 / 54 / 68 / 63 / 62 / **34** / 82 / 108cr. **A business this size cannot smooth a customer's decision to pause.**
- **Working capital is guided to deteriorate** from 130 to **160+ days** by FY27 end.
- **Gross block is guided to roughly double in FY27**, mostly on a Saudi JV that has no revenue yet.
- **No dividend, ever**, on ₹63–83cr of annual profit.
- **Domestic institutions are leaving** — DII 7.76% → 5.60% across six quarters. FII is 0.43%.
- **Free float ~20%** on a ₹7,592cr market cap. **Thin float is why the multiple can be what it is.**

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — the qualification flywheel is the whole business model.** 165 FAIs in one quarter, 6 new prospective customers, *"targeting to meaningfully increase our qualification rates over the previous year."* Each qualification is a cost today and an annuity later. **Management is explicit that this is why precision margins lag: *"these qualification programs naturally carry higher initial cost before transitioning into serial production."*** The thread works only if conversion rates are decent — and management concedes *"not all qualifications will necessarily convert into production orders."*

**Thread B — the shift from tooling to flying parts is the strategic prize, and it has just started.** Aero tooling is **76% of revenue** and is inherently lumpy: an OEM buys a toolset once per programme. **Flying components are recurring, programme-linked and multi-year.** The FACC agreement is the first one. *"Discussions with one leading engine Tier-1 are at an advanced stage."* **If this thread delivers, the revenue volatility problem in §4 largely solves itself. If it doesn't, the volatility is permanent.**

**Thread C — the FTWZ is the answer to the December-2025 quarter, and it is now live.** The mechanism that broke that quarter was customers refusing to hold Indian-made inventory under a 50% tariff. A Free Trade Warehousing Zone lets them hold it duty-free. **This is the single most concrete piece of risk mitigation in the file, and it is already operational.**

**Thread D — Hobel converts a treasury asset into an operating one.** ₹450cr of IPO cash earning ~9% became a business earning >50% EBITDA margins. **The earnings quality improves immediately; the earnings quantity improves slowly** (§4).

**Thread E — nuclear and energy is the domestic hedge against the aerospace/US cycle.** ₹87cr of orders, execution H2FY27, ₹400–500cr of content per reactor claimed as the addressable prize. **It is real but unexecuted — not one rupee has been recognised yet.**

**Thread F — the Saudi JV and Dheya are optionality, not thesis.** Kanoo Group JV (Middle East energy) takes **USD 10m** and doubles gross block without near-term revenue. **Dheya** — a minority investment in indigenous small gas-turbine engines (DET-500 hit self-sustained operation at 34,000 RPM; DET-200 in CEMILAC/DRDO certification) — is *"looking to raise approximately USD10 million."* **Both are interesting; neither should carry any weight in a valuation today.**

**How the threads interact.** A–C are the same thread seen three ways: **convert one-off tooling into recurring content, and stop being at the mercy of a customer's restocking decision.** D and E buy time and diversification while that happens. **The stock's price assumes A–C are already done. The financials say they are beginning.**

### 🔴 5A. The Hobel Bellows acquisition — the largest capital decision in the company's history

**₹450cr, all cash, completed 27-Apr-2026** — against a market cap of ₹7,592cr and an investments balance of ₹480cr. **They spent essentially the entire IPO treasury on one deal.** A dedicated analyst call was held on 28-Apr-2026 and is the source for everything below.

**What Hobel is:** a Vizag-based manufacturer of **metallic bellows, flexible tubing and precision-engineered assemblies** — bellows from 2 to 16 inches, 500g to 330kg, for engines spanning **900 to 9,000 BHP** across power generation, automotive engines, locomotives and industrial systems.

|---|---|
| Revenue | **~₹129 cr** |
| **EBITDA margin** | **"exceeding 50%"** |
| Debt | **Nil — debt-free** |
| **Pre-acquisition ROCE** | **"over 50%"** |
| Asset intensity | *"the fixed asset size is very small… capability-led and asset-light"* |
| Customer concentration | **🔴 two OEM groups ≈ 93% of revenue** |
| Geographic spread | UK, US, Singapore, China |
| **Price paid** | **₹450 cr EV** — *"3.5x on the revenue"*, **"a fairly good deal at a seven or 6x to 7x EBITDA"** |

**The arithmetic checks: ₹129cr × ~50% = ~₹64.5cr EBITDA; ₹450cr ÷ ₹64.5cr = 7.0x.** Management's stated multiple is internally consistent.

**🔴 The question an analyst asked, and the answer he did not get.** Kamlesh Bagmar put it directly: *"what could be the likely reason that they are exiting at seven times EV EBITDA when they have stabilized their operations, they have got the customer accreditations? So what is the reason for them to sell at these valuation, seven times EV EBITDA?"*

Management's reply: *"Kamlesh, I do understand your concern. So this has been one of the thoughtful areas where we had carefully looked into and after multiple deliberations at our board's end, this is how it came…"*

***This is the sharpest question on any of the eight calls and it was not answered.*** **A debt-free, asset-light business earning a >50% EBITDA margin and >50% ROCE does not usually change hands at 7x EBITDA.** Businesses like that are rare and their owners know it. **There are benign explanations** — a family with no succession, a strategic seller exiting a non-core unit, a buyer with unique synergies — **and management gave none of them.** *Flagged as an open item, not an allegation. It is the kind of thing the FY27 annual report's related-party note and the subsidiary's own filed accounts should resolve, and it is worth resolving.*

**Three further things a holder should hold onto:**
1. **Customer concentration of ~93% in two OEM groups is extreme**, and management's mitigation — that the exposure is *"distributed across multiple entities operating in different geographies"* with independent procurement — softens it without removing it. **Unimech has bought a business whose downside looks a lot like its own December-2025 quarter.**
2. **Most of the ₹450cr becomes goodwill.** Management confirmed the asset base is small, so the consideration sits on the balance sheet as goodwill/investment rather than depreciating assets. **That flatters reported earnings** (no amortisation charge on a subsidiary investment) **and it means an impairment, not a depreciation line, is how a disappointment would show up.**
3. **The claimed post-deal ROCE of ~25% is management's estimate at 50–60% utilisation**, contingent on planned robotics and higher throughput. **It is a forecast, not a result.**

### Weighted return framework (2-year, to FY28)

*Built on management's disclosed inputs. Shares outstanding ~5.08cr, derived from TTM PAT ₹72cr and EPS ₹14.16.*

**Base case — 55%.** The recovery holds. FY27 consolidated revenue ~₹440cr (organic ~₹300cr plus a full year of Hobel at ~₹136cr); EBITDA ~₹165cr; other income collapses to ~₹15cr as the treasury is spent; depreciation and Saudi-JV costs rise. **FY27 PAT ~₹102cr (EPS ~₹20). **Bull case — 25%.** The flying-parts thread converts: the engine Tier-1 agreement lands, nuclear executes on schedule, Hobel grows above its assumed 15-17%, utilisation moves from 58% toward capacity and the operating leverage that management claims (3.5x tooling revenue at peak) starts showing. **FY28 EPS ~₹32, re-rated to 60x → ₹1,920. ≈ +29%.**

**Say the uncomfortable thing plainly, per the standing quality bar: at ₹1,491 this framework produces a negative expected return, and the base case is negative on its own.** **That is a statement about the price, not about the company.** Everything good in Sections 3 and 5 remains true — the moat is real, the margins are real, the cash conversion is real, the promoters have not sold a share, and the FACC contract is a genuine strategic step forward. **None of it is worth 105x reported and ~190x operating earnings on an 11.2% ROCE.**

### Where this becomes interesting

**At ~45x FY28 EPS of ₹27.6 — roughly ₹1,240 — the base case is flat rather than negative.** **At ₹950–1,000 (≈35x FY28), the weighted return turns clearly positive and the bear case stops being the dominant term.** The stock traded at **₹695 within the last twelve months.** *This is a name to hold on a watchlist with a price alert, not a name to buy at the high.*

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

*Written from a starting position of scepticism about the price. Risks 1-9 are what could make the bear case real; Risks 10-12 are what could make me wrong about the price.*

**🔴 Risk 1 — Customer restocking behaviour, not demand, sets the revenue line.** Unimech sells tools to OEMs and MROs who decide when to buy them. **In Q3FY26 they decided not to, and revenue halved in a single quarter with operating margin going to 4.6%.** Management is right that this was not structural — but it was also not forecastable, and it can recur for reasons unrelated to tariffs. **Signal to watch: sequential revenue, and any language about customers moving to "drop-shipment-only."**

**🔴 Risk 2 — US tariff policy is a live, external, binary input.** 18% → 50% → 18% inside twelve months. **The FTWZ genuinely mitigates it and is now operational, which is the strongest counter available** — but it manages the inventory friction, not the underlying landed cost. Management's own posture: *"we remain watchful on any further developments."* **Signal to watch: any change in the India tariff line; whether FTWZ throughput is quantified.**

**🔴 Risk 3 — Roughly half of reported pre-tax profit has been interest income.** FY26 PBT ₹80cr on ₹46cr of other income; TTM ₹93cr on ₹43cr. **The screen has been showing an earnings base that operations did not produce.** ✅ **This is now resolving in the right direction** — the treasury has been deployed into Hobel and other income halved to ₹7cr in Q1FY27 — **but it means FY27's reported growth will be flattered by the swap and FY26's base was flattered by the treasury.** **Signal to watch: PBT excluding other income, quarter by quarter.**

**🔴 Risk 4 — The ₹450cr Hobel price implies a seller's motive nobody has explained.** A debt-free, asset-light business at >50% EBITDA margin and >50% ROCE sold at ~7x EBITDA. **Asked directly why, management did not answer** (§5A). Most of the consideration becomes goodwill, so a disappointment surfaces as an impairment rather than gradually. **Compounding it: two OEM groups are ~93% of Hobel's revenue.** **Signal to watch: the FY27 annual report's related-party note and the subsidiary's filed accounts; Hobel's revenue and margin once three full quarters are consolidated.**

**🔴 Risk 5 — The order book is thin and going the wrong way.** **₹280cr consolidated at 30-Jun-2026, down from ₹314cr in May.** Against TTM revenue of ₹285cr that is roughly 1.0x coverage — in a business with multi-quarter production cycles and, on management's own account, long customer acceptance before billing. Management attributes the decline to strong execution and customer pull-ins, which is plausible. **It is still a declining number.** **Signal to watch: order book at Q2FY27 — a second consecutive decline would be a genuine problem.**

**🔴 Risk 6 — The ₹1,000cr FY29 target has silently disappeared, and it is what the multiple is paying for.** Nine mentions in Nov-2025; **zero across the last three calls.** Never withdrawn, never restated. **Signal to watch: whether it is reinstated, formally revised, or explicitly abandoned. Any of the three would be more useful than the current silence — and a reinstatement with a number attached would be a real positive.**

**⚠️ Risk 7 — Working capital is guided to deteriorate materially.** **130 days now, "160 days plus by end of this year"** on management's own guidance, as long-cycle aerospace and nuclear work requires inventory ahead of billing. Cash conversion has been the best thing on this balance sheet (CFO/OP 102%). **Management is telling you it gets worse.** **Signal to watch: CFO and CFO/OP at H1FY27.**

**⚠️ Risk 8 — Gross block is guided to roughly double in FY27, largely on a JV with no revenue.** The Saudi Kanoo JV takes a **USD 10m infusion** and is not yet operational. Meanwhile depreciation is already +136% YoY in FY26 and ROCE is 11.2%. **Doubling the asset base before the last expansion has been filled — utilisation is 58% — is how a good margin becomes a mediocre return.** **Signal to watch: ROCE trajectory; whether the JV produces revenue in FY27 as implied.**

**⚠️ Risk 9 — Guidance framing has consistently favoured the flattering basis** (§3.5): a 35% margin floor replaced then described as *"broadly in line"*; a revenue commitment declared met on a gross-of-concessions basis the statements do not use; a long-range target that stopped being mentioned. **None is improper; the direction is consistent.** **Signal to watch: whether any future commitment is reported on the same basis it was made.**

**⚠️ Risk 10 — Domestic institutions have been selling into strength.** DII **7.76% → 5.60%** over six quarters, falling in five of them, including quarters when the stock doubled. FII 0.43%. **A ₹7,592cr company that professional investors are steadily leaving is either mispriced or correctly priced by people who know more.** **Signal to watch: any DII stabilising or adding.**

**⚠️ Risk 11 — Free float is ~20% and there is no analyst community around the name.** **No dedicated ValuePickr thread exists** (§9). A ~₹1,530cr float with thin independent scrutiny supports high multiples on the way up and offers no support on the way down.

**🟢 Risk 12 — the risk that I am wrong about the price, and it deserves equal billing.** This is a **65%-gross-margin, 36%-EBITDA, 102%-cash-conversion business at 58% capacity utilisation**, with a promoter group that has not sold a share, in a sector with a genuine decade-long tailwind, that has just signed its first recurring flying-parts contract with a European Tier-1 and bought a >50%-margin business at 7x. **If the flying-parts thread converts — the engine Tier-1 discussion described as "at an advanced stage" — the revenue model changes from lumpy tooling to recurring content, and the multiple I am calling absurd becomes merely expensive.** **Indian aerospace has re-rated as a category and has stayed re-rated for two years. Being early and right is indistinguishable from being wrong for a long time.** **Signal to watch: a second and third long-term supply agreement. That, more than any margin print, is what would change this profile's conclusion.**
