## 1. Business in Plain English

If you've ever opened up an aircraft, a missile guidance box, a semiconductor test rig, or a satellite bus, you'll find a chaotic-looking web of wires, connectors, ribbons, and cable trays running between subsystems. That's the **electrical wiring and interconnect system (EWIS)** — the nervous system of every complex machine. **Rossell Techsys makes these EWIS assemblies** for high-reliability applications: fighter jets, commercial aircraft, satellites, semiconductor manufacturing equipment, and industrial-grade platforms.

Think of it as **the Foxconn of high-reliability electronics — but for defence + aerospace, not consumer**. Rossell doesn't design the aircraft or satellite. It's a Tier-1 or Tier-2 contract manufacturer that takes the design + component specs from an OEM (Boeing, Lockheed, Raytheon, Israel Defence, semiconductor equipment makers) and produces the physical wire harness assemblies to exact tolerance, delivering to global supply chains.

The 30-second business snapshot:
- **Headquartered in Bengaluru** — 225,000 sqft LEED Gold-certified facility (with a second 210,000 sqft facility being leased from April 2026)
- **Wholly-owned US subsidiary** — Rossell Techsys Inc (RTI) in Tempe, Arizona — for customer engagement + light manufacturing
- **~1,200 employees** (up from 680 two years ago)
- **Demerged from Rossell India Ltd in December 2024** and independently listed — this is a young standalone entity (~18 months) even though the operating business is 15+ years old
- **FY26 revenue INR 485 cr (+87% YoY)** — one of the sharpest scale-ups in Indian defence tier-2 space this year

**Business by segment (FY27 target mix, per Q4FY26 concall):**
1. **Aerospace & Defence** (~50% of FY27 target revenue) — wire harness for Boeing T-7, F/A-18, C-17; defence programs for Israeli customers + Indian defence PSUs
2. **Semiconductor** (rapidly rising) — interconnect assemblies for semiconductor equipment (etch, deposition, litho); Applied Materials, ASML supply chain
3. **Space** (rapidly rising) — INR 400 cr multi-year contract secured FY26 for a landmark space program; delivered first large production batches
4. **MRO (Maintenance, Repair, Overhaul)** — new segment, DPL license + AS9110 certification received FY26; commercialisation in FY27

Rossell's story is essentially **"aerospace + defence + semi + space, all riding secular tailwinds, run by a hungry newly-listed management pushing hard on scale."** Whether that pace is sustainable — or is a set-up for a stumble — is the central question.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

Four durable forces are lifting Rossell's addressable market:

**Force 1 — Commercial aerospace production ramp.** Boeing and Airbus have combined backlog of ~14,000 aircraft representing $2 trillion+ in future production. Post-Covid ramp-up back to 60+ deliveries/month (Boeing) and 70+ (Airbus) is happening through FY27–FY29. Every aircraft needs 60–80 km of wiring; every wire harness assembly is worth $50K–$500K depending on complexity. India + Mexico are the two dominant "China+1" sources for outsourced wire harness. Rossell is one of only 3 Indian companies at the required certification level.

**Force 2 — Defence spend expansion.** NATO commitments of 3% of GDP to defence, Middle East at 5%, Asia at 2.5% by 2030. Fighter jet, missile, radar, and drone platforms all need EWIS. Post-Ukraine, defence OEMs are aggressively derisking supply chains — moving low-labour-content assembly to Indian partners. Rossell is a preferred vendor for multiple defence programs already.

**Force 3 — Semiconductor capex boom.** Global semi capex went from $130B (2020) to $190B (2024) and projected $230B+ by 2027 (per SEMI). Every semi equipment tool needs high-purity interconnects, sensor cables, and precision harnesses. Rossell's semi segment scaled from ~zero (FY25) to INR 10+ cr in Q3FY26 alone, and management guides 300–400% growth in FY27. This is a genuine breakout adjacency.

**Force 4 — Space commercialisation.** Global commercial + defence space revenue projected to grow from $400B (2023) to $1T+ (2030). Satellites, launch vehicles, ground stations — all need specialised interconnect assemblies certified to space-grade specifications. Rossell secured a landmark INR 400 cr multi-year space contract in FY26; first large production batches delivered.

**Where Rossell sits in the map.** It's a **tier-2 EMS/contract manufacturer** in high-reliability niches. Peers:
- **Dixon Technologies, Amber Enterprises** — consumer EMS, different economics
- **Data Patterns, Astra Microwave** — full-system defence electronics, different capability
- **Cyient, Tata Elxsi** — engineering services, not manufacturing
- **KPIT, L&T Technology Services** — software + ER&D services

Closest peers globally: TE Connectivity (much larger), Amphenol, Belden. Rossell is niche but at a *stage* where 2–3 years of 60–80% growth are structurally possible if execution holds.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Boeing + Fortune defence OEM relationships.** From Q4FY26 concall (Rishab Gupta): *"Boeing not only appreciated our efforts but also agreed to level load future schedules, a powerful endorsement."* Rossell has **US$ 200 million strategic agreements on Boeing T-7 program** alone — meaning multi-year visibility from one customer. Getting into Boeing's approved vendor list takes 5–7 years and requires 4 different certifications; new entrants can't threaten Rossell in the short-medium term.

**Moat 2 — Certification stack.** AS9100, AS9110 (for MRO), CMMC 2.0 (for US defence supply chain), DPL license (Indian defence), ISO 27001 (security). This certification portfolio is rare in Indian EMS — it took Rossell 8+ years to accumulate. New Indian entrants face 5–7 year certification lag.

**Moat 3 — Semiconductor equipment breakthrough.** Rossell qualified with a major semiconductor equipment customer in Q2FY26, ramped to INR 10+ cr in Q3FY26 (first quarter after qualification). Onboarding a second overseas semi customer through FY27. Semi customers demand extreme process control — once qualified, switching cost is high.

**Moat 4 — Space program qualification.** From "qualification to volume-ready" during FY26, with the first large production batches delivered. Rossell is one of only 2–3 Indian EMS with space-grade capability certified by multiple customers.

**Moat 5 — Bengaluru location + US subsidiary.** Bengaluru gives access to skilled electronics workforce (aerospace ecosystem includes HAL, BEL, ISRO). US subsidiary (RTI, Arizona) gives customer proximity + inventory hedging + US Government contract eligibility.

**Moat 6 — MRO license just received.** DPL + AS9110 certifications formally unlock Indian MRO market — an entirely new revenue stream not present in FY26 numbers. Management guides significantly higher margins from MRO (25%+ vs 17–22% on manufacturing).

**Where the moat is weaker.**

## 5. Connecting the Dots

Rossell's story is **rapid capacity + certification stacking** across four adjacent segments:

**Thread A — Aerospace commercial (Boeing T-7 + potential commercial aviation entry).** Boeing T-7 (Advanced Pilot Training) strategic agreement of US$ 200 million represents 5–7 years of visibility. Management guides "commercial aerospace opens by FY28" — potentially transformational because commercial orders are 5-10x larger than defence.

**Thread B — Defence (US + Israel + India + other allies).** Multiple defence customers globally. Duty-free entry into US via US subsidiary + trade preferences. MRO license opens Indian defence maintenance market. Boeing + Israeli + Indian defence together drive Thread A + Thread B growth of 40–60% CAGR through FY28.

**Thread C — Semiconductor (rapid expansion).** From 0 to INR 10+ cr/quarter in 1 quarter post-qualification. Guides 300–400% growth in FY27. Second semi customer onboarding. If this compounds at guided pace, semi could be INR 100+ cr by FY28.

**Thread D — Space (landmark contract executing).** INR 400 cr multi-year contract secured FY26. First large production batches delivered. Additional space customers onboarding. Guides 300–400% growth in FY27.

**Thread E — MRO (services with high margin).** DPL + AS9110 licenses in place. Foundation-stage; but management flags MRO margins are "much higher" than manufacturing (implying 25%+ vs 17–22%).

**Where threads reinforce.** All four production threads (A/B/C/D) use similar wire-harness + interconnect capability. Certifications for one open doors for others. MRO (Thread E) uses the same certifications + customer relationships.

**Where they don't compound.** Different customer sales cycles. Semi customers demand different quality control specs than aerospace. Space demands radiation-hardened materials. Different tooling. Each thread requires dedicated capex + certifications — meaning capital is spread across four segments.
