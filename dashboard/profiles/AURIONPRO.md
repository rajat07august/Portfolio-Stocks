## 1. Business in Plain English

Aurionpro sells **the software and the physical systems that mission-critical institutions run on** — and it does so across four businesses that share a customer type rather than a product.

Think of it as a **specialist contractor to institutions that cannot afford downtime**: banks that need trade-finance and transaction-banking platforms, metro systems that need the gates and fare-collection engines that let 8 million people through a turnstile each day, and — newest and fastest-growing — data-centre operators building India's AI compute. The common thread is that in all three, being cheap matters less than being trusted, and the sales cycle is measured in years.

The four legs, as management now groups them:

| Business | What it actually sells | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| **Banking** | Core lending, transaction banking, trade finance — now being rebuilt as an **AI-native ("agentic") stack** under AurionAI | The historic core. Currently **absorbing capacity into R&D rather than converting it into revenue** — management's own words |
| **Transit (TIG)** | Automated fare collection, gates, tolling, traffic management | India leadership; **Mumbai Metro Line 5 (₹50cr, Jul-2026) and Delhi Metro** won in the quarter |
| **Data Centre** | Design, build and manage high-availability DC infrastructure; part of TIG | **~a third of TIG, which is ~45% of the enterprise.** Grew 40–50% CAGR for 4–5 years; management expects *above* that band now |
| **Enterprise AI** | Arya.ai and Lexsi Labs — models and agents tuned for BFSI | *"Moving from experimentation to production"* per Q1FY27 |

**The 30-second snapshot:**
- **Group CEO & Vice-Chairman:** Ashish Rai (joined 2022; runs the AI strategy personally) · **CFO:** Vipul Parmar · **Promoter:** Amit Sheth family, **26.86%**
- **FY26 revenue ₹1,411 cr (+20.2%)** — against a 30% plan · **EBITDA 20.02%** · **PAT margin 15.02%** — both at the floor of their guided bands
- **Q1FY27 revenue ₹358 cr (+6.2% YoY)** — the slowest growth in over three years · **OPM 17.0%**, below the guided 20% floor · **PAT ₹45 cr (−11.8%)**
- **Order book ₹1,800 cr+** entering FY27, reaffirmed — **the problem is conversion, not demand**
- **Stock ₹696 — down ~49% from its ₹1,370 high, and 2.4% above its 52-week low of ₹680**

**Big picture in one sentence.** Aurionpro is a **credible, IP-owning, four-legged enterprise-tech franchise going through a genuine architectural rebuild — priced at 18.3x while it does so** — but it has now missed its own revenue plan for a full year, breached its own margin band, and responded by **withdrawing guidance altogether**; the investable question is no longer whether the strategy is right, but whether this management can convert an order book it keeps winning.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Banking software is being rewritten, not upgraded.** The last generation digitised workflows. The next one, as Rai puts it, must *"reason over context, coordinate work through agents, act in real time."* For institutions whose systems of record are becoming systems of *action*, domain depth becomes more valuable, not less — which is the whole bet behind spending 10.5–11% of revenue on R&D.

**Force 2 — India's urban transit build-out is a decade-long public-capex cycle.** Metro networks in Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai, Bangalore and a dozen tier-2 cities all need fare collection, gates and traffic management. This is procurement-driven, slow, and very hard to dislodge an incumbent from once installed.

**Force 3 — the AI infrastructure super-cycle.** India is building sovereign AI compute from a low base. Rai's framing on the Q1FY27 call: *"This is not an ordinary cycle. This is a massive infrastructure super cycle that this country has never seen."* Power and data-residency rules make it a domestic-delivery game.

**Force 4 — Middle East BFSI capex, cyclically broken.** MEA was Aurionpro's growth engine into FY25 and has been disrupted by conflict since. Management's own retrospective: *"We became too dependent on Middle East for growth on the software side, at least that we have pivoted away from."*

**Where Aurionpro sits.** A ₹3,800cr diversified mid-cap. Peers by multiple rather than by business: Persistent, Coforge, Tata Elxsi, Newgen, Intellect Design (BFSI product), and — for the DC leg — a very different comp set entirely. **No listed peer maps cleanly onto all four legs, which is both the differentiation and the reason the market struggles to price it.**

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Four legs with genuinely different economic profiles, under one balance sheet.** Rai is explicit that this is deliberate: *"different businesses have very different economic profiles. At the enterprise level, it nets to a certain number, it does not mean every business in the enterprise is doing the same number."* Software carries high margin and high R&D; DC carries lower margin but **consumes almost no capital**, so returns on capital are high.

**Moat 2 — Owned IP, not billed hours.** Cumulative product-and-platform investment of **₹470–530 cr** (Investor Day deck). This is a product company that reports like a services company, which is why R&D expensing depresses reported EBITDA — a point management makes repeatedly: *"We do expense a lot of R&D. So, that does tend to mask the actual real EBITDA in the business."*

**Moat 3 — Incumbency in Indian metro fare collection.** Q1FY27 alone brought **Mumbai Metro (MMRDA, Line 5, ~₹50cr)** and **Delhi Metro** mandates. Gates and AFC are replacement-hostile once installed.

**Moat 4 — A real, executing data-centre business.** 40–50% revenue CAGR over the past 4–5 years; Uptime Institute-grade builds; AWS/RedHat/Zabbix partnerships. **Q1FY27 started execution on its largest-ever DC project; a further ~₹100cr hyperscale-operator win landed 13-Aug-2026.**

**Moat 5 — Sustained FII accumulation.** FII holding went **2.90% (2023) → 16.02% (Jun-2026)**. Institutional money did the diligence and stayed through the drawdown (16.29% → 16.02% is noise, not an exit).

**Moat 6 — Clean promoter encumbrance record.** The Reg 31(4) declaration filed 29-Jun-2026 states that during FY2025-26 the promoters **created no encumbrance** on their shares. *(Verified from the filing itself, not from a ratio screen — see §4A of the standing brief.)*

**Moat 7 — Candour.** Rai flags his own unreliability, unprompted: *"I have made the same statement about accelerating in second half for the last several years but last year it wasn't true, so we need to remain watchful."* That is a rare sentence on an Indian earnings call and it should be credited.

**Where the moat is weaker — and this list has grown.**
- **Guidance has been withdrawn.** A company that guided 20–21% EBITDA and 15–16% PAT now declines to give a range on either revenue or margin (§3.5).
- **Conversion, not demand, is the binding constraint** — and it has bound for four consecutive quarters.
- **Cash conversion has broken down.** FY26 CFO **₹56cr vs ₹157cr** (−64%); FY26 FCF **−₹114cr**.
- **Promoter at 26.86%** — below 30%, and diluted 31.19% → 26.88% across 2023-24.
- **DII at 1.47%.** Almost no domestic institution owns this. Five years of FII buying has not been matched by a single meaningful domestic fund.
- **A SEBI PIT show-cause notice (29-May-2026) remains unresolved** — and, notably, **was not raised by a single analyst on either of the two calls since** (§9).
- **Public holding 55.65% across 55,618 shareholders**, up from 31,827 five quarters ago — retail has been *buying the fall*, which raises the price of a further disappointment.
- **Growth has been substantially inorganic.** The historical ~32-33% CAGR that underwrites the long-range aspiration was built on a run of acquisitions.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — the R&D build is the margin.** Aurionpro is expensing 10.5–11% of revenue rebuilding its banking stack for agentic AI. Rai will not cut it to rescue a quarter. That single decision explains the 300bp OPM compression, the −₹114cr FCF, and the withdrawn guidance, all at once. **Everything else in this profile is downstream of it.**

**Thread B — capacity is the bottleneck, and it is the same capacity.** The people building the AI-native stack are the people who would otherwise be converting the order book. Rai: *"as the build outs mature, we will shift capacity towards implementation, accelerating the revenue conversion for banking business."* This is why demand and revenue have decoupled — and why the acceleration, if it comes, should come as a step rather than a drift.

**Thread C — the data-centre business changes the shape of the company.** ~15% of revenue, growing 40–50%+, **lower margin but near-zero capital**, so it dilutes the OPM optic while raising return on capital. Rai's defence is explicit: *"even if the margin is lower, the volume will more than make up for it… it consumes almost no capital. So, the return on capital is exceptionally high."* **A reader who judges this company on OPM alone will misread a mix shift as a deterioration.**

**Thread D — MEA dependence has been unwound, at the cost of the growth it provided.** The pivot to US, SE Asia and Europe is real and disclosed. The $33M US fintech engagement is the proof point. But new geographies are slower to convert than an entrenched one, which is part of why FY27 opens flat.

**Thread E — transit keeps winning while everything else waits.** Mumbai Metro Line 5 (₹50cr), Delhi Metro, and a rare Q1 transaction-banking mandate. **Order intake is not the problem in any leg.**

**Thread F — cash conversion is where the threads either resolve or fail.** If A and B are true, CFO/OP returns toward 70-80% in FY27-28 as capitalised build turns into billed implementation. If they are excuses, it does not. **There is no third outcome, and it is observable within four quarters.**
