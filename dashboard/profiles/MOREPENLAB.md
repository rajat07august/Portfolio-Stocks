## 1. Business in Plain English

**Morepen makes the active ingredient inside other people's medicines, and it makes the device your diabetic relative uses at home.** Two businesses, roughly 73% / 27% by revenue as management described it in May-2025:

**1. Pharma (~73%).** At its core an **API** business — the actual chemical that does the work in a drug, sold in bulk to formulators worldwide. Forty years of it, **exports to more than 90 countries, over 1,000 API customers.** On top sit prescription drugs and an OTC consumer range.

**2. Medical Devices (~27%).** The **Dr Morepen** home-diagnostics business — blood glucose meters, blood pressure monitors, and the consumable test strips that follow. **A reported installed base of 20 million glucometers and an annual scale of 500 million strips**, sold through roughly **3.5 lakh retail touchpoints.** *That is a razor-and-blades annuity attached to a chronic-care population that only grows.*

**🆕 And a third thing, which is why the stock has doubled: CDMO.**

**Contract Development and Manufacturing** — where instead of selling a commodity API into a spot market, you become the dedicated manufacturer for a specific customer's specific molecule under a long-duration contract. **Morepen has an ₹825cr CDMO mandate that entered full-scale commercial execution in the June 2026 quarter, with ₹58cr already dispatched.**

**Management's framing, which they call "Morepen 2.0":** *"moving from traditional commodity APIs toward innovation-led manufacturing, long-duration CDMO partnerships, recurring customer programs and a scalable operating platform."*

**The 30-second snapshot:**

- **Q1FY27: revenue ₹575.31cr (+34%) · EBITDA ₹87.72cr (+207%) at 15.25% vs 6.65% · PAT ₹56.35cr (+394%)** — highest-ever quarter
- **🟢 ₹825cr CDMO mandate in full-scale commercialisation; ₹58cr dispatched in Q1**
- **🟢 Four consecutive USFDA inspections with Nil Form 483 observations**
- **🔴 But FY26 was a bad year: revenue ₹1,806cr (flat), PAT ₹96cr (−19%), OPM 10% → 7%, ROCE 15% → 8%**
- **🔴 Free cash flow negative in five of the last six years: −₹127cr in FY26 after −₹182cr in FY25**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — 🟢 The CDMO shift is the single biggest structural story in Indian pharma.** Global innovators are moving manufacturing out of China and away from in-house capacity. **A CDMO contract is worth far more than a commodity API sale: it is long-duration, single-source, priced on capability rather than spot, and it comes with switching costs measured in years of requalification.**

**Force 2 — 🟢 And the gate to it is regulatory, which is where Morepen's real asset sits.** **Four consecutive USFDA inspections with Nil Form 483 observations** is the credential that lets a mid-size Indian API maker be considered for a global mandate at all. *You cannot buy this, and one bad inspection removes it.*

**Force 3 — Commodity APIs are a cyclical, price-taking business.** Morepen's own numbers show it: **operating margin 12% → 6% → 9% → 10% → 7% across FY15–FY26.** *This is the business the company is explicitly trying to grow out of.*

**Force 4 — 🟢 Chronic-care home diagnostics is a demographic annuity.** India's diabetic population is among the world's largest and growing. **An installed base of 20 million glucometers generates 500 million strips a year regardless of what the API cycle is doing** — and the future opportunity management names is **CGM (continuous glucose monitoring)**, a higher-value category.

**Force 5 — Vertical integration across pharma and devices is unusual.** Very few Indian companies sit in both bulk chemistry and consumer medical devices with a national retail footprint.

**Where MOREPEN sits.** ₹5,682cr in **Sector 12 (Pharma & Excipients)**, joining **CONCORDBIO**. *It is the largest healthcare name on the watchlist by revenue.*

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — 🟢 The USFDA record, and it is the most valuable thing the company owns.** **Four consecutive inspections with Nil Form 483 observations.** *For a CDMO customer choosing where to place a multi-year mandate, this is the first filter and most Indian mid-caps fail it.*

**Moat 2 — 🟢 The ₹825cr CDMO mandate is real and is now shipping.** **₹58cr of commercial dispatches completed in Q1FY27.** *An announced order is a hope; a dispatched order is a fact, and this one has crossed from the first to the second.*

**Moat 3 — 🟢 The medical devices annuity is genuinely hard to replicate.** **20 million installed glucometers and 3.5 lakh retail touchpoints** took twenty-five years to build. **The strips are the business** — the meter is close to a customer-acquisition cost — and it grew 19% in Q1FY27 while the API cycle was recovering.

**Moat 4 — Forty years and 1,000+ API customers across 90+ countries.** *Long-tail customer relationships are what let a commodity business survive its own margin cycle.*

**Moat 5 — Capacity is being built for the strategy, not after it.** **Roadmap to 1,200 KL by FY30**, phased. Fixed assets have already gone ₹307cr → ₹489cr → ₹513cr across FY24–FY26.

**Moat 6 — Vertical breadth gives two independent cycles.** *In FY26 the API business had a poor year and devices carried; in Q1FY27 both worked at once.*

### Where the moat is weaker — and there is a lot here

- **🔴 The disclosure gap.** **No earnings call in fifteen months at a ₹5,682cr company with 4.35 lakh shareholders.** *This is the single most important negative in the profile and it has nothing to do with the operations.*
- **🔴 Long-run returns are mediocre.** **ROE: 10-year 12%, 5-year 10%, 3-year 10%, last year 6.20%. ROCE 8.05%.** **Five-year profit CAGR is −5%.**
- **🔴 The cash has never followed the profit.** **Free cash flow: −₹127cr (FY22), −₹137cr (FY23), −₹8cr (FY24), −₹182cr (FY25), −₹127cr (FY26).** **CFO/OP 46% in FY26, 40% in FY25, and negative in FY22–23.**
- **🔴 Working capital has deteriorated for six straight years.** **Working-capital days 17 → 55 → 98 → 94 → 107 → 122.**
- **🔴 Debt is climbing fast.** **Borrowings ₹29cr → ₹105cr → ₹208cr across FY24–FY26**, +617% in two years.
- **⚠️ Promoter holding is only 35.66%**, down from 38.22%, and **institutions hold 2.74% between them.** *The register is 61.59% retail.*
- **⚠️ One mandate is doing the heavy lifting.** **₹825cr against FY26 revenue of ₹1,806cr.** *Concentration is the price of the CDMO model, and nobody has been able to ask who the customer is.*
- **⚠️ The medical devices business is being hived off** into a subsidiary by slump sale — a **material related-party transaction** whose timeline has already needed extending. *(§3.5.)*

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — 🟢 The CDMO pivot is real, and it has crossed from announcement into dispatch.** **₹58cr shipped against an ₹825cr mandate**, with the USFDA record that made it possible extended to a fourth clean inspection. *This is not a press-release strategy.*

**Thread B — 🟢 And the operating leverage underneath it is genuine.** **860 basis points of margin in one year on 34% revenue growth.** *A business with years of underutilised fixed capacity converts incremental volume at very high incremental margin — that is exactly what happened.*

**Thread C — 🔴 But it is a recovery from a bad year, not a continuation.** **FY26 revenue was flat and PAT fell 19%; ROCE halved to 8%.** *Every FY27 year-on-year comparison will look spectacular partly because FY26 was weak. The five-year profit CAGR is −5%.*

**Thread D — 🔴 The cash has never followed the profit, and that predates the CDMO story.** **Five negative FCF years in six; working-capital days 17 → 122 over six years; borrowings up 617% in two.** *A CDMO ramp consumes working capital by design — but this pattern was in place four years before the mandate existed.*

**Thread E — 🔴 One mandate is doing the work.** **₹825cr against ₹1,806cr of annual revenue.** *Customer, duration, margin and firmness are all undisclosed.*

**Thread F — 🔴 And the forum in which any of that could be tested has been closed for fifteen months.** ***The CDMO word does not appear once in the last transcript. Everything an investor knows about this transformation comes from documents the company wrote and nobody questioned.***

**Thread G — 🟡 The best asset in the group is being moved.** **The 20-million-glucometer devices annuity is being slump-sold into Morepen Medipath, a material related-party transaction whose timeline has already slipped and whose subsidiary has separately taken a JV partner.**

**Thread H — The register is retail and thinning.** **Promoter 35.66%, institutions 2.74%, public 61.59% — and the shareholder count has fallen from 4.69 lakh to 4.35 lakh while the stock doubled.**

**How they interact.** ***A genuine and well-earned operating inflection — won on a regulatory record that takes decades to build — is being reported by a company that has stopped answering questions, funded by borrowings on a cash-flow record that has been poor for six years, and priced at 48 times earnings after a 123% run.*** **The business deserves the attention it is getting. The disclosure does not deserve the benefit of the doubt that a 48x multiple implies.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — 🔴 It may be a CMO, not a CDMO — and the multiple depends on which.** ***This is the sharpest challenge to the thesis and it comes from ValuePickr, not from any analyst.*** **A CDMO does development *and* manufacturing: process chemistry, route optimisation, scale-up — sticky, high-margin, hard to move. A CMO just manufactures someone else's defined process, and is far more replaceable.** **The peer multiples in §6 — Laurus at 92x, Divi's at 84x, Neuland at 60x — are CDMO multiples.** *If Morepen's ₹825cr mandate is contract manufacturing of a defined molecule, the correct comparison set is cheaper and the re-rating is partly mislabelled. **The company uses "CDMO" throughout; nobody has been able to ask it to define the development content.***

**Risk 2 — 🔴 Fifteen months without an earnings call.** *Every number in §4 is self-reported and unchallenged, at a ₹5,682cr company with 4.35 lakh mostly-retail shareholders and 2.74% institutional ownership.*
**⚠️ This is the Under Watch trigger.**

**Risk 3 — 🔴 Single-mandate concentration.** **₹825cr against ₹1,806cr of annual revenue, with the customer, duration, firmness and margin all undisclosed.** *₹58cr has shipped. The other ₹767cr is a contract nobody outside the company has seen.*

**Risk 4 — 🔴 Cash conversion has been poor for six years and predates the story.** **FCF negative in five of six years; CFO/OP 46% at best recently; working-capital days 17 → 122; borrowings ₹29cr → ₹208cr in two years.** *A CDMO ramp consumes working capital — but this began in FY22.*

**Risk 5 — 🔴 Q1FY27's margin may be a ramp artefact.** **15.25% against a five-year average nearer 9% and an FY26 of 7%.** *Initial CDMO dispatches can carry unusually favourable economics; the run-rate is unproven at one quarter.*

**Risk 6 — 🟡 API is a price-taking cycle and it has bitten twice recently.** **OPM 9% (FY22) → 6% (FY23) → 9% → 10% → 7% (FY26).** *FY26 was a bad year for reasons that have not gone away.*

**Risk 7 — 🟡 The best asset is being moved.** **The devices annuity is being slump-sold into Morepen Medipath as a material related-party transaction, with the timeline already extended once and a JV partner (Bimedical FZE) already signed into the same subsidiary.** *Consideration and valuation undisclosed.*

**Risk 8 — 🟡 A 35.66% promoter and a 61.59% retail float.** *Institutions have sold every period for two years, down to 2.74% combined; the shareholder count has fallen from 4.69 lakh to 4.35 lakh while the stock doubled.*

**Risk 9 — 🟡 One inspection ends the moat.** **The entire CDMO candidacy rests on four consecutive Nil-483 USFDA outcomes.** *A single adverse inspection would not just cost a quarter — it would remove the qualification that makes Morepen biddable for global mandates at all.*

**Risk 10 — 🟢 What would genuinely surprise on the upside.** **The 1,200 KL roadmap is being built for more than one mandate.** *If the USFDA record converts a second and third customer, the operating leverage demonstrated in Q1FY27 repeats on a bigger base — and that is the bull case.*
