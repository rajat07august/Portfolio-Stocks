## 1. Business in Plain English

Garware Hi-Tech Films makes **the film you look through without noticing** — the tinted layer on a car window, the near-invisible urethane skin protecting a bonnet from stone chips, the coating on an office window that keeps heat out and light in.

Two product families do most of the work:

| Family | What it is | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| **Sun Control Film (SCF)** | Automotive and architectural window film — heat rejection, glare, privacy, safety | ~50% of revenue; the older, larger franchise |
| **Paint Protection Film (PPF)** | Transparent TPU film applied over automotive paint; self-healing, high-value | The growth engine; **new line at ~60% utilisation, old line at ~100%** |

**The useful analogy is a speciality chemicals company that sells through detailers.** The moat is in the coating chemistry and the extrusion, but the customer relationship increasingly runs through **branded application studios** — a deliberate move from B2B private label toward a consumer-facing brand.

**The 30-second snapshot:**
- **Mumbai (Vile Parle), incorporated 1957** · **CIN L10889MH1957PLC010889**

- **FY26 revenue ₹2,120 cr — essentially FLAT on FY25's ₹2,109cr.** PAT ₹338cr, +2%

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Cars are getting more expensive to repaint.** PPF converts a ₹1-2 lakh respray risk into a ₹50,000 consumable. **As the Indian car parc premiumises, the attach rate rises structurally.**

**Force 2 — Heat is a building-code problem.** Architectural sun control cuts cooling load; retrofit demand grows with electricity prices and glazed façades. Management cites architectural SCF growing **25–30%**, domestic **35–40%**.

**Force 3 — Backward integration into TPU changes the economics.** PPF's key input is a urethane film currently bought in. **Garware's own TPU line commissions in Q3FY27 and management guides +1.5–2% of EBITDA margin from FY28.**

**Force 4 — 🆕 Trade protection is arriving.** **DGTR has recommended an antidumping duty on TPU-based paint protection film imports from China** — *"an important step towards creating a more level playing field for domestic manufacturers."* **Recommendation, not yet a customs notification.**

**Force 5 — The channel is moving closer to the consumer.** **14 international application studios across the Middle East and US**, a domestic GAS network of 250+, and a Garware Home Solutions studio rollout targeting **50 stores in FY27**.

**Where Garware sits.** ₹16,550cr, in **Sector 11 (Packaging & Materials)** alongside EPL. **No close listed Indian comparable in speciality films** — the peer set is global (3M, Eastman, XPEL) and structurally different.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Coating and extrusion capability that is genuinely scarce.** Multi-layer speciality film with optical and durability specifications is a chemistry-and-process business, not a converting one.

**Moat 2 — 🆕 A brand transition that is actually working.** **55% own-brand / 45% private label**, up from a private-label past. Management: *"our strategy of moving closer to the end customer is progressing well and remains one of the key differentiators of our business model."*

**Moat 3 — Backward integration into TPU, dated and quantified.** Commissions **Q3FY27**, guided **+1.5–2% EBITDA margin in FY28**, with *"three products already in target"* beyond the captive use.

**Moat 4 — Operating leverage that has now been demonstrated, not just promised.** **Revenue +28% produced operating profit +56%** (₹110cr → ₹172cr). *That is the whole FY26 investment thesis arriving in one quarter.*

**Moat 4b — Utilisation headroom across the board.** At the Q4FY26 call: **SCF 75-80%, PPF 85%+** on a 66,000 MT p.a. plant. **Value-added products are 87% of revenue**; the Industrial Product Division (Shrink Film, ~70% of the Indian market) is 26%. *Architectural films were ~₹300cr in FY26 and guided higher.*

**Moat 5 — A fortress balance sheet.** **Debt-free with ₹850cr of cash and liquid investments**, after **₹700cr+ of expansion capex already spent**. Interest is ₹2cr a quarter against ₹172cr of operating profit.

**Moat 6 — Cash conversion that holds up.** CFO/OP: 90%, 117%, 84%, 100%, **83%** across five years; **FCF positive every year**, ₹114cr in FY26 during a heavy capex year.

**Moat 7 — Capacity already committed for the next leg.** A new facility adding **~1,200 lakh sq ft of annual capacity, commercial production H1FY28**; the **SCF line at ₹191cr** supports **₹500–550cr of peak revenue**.

**Moat 8 — Promoter holding rock-steady at 60.73%** for five quarters, with **DII rising 4.89% → 6.35%**.

**Where the moat is weaker.**
- **🔴 FY26 was flat.** Revenue ₹2,109cr → ₹2,120cr, PAT ₹331cr → ₹338cr. **One quarter does not undo a year.**
- **🔴 42.7x and 6.2x book** on a company whose last full year grew 0.5%.
- **🔴 A ₹50cr+ tariff refund lands in Q2 and is not in guidance** — Q2 will look better than the business.
- **⚠️ Management reaffirmed 25% ± 2% rather than raising it** after a 30.3% quarter.
- **⚠️ US exposure is large and tariff-sensitive**; PPF revenue share fell 31% → 25% during the 50% tariff episode.
- **⚠️ Physical logistics bite** — *"we lost some volume last quarter because of some delays in the shipping… ship got stuck in Jebel Ali because of the war."*
- **⚠️ The permanent-CFO question was not asked on the call and remains open.**
- **⚠️ Stock is +166% off its low.**

> **⚠️ THE MARGIN HAS TWO NUMBERS AND THEY DIFFER BY 310bps. State both; never blend.**
> **GHFL's EBITDA definition includes other income.** Q1FY27: operating profit ₹172cr + other income ₹20cr = ₹192cr ÷ ₹633cr = **30.30%**. **Strip the other income — which is treasury yield on an ₹850cr cash pile, not film-making — and the clean operating margin is 27.2%.**
> **The convention is consistent, not a one-quarter presentation choice:** Q1FY26 (₹110 + ₹13 = ₹123cr ✓) and Q4FY26 (₹135 + ₹21 = ₹156 ≈ ₹157cr ✓) both reconcile the same way.
> **🆕 And management's own language now sits on the clean number:** *"27%, 28% seems to be a very logical number for us."* **Screener's OPM for the quarter is 27%.** *Three independent readings agree on 27%; only the press-release headline says 30.3%.*

> **⚠️ And note what the two bases do to the headline.** On the **company definition** the margin beat is **+544bps**; on a **like-for-like operating basis** it is **+500bps**. *Against the guided band, the company-basis reading makes the beat look like ~330bps above the ceiling; on the clean basis it is nearer ~220bps.* **Both are defensible; state which one you are using.**

**The beat is real, not accounting.** There is **no tax credit, no exceptional item, and no other-income rescue** driving the profit line — operating profit rose ₹110cr → ₹172cr on 28% revenue growth. **That is operating leverage doing precisely what management said it would.** And it was tested directly on the call, with a specific answer (§3.5).

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — PPF is the growth engine and it is half-full.** Old line ~100% utilised, new line ~60% after doubling in Sep-2025. **The headroom is already built and paid for.**

**Thread B — TPU backward integration is Thread A's margin.** Commissions **Q3FY27**; guided **+1.5–2% EBITDA in FY28**; three further products already in development. **It turns PPF's key bought-in input into an owned one.**

**Thread C — Trade protection would compound both.** **DGTR has recommended an antidumping duty on Chinese TPU-based PPF.** *A recommendation is not a notification* — but if it lands, it arrives exactly as Garware's own TPU line starts.

**Thread D — The channel is moving toward the consumer.** 55% own-brand vs 45% private label; **14 international studios**; 250+ GAS domestically; **50 GHS stores targeted in FY27** against 9 at the last count. **Higher margin, and materially behind pace.**

**Thread E — The balance sheet is not a constraint on any of it.** Debt-free, ₹850cr of cash, FCF positive five years running, financing outflows for four. **Everything above is self-funded.**

**Thread F — The US is the swing factor in both directions.** It drove the Dec-2025 trough (15% OPM), it is driving the recovery, and it is delivering a **₹50cr+ refund into Q2FY27**. **~75% of revenue is export.**

**How they interact.** **Capacity is built, backward integration lands in two quarters, and protection may arrive alongside it** — three margin tailwinds converging on FY28. **What is not yet proven is that 27% survives a Q3 that management says will dip, and the consumer channel is the one guided metric visibly behind.**
