### 🔴 The screening metric that says the exact opposite of the truth

**Screener shows debtor days improving from 123 (FY25) to 34 (FY26).** On any screen that reads as a dramatic working-capital improvement. **It is the reverse.** Trade receivables genuinely fell ₹118cr → ₹77cr — **because the money moved out of "trade receivables" and into "other current assets," which rose ₹290.6cr.** Nothing was collected; the claim was reclassified from *billed* to *unbilled*.

**Management disclosed the number on the Q1FY27 call, in an exchange with Ashish Khurana:**

> **Ashish Khurana:** *"So, I think on 31st March, 2026, our unbilled revenue was I think around INR234 crores. So, out of that if you can share the figure for 30th June, 2026, if you have it handy?"*
>
> **G. Thiyagu:** *"Yes, sir. As of now as I mentioned, **I'm right now at the Delhi airport**… Maybe once you mail us, we will come back to you, sir."*

**₹234cr of unbilled revenue is 28.5% of FY26 revenue.** The single most important working-capital number in the company was asked for, and the answer was deferred to email. **That is the one thing on this call that should have been answered and was not.**

**The fair reading, both ways.** Unbilled revenue (contract assets) is **entirely normal in EPC** — work is certified as it progresses but billed only at contractual milestones, so a business doubling its revenue will mechanically carry a much larger unbilled balance. Revenue rose 105% in FY26 and the CFO joined a company scaling from ₹399cr to ₹820cr. **But the growth does not fully explain it:** revenue rose 2.05x while other current assets rose 7.0x. And the company is financing the gap with **equity (₹90cr) and short-term bank debt (₹81cr)**, not operations. **Until unbilled converts to cash, the reported PAT is an accounting fact rather than an economic one** — and that, far more than SME illiquidity, is why this trades at 7.9x.

**Bank lines are close to fully drawn.** On the call, Thiyagu: *"long term overall around INR250 crores overall bankers we have got the sanction and out of that 70-30, 30% for bank guarantee and 70% for working capital limit. And at this juncture, we have almost utilized 70% of the money being taken for the cash flows"* — CFO P. Vijayakumar adding *"Around 180, sir."* **Roughly ₹180cr of a ₹250cr sanction is drawn.** Receivables at 30-Jun-2026 were given as **~₹99cr including retention, ~₹60cr excluding**. **To fund 70% growth in FY27 the company needs either the unbilled balance to convert, or more capital.** Management named *"strengthening our working capital management"* as a stated priority for the rest of the year — an acknowledgement worth crediting.

## 🔴 The NCLT proceedings against the MD

**This is the item that moves conviction, and it deserves precise, unembellished statement.** Everything below is from the company's own Regulation 30 disclosures of **13-Jun-2026** and **10-Jul-2026**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Order** | NCLT, Division Bench-I, Chennai, **dated 09-Jun-2026**, in **CP(IB)/43(CHE)/2024** |
| **Subject** | **Initiation of insolvency proceedings against Mr. G. Thiyagu**, Chairman, MD and Promoter, **in his capacity as a Personal Guarantor** |
| **Underlying obligation** | Credit facilities availed by an **unrelated entity in January 2014** |
| **Amount claimed** | **₹3,65,28,644** (₹3.65cr) plus further interest |
| **Appeal** | Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins.) No.307 of 2026 before **NCLAT Chennai**, on grounds *inter alia* of **limitation**, with I.A. No.871 of 2026 seeking stay |
| **Status** | **NCLAT order dated 08-Jul-2026 kept the NCLT order "in abeyance" until the next hearing**; respondents to file counter affidavits within three weeks |
| **Company's position** | *"Neither the Company nor any of its other promoters/Directors/KMPs are party to the proceedings"*; the matter *"pertains exclusively to the personal obligations of Mr. G Thiyagu and are not connected with the affairs, operations, assets, liabilities or financial position of the Company"*; *"The Company has not undertaken any guarantee, security or financial commitment"*; the Board, on legal advice, considers there is **"no material impact on company's business operations, financial performance, assets, contracts, customers, employees or ongoing projects."** |

**What I am not saying.** I make no judgement about Mr. Thiyagu's conduct. A twelve-year-old personal guarantee for a third party, contested on limitation grounds and currently stayed by the appellate tribunal, is a long way from a finding against him. The company's disclosure was prompt, detailed and unprompted, which is to its credit — and the ₹3.65cr claim is trivial beside a ₹814cr market capitalisation.

**What I am saying.** **v1 rated this High and wrote that one-person management was "the singular caveat… upside far exceeds risk."** SSEGL is a company where one individual is simultaneously **Promoter, Chairman, Managing Director and CEO**. The specific mechanism by which that concentration becomes a shareholder problem is any event that impairs that individual's ability to serve — and an admitted personal insolvency proceeding is exactly such an event, because commencement of a personal insolvency resolution process carries **director-disqualification consequences under the Companies Act**. The order is stayed, not vacated; the appeal is undecided; **the ₹3.65cr is not the risk — the status is.**

**Watch:** the next NCLAT hearing date and outcome, and whether the company discloses a succession or delegation arrangement. **This is now the single largest binary in the thesis.**

## 🏭 PEB backward integration — the margin lever, still ahead

- **Factory building inauguration: 30-Aug-2026** (the company's annual day). **Commercial production within 60 working days thereafter**, subject to commissioning, trial runs and statutory approvals — so **realistically Nov–Dec 2026.**
- **Today there is no owned PEB capacity.** Thiyagu: *"we don't have own facility. As of now, we just taken over two-three facilities under job order format where we deliver the steel and they do processing and job labor payment we are paying them."*
- **Expected margin benefit: "1%-plus or 1.5%"** on a current ~10% net margin — *"more than profit, it's our own facility where we are getting faster in construction."*
- **Third-party PEB revenue should not be modelled yet:** *"our own requirements are huge, so obviously… we are unable to."*
- **Long-term vision unchanged: 5-6 PEB facilities across India, Odisha next.**

**🟢 Class 1A PWD registration — obtained.** v1's pending watch item, now delivered: *"Our recently obtained Class 1A PWD registration further enhances our eligibility to participate in larger government projects and infrastructure projects too."* **No government win under it has been announced yet** — eligibility, not revenue.

**🟢 CRISIL BBB+/Positive + A2 on ₹200cr facilities.** First-ever rating. **The Positive outlook is the notable part** — it is one notch of outlook better than OBSC's BBB+/Stable, and management flags it as competitively useful: *"further strengthens our ability to participate in larger projects where financial strength and credit quality form an important part of the qualification criteria."* An upgrade to A− would be a genuine catalyst.
