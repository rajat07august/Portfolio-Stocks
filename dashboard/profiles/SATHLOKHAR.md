## 1. Business in Plain English

When a pharma company wants a new factory, a warehouse operator needs a distribution centre, or a data-centre developer wants a full campus built, they need an **integrated EPC contractor** that can do everything in-house — architecture, civil works, **Pre-Engineered Buildings (PEB)**, mechanical/electrical/plumbing, solar and interior fit-outs.

**Sathlokhar is a Chennai-based integrated EPC contractor** serving industrial, warehousing, healthcare, pharmaceutical, food-processing and renewable-energy clients.

**The snapshot:**
- **HQ Chennai. Founded 2013 by G. Thiyagu**, who is **Promoter, Chairman, Managing Director and CEO — all four roles in one person.**
- **NSE-SME listed**, ~5,583 shareholders. **No BSE listing.**
- **FY26 revenue ₹820cr** (₹823cr ex-GST), **PAT ₹82cr**, **ROCE 37.1%**, **ROE 31.0%**.
- **Q1FY27: total income ₹206.19cr (+67.76%), EBITDA ₹31.35cr (+131.4%) at a 15.2% margin (+418bps), PAT ₹21.41cr (+132.59%).**
- **Confirmed order book ₹1,101.59cr** (11-Aug-2026) against a **₹22,155cr bid pipeline.**
- **Explicit FY27 guidance: ~70% revenue growth**, reaffirmed twice — on the call and in writing.
- **🔴 FY26 operating cash flow −₹163cr against ₹110cr of pre-tax profit**, after **−₹91cr to −₹96cr** in FY25.
- **🔴 NCLT admitted personal insolvency proceedings against the MD on 9-Jun-2026**; **NCLAT stayed the order on 8-Jul-2026.**

**Business model note:** only **30-35% is civil labour**; the majority is engineering-driven, which is unusual for EPC and is what makes the margin scalable. Behind it sits a **nine-year, ₹7cr+ marketing investment** that has generated **60,000+ leads, 23,717 prospects and 29+ industrial expos** — an unglamorous asset that explains the bid pipeline.

**Big picture in one sentence:** SSEGL is the fastest-compounding name on this watchlist — **3-year sales CAGR 111%, profit CAGR 147%, 37% ROCE** — trading at **7.93x** because **two things could break it: a cash cycle that has consumed ₹259cr of operating cash over two years, and a promoter who holds all four senior roles and is subject to stayed personal insolvency proceedings.**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — India's industrial capex cycle.** Pharma, electronics, food processing and warehousing are all building new facilities simultaneously.

**Force 2 — Warehousing and logistics.** E-commerce, 3PL and cold chain drive continuous warehouse construction.

**Force 3 — Data-centre build-out.** Google, AWS, Microsoft and MEITY are all pushing Indian capacity.

**Force 4 — Renewable energy execution.** Solar and wind installations need EPC partners with electrical depth.

**Force 5 — The shift from labour-intensive to engineering-driven construction.** Automation, PEB and modular methods reduce labour dependency — **and this is precisely where SSEGL has positioned itself**, at 30-35% civil labour against an industry norm far higher.

**Where SSEGL sits.** A small integrated EPC contractor in mid-value industrial and institutional buildings. **L&T** is orders of magnitude larger; **NCC, Ircon, GR Infraprojects and Ceigall** operate at different scale or in different verticals. **The honest peer set for valuation purposes is Ahluwalia Contracts, Capacite Infraprojects and — for the PEB leg — Interarch**, and that comparison is in Section 6.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Genuinely integrated: architecture, civil, PEB, MEP and solar in-house.** Rare at this size, and it is what lets SSEGL bid single-point for a full industrial campus.

**Moat 2 — An engineering-driven cost structure.** 65-70% non-civil-labour. Scalable and margin-stable in a sector where neither is normal.

**Moat 3 — Client quality that does not match the market cap.** **Reliance Consumer Products** (a repeat client *"last 3-plus years… consistently booking every year a good order"*), **Carrier Airconditioning & Refrigeration** (US-owned, ₹94.11cr at Sri City), **TAFE**, **EDAC Engineering**, **Ceylon Beverages**, **Anabond**, and a **Taiwanese SEZ developer** (Grand Atlantia Panapakkam). Thiyagu: *"A significant portion of these orders has come from existing clients."* **This is not the client list of a marginal contractor.**

**Moat 4 — Elite return ratios.** **ROCE 37.1%, ROE 31.0%** — best in the peer set by a wide margin, and 2-3x what mainboard EPC peers earn.

**Moat 5 — PEB backward integration, arriving.** Factory inauguration **fixed for 30-Aug-2026**, commercial production within **60 working days** thereafter — realistically **Nov–Dec 2026**. Today SSEGL owns no PEB capacity: *"we just taken over two-three facilities under job order format where we deliver the steel and they do processing."* Expected benefit **"1%-plus or 1.5%"** on a ~10% net margin — and, per management, *"more than profit, it's our own facility where we are getting faster in construction."* **Long-term vision: 5-6 PEB facilities across India, Odisha next.** **Third-party PEB revenue should not be modelled yet** — *"our own requirements are huge."*

**Moat 6 — Class 1A PWD registration, obtained.** The highest category, unlocking large government and infrastructure work. **Eligibility, not revenue — no government win has been announced under it.**

**Moat 7 — A ₹22,155cr bid pipeline against a ₹814cr market cap.** Management now assumes a **"conservative 10%"** conversion and calls it *"lower than the Company's historical win rate."*

**Moat 8 — CRISIL BBB+/Positive plus A2** on ₹200cr of facilities — the company's first rating. **The Positive outlook is the notable part**, one notch of outlook better than OBSC's BBB+/Stable, and management flags it as competitively useful for qualification criteria. **An upgrade to A− would be a genuine catalyst.**

**Moat 9 — Multi-sector diversification** across pharma, healthcare, food, textile, institutional and renewable.

**Where the moat is weaker — and this is where the whole investment case lives:**

- **🔴 The cash cycle.** **FY26 operating cash flow −₹163cr, FY25 −₹96cr.** Across two years the company reported **₹125cr of cumulative PAT and consumed ₹259cr of operating cash**, funded by ₹261cr of external capital. **This, not SME illiquidity, is why the stock trades at 7.9x.**
- **🔴 ₹234cr of unbilled revenue** at 31-Mar-2026 — **28.5% of FY26 revenue** — whose current level management declined to state on the call.
- **🔴 One person holds all four senior roles**, and now has an admitted (stayed) personal insolvency proceeding attached to that concentration.
- **🟡 Bank lines ~72% drawn** — roughly ₹180cr of a ₹250cr sanction — against guided 70% growth.
- **🟡 SME listing** — ~5,583 shareholders, negligible FII participation, and a structural liquidity discount.
- **🟡 Small absolute size** brings customer concentration risk.

**🔴 And one weakness that only appears if you read the balance sheet: the debtor-days series is actively misleading.** **Screener shows debtor days improving 123 → 34 in FY26.** Trade receivables did fall ₹118cr → ₹77cr — **but only because the claim moved from *billed* receivables into *unbilled* "other current assets," which rose ₹290.6cr. Nothing was collected.** **Any screen that reads debtor days here will draw the opposite of the correct conclusion.**

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Six threads, and they are genuinely connected rather than listed:**

**A — India's industrial and warehousing capex cycle** creates the demand. **B — The engineering-driven model** (30-35% civil labour) is what lets SSEGL take that demand at 15% margins where mainboard peers earn 9%. **C — PEB backward integration** converts a bought-in input into an owned one, worth *"1%-plus or 1.5%"* and, more importantly, speed. **D — Class 1A PWD registration** opens a customer set — government and large infrastructure — that was previously closed. **E — Multi-sector diversification** across pharma, food, warehousing, healthcare and renewables means no single vertical's cycle can stop the company. **F — A ₹22,155cr bid pipeline** against ₹820cr of revenue means the constraint is conversion and execution capacity, not opportunity.

**Where the chain actually runs:** integrated capability wins blue-chip repeat clients (Reliance three years running, Carrier, TAFE) → repeat clients reduce bidding cost and improve conversion → conversion fills a book that grew **57% in six weeks** → the book funds 70% growth → growth delivers operating leverage, and margin duly went **11% → 15% and held there four quarters.** **Every link in that chain is evidenced.**

**And here is where it breaks, precisely.** The chain converts orders into *revenue* and revenue into *reported profit* — **but not into cash.** ₹125cr of cumulative PAT across FY25-FY26 came with **₹259cr of operating cash consumed**, because the claim on the customer moved into **unbilled revenue faster than the business could bill it.** The gap was funded by **₹90cr of equity and ₹81cr of short-term debt**, and the bank lines are now **~72% drawn.**

**So the whole thesis reduces to a single question: does the ₹234cr of unbilled revenue convert?** If it does, a company growing 70% at 37% ROCE on 6x forward earnings is dramatically mispriced. If it does not, growth must be funded by dilution — **and the second-order risk is that a promoter under stayed insolvency proceedings is the person who would have to raise it.**

**That is why the two risks are not independent.** *My inference, not management's position:* **the cash cycle and the legal status are the same risk seen from two angles**, because both bear on the company's ability to finance growth it has already committed to in writing.
