## 1. Business in Plain English

**AXISCADES is in the middle of turning itself into a different company, and the accounts currently contain both of them.**

**What it was:** an **engineering services** firm. A client — Airbus, Honeywell, a Tier-1 automotive supplier — signs a contract; AXISCADES puts a team of engineers on the client's programme in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Vietnam or Silicon Valley; it delivers designs, drawings, test rigs and prototypes and bills per project or per engineer-month. **Founder-Chairman Dr. Sampath Ravinarayanan's own analogy for why that is a poor business: *"an architect gets 3% of a building's cost, a builder gets the other 97%."***

**What it is becoming: the builder.** Four streams:

1. **Defence Solutions** — the largest, **₹125.0cr in Q1FY27, +111% YoY.** Systems and products, not manhours. **₹4,500cr+ of "assured forecast visibility"** after 8 design and order wins since April.
2. **XiDA** *(formerly ESAI — electronics, semiconductors, AI)* — the best margin. **₹49.5cr, +62% YoY, at a 29.7% EBITDA margin.** Added **two of the world's largest technology companies** as customers this quarter.
3. **Aerospace manufacturing** — a **new stream built from nothing**, ₹6.1cr of revenue against **−₹5.4cr of EBITDA**. This is where the money is going.
4. **Add Solutions GmbH** — a **non-core European unit being exited by Q4FY27**; ₹4.8cr EBITDA loss and ₹6.7cr PAT loss in one quarter.

**And what it is selling:** the entire **Engineering Services** business (May-2026) and the **Aerospace Services** business (June-2026), both to the **Akkodis Group**, approved by shareholders on **27-Jul-2026** as a slump sale under s.180(1)(a). **Headline value USD 237 million / ~₹2,256cr.**

**The 30-second snapshot:**
- **Bengaluru** · founded 1990 · **Dr. Sampath Ravinarayanan, Founder Chairman & MD** *(took the chair February 2025)*
- **Q1FY27 total revenue ₹346.7cr (+42.2% YoY)** — a record, **and a reported loss of ₹14.8cr**
- **Continuing operations ₹183.4cr (+94.2%)**, normalised EBITDA **₹18.3cr (10.0%)**, normalised PAT **₹2.6cr**
- **🔴 ₹17.6cr of the group's ₹20.2cr normalised PAT came from the business being sold**
- **🆕 28-Aug-2026: agreed to acquire 90% of Cloud Wave Technologies for ~₹234cr cash**, EV ~₹260cr
- **FY27 guidance ₹1,377cr revenue and ₹270cr EBITDA — both "proforma annualised", not reported**
- **Power 930: ₹9,000cr of revenue by FY2030**, described by the Chairman as *"non-negotiable"*
- **211x earnings · 9.5x book · ROCE 3.57% · ROE 4.06% · no dividend · promoter 58.03% and falling**

**Big picture in one sentence.** AXISCADES is **selling a profitable services business for up to a third of its own market value and spending the proceeds on becoming an aerospace and defence manufacturer** — a strategically coherent trade that is being executed **all at once**, funded by **tranched consideration whose size the company has now described four different ways**, against a 2030 revenue target its own FY27 guidance cannot arithmetically reach.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — India's defence indigenisation is a decade-long, policy-backed reallocation.** Offset obligations, positive-indigenisation lists and emergency procurement have moved work from imports to Indian systems houses. **AXISCADES' ₹4,500cr+ of stated defence visibility is what that looks like on one company's order book.**

**Force 2 — Global aerospace OEMs are qualifying Indian manufacturing capacity, not just Indian engineers.** The engineering-services offshore model is twenty years old and consolidating; the manufacturing build-out is the newer, scarcer, stickier opportunity. **Management's stated reason for choosing to sell rather than buy in services: *"we do see many of our services OEMs looking for consolidation in this market."***

**Force 3 — Manufacturing is stickier than services, and management says so plainly.** Deputy CEO KP Mohanakrishnan: *"the manufacturing aspect… is more of a sticky business. So we are getting into long-term contracts, a highly predictable business."*

**Force 4 — Electronics and semiconductor content is growing inside every platform.** XiDA at a **29.7% EBITDA margin** is the highest-return stream in the company and grew 62% in a quarter.

**Force 5 — Scale economics favour the builder.** *The architect/builder analogy is the whole strategy: the same programme knowledge, applied one step down the value chain, against a far larger revenue pool.*

**Where AXISCADES sits.** ₹6,907cr in **Sector 1 (Aerospace & Defence)** alongside AZAD, PARAS, DYNAMATIC, ROSSELL and UNIMECH. ***Note it is the only one attempting a wholesale business-model transition rather than an expansion.***

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — 🟢 The defence order visibility is large and growing, and it rests on real programme wins.** **₹4,500cr+ of "assured forecast visibility"**, against FY27 guided revenue of ₹1,377cr. **Defence revenue more than doubled year on year in Q1FY27** on 8 wins since 1 April. **The pedigree behind it: a DRDO relationship that has produced hard orders, including a ₹150cr fighter-jet mission-computer contract in FY26**, and design-win positions that convert — *management's own description of the mechanism is that winning the design brings manufacturing rights when a programme moves to production, turning **"a ₹5 Cr design contract into a ₹50–500 Cr manufacturing contract over the programme's life."*** ***That conversion mechanic is the real reason to own this rather than a services multiple.***

**Moat 2 — 🟢 XiDA's margins are genuinely differentiated.** **29.7% EBITDA** in Q1FY27, with a business acquired by transfer contributing at a **46.2% margin** in its first quarter. **Two of the world's largest technology companies added as customers.** *This is the stream that most justifies a premium multiple, and it is the smallest.*

**Moat 3 — Programme knowledge is the bridge from architect to builder.** Twenty years of designing aerospace and defence parts is a real qualification advantage when bidding to make them — you already know the drawings, the tolerances and the customer's approval process.

**Moat 4 — 🟢 The transition is being funded by a disposal, not by dilution.** Management have been explicit and consistent on this: *"we would do all this without any equity dilution or any material long-term debt"*, and *"the deployment of the proceeds… would enable us to do that without any incremental equity dilution."* **On the evidence to date they have kept that promise — no equity has been issued.**

**Moat 5 — Physical assets are going in the ground now.** **PP&E rose ₹40cr in a single quarter.** Land at **IP Hardware Park, Telangana (32,847 sq m) completed 24-Jul-2026**; the **Centre for Advanced Manufacturing at Devanahalli — 240,000 sq ft on 20 acres**, a few kilometres from DAC and DAL.

**Moat 6 — Cloud Wave brings capability that would otherwise take years.** **AS9100D-certified, seven manufacturing units**, precision machining, sheet metal, tooling, injection moulding, 3D printing, surface treatment; aerospace, defence and semiconductor customers, domestic and export.

**Moat 7 — A board with real domain weight.** The Audit Committee is chaired by **Giridhar Aramane**; the Stakeholders Relationship Committee by **Lt Gen Codanda Poovaiah Cariappa**; the NRC by **Tanmoy Chakrabarty**; and **Ashmita Sethi**, described in the company's own Reg-30 filing as *"a globally recognized C-suite leader in the Aerospace & Defence industry, with more than 25 years of experience"*, joined on 11-Aug-2026.

### Where the moat is weaker

- **🔴 The profitable business is the one being sold.** **Discontinued operations produced ₹17.6cr of the group's ₹20.2cr normalised PAT in Q1FY27 — 87%.** The retained business earned **₹2.6cr**.
- **🔴 Aerospace manufacturing is currently a cash cost, not a business.** **₹6.1cr of revenue against ₹12.9cr of operating cost and ₹6.4cr of finance cost — which is 72% of the entire retained finance cost of the group.**
- **🔴 Returns are close to nil.** **ROCE 3.57%, ROE 4.06%.** Ten-year and five-year ROE are both **5%**.
- **🔴 Ten-year sales CAGR 4%; five-year 0%; three-year −14%.** *The −14% is the divestment reclassification, but the 4% and 0% are not.*
- **⚠️ Cash generation has gone.** **FY26 CFO −₹1cr and free cash flow −₹135cr**, against FCF of +₹59cr and +₹61cr in the two prior years. **Borrowings ₹258cr → ₹388cr, +50%.**
- **⚠️ Promoter holding has fallen for six straight years** — 67.95% (FY20) → **58.03%** — while institutions came and went and the retail register tripled.
- **⚠️ Everything depends on transactions closing on time.** *Two disposals in two phases, at least two acquisitions, three facility builds and one unit exit, all inside four quarters.*

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — 🟢 The strategic logic is sound, and management's own articulation of it is the best argument for the stock.** *The architect gets 3% of the building; the builder gets 97%.* **Twenty years of designing aerospace and defence parts is a genuine qualification advantage when bidding to make them.** *This is not a conglomerate wandering into a hot sector.*

**Thread B — 🟢 And they are doing what they said.** **Services divested and shareholder-approved; no equity issued; an aerospace acquisition announced fifteen days after saying one would close "this quarter"; an aerospace C-suite veteran added to the board.** **On transactions, this management executes.**

**Thread C — 🔴 But the profitable half is the half being sold.** **₹17.6cr of ₹20.2cr of normalised group PAT came from discontinued operations. The retained business earned ₹2.6cr on ₹183.4cr of revenue.** ***The entire investment case is that the ₹2.6cr becomes ₹135cr. Nothing in the reported numbers yet demonstrates it — by construction, because the capacity was installed before the revenue.***

**Thread D — 🔴 The bridge between the two is a cash number the company has stated four different ways.** **₹2,256cr / ₹1,685cr / ~₹910–920cr / ₹715cr.** **The gap between the top and bottom figures is ₹1,541cr — more than a fifth of the market capitalisation — and no analyst asked.** *The capital plan, the acquisitions, the debt retirement and the "no dilution" promise all rest on this number.*

**Thread E — 🟡 The guidance has been quietly rebased and the market may not have noticed.** **₹1,377cr and ₹270cr are "proforma annualised", not FY27 reported.** **Q1FY27 continuing revenue annualises to ~₹734cr — 53% of the headline.** *The deck says so openly. The risk is not concealment; it is that a reported FY27 number roughly half the guided one will read as a catastrophic miss to anyone who did not read the footnote.*

**Thread F — 🔴 And more acquisitions are required to make even the proforma number.** The replacement bridge is **₹74cr of divested EBITDA = ₹39cr (Cloud Wave) + a "Balance" from "XiDA business transfer + other Acquisitions in progress."** ***Unannounced deals are inside published guidance.***

**Thread G — Power 930 is now three data points further from reach.** **₹9,000cr by FY2030 from a ₹1,377cr FY27 proforma base needs ~87% compound growth for three years.** *The 28-Jul refresh found that an analyst put this to management and got belief rather than a bridge. A quarter later the FY27 base has been rebased downward in substance, not up.*

**Thread H — 🟡 The register is the quiet risk.** **Promoter 67.95% → 58.03% over six years; DIIs bought a promoter block in Mar-2024 and were out inside eighteen months; shareholders 15,257 → 46,002.** *A ₹6,900cr transformation is being underwritten by a tripled retail base at 4.06% ROE with no dividend.*

**How they interact.** ***A coherent, well-executed strategic pivot — sell the low-value half, buy the high-value half, fund it without dilution — is being run at maximum simultaneity: two disposals in two phases, at least two acquisitions of which one is unannounced, three facility builds and one unit exit, all inside four quarters, against a public target the arithmetic cannot reach, with the funding number disclosed four incompatible ways.*** **The direction is right. The margin for error is very thin, and the disclosure quality around the single most important number is not what a transaction of this size deserves.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — 🔴 Transaction execution, and there are six live transactions at once.** **Two divestment phases, at least two acquisitions (one unannounced), three facility builds, and one unit exit — all inside four quarters.** *Any single slip is survivable; the correlation between them is the risk, because they share management bandwidth and the same cash pool.*
**⚠️ LIVE NOW: Phase 1 was targeted for 31 August 2026 and no completion filing has appeared.**

**Risk 2 — 🔴 The divestment cash could be materially less than the headline.** **₹2,256cr / ₹1,685cr / ~₹910–920cr / ₹715cr.** **If the realised post-tax figure is at the bottom of that range, the plan funds Cloud Wave (₹234cr) and debt retirement and little else** — and the *"other Acquisitions in progress"* inside FY27 guidance would need either debt or the equity dilution management has promised to avoid.

**Risk 3 — 🔴 The retained business does not yet earn anything.** **₹2.6cr of normalised PAT on ₹183.4cr of revenue.** **Aerospace manufacturing is −₹5.4cr of EBITDA and consumes 72% of the group's retained finance cost.** *Everything rests on operating leverage that has been paid for but not yet delivered.*

**Risk 4 — 🔴 The guidance basis will confuse the reported outcome.** **₹1,377cr "proforma annualised" against a Q1 annualising to ~₹734cr.** *When FY27 reports roughly half the guided number, the market has to decide whether it was told or misled. The company did disclose the basis — but a stock held by 46,002 mostly-retail shareholders is not a stock where footnote-reading can be assumed.*

**Risk 5 — 🔴 Power 930 is unreachable and remains public.** *The 28-Jul refresh worked the target's implied end-point: **₹9,000cr of revenue at 25%+ EBITDA lands PAT near ₹960cr, which at a 30x defence mid-cap exit multiple is a ₹28,800cr market capitalisation — roughly 4x today's.** **The mirror case is that the pivot stalls, the multiple collapses to services levels on 12–15% growth, and FY30 revenue is ~₹1,800cr rather than ₹9,000cr.** **That is the actual spread of this stock over five years, and it is why the two-year band below is +148% to −39%.*** **₹9,000cr by FY2030 needs ~87% compound growth from the FY27 proforma base.** *A "non-negotiable" target that is arithmetically out of reach eventually becomes a credibility event, whichever way it is resolved.*
**⚠️ ACCUMULATING — the gap widened this quarter, not narrowed.**

**Risk 6 — 🔴 Cash generation has stopped.** **FY26 CFO −₹1cr and FCF −₹135cr against +₹61cr the year before; borrowings +50% to ₹388cr.** *Management attribute much of it to the ₹142cr deferred revenue, of which "over 60 crores" has been recovered. **Checkable at Q2FY27 — and it should be checked.***

**Risk 7 — 🟡 The sector multiple is the single biggest swing factor and it is exogenous.** **The mid-cap A&D cohort trades at 104–157x on high-single-digit ROEs while Bharat Electronics trades at 48x on 27.4%.** *A cohort de-rating hurts AXISCADES more than most, because its own earnings are the least established in the group.*

**Risk 8 — 🟡 Unannounced acquisitions are inside published guidance.** The deck's replacement bridge requires *"other Acquisitions in progress"* to fill the gap between ₹39cr and ₹74cr of EBITDA. **Deals not yet announced, at prices not yet known, are already load-bearing.**

**Risk 9 — 🟡 Register composition.** **Promoter 67.95% → 58.03% over six years; DIIs bought a promoter block in Mar-2024 and exited within eighteen months; retail shareholders tripled to 46,002.** *At 9.5x book with a 4.06% ROE and no dividend, there is no institutional anchor.*

**Risk 10 — 🟡 Key-man, in both directions.** **Dr. Sampath Ravinarayanan is the Founder-Chairman-MD and part of the multiple is faith in him.** **He did not attend the Q1FY27 call**; his speech was read aloud by the head of IR. *Institutionalising a founder-led company is right; but on the quarter that produced a loss, a ₹2,256cr disposal and a pending ₹234cr purchase, the founder took no questions.*

**Risk 11 — 🟢 TRACKING WELL, and it deserves recording.** The 28-Jul refresh's central worry was that the disposals and acquisitions would prove slower and vaguer than announced. **In five weeks: shareholder approval obtained, Phase 1 scheduled, an acquisition signed at a defensible price, and a board strengthened.** ***Execution has outrun the scepticism so far.***

**Risk 12 — 🆕 Disclosure quality around the largest number.** **Four incompatible proceeds figures across three same-week documents; a ₹193cr deck-versus-transcript disagreement on Phase 2; a "minimum consideration of ₹1,685 crore" that appears in the press release and in no other document.** *Not an allegation of anything — but for a transaction worth a third of the market capitalisation, one reconciled bridge should exist.*

## 8. 30-Second Memory Hook

> **AXISCADES is a company mid-way through swapping its own engine at altitude.** **It has sold the profitable half — engineering services, to Akkodis, shareholders approving on 27 July — and is spending the money on becoming an aerospace and defence manufacturer.** **The Founder-Chairman's own line is the thesis: *the architect gets 3% of the building, the builder gets the other 97%.***
>
> **Q1FY27 is exactly what that looks like from inside the accounts: record revenue of ₹346.7 crore, up 42%, and a reported loss of ₹14.8 crore. Continuing operations nearly doubled. Defence more than doubled with ₹4,500 crore of visibility behind it. XiDA earned a 29.7% margin and added two of the world's largest technology companies. And 87% of the group's normalised profit came from the business being sold.**
>
> **They are executing.** *Services divested, no equity issued, an aerospace acquisition announced fifteen days after they said one was coming — 90% of Cloud Wave for ₹234 crore, at about 6.6 times forward EBITDA on a business that grew 85% then 58% — and an aerospace C-suite veteran added to the board.*
>
> **What stops this being a straightforward buy is the number everything depends on.** **The company has now described the divestment proceeds four ways in three documents — ₹2,256 crore, ₹1,685 crore, about ₹910–920 crore, and ₹715 crore.** **The top and bottom are ₹1,541 crore apart, more than a fifth of the market capitalisation. The deck and the transcript disagree on Phase 2 alone by ₹193 crore. Nobody on the call asked.** **Meanwhile FY27 guidance of ₹1,377 crore has been quietly restated as "proforma annualised" against a first quarter annualising to ₹734 crore, and Power 930 — ₹9,000 crore by 2030, called "non-negotiable" — needs 87% compound growth from a base that just went down.**
>
> **What you're buying:** a coherent pivot into the best-funded sector in Indian industry, executed on schedule so far, without dilution, with a real asset floor arriving as the divestment gain nearly triples book value. **What you're risking:** a retained business earning ₹2.6 crore a quarter, six simultaneous transactions, unannounced acquisitions already inside published guidance, and a funding number nobody has reconciled.
>
> **Weighted 2-year return ≈ +43%** — base **+25%**, bull **+148%**, bear **−39%**. ***Attractive expected value, enormous variance, and every upside case inherits a sector multiple of 100x+ that Bharat Electronics does not need to justify its own 48x.*** **HELD at MEDIUM (Under Watch).**
>
> **What to watch, in order:** **whether Phase 1 closed — it was due 31 August and there is no filing yet**; whether Q2FY27 reconciles the proceeds; whether Cloud Wave closes by 30 September; and whether the retained business turns a reported profit.
