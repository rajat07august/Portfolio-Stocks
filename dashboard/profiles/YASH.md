## 1. Business in Plain English

Yash Highvoltage makes **transformer bushings** — the insulated porcelain-or-composite fittings that carry a high-voltage conductor through the steel wall of a transformer without arcing to earth.

They look like ribbed ceramic cones on top of a substation transformer. **They are also the single most common cause of transformer failure**, which is why utilities and OEMs qualify suppliers over years and switch reluctantly.

**Two technologies, and the shift between them is the whole investment case:**

| Type | What it is | Where YASH stands |
|---|---|---|
| **OIP** (oil-impregnated paper) | The older technology; oil-filled, heavier, maintenance-prone | The established base. **Capacity-constrained** — *"the OIPs we are not able to deliver because we are limited on capacity and there is a huge amount of order"* |
| **RIP / RIS** (resin-impregnated paper/synthetic) | Dry-type, lighter, safer, higher-value, the global standard | **The growth engine — and until now YASH imported the capacitive core** |

**🔴 That imported core is why this company could not export.** Asked directly whether backward integration removes the restriction: ***"Yes, there's no restriction. Once we have our own bushing, we can [sell]."*** And on the terms: ***"Clean. No restrictive clause."*** **The greenfield plant localises RIP/RIS core manufacture — and in doing so unlocks the export market.**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 0 — The market itself is tripling.** **India's transformer bushing market was ~₹1,500cr in FY24 and is projected at ~₹4,000cr**, so even flat share implies a long runway. *v1's sizing, carried forward.*

**Force 1 — India's transmission build-out is a multi-decade programme.** Every new substation and every transformer replacement needs bushings, and the National Electricity Plan implies a long runway.

**Force 2 — Bushings are the failure point, so qualification is the moat.** Management's own framing: *"In a highly specialized industry such as ours, **customers' trust and technical reliability are built over decades** and we remain committed to protect that positioning."*

**Force 3 — The global standard is migrating from OIP to RIP/RIS.** Dry-type is lighter, safer and doesn't leak. **The value per unit is higher and so is the margin.**

**Force 4 — 🆕 Import substitution in the capacitive core is a genuine unlock, and v1 sized the margin at 400–500bps.** The greenfield *"will enable **localization of RIP and RIS cores manufacturing, reducing dependency on imported capacitive cores**, improve cost competitiveness, and **unlock export opportunities** in RIP, RIS segment."*

**Force 5 — Voltage class is the addressable-market lever.** **The greenfield's design capability was expanded from a planned 220 kV to 550 kV**, *"significantly enhancing our addressable market opportunity."*

**Where YASH sits.** ₹2,690cr in **Sector 14 (Power T&D Components)** alongside DIACABS, DYCL and SYSTEMATIC. **BSE-only — it does not appear in NSE's `EQUITY_L.csv` at all**, which makes BSE 544310 the sole feed.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Return metrics that are genuinely best-in-class for the sector.** **ROCE 28.6%, ROE 23.5%**, on a **D/E of 0.17x**. *Very few names on this watchlist earn this on this little leverage.*

**Moat 2 — Qualification lock-in in a failure-critical component.** Utilities and transformer OEMs re-qualify slowly and rarely.

**Moat 3 — 🆕 Backward integration that removes a commercial restriction, not just a cost.** **The export bar comes off when the in-house RIP core is type-tested.** *"Today the situation is such that **everybody is waiting for the factory to get ready and a successful type test so they can pick up the material**."*

**Moat 4 — A capacity step-change with the demand already visible.** Current capacity **9,000–10,000 bushings**, of which **7,000+ produced in FY26 (75–80% utilisation)**. Post-expansion: **~15,000 bushings, ~₹700cr revenue potential** (~₹100cr OIP, the balance RIP). **Utilisation guided at 65–70% on the enlarged base in FY27.**

**Moat 5 — An order book at 1.7x revenue and growing.** **₹400cr+ at 31-Mar-2026** — all-time high, *"healthy execution visibility over the next one to two years"* — against a **₹500cr+ booking target for FY27.**

**Moat 6 — International infrastructure already built ahead of the product.** **USA subsidiary operational**; distribution partnerships with **Widemann and Electrolink** live across select European, North African and UK markets. *The channel is in place for the moment the export restriction lifts.*

**Moat 7 — 🆕 An institutional register bought at ₹721 in August.** **Malabar India Fund, ValueQuest, Motilal Oswal, WhiteOak Capital** and others committed **₹106cr** three weeks ago. *That is a real diligence signal on a ₹2,690cr company.*

**Moat 8 — Sukrut Electric extends the product ecosystem.** Acquired and closed in FY26; **current revenue ₹25–26cr**, with management targeting **₹150–160cr in four to five years (5–6x)**.

**Where the moat is weaker.**

- **🔴 Cash conversion is the weak line.** **CFO/OP has fallen 112% → 87% → 49% → 42%**, and **free cash flow was −₹29cr (FY25) and −₹50cr (FY26).**
