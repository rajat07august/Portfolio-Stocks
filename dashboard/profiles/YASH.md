## 1. Business in Plain English

Every transformer — the big electrical device that steps voltage up or down between power stations, substations, factories, and homes — needs **bushings** on top of it. A bushing is a specialised insulator that allows a high-voltage electrical conductor (up to 800 kilovolts) to pass from inside the transformer's oil-filled tank to the outside world without shorting or arcing. Think of it as a **high-voltage plug + insulator + safety seal, all in one**. Every transformer needs 3–5 bushings; every high-voltage substation has hundreds. **Yash Highvoltage is India's largest independent transformer bushing manufacturer**.

The 30-second business snapshot:
- **HQ Vadodara, Gujarat** — founded 2002 by the Shah family (Keyur Shah, Chairman & MD)
- **IPO'd late 2024** — recent listing, only ~18 months of public reporting
- **FY26 revenue INR 235.1 cr** (+57% YoY from INR 150 cr) — one of the fastest-growing power T&D component names
- **FY26 EBITDA margin 25.7%** (up from 23.1%) — high for a component maker
- **FY26 PAT INR 37.4 cr** (+75% YoY)
- **Order book INR 400+ cr** — 1.7x FY26 revenue coverage
- **60+ countries** served; **96% repeat order rate** — best-in-class stickiness
- **3 product lines**: Oil-Impregnated Paper (OIP) bushings — legacy tech; Resin-Impregnated Paper (RIP) bushings — modern; Resin-Impregnated Synthetic (RIS) — next-gen
- **Greenfield capacity expansion**: from 220 kV to **550 kV** design capability + 5,000–7,000 units/year additional capacity; Q2FY27 trial, H2FY27 commercial

**Big picture in one sentence:** Yash is a specialty component manufacturer riding India's largest-ever power T&D investment cycle + the global structural shift from OIP → RIP bushings + import substitution in RIP cores — with founder-family execution track record delivering 38–40% CAGR for 5+ years and guiding 40–42% CAGR for the next 4–5 years. Trades at 61x trailing P/E on ~28% ROCE — expensive but reflects genuine compounding potential.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

Global transformer bushing demand rides four durable forces:

**Force 1 — India's largest-ever power infrastructure cycle.** India's National Electricity Plan (2024–2032) targets INR 9+ lakh crore of investment in transmission + distribution. Peak power demand projected to grow from ~250 GW (2024) to ~370 GW by 2030. Every substation upgrade + new HVDC line + renewable integration + urban distribution rebuild needs new transformers. Every transformer needs bushings. India's transformer bushing market: ~INR 1,500 cr (FY24), projected INR 4,000+ cr by 2030.

**Force 2 — Global grid modernization.** US, Europe, Southeast Asia are all replacing 30–50 year old transmission infrastructure. Rising renewable integration + EV charging + data centres + AI-driven load growth all require grid rebuild. Global bushing market: ~USD 3B (2024) → USD 6B (2030). India-based suppliers like Yash are natural China+1 alternatives — cost-competitive + certified across major utilities.

**Force 3 — Structural technology shift from OIP → RIP → RIS.** Traditional Oil-Impregnated Paper (OIP) bushings dominate legacy transformers but require oil monitoring + maintenance. RIP (Resin-Impregnated Paper) bushings are dry, safer, and lower-maintenance. RIS (Resin-Impregnated Synthetic) is the next-gen with better thermal + dielectric properties. Utilities globally are progressively migrating from OIP → RIP → RIS. Yash makes all three — with local RIP core manufacturing (via new greenfield facility) as the game-changer for margin capture.

**Force 4 — Import substitution in RIP capacitive cores.** Currently, India imports ~80% of RIP bushing cores from Europe/China. Yash's Greenfield facility (opening H2FY27) will make RIP cores in-house — reducing import dependency + capturing 400–500 bps of additional margin.

**Where Yash sits in the map.** It's the **largest independent Indian transformer bushing player** in a globally consolidated industry:
- Global leaders: HSP (Germany), Trench (Siemens), ABB Micafil (Switzerland), CG Power (India-listed, larger but bushings are only a small division)
- Yash is India's leader in the pure-play bushing space
- Long qualification cycles (5–7 years for a new supplier to be certified at a major utility) — this is a *deep* moat once inside

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — 96% repeat order rate.** From Q4FY26 concall: *"Yash Highvoltage has built approvals across leading utilities, transformer OEMs, and industrial customers in more than 60 countries, supported by repeat order rate of 96%."* A 96% repeat rate for a component with 15–20 year replacement cycles = extraordinary customer stickiness. This is the single most defensible moat number in the profile.

**Moat 2 — 60+ country customer base.** Global utility approvals in 60+ countries = 60+ separate 5–7 year qualification cycles already completed. No new entrant can replicate this footprint quickly.

**Moat 3 — Full product portfolio (OIP + RIP + RIS).** Most competitors focus on one bushing type. Yash's ability to supply OIP legacy + RIP modern + RIS next-gen from the same relationship gives it upgrade path revenue — as utilities migrate technology, they stay with Yash.

**Moat 4 — Greenfield RIP core manufacturing (550 kV design capability).** Post-commissioning H2FY27, Yash will manufacture RIP capacitive cores in-house at 550 kV design — one of very few companies globally at this capability. This is the **margin lever + growth lever + strategic moat rolled into one**.

**Moat 5 — Sukrut Electric acquisition (closed FY26).** Strengthens transformer component ecosystem participation. Complements bushings with adjacent products — cross-sell opportunity.

**Moat 6 — USA subsidiary operational + European distribution partnerships.** YASH HV USA (operational), plus Widemann + Electrolink distribution partnerships in Europe + UK. Direct customer engagement + faster approval cycles for a market that historically took Indian exporters years to crack.

**Moat 7 — 245 kV oil-to-oil bushing in-house testing milestone (FY26).** First Indian company to test 245 kV oil-to-oil bushings in-house. Higher voltage capability = access to higher-value contracts (HVDC + high-voltage AC substations).

**Moat 8 — Debt-light balance sheet + high ROCE.** D/E 0.17x, ROCE 28.6%, ROE 23.5%. Growth is being funded from operating cash flow + minimal debt — sustainable capital structure.

**Where the moat is weaker.**
- Yash is small (INR 235 cr revenue). A single major client loss hurts materially.
- Customer concentration at ~60% top 10 (typical for T&D component space).
- Import dependency on RIP cores (until greenfield operational) creates FX + supply chain risk.
- Only 2 years of public financial reporting — no long-cycle track record.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

Yash's story is **specialty component compounding**, with four threads reinforcing:

**Thread A — India T&D volume growth.** India's transformer market is growing at 15–18% CAGR through FY30. Yash's revenue growth of 40–42% CAGR is materially ahead of underlying market because it's gaining share + expanding product portfolio (OIP → RIP → RIS upgrade capture) + entering new voltage classes (220 → 550 kV).

**Thread B — RIP core import substitution (margin lever).** Currently importing 80% of RIP cores from Europe/China. Greenfield facility (H2FY27) makes RIP cores in-house. Estimated margin capture: 400–500 bps. Impact from FY28 onwards.

**Thread C — International expansion (60 → 80 countries).** USA subsidiary + Widemann + Electrolink partnerships + Sukrut acquisition. FY27–FY29 target: expand export revenue from ~40% to 55%+ of mix. Higher-margin exports.

**Thread D — Adjacent product expansion via Sukrut.** Sukrut Electric brings transformer components adjacent to bushings — cross-sell opportunity + broader wallet share at existing customers.

**Where threads reinforce.** Greenfield RIP core (Thread B) + higher voltage capability (Thread A) + international expansion (Thread C) all leverage the same manufacturing setup + customer relationships. Product portfolio breadth (Thread A + D) makes Yash more valuable to each customer relationship.

**Where they don't compound.** Sukrut integration is a separate operational lift — could distract from bushing focus. USA subsidiary requires local ops + regulatory expertise — different from India-based manufacturing.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — Greenfield delays.** Greenfield facility commissioning is central to FY28 margin expansion. Any 6–9 month slip pushes RIP core in-house manufacturing to FY29 and delays the margin lever.

**Risk 2 — RIP core import substitution economics don't hold.** If import prices drop (Chinese oversupply) or in-house cost is higher than expected, margin capture is less than 400–500 bps.

**Risk 3 — Growth undershoots 40% CAGR.** Even 30% growth would still be strong but would compress multiple materially given valuation.

**Risk 4 — International expansion slower than expected.** USA + Europe approvals take 5–7 years typically. Widemann + Electrolink are relationships, not guaranteed sales.

**Risk 5 — Fourth dilution round (post ~15.7% already done since IPO).** Any QIP announcement dilutes valuation and questions capital discipline.

**Risk 6 — Customer concentration.** Top 10 customers likely 55–65% of revenue. Loss of any major transformer OEM allocation hurts materially.

**Risk 7 — Raw material cost inflation.** Epoxy resin + copper + specialty paper all subject to commodity cycles. Yash passes through in most contracts but not all.

**Risk 8 — Middle East + geopolitical tensions.** Yash mentioned indirect cost escalation from Middle East war. If broader supply chain disruptions escalate, cost inflation compresses margins.

**Risk 9 — Multiple compression in small-cap segment.** At 61x trailing + 13x P/B, Yash is priced richly. A broader small-cap correction (10–20%) hits Yash more than average.

**Bear case scenario (15% probability):** Greenfield delayed + growth undershoots. FY28 revenue INR 400 cr, PAT INR 55 cr, multiple to 40x = INR 2,200 cr. **~10% downside.**

**Base case (55%):** Guidance delivered. FY28 revenue INR 480 cr, PAT INR 80 cr, multiple 45x = INR 3,600 cr. **~50% upside.**

**Bull case (30%):** Greenfield operational + margin expansion early. FY28 revenue INR 550 cr, PAT INR 105 cr, multiple 50x = INR 5,250 cr. **~120% upside.**
