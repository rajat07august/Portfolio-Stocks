## 1. Business in Plain English

Interarch designs, engineers, manufactures and erects **pre-engineered steel buildings (PEB)** — factories, warehouses, data centres, semiconductor fabs — and is now moving into **heavy steel structures** for larger and more complex industrial projects.

**The analogy is a bespoke tailor who also runs the mill and sends the fitters.** A customer arrives not with a suit design but with a description of the person and the occasion — *"a design basis RFQ and the architectural drawings"* in the company's words. **Interarch does the structural design itself**, calculates exactly how much steel of which grade goes where, quotes on that, fabricates it in its own plants, and sends its own erectors to put it up on site. **This is the single most important thing to understand about the business**, and it is stated flatly by the CEO:

> *"We do not get the final structural design from either the consultant or the customer… For every bid, we only get what we call a design basis RFQ and the architectural drawings. This is which **we carry out the entire structural design**, not the building layout design. Calculate the bill of quantity, how much of which steel is going to get used and then bid… **There is no design that comes to us from the consultant. That is what our business is.**" — Manish Garg, CEO, Q1FY27 call*

**So the 155+ structural design engineers are the product, not overhead.** A competitor bidding to someone else's drawing is a fabricator; Interarch is bidding on a design it created, which is a materially higher barrier.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Steel is replacing concrete as a building material, and not only in factories.** This is the structural change management keeps returning to. Arvind Nanda: *"the use of steel as a building material is also changing… data centers, high-rise buildings, these are also moving a lot towards steel."* The CEO put numbers on it: the **"buildings" segment — institutions, commercial, offices, multi-storey, flight kitchens, process buildings — went from ~2% or even 0% of revenue to 10% in Q1FY27**, and *"is converting very fast from the RCC-framed buildings into steel-framed buildings."*

**Force 2 — India's industrial capex is arriving in very large lumps.** Nanda: *"a lot of the large projects are coming into India because India is trying to encourage them through PLI schemes… semiconductor plants, renewable plants, lithium battery plants, EVs. And now again, the car industry has started moving… **Most of these industries require very large plants. So therefore, the size has increased dramatically.**"*

**Force 3 — Speed.** A pre-engineered building goes up in a fraction of the time a conventional structure takes. For a semiconductor fab or a data centre where the equipment inside costs orders of magnitude more than the shed, **time-to-operation is the whole economics.**

**Force 4 — Formalisation.** PEB is shifting from local fabricators to national players with design capability, quality certification and erection crews. Interarch's 65 empanelled erectors are a distribution network a regional fabricator cannot assemble.

**🎯 And the force that matters most right now — the industry is capacity-short, not demand-short.** Nanda again: *"most of the large orders today are all divided between two to three companies. **There's no one company which can do it even now**… both these things happening together is straining everybody's capacity."* **This is the premise the entire investment rests on**, and it is why a 31% capacity addition is the central event rather than a risk.

**Where Interarch sits.** The listed comparison set is thin — **EPACK Prefab Technologies** (recently listed), **Pennar Industries**, **Everest Industries** — with **Kirby** and **Tata BlueScope** unlisted or MNC-owned. **No listed peer in India matches Interarch's 2,21,000 MTPA integrated capacity**, which is both the moat and the reason clean comparables are scarce.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Genuine vertical integration.** Design, engineering, manufacturing, project management **and erection**, all in-house. **Most competitors do some subset.**

**Moat 2 — It designs what it bids, and that is the real barrier.** See §1. The customer supplies an RFQ and architectural drawings; Interarch produces the structural design, the bill of quantities and the price. **155+ qualified structural design engineers and detailers** working on Staad Pro, MBS, FrameCad, Tekla, AutoCAD and ZWCAD. **The engineering team is the product, not overhead — and it is why "capacity" here means engineers as much as presses.**

**Moat 3 — Scale no listed Indian peer has.** 2,21,000 MTPA across six plants, heading to ~2,90,000 by Q4FY27. In a business where *"most of the large orders today are all divided between two to three companies"*, size is the qualification.

**Moat 4 — Pan-India footprint, now completed.** Freight on steel buildings is material; proximity wins bids. Uttarakhand (Pantnagar, Kichha), Tamil Nadu (two plants), Andhra Pradesh, and **Kheda, Gujarat from July 2026** — which closes the western and central India gap and puts capacity near Gujarat's seaports for export.

**Moat 5 — 65 empanelled erectors plus 90+ in-house project managers.** Erection is the part customers cannot self-serve and small fabricators cannot staff. It is also why "Erection & installation" is a real cost line rather than a subcontracted afterthought.

**Moat 6 — References that pre-qualify.** **Micron's Sanand fab** and **Tata Electronics Assam** are the two most scrutinised industrial builds in India. Having executed them is a credential competitors cannot buy.

**Moat 7 — Running at ~88% of usable capacity.** Disclosed only on the Q1FY27 call: FY26 volume **1,62,000 t** against installed **2,00,000 t** = 80% of installed, but *"in our business, the utilizable capacity is about 85% to 90% of the installed capacity. So if I look at the utilizable capacity, **we were at about 88%**."* **That is genuinely full for a project business**, and it substantiates management's repeated claim that it turns away work.

**Moat 8 — Repeat business.** The deck cites repeat orders as a headline FY26 metric, and customer groups with **multi-year association**. In a business where each project is bespoke, a customer coming back is the only real quality certificate there is.

**Moat 9 — Nearly debt-free with 22.8% ROCE.** **₹18cr of borrowings against ₹881cr of net worth.** The growth has been funded from cash and the IPO, not leverage.

**Moat 10 — An export arbitrage that is structural, not opportunistic.** Nanda on why North America works: *"this is an item which requires a lot of manpower and manufacturing, and the U.S. and Canada don't seem to be very competitive in it… **Exports will be more profitable. Prices there are higher. We will make more profit. The expense on sales marketing is 0. Expense on project management is 0.**"* With ER Steel taking sales, engineering, collection and installation, **Interarch's cost of serving North America is manufacturing only.**

**Where the moat is weaker — and this list is the reason conviction is MH and not H.**

- **🔴 Margins are thin and have not expanded.** 8.6% EBITDA in Q1FY27, 9.3% in FY26, and roughly 9-10% for three years while revenue nearly doubled. **Steel is a pass-through and competition is real.** Management is candid about the ceiling: *"you are not the only company. **So it's not that I can demand whatever I want**… Sometimes I have to take a very large order at a low margin."*
- **🔴 FY26 operating cash flow was −₹19cr on ₹135cr of PAT.** CFO/operating profit **15%**, FCF **−₹138cr**, working capital days **21 → 59** in two years. Management has named three causes and claimed a reversal (§4) — **but Interarch publishes no quarterly balance sheet, so it is unverified until H1FY27.**
- **🟡 Order book is only ~1x revenue.** ₹1,864cr against FY26 revenue of ₹1,898cr. Thin by EPC standards, normal for PEB, and consistent with a stated policy of only booking what can be built — **but it means utilisation, not backlog, decides returns.**
- **🟡 ROCE has already fallen 31% (FY23) → 23%.** Adding 31% capacity and 8-9% equity dilution means it has further to fall before it recovers.
- **🟡 Project lumpiness.** A ₹165cr order is ~9% of annual revenue. One delayed large project appears to have driven FY26's receivables.
- **🟡 Eight quarters of listed history.** IPO August 2024. **There is no observed downturn in the listed record.**

**🔴 The headline is "+20.7% revenue growth." The number that matters is that PAT went nowhere.**

**Here is the bridge, and it is entirely explicable:** EBITDA rose **₹7.8cr**, other income fell **₹6.9cr** (₹9.9cr → ₹3.0cr), and depreciation rose ₹0.9cr. **Net effect on PBT: −₹0.2cr.** So **the operating business genuinely improved by 24.6% and the entire gain was consumed by a fall in non-operating income.** That is **a better quarter than the flat PAT suggests, and a worse one than the revenue headline suggests.** *(The bridge is mine; the line items are the company's.)*

> **✅ And the call explains why the other income fell, which improves the read further.** Nanda: *"out of INR180 crores that we raised during the IPO, **we've already spent INR230-240 crores**. So the money has -- earlier, the treasury funds were coming in and interest coming in because we had that money with us. **Now it is getting spent. So in a way, it is a good thing**… instead of holding it, we have now built up the capacity. But that should give us more operational profit going forward."* **The lost ₹6.9cr is IPO cash being converted into plant — the same fact that is pushing depreciation up 28%.** This is a company spending its treasury on capacity, not a company losing income. *Capex of ~₹240cr since 1-Apr-2024 against ₹180cr raised at IPO is consistent with the explanation.*

**Two further things the quarter says:**

1. **Sequentially it is a step down** — revenue −8.7%, EBITDA −25.3%, margin 10.5% → 8.6%. **But Q1 is structurally the weakest quarter here:** FY26's shape was ₹381 / ₹491 / ₹524 / ₹504cr. Q1 was **20.1% of FY26 revenue**; on the same seasonality Q1FY27's ₹459.6cr implies a full year of **~₹2,290cr**, comfortably at management's ₹2,150–2,200cr target. **The naive annualisation of ₹1,838cr is the wrong way to read this business.** Management explained the mechanism: *"nearly every year the pickup is the first quarter is lower than second quarter and then third and fourth… sometimes the clearances and site conditions don't allow us to dispatch these customized materials… there is rains or there is no clearances at certain sites… the sites are flooded."*
2. **Erection and installation charges rose 38.2% against revenue up 20.7%** — the fastest-growing cost line. Worth watching: it suggests either mix shift toward erection-heavy contracts or site-cost inflation.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — Capacity is the binding constraint, and it is being released.** Management says repeatedly it turns away work it cannot deliver, and the **88% usable-capacity utilisation figure substantiates it.** Three new phases land between July 2026 and Q4FY27, lifting capacity 31%. **If the demand commentary is honest, revenue follows capacity almost mechanically.**

**Thread B — The end-markets are the ones with policy tailwinds.** Semiconductors (Micron Sanand, Tata Electronics Assam already executed), data centres (RailTel, Noida), renewables, EV/battery. **These are precisely where private capex is being directed** — and the two August orders (energy transmission ₹83cr, FMCG manufacturing ₹128cr) show the breadth is real.

**Thread C — Steel is taking share from concrete in *buildings*, not just factories.** The "buildings" segment went from ~2% to **10% of Q1FY27 revenue**. This is a second growth vector that did not exist two years ago, and it is the one management sounds most animated about.

**Thread D — Exports are a margin upgrade, not just a volume adder.** The ER Steel JV targets **~20% EBITDA against a group 8.6%**, and management now guides **exports to ~10% of turnover within 1–2 years.** Kheda's seaport proximity was chosen with this in mind.

**Thread E — The Canada JV converts a trading relationship into an owned platform.** Interarch 76% / ER Steel 24%, ₹80cr, and — the term that matters — **ER Steel must sign a 100% offtake agreement.** Interarch supplies engineering and manufacturing from India; ER Steel supplies the market, the sales force, the collections and the installation. **This is the cleanest expression of the export-arbitrage thesis in the file.**

**Thread F — Heavy steel structures is a step up the value chain**, not just more tonnage. Larger, more complex projects; 49,000 MTPA across Q2 and Q4 FY27, and a Phase 3 taking it toward 75,000–80,000t by December 2027.

**Thread G — The financing bridges Threads A–F.** A **₹250cr QIP** plus a 5-for-1 stock split. **The split is liquidity cosmetics; the QIP is what pays for the capacity.**

**🎯 Where the threads meet — and where they strain.** Threads A through F are one coherent story: a capacity-short category leader releasing capacity into policy-driven demand, with a margin upgrade attached at the far end. **The tension is between Thread A and Thread G on returns: more capacity plus more equity means ROCE has further to fall before it recovers** — it has already gone 31% → 23% since FY23, and 8–9% dilution is arriving.

**And the whole structure rests on one unverified plank.** Thread A depends on working capital behaving, which **in FY26 it did not** — ₹1,898cr of revenue and ₹135cr of profit produced **−₹19cr of operating cash.** Management has named three self-reversing causes and said *"we are back to positive now."* **Until the H1FY27 cash flow statement, that is a claim, not a fact — and it is the plank the QIP is being laid on top of.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — 🟡 Operating cash flow.** FY26 CFO was **−₹19cr** on ₹135cr of PAT; CFO/operating profit **15%**; FCF **−₹138cr**; working capital days **21 → 59** in two years.

**Risk 2 — 🔴 Margins are thin and structurally so.** 8.6% EBITDA in Q1FY27, 9.3% in FY26, ~9-10% for three years while revenue nearly doubled. **A 9% margin business has very little room between a good year and a bad one.** Management is candid that it cannot price freely: *"you are not the only company. So it's not that I can demand whatever I want… Sometimes I have to take a very large order at a low margin."*

**Risk 3 — 🔴 QIP dilution, and the size of the revision.** The ask went from **₹100cr (approved 27-Mar-2026) to ₹250cr (6-Aug-2026)** — a 2.5x increase in five months, superseding the earlier approval, citing *"revised funding requirements… and proposed growth initiatives."* On a ₹2,842cr company that is **8–9% dilution**, taking promoters toward ~54–55%. **The revision itself is the signal worth watching: either the opportunity got bigger or the funding need did.**

**Risk 4 — 🟡 Capacity arriving faster than orders.** Order book is ~1x revenue. Adding 31% capacity into a book-and-build business means **utilisation, not tonnage, decides returns. ROCE has already fallen 31% → 23%.**

**Risk 5 — 🟡 Customer concentration in project lumps.** A ₹165cr order is ~9% of annual revenue; the 18-Aug FMCG order is ~7%. Delays or disputes on one large project move the quarter — and appear to have moved FY26's receivables.

**Risk 6 — 🟡 Steel price and pass-through timing.** Management reports no supply disruption thanks to ties with SAIL and JSW, but **the pass-through lags** — and FY26 showed what happens when the company pre-buys inventory to get ahead of it.

**Risk 7 — 🟡 Execution risk in a genuinely new geography.** The ER Steel JV puts Interarch into North American structural codes, certification and liability. **The 100% offtake is strong protection on volume, not on cost overruns or code compliance.** Production is not due until ~1-Jul-2027, so there is no evidence either way yet.

**Risk 8 — 🟡 Heavy-structures slippage.** Phase 1 has already moved from *"July–August"* (said in May) to *"Q2FY27"* (said in August). **Watch the Q4FY27 phase and the Dec-2027 Phase 3.**

**Risk 9 — 🟡 Competition from better-capitalised entrants.** Tata BlueScope and Kirby have deep pockets; **EPACK Prefab has just listed and will have IPO money to spend** into the same demand.

**Risk 10 — 🟡 Labour codes and statutory cost creep.** FY26 carried a **₹3.2cr exceptional item** for the new labour codes, with management putting total profitability impact at **₹5–5.5cr**.

**Risk 11 — ⚪ Short listed history.** IPO August 2024. **Eight quarters of public reporting and no observed downturn in the listed record.** Every judgement in this profile about how the company behaves under stress is an inference.

**Risk 12 — ⚪ Management's verbal figures need checking.** Two on one call: FY26 volume estimated at 190,000t against an actual 1,62,000t (corrected by the CFO on air), and a *"25% increase"* against a filed +20.7%. **Not a governance issue — but take numbers from the CFO and the filings.**
