## 1. Business in Plain English

WABAG designs, builds and runs **water and wastewater treatment plants** — sewage treatment, drinking-water treatment, seawater desalination, industrial effluent and zero-liquid-discharge systems, and recycled water for refineries. It is India's largest pure-play water-treatment company and claims to rank **top three globally** in both desalination and private water operation *(management; not independently verified)*.

**What it actually sells is the process, not the concrete.** WABAG engineers the treatment scheme — which filters, membranes, biological stages and sludge handling, in what order, at what energy cost — procures the specialised equipment, and supervises commissioning. Civil construction is increasingly handed to partners and treated as a pass-through: *"We are a technology system integrator, not a EPC organization"* (CFO, Q1FY27). It then often runs the plant for 5 to 20 years under an **O&M contract**.

**The analogy: a heart surgeon who also runs the ICU.** The hospital building (civil works) is someone else's; WABAG decides the procedure, brings the instruments, performs the operation, and then stays on to keep the patient alive for fifteen years — which is where the steady money is.

**Three business lines, by revenue (Q1FY27):**

| Line | Share of revenue | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **EPC / DB (municipal)** | ~60% | Sewage & drinking-water plants, desalination for cities — funded by World Bank, ADB, JICA, KfW, or Indian central schemes |
| **EPC / DB (industrial)** | ~22% | Refinery and petrochemical water systems (Reliance, BPCL, GAIL, CPCL, Aramco), ultra-pure water for solar cells |
| **O&M** | **~18%** | Long-dated operation contracts; **₹6,666cr, a third of the order book** |

**Geography: ~48% India, ~52% overseas** (Q1FY27) — Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, UAE, Bahrain, Oman, Qatar, Senegal, Zambia, Bangladesh, Nepal, Philippines, Georgia, Austria.

**History in one line:** founded in Germany in 1924; the Indian arm was set up in 1996; a **management buyout in 2005**; in **2007 the Indian subsidiary bought its former parent's global business from Siemens**; listed in 2010. A painful 2015-2020 stretch (large receivables, Europe losses, a TSGENCO dispute) was followed from FY22 by the **"Wriddhi" strategy** — sovereign/multilateral-funded jobs only, exit construction risk, exit European subsidiaries, grow O&M and international.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

1. **Water supply is falling while demand rises.** Management cites a ~7% decline in usable freshwater per decade against rising per-capita use; India treats only **~28% of the sewage it generates** (India CEO, May-2026).
2. **"Manufactured water."** The CMD's frame: seawater and treated sewage are the only **drought-proof** sources. Desal at *"8 paise a litre"* and reuse at *"5 to 6 paise"* are cheap relative to tanker water — so coastal cities (Chennai, Mumbai) and the Gulf build desal, and industries near sewage plants are pushed onto recycled water.
3. **Funded by multilaterals, not municipalities.** In India water is a state subject, but WABAG only bids where money comes from the World Bank/ADB/JICA/KfW or central schemes (Namami Gange, AMRUT) — *"we don't take projects which are funded by state government or municipalities"* (CMD, May-2026). This is the payment-security filter that turned the business around.
4. **The Gulf's structural build-out.** Saudi Vision 2030, FIFA 2034, privatised water utilities (SWPC, SWA, NWC's long-term O&M programme), and now Kuwait and UAE. Management sizes GCC at ~$5bn/yr of opportunity.
5. **New industrial demand.** Ultra-pure water for solar-cell and semiconductor fabs (~1 MLD per GW of solar cell capacity; ~₹3,500cr Indian UPW market over 3-5 years per management), data-centre cooling, green hydrogen, and compressed biogas from sludge.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

1. **Process IP used only in its own projects** — 125+ patents and trademarks, R&D in Vienna and Chennai, never licensed. The example management gives is a proprietary filter valve that competitors must buy in. *"We use our own patented technology... many of our competitors have to buy it as a bought out item"* (Q1FY27).
2. **Reference list that takes decades to build** — ~7,000 plants since inception, 1,500+ since 1995; the Windhoek (Namibia) direct potable reuse plant, Chennai's Nemmeli and Perur desal plants. In desal and complex industrial effluent, **the client buys a track record**.
3. **Cost base in India, Turkey and Egypt competing against Spain, France and Israel** — *"European quality at Indian prices"* (Q1FY27). Competition on technology-heavy bids is European, not Chinese; the Chinese appear as construction partners.
4. **Build-and-operate knowledge.** Running plants gives WABAG operating data that pure contractors lack — which is what wins developer-led PPP bids in Saudi Arabia, where the developer is scored on 25-year lifecycle cost (*"In the last six STPs, we've won three"*, May-2026).
5. **Payment security as a filter** — ~95-98% of the book is multilateral-funded, sovereign-backed or LC-backed per management. This is the reason the company went from net debt to **₹965cr net cash (ex-HAM)** in five years.
6. **Asset-light by design** — fixed assets of ₹66cr against ₹3,944cr of revenue. *"Our only asset is working capital"* (CFO).
7. **A 4x order book** — ₹19,394cr at Jun-2026, excluding framework contracts, with a third of it multi-year O&M.
8. **A AA- credit rating in a sector where most EPC names are A or below** — it lowers guarantee costs, which matter: bank charges ran **₹49.8cr in FY26**.

### Where the moat is weaker — honestly

- **It is still a bid business.** The Yanbu desal re-tender was won by **0.27%** (*"we have just won this project by just 0.27%"*, CMD, Aug-2025), and the CMD describes pricing *"sometimes just less than 1% lower than the next bidder."* That is precision, but it is also thin.
- **Construction is only partly a pass-through.** JICA required single-point responsibility on Perur, including marine works; Q1FY27's gross margin fell 490bps because *"the top 5 projects would be largely EPC."*
- **Indian domestic growth has stalled.** India cluster revenue was roughly flat in FY26 (~₹1,800cr → ~₹1,860cr, questioner at May-2026 meet; management did not dispute it), and a questioner put India's quarterly segment profit at ₹127cr → ₹60cr in Q4FY26 (the CFO attributed it to cost allocation and construction phase).
- **Margins are at the guided band only with forex** (§4).
- **Working capital is the whole balance sheet** — ₹4,356cr of trade receivables, retentions and unbilled work against revenue of ₹3,944cr.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

The Wriddhi strategy is a chain, and every link has held: **filter for payment security → collect → go net cash → get upgraded to AA- → cheaper guarantees and bigger bid capacity → win larger international jobs → larger order book → faster growth.** That chain is real, and it explains why WABAG compounded PAT at ~30% for five years while most Indian water EPC names stumbled.

**Three threads now pull against it.**

1. **Internationalisation brings forex as a permanent line.** Over half of revenue is billed in USD-pegged currencies with costs partly in rupees; a falling rupee creates transaction gains. **FY26 and Q1FY27 were a period of steady rupee depreciation.** If the rupee stabilises (RBI is defending ~94.70 per `MACRO_FACTOR_MAP.md`), those gains fade; if it recovers, they reverse. **The 13-15% band has not yet been demonstrated in a flat-rupee year since the international mix reached 50%.**
2. **Construction-heavy EPC is dominating execution right now** — Perur, Pagla, Al Haer, and soon Yanbu and Doha — which is lower-margin by design. The O&M and EP mix that lifts margins is a 2028+ story (Perur, Kodungaiyur and Doha O&M phases).
3. **The municipal platform, Hadda and Indosol keep slipping.** None is large on its own, but the pattern — *"next quarter"* repeated — is the one weakness in an otherwise disciplined record.

**What would reconcile them:** a full year of 13%+ EBITDA **excluding** forex, CFO/PAT back above 0.8x, and the Norfund platform signed.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**1. 🔴 Forex reverses.** Q1FY27's ₹36.8cr gain was ~31% of PAT. **A flat rupee removes it; a rising rupee turns it into a loss** — as in Q1FY26 (−₹13.6cr). The natural hedge covers much of the exposure, but the size of the gains shows it does not cover all of it.

**2. 🔴 The margin is weaker than it is presented.** Ex-forex EBITDA: FY25 12.8% → FY26 12.1% → Q1FY27 9.0%. **Management's guidance is being met on a reported basis while the underlying margin has trended down for five quarters.**

**3. 🔴 Cash conversion.** FY26 CFO ₹207cr on PAT ₹370cr; receivables +25%. The "FCF to firm" of ₹296cr adds forex and interest. **A net-cash story needs a CFO/PAT near 1x again in FY27.**

**4. 🔴 Promises arrive late.** Norfund platform (18 months), Hadda (9 months preferred), Indosol (stalled ~2 years), Senegal (4 quarters late), Kuwait (10 months L1-to-LoA). **None is fatal; the pattern is the risk.**

**5. 🟡 Gulf concentration and geopolitics.** ~35-40% of the order book is Middle East, now including Kuwait. Management reports *"no material impact"* from the Gulf conflict and has served force-majeure notices to customers. `MACRO_FACTOR_MAP.md` scenario S4 (crude/Hormuz shock) is live.

**6. 🟡 Thin winning margins.** Yanbu at 0.27%; bids placed *"just less than 1% lower than the next bidder."* **One mis-estimated mega job (Yanbu $272m, Doha ~$200m share) can take a year's margin.**

**7. 🟡 India has stalled.** India cluster revenue was flat in FY26; industrial fell to 12% of the book; the municipal market is L1-driven. Growth is now almost entirely international.

**8. 🟡 Legacy and legal overhangs.** TSGENCO retention (~₹140cr, written off, Supreme Court ruling in WABAG's favour, recovery untimed) is upside; the **₹87cr customs order** and **₹228cr of indirect-tax disputes** (doubled in a year) are downside.

**9. 🟡 Succession.** The CMD is visibly stepping back; the Deputy MD joined in May-2026; the CMD's son now runs the growth engine. **The culture that produced Wriddhi was founder-led.**

**10. 🟡 Low promoter stake.** 19.08% — the market is effectively trusting professional managers and a family-office holder, not an owner-operator.
