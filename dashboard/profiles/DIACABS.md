## 1. Business in Plain English

**Diamond Power makes the cables and conductors that carry electricity from where it is generated to where it is used** — and it makes almost all of the value chain itself, at one site.

**The product ladder is the whole story, because it runs from commodity to specialty:**
- **Low voltage (LV) cables** — the commodity end. Many competitors, thin differentiation.
- **Medium voltage (MV), 11kV to 66kV** — qualification matters, fewer credible suppliers, better realisations.
- **Extra high voltage (EHV), above 66kV** — hardest to qualify, fewest suppliers, best economics.
- **Conductors** — the bare aluminium/alloy wire strung on transmission towers.

**Management's own framing, from the Q1FY27 call:** *"A very large share of our industry volume sits at the commoditized end, low voltage cables, where competitors are many and differentiation is thin. The enduring value sits higher up the voltage ladder… where qualification is difficult, the field of credible suppliers is narrowed, and the realizations are materially better. That is where Diamond Power has chosen to build, and where we intend to lead."*

**The integration is the moat and it is unusually complete:** *"From wire rod through to finished extra high voltage cable, the entire value chain sits under one roof… Very few companies in our industry anywhere in the world carry that degree of integration."* **One 10-acre integrated complex at Vadadala, Savli, Vadodara, Gujarat.**

**🔴 And the history matters more here than at any other name on the watchlist.** This was a **₹3,153cr revenue business in FY14** that lost **₹805cr in FY17**, went through insolvency, and was acquired out of the **NCLT resolution process by a GSEC-led promoter group in 2022.** **It carried a criminal embargo from the CBI and Enforcement Directorate from 2018 until May 2026.** ***Every number below has to be read against that.***

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — India's grid is being rebuilt, not merely extended.** Management lists the engines: renewable evacuation from generation parks, ageing distribution networks being reinforced, smart-city mandates requiring underground urban cabling, and data centres.

**Force 2 — 🟢 Climate resilience has become a procurement driver in its own right, and DIACABS just got paid for it.** *"utilities in flood and cyclone exposed regions convert overhead networks to the underground systems to keep lights on through exactly the weather we saw this quarter."* **The concrete proof: a 1,370-km medium-voltage order for PGVCL's State Disaster Mitigation Fund and Urban Underground programmes across coastal Gujarat.**

**Force 3 — 🟢 Data centres need copper, and copper is a higher-realisation product.** DIACABS has a **₹435cr data-centre order** inside its book and **commissioned a dedicated copper line on 20-Aug-2026** specifically for it.

**Force 4 — Underground beats overhead as cities densify**, and underground means cable rather than conductor — a structural mix shift toward the higher-value product.

**Force 5 — 🟢 Two named policy and channel engines, carried forward from v2's read of the Q3FY26 deck.** **RDSS — the Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme — is a ₹3.03 lakh crore Central-plus-State programme running through FY26 that mandates distribution-network strengthening**, which is MV underground cable demand by another name. And **DICABS was named CREDAI's National Cable Partner for FY26**, against a real-estate pipeline of **~30 million housing units** — the channel behind the retail LV push.

**Force 6 — 🟢 A reconductoring product with a specific technical claim.** v2 recorded the **ECHO conductor** as offering **3x transmission capacity on existing towers without retrofit, at a 30–40% lower reconductoring cost.** *If that holds commercially it is genuinely differentiated; the Q1FY27 call did not update on it, and I have not verified the claim independently.*

**Force 7 — Qualification is the barrier at the top of the ladder.** EHV cable approval is slow and customer-specific, which is precisely why margins survive there.

**Where DIACABS sits.** ₹20,394cr in **Sector 14 (Power T&D Components)** alongside **YASH, SYSTEMATIC and DYCL**. ***Note two watchlist companies are now DIACABS customers: RAJESH POWER placed the ₹195.48cr PGVCL order on 11-Aug, and AURIONPRO placed a ₹52.86cr LoI on 22-Aug for a hyperscale data centre campus at Hyderabad.***

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — 🟢 Single-site integration from wire rod to EHV cable.** Three rod mills, four CCV lines, four silane MV lines operational, all on one 10-acre complex. **The cost consequence is visible: operations cost has fallen from 9% to 6.5% of revenue, with an internal target of 6%** — which management contrasts, fairly, with listed peers.

**Moat 2 — 🟢 A deliberate climb up the voltage ladder, financed and installed.** Two aluminium corrugation lines approved at **₹17cr of capex** convert 33kV capacity into **66kV and 132kV** — *"moving our cable mix decisively up the value curve"* on the same CCV lines. **A sixth CCV line is being ordered with no additional civil or utility capex** because the existing infrastructure has provision for it.

**Moat 3 — 🟢 The copper entry is new capability, not an extension.** *"The Company has hitherto been primarily a manufacturer of aluminium-based cables and conductors. The commissioning of this line marks the Company's entry into copper-based cables, being a higher realisation product."* **1,500 MT a month, commercial production from 20-Aug-2026, aimed at data centres, power plants and industrial installations.**

**Moat 4 — 🟢 An LV project built out of existing assets.** The old rod mill and legacy conductor plant are being converted into a modern LV and control cable facility for aluminium and copper — **a brownfield upgrade with no civil construction cost, ~42,000 km of capacity and revenue potential approaching ₹1,880cr.** Commercial production FY28.

**Moat 5 — Order-book depth relative to size.** **₹3,688cr against FY26 revenue of ₹1,910cr — roughly 2x** — across 12 product lines and 200+ customers, from a base of **670 active customers**.

**Moat 6 — 🟢 A tax shield that is real and quantified.** Accumulated pre-resolution losses mean the tax charge is *"negligible… at least for the next two years."* **The quarterly tax rate has been 0–4% for eight quarters.**

**Moat 7 — 🟢 The balance sheet is now clean, and this is new.** Post-QIP: **net worth +₹691cr**, and *"virtually zero debt on the books"* — ₹75cr of LC and ₹5cr of CC limits, unutilised.

### Where the moat is weaker

- **🔴 The valuation.** **104x trailing earnings and ~10.7x sales**, against a peer set at 24–61x. *§6 works through whether the tax shield justifies it.*
- **🔴 Sequentially, the business did not grow.** **Q4FY26 ₹696cr → Q1FY27 ₹690cr.** The +129% is a year-on-year comparison against a much smaller base. **Management attributes the flat quarter to an early monsoon and estimates the rain cost ₹70–80cr of top line** — plausible and specific, but it is an explanation, not a result.
- **🔴 The FY27 guidance needs a very large step-up.** ₹4,300–4,500cr from a ₹690cr first quarter means **Q2–Q4 must average ₹1,200–1,270cr, roughly 1.8x the Q1 run-rate.** Management's own words: *"the balance sheet of the year needs to run at close to twice the pace of the first quarter."*
- **🔴 ₹350cr of the QIP — 22% of it — goes to repaying an unsecured promoter loan.** *Disclosed, legitimate, and still the fact that public money retired a related-party liability.*
- **🔴 Cash generation is poor.** **FY26 CFO −₹80cr, free cash flow −₹162cr, CFO/OP −36%.**
- **⚠️ ₹957cr of legacy receivables sit on the books** from the pre-CIRP era, with recovery only now being pursued.
- **⚠️ Margin is metal-dependent and management says so.** *"aluminum was $3,800 [in May]. And by the end of June, it came down to $3,000."*
- **⚠️ NSE series BE** — the trade-for-trade/surveillance segment, which constrains liquidity.

### Debt structure — the ₹2,536cr line is not what it looks like

**From the call, in answer to an analyst's direct question about ₹2,400cr of long-term borrowings at March 2026:**
> *"that was **INR1,900 crores of bonds, 0.01% bonds payable after 30 years. These bonds are owned by the promoter.** The next part was a **INR400 crores unsecured loan from the promoter, which has been paid back post-QIP.** There is no debt as such other than the residual value of those bonds if discounted on a net present value, **they stood at INR47 crores in March 2026.** … This was the **unsustainable debt of the company in the NCLT plan, which was converted to bonds payable after 30 years.**"*

***So: ₹1,900cr of face value with a ₹47cr present value, held by the promoter, repayable in 2052 at a 0.01% coupon.*** **That single item is most of why book value was negative ₹11.5 a share, and it is economically close to equity rather than debt.** **Working debt today: ₹75cr of LC and ₹5cr of CC limits, both unutilised.**
