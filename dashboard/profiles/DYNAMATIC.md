## 1. Business in Plain English

Dynamatic makes three unrelated things across three continents, and the market has been trying to figure out how to price the sum for two decades.

**Aerospace (48% of revenue):** at Bangalore's Aerospace SEZ and Swindon (UK), Dynamatic builds airframe assemblies — Airbus A320 Flap Track Beams (single-source globally), Airbus A220 doors, Boeing P8 sub-assemblies, Bell helicopter components, Dassault Falcon fuel tanks. Highest-margin business. Long-cycle contracts.

**Hydraulics (30% of revenue):** the original business — hydraulic gear pumps for tractors and construction equipment. World's largest hydraulic gear pump maker, ~70% share of India's organised tractor pump market. Customers: John Deere, Cummins, Escorts, Mahindra, JCB. Also has a UK arm (Swindon) selling into European agri and industrial markets.

**Metallurgy (22% of revenue):** Eisenwerk Erla GmbH in Germany — a ferrous foundry making high-precision castings for turbochargers, exhaust manifolds, and other performance-critical automotive parts. Customers: BMW, Audi, Daimler, VW, BorgWarner, MAN.

The three businesses share almost nothing — different customers, different cycles, different geographies, different technologies. What holds them together is that Dr. Udayant Malhoutra bought and built each of them over 40 years, and hasn't sold any of them. That's it.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

Three different industry answers, and this fragmentation is why the market has never given Dynamatic a clean multiple.

**Aerospace ESP+manufacturing** — same industry logic as AXISCADES and Azad. Global OEMs need Tier-1 airframe suppliers, qualification cycles are years, contracts are 5-8 years and sticky. Demand backdrop is strong (Boeing + Airbus backlog 15,000+ aircraft). Margins in the 20-25% EBITDA range at scale.

**Hydraulics** — a mature, mid-single-digit-growth industry. Tractor sales in India grow with monsoons + rural income. Global demand grows with construction cycles. Margins 10-15% EBITDA in normal conditions. The Indian domestic business is healthy; the UK business has been contracting for 5+ years because European agri-mech demand is structurally weaker.

**Metallurgy** — the hardest business right now. German automotive foundries are structurally impaired: energy costs rose 3x post-Ukraine, ICE-vehicle content is declining as OEMs shift to EVs (which need less casting), and Chinese competitors have entered aggressively. Eisenwerk Erla is a good specific asset in a bad industry.

The sum-of-parts investor question: **should Dynamatic keep all three?** The answer inside management is clearly "yes, we're restructuring not exiting." The answer from the market — proxied by valuation — is closer to "we don't know, we're paying only for aerospace."

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

Aerospace alone is genuinely differentiated. **First Indian private company to become a Tier-1 supplier to Airbus.** Single-source globally on the A320 Flap Track Beam for the last ~15 years — an unusual position that requires an OEM's active decision to entrust one supplier with a flight-critical part. That's a durable, real, defensible position.

Hydraulics is a good business at scale — 70% market share in India's organised tractor pump market is meaningful. But it's not high-margin, growth is slow, and there's no obvious moat beyond long customer relationships.

Metallurgy has no moat visible in the numbers. FY26 EBITDA margin: 0.8%. EBIT: negative ₹110 Cr. This is capital being consumed, not deployed. Management commentary has said the same thing for at least 4 quarters now: "focus on high-margin product mix, rationalization of low-margin products, development of aerospace castings and forgings."

**The honest read:** Dynamatic *has* one great business (Aerospace, ~₹775 Cr revenue at 23% margin), one decent business (Hydraulics, ~₹487 Cr at 7% margin), and one structurally-broken business (Metallurgy, ~₹358 Cr at ~0.8% margin). The ValuePickr community has been asking, essentially, "why not just sell Metallurgy?" for years. The answer from management has been "restructuring, patience." Believers say the German foundry can be pivoted to aerospace castings. Skeptics say four years of restructuring should have shown some P&L conversion by now.

## 3.5 Management Track Record & Promoter Background

**Promoter:** Dr. Udayant Malhoutra, CEO & Managing Director. **40+ year tenure** — one of the longest in Indian mid-caps. Holds primarily via **JKM Holdings Pvt Ltd** (16.38% of Dynamatic). Total promoter shareholding: 41.5% as of Mar-26 (slightly declining from 41.9% Jun-24 — not a red flag, but not accumulating either).

**Other listed entities of same promoter group:** Based on public filings I could locate, **none**. JKM Group's holdings are private — JKM DAE RIM Automotive, JKM Holdings, JKM Offshore India. Dynamatic is the sole listed vehicle. This is a plus for accountability (no channel for related-party transfers to a group listco) and a plus for focus (no CEO attention drift).

**Board independence:** 5 of 8 directors are independent (Q4 FY26 disclosure) — was 3 of 7 in Q1 FY26 (implying 2 new independent directors added mid-year, both an improvement in optics and evidence of governance maturation).

**Financial delivery — 5-year lookback:**

- **Revenue CAGR FY22-FY26: 6.5%.** ₹1,254 Cr → ₹1,621 Cr over 5 years. **This is slow.** Same period, Azad grew 5x. AXISCADES grew ~2x with a full restructuring underway. For a company positioned in aerospace/precision components with the sector tailwinds we've seen, 6.5% CAGR is a delivery failure at the aggregate level.
- **EBITDA margin FY22 → FY26: 13.5% → 11.3%.** Margins have *declined* 220 bps over 5 years while every peer has expanded margins. This is not sector-headwind, it's mix-drag from Metallurgy and Hydraulics UK.
- **Net Debt/EBITDA FY22 → Q4 FY26: 2.1x → 2.5x.** Leverage rising modestly while cash flow struggles.
- **Interest coverage: 1.4-2.2x range.** Very low. Small EBITDA swing puts debt service at risk. This is the single hardest metric to reconcile with the current market cap.

**Governance flags:** none serious found. No pledge on promoter shares (as far as public disclosure). No auditor changes flagged. Related-party transactions appear modest and disclosed in the annual report. Board added independent directors during FY26 (positive).

**Bottom line on management:** Dr. Malhoutra is technically qualified (doctorate, decades of industry) and has been a stable steward. But **five-year delivery has been mediocre relative to opportunity**. The aerospace business is proof he can build a real asset. The Metallurgy business has been in "restructuring" long enough that at some point restructuring becomes the strategy, not a transition. **Trust him to preserve, not to compound aggressively.**

## 4. Numbers Decoded

**Revenue FY26: ₹1,621 Cr (+15.5% YoY).** Segment breakdown: Aerospace ₹776 Cr (+27.6%), Hydraulics ₹487 Cr (+6.4%), Metallurgy ₹358 Cr (+6.9%). Interpretation: **only Aerospace is growing at a rate that makes strategic sense**. Hydraulics + Metallurgy together (52% of revenue) are growing at 6-7% — barely above inflation, no margin uplift.

**EBITDA FY26: ₹183 Cr (11.3% margin, flat YoY).** Breakdown: Aerospace ₹178 Cr (22.9%), Hydraulics ₹36 Cr (7.4% — recovering from prior year's 5.3%), Metallurgy ₹3 Cr (0.8%). **Aerospace alone is ~97% of consolidated EBITDA.** Everything else is a rounding error or a drag. The market has understood this — the price is priced for Aerospace and pays little for the other two.

**Q4 FY26 PAT ₹19 Cr (or ₹13 Cr reported after exceptional).** FY26 PAT ₹32 Cr reported, ₹60 Cr excluding ₹28 Cr exceptional items (portfolio restructuring costs). **This is where the current valuation problem lives — at ₹6,700 Cr Mcap on ₹60 Cr adjusted PAT, trailing P/E is 112x.** For a company growing revenue at mid-teens with margins declining, this is untenable unless something changes structurally.

**Net Debt ₹4,614 Cr, D/E 0.6x, Net Debt/EBITDA 2.5x, Interest Coverage 2.0x.** Debt is manageable but not comfortable. Finance costs ₹58 Cr in FY26 essentially wiped out reported PAT. **Any EBITDA weakness pushes this into distress zone.**

**Segment margin evolution FY22-FY26:** Aerospace 22-27% band (declined recently); Hydraulics 4-8% (volatile); Metallurgy 0-4% (structurally declining). Understanding the volatility here matters more than the absolute numbers.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

The Dynamatic mental model is uncomfortable because it doesn't compound simply. Three parallel value streams:

**Aerospace flywheel (positive, working):** win Airbus/Boeing program → build dedicated production line → deliver first article → ramp to serial production → capture 5-8 years of revenue → win adjacent program. A220 doors delivering ahead of schedule + Hutchinson MoA (Q4 FY26) for aerospace composites are recent wins. This part of the business could realistically hit ₹1,200 Cr revenue by FY29 at 20-22% EBITDA (assuming margin holds).

**Hydraulics flywheel (mixed):** India Hydraulics grows with tractor + construction cycles at ~10-12%. UK Hydraulics contracts by 5-10% annually and is being "rationalized." Net for consolidated Hydraulics: 5-8% growth at 7-10% EBITDA. Not exciting, not disastrous.

**Metallurgy flywheel (broken):** German automotive EV shift → declining ICE castings demand → energy cost squeeze → foundry underutilised → cash burn. Attempts to pivot to aerospace castings have been ongoing for 3+ years without visible revenue conversion. **The realistic path forward is either (a) genuine turnaround via aerospace pivot by FY29 or (b) eventual divestment.** Neither is cheap or fast.

**Sum-of-parts arithmetic:**
- Aerospace FY29 potential: ₹1,200 Cr × 22% = ₹264 Cr EBITDA × 15x EV/EBITDA = ₹3,960 Cr
- Hydraulics FY29 potential: ₹550 Cr × 9% = ₹50 Cr × 8x = ₹400 Cr
- Metallurgy FY29 potential: ₹400 Cr × 5% (optimistic recovery) = ₹20 Cr × 6x = ₹120 Cr
- Net debt: ₹4,600 Cr today, likely ₹3,500 Cr FY29 after working capital normalization
- **SOTP equity value FY29: ₹4,480 Cr - ₹3,500 Cr = ₹980 Cr**

That's below current market cap of ₹6,700 Cr. Meaning: **either Aerospace has to double from current base to justify the price, or Metallurgy has to genuinely recover to 10%+ EBITDA, or both.**

The bull case is that both happen because Aerospace is compounding at 25%+ and Metallurgy's aerospace pivot works. The bear case is that Aerospace continues at 20% growth with declining margins and Metallurgy stays at 1-3% margins. The realistic case sits somewhere between and doesn't clearly justify the current multiple.

## 6. Why the Market Is Paying This Multiple

At CMP ₹10,397, Mcap ~₹6,700 Cr, on FY26 adjusted PAT ~₹60 Cr — trailing P/E is 112x. On FY27 estimates of ~₹90-100 Cr PAT (assuming Aerospace holds margins), forward P/E is 65-75x. **This is priced like a high-conviction aerospace pure-play. It is not one.**

Three factors explain the multiple:

1. **The Aerospace tailwind trade.** The market is buying Dynamatic largely for its Aerospace segment and assigning near-zero value to Hydraulics and Metallurgy. Aerospace alone at ₹776 Cr rev × 22% EBITDA = ₹178 Cr, and applying a 30x EV/EBITDA (in-line with peers like Data Patterns / MTAR), that segment alone is worth ~₹5,300 Cr. Subtract net debt of ₹4,600 Cr and add back Hydraulics + Metallurgy at ~₹1,500 Cr optimistic → equity value ~₹2,200 Cr. **Current Mcap is 3x that.** The market is paying much more than fair sum-of-parts, so either the tailwind narrative is overpriced or the market expects Hydraulics + Metallurgy to inflect.

2. **Restructuring optionality.** If Metallurgy actually turns around, the segment could add ₹30-50 Cr EBITDA and get a small re-rating. This is being paid for at maybe 15-20% probability, which is a reasonable price for optionality.

3. **The founder-longevity premium.** Dr. Malhoutra has run this company for 40 years. That is worth *something* in terms of continuity and lack of surprise, but at 112x trailing it's paying premium for a founder who has delivered 6.5% revenue CAGR over 5 years. That's a strange price to pay.

**Compared to peers:** Data Patterns ~70x forward earnings (higher growth, pure-play defence, no drag segments). MTAR ~55x (pure-play, high growth). Azad Engineering ~65-75x forward (dramatically better OB coverage, cleaner story). Dynamatic at 65-75x forward is **priced comparable to focused, high-growth peers despite carrying two segments that are structurally impaired**. This does not compute long-term.

**What is priced in that gives me real pause:** the market seems to assume both that Aerospace continues 25%+ growth *and* that Metallurgy resolves without a further write-down. If either fails, the multiple has ~30-50% downside to a defensible SOTP level.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Bad-luck outcomes (macro/cycle/timing):**
- German recession or Eurozone GDP contraction → Metallurgy losses widen further, could require a Germany-restructuring provision.
- Airbus / Boeing production cut on A320 or A220 → immediate Aerospace revenue hit.
- Currency: significant EUR + GBP + USD exposure; INR appreciation of 5% compresses margins by 100-150 bps.
- Tractor cycle downturn in India → Hydraulics revenue softens.

**We-were-wrong-about-the-business (moat, execution, sector):**
- **Metallurgy fails to turn around by FY29.** This is the number-one risk. If the aerospace-casting pivot doesn't produce meaningful revenue in FY27 or FY28, the segment ends up needing a write-down. That's a 25-30% derating trigger.
- **Aerospace margin compression continues.** FY26 Aerospace EBITDA margin fell from 26% to 22.9% despite +27.6% revenue growth. If this trend continues, the Aerospace-story valuation base collapses.
- **UK Hydraulics restructuring never really finishes.** Management has said "final stages" for 4 quarters. If UK Hydraulics is a permanent drag, consolidated Hydraulics margins stay in single digits forever.
- **Debt servicing gets harder.** Interest coverage is 2.0x. Any EBITDA slip pushes this near 1.5x, at which point banks start asking questions and possibly asking for equity infusion.
- **Founder-transition risk.** Dr. Malhoutra is 40+ years into CEO tenure. He's the strategic decision-maker. Succession is not publicly discussed. Any health event or transition would create real uncertainty.
- **Governance / capital allocation.** For a founder-run company with 40+ year tenure, the historical capital allocation record is only okay. Multiple businesses acquired over decades that haven't been rationalised. If the current restructuring is another false start, it fits a pattern.

## 8. 30-Second Memory Hook

> "Dynamatic is a veteran all-rounder in his 40th season — still a genuine spinning threat with the old ball (Aerospace, single-source on Airbus A320 Flap Track Beam for 15 years) but the batting form has slipped (Hydraulics UK), and his fielding at slip is now dropping catches most Test wicketkeepers would take (Metallurgy Germany, 0.8% margin). The captain (Dr. Malhoutra, in charge for 40 seasons) refuses to drop him from any format — believes the batting will come back. Selectors (the market) are still paying him a captain's fee because of the spinning form alone. The honest question: could he play in the T20 side (pure aerospace) and let someone else worry about the rest?"
