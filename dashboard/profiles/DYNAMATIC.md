## 1. Business in Plain English

**Dynamatic makes three unrelated things across three continents, and the market has been trying to figure out how to price the sum for two decades.**

**Aerospace (48% of revenue).** At Bangalore's Aerospace SEZ and Swindon (UK), Dynamatic builds airframe assemblies — **Airbus A320 Flap Track Beams (single-source globally)**, **Airbus A220 doors**, Boeing P8 sub-assemblies, Bell helicopter components, Dassault Falcon fuel tanks. Highest-margin business, long-cycle contracts.

**Hydraulics (27% of revenue).** The original business — hydraulic gear pumps for tractors and construction equipment. **World's largest hydraulic gear pump maker, ~70% of India's organised tractor pump market.** Customers: John Deere, Cummins, Escorts, Mahindra, JCB. Also a UK arm at Swindon selling into European agri and industrial markets.

**Metallurgy (25% of revenue).** **Eisenwerk Erla GmbH** in Germany — a ferrous foundry making high-precision castings for turbochargers, exhaust manifolds and other performance-critical automotive parts. Customers: BMW, Audi, Daimler, VW, BorgWarner, MAN.

**The three share almost nothing** — different customers, cycles, geographies and technologies. **What holds them together is that Dr. Udayant Malhoutra bought and built each over 40 years and has not sold any of them. That is it.**

**The snapshot:**
- **8 facilities across India, the UK (Swindon, Bristol) and Germany (Schwarzenberg).**
- **Q1FY27: revenue ₹424.8cr (+14.5%), EBITDA ₹55.1cr (+45.9%) at 13.0% (+280bps), PAT ₹20.8cr (+93.0%).**
- **🔴 But constant-currency revenue growth was 3.9%** — the company's own disclosure. **₹39.3cr of the ₹53.9cr revenue increase was translation.**
- **🟢 Hydraulics EBITDA margin 3.4% → 12.5%** as the UK restructuring flowed through.
- **Ten-year revenue CAGR 1%. ROE 6.62%. ROCE 9.98%.**

**Big picture in one sentence:** Dynamatic is a genuinely differentiated aerospace Tier-1 — the first Indian private company to reach that status with Airbus — **bolted to a slow hydraulics business and a structurally impaired German foundry**, where **the operating turnaround is real, the reported growth is mostly the rupee, and the stock trades at 130x on a 6.62% ROE.**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Three different industry answers, and this fragmentation is why the market has never given Dynamatic a clean multiple.**

**Aerospace.** Global OEMs need Tier-1 airframe suppliers; qualification cycles run years, contracts 5-8 years, and they are sticky. **The demand backdrop is strong — Boeing and Airbus backlogs exceed 15,000 aircraft.** Margins reach **20-25% EBITDA** at scale.

**Hydraulics.** A mature, mid-single-digit-growth industry. Indian tractor sales track monsoons and rural income; global demand tracks construction. **Margins 10-15% EBITDA in normal conditions.** **The Indian domestic business is healthy; the UK business has been contracting for 5+ years because European agri-mech demand is structurally weaker.**

**Metallurgy — the hardest business right now.** **German automotive foundries are structurally impaired:** energy costs rose 3x post-Ukraine, ICE-vehicle content is declining as OEMs shift to EVs (which need less casting), and Chinese competitors have entered aggressively. **Eisenwerk Erla is a good specific asset in a bad industry.**

**The sum-of-parts question is: should Dynamatic keep all three?** Management's answer is clearly *"yes, we're restructuring not exiting."* **The market's answer, proxied by valuation, is closer to *"we don't know, we're paying only for aerospace."***

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Aerospace alone is genuinely differentiated.** **The first Indian private company to become a Tier-1 supplier to Airbus.** **Single-source globally on the A320 Flap Track Beam for roughly fifteen years** — a position that requires an OEM's active decision to entrust one supplier with a flight-critical part. **That is durable, real and defensible.**

**The A220 doors programme is the proof it can be repeated.** Q4FY26 delivered *"successful delivery readiness of the first complete ship-set of all eight Airbus A220 doors from Bengaluru facility, **completed ahead of schedule**"*, and Q1FY27 reports it *"made steady progress"*, supported by a sheet-metal and detail-parts ramp at wholly-owned **Dynamatic Manufacturing Limited**.

**Hydraulics is a good business at scale** — 70% of India's organised tractor pump market is meaningful. **But it is not high-margin, growth is slow, and there is no obvious moat beyond long customer relationships.** *What changed in Q1FY27 is that the UK restructuring finally reached the P&L — margin 3.4% → 12.5%.*

**Metallurgy has no moat visible in the numbers.** FY26 EBITDA margin **0.8%** (₹3cr on ₹358cr). **This is capital being consumed, not deployed.** Management commentary has said the same thing for at least four quarters: *"focus on high-margin product mix, rationalization of low-margin products, development of aerospace castings and forgings."*

**🆕 Dynauton Systems — a new unmanned-systems arm.** Incorporated 20-Apr-2026; **MoU with Aerodata AG (Germany)** announced 23-Apr-2026 to develop *"a new unmanned, airborne surveillance and reconnaissance solution for the Indian region."* **Strategically coherent** — Dynamatic already builds aerostructures for Airbus, Boeing, Bell, Dassault, Deutsche Aircraft and HAL. **But it is an MoU with no disclosed value, timeline or funding, and India's ISR-drone market is crowded. Optionality, not a forecastable line.** *(Compare PARAS's ₹6,200cr semiconductor MoU this cycle — Dynauton is far smaller and far closer to the core.)*

**Where the moat is weaker — and the honest read of the whole company:**

**Dynamatic has one great business, one decent business and one structurally broken one.** Aerospace ~₹775cr at 23% margin; Hydraulics ~₹487cr, now improving; Metallurgy ~₹358cr at ~0.8% FY26 margin. **The ValuePickr community has been asking "why not just sell Metallurgy?" for years.** Management's answer has been *"restructuring, patience."* **Believers say the German foundry can pivot to aerospace castings. Sceptics say four years of restructuring should have shown some P&L conversion by now.**

- **🔴 Growth.** **Ten-year revenue CAGR 1%**, five-year 6.5% (₹1,254cr → ₹1,621cr). **Over the same period Azad grew 5x and AXISCADES roughly 2x with a full restructuring underway.**
- **🔴 Margin drift.** **FY22 → FY26 EBITDA margin 13.5% → 11.3%**, down 220bps while peers expanded. **Mix-drag from Metallurgy and UK Hydraulics, not a sector headwind.**
- **🔴 Returns.** ROE **6.62%**, ROCE **9.98%**.
- **🟡 Leverage.** Net debt/EBITDA 2.1x (FY22) → **2.4x**, and **net debt is still rising in absolute terms**.
- **🟡 No earnings call since February 2024.**
- **🟡 A tiny shareholder base — 25,288** — a function of an ₹11,188 share price, which makes the stock structurally illiquid and volatile.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**The Dynamatic mental model is uncomfortable because it does not compound simply. Three parallel value streams:**

**Aerospace flywheel — positive, working.** Win an Airbus/Boeing programme → build a dedicated line → deliver first article → ramp to serial production → capture 5-8 years of revenue → win an adjacent programme. **A220 doors delivered ahead of schedule** and the **Hutchinson MoA (Q4FY26) for aerospace composites** are recent wins. This segment could realistically reach **₹1,200cr of revenue by FY29 at 20-22% EBITDA.**

**Hydraulics flywheel — mixed, and improving.** India Hydraulics grows with tractor and construction cycles at ~10-12%; UK Hydraulics has contracted 5-10% annually and is being rationalised. **Net consolidated: 5-8% growth at 7-10% EBITDA — not exciting, not disastrous.** *Q1FY27's 12.5% margin is the first evidence the UK drag is being removed rather than merely managed.*

**Metallurgy flywheel — broken.** German automotive EV shift → declining ICE castings demand → energy-cost squeeze → foundry underutilised → cash burn. **Attempts to pivot to aerospace castings have been ongoing for 3+ years without visible revenue conversion.** The realistic path is either **(a) genuine turnaround via the aerospace pivot by FY29, or (b) eventual divestment. Neither is cheap or fast.**

### The sum-of-parts arithmetic, and why it is uncomfortable

- **Aerospace FY29 potential:** ₹1,200cr × 22% = **₹264cr EBITDA × 15x = ₹3,960cr**
- **Hydraulics FY29:** ₹550cr × 9% = ₹50cr × 8x = **₹400cr**
- **Metallurgy FY29 (optimistic recovery):** ₹400cr × 5% = ₹20cr × 6x = **₹120cr**
- Less net debt, assumed normalising

**Add it up: roughly ₹4,480cr of enterprise value, less net debt of ~₹473cr, gives equity value near ₹4,000cr against a market capitalisation of ₹7,598cr.**

> ⚠️ **The initiation profile ran this same arithmetic with net debt at "₹4,600cr today, likely ₹3,500cr by FY29" — a ₹ million / ₹ crore unit slip — and reached an FY29 equity value of ₹980cr.** It also ran a second version valuing Aerospace alone at ~₹5,300cr, adding **~₹1,500cr** for Hydraulics and Metallurgy optimistically, subtracting the same overstated net debt, and arriving at **~₹2,200cr** of equity value against a ₹6,700cr market cap — concluding *"current Mcap is 3x that."* **Corrected for the unit slip, the gap is far smaller than v1 implied, but it points the same way.** **Either Aerospace has to roughly double from the current base, or Metallurgy has to genuinely recover to a 10%+ EBITDA margin — or both.**

**The bull case is that both happen.** **The bear case is that Aerospace continues at ~20% growth with declining margins and Metallurgy stays at 1-3%.** *The realistic case sits between, and does not clearly justify the current multiple.*

**And Q1FY27 sharpens the question rather than answering it.** The margin improvement is real and constant-currency EBITDA still grew ~32%. **But the revenue engine underneath grew 3.9%.** *A sum-of-parts that needs Aerospace to double is much harder to underwrite when the consolidated top line compounds at 1% over a decade.*

## 6. Why the Market Is Paying This Multiple

**At ₹11,188: 130x trailing, 8.9x book, on 9.98% ROCE and 6.62% ROE, after a 74% year.**

**Three factors explain the multiple:**

**1. The Aerospace tailwind trade.** The market is buying Dynamatic largely for Aerospace and assigning near-zero value to Hydraulics and Metallurgy. **Aerospace alone at ~₹776cr revenue × 22% EBITDA = ₹178cr, at a 30x EV/EBITDA in line with Data Patterns or MTAR, is worth ~₹5,300cr.** Add Hydraulics and Metallurgy optimistically and subtract net debt, and **the market cap is well above a defensible sum-of-parts.** *Either the tailwind narrative is overpriced, or the market expects Hydraulics and Metallurgy to inflect.*

**2. Restructuring optionality.** If Metallurgy genuinely turns, the segment could add ₹30-50cr of EBITDA and earn a small re-rating. **This is being paid for at maybe 15-20% probability — a reasonable price for optionality.** *And Q1FY27's EBIT crossing zero is the first datum in its favour in three years.*

**3. The founder-longevity premium.** Forty years of continuity is worth something. **But at 130x it is paying a premium for a founder who has delivered a 6.5% five-year and 1% ten-year revenue CAGR. That is a strange price to pay.**

**Against peers:** **Data Patterns ~70x forward** (higher growth, pure-play defence, no drag segments), **MTAR ~55x**, and on FY27 estimates of **~₹90-100cr of PAT** Dynamatic itself sits at roughly **65-75x forward**, **Azad ~126x trailing** (dramatically better order-book coverage). **Dynamatic is priced comparable to — or above — focused, high-growth peers while carrying two segments that are structurally impaired.**

**The earnings base is small and volatile, which is why the trailing multiple looks extreme.** FY26 PAT was **₹32cr** (quarters of ₹11 / ₹3 / ₹6 / ₹13cr), and **Q1FY27 alone delivered ₹20.8cr — roughly two-thirds of the entire prior year.** **If the Q1 run-rate holds, the trailing multiple compresses very fast.** *That is the bull case in one sentence, and it depends on operating leverage over a fixed ~₹60cr annual interest bill.*

**What is priced in that gives me real pause:** the market seems to assume **both** that Aerospace continues compounding **and** that Metallurgy resolves without a further write-down. **If either fails, there is 30-50% downside to a defensible sum-of-parts level.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — 🔴 The reported growth is mostly currency.** **14.5% reported, 3.9% constant currency**; ₹39.3cr of a ₹53.9cr increase was FX (EUR +13.9%, GBP +11.8%, USD +10.9%). **With 8 facilities across India, the UK and Germany, a reversing rupee turns this tailwind into a headwind on a business whose ten-year revenue CAGR is 1%.** *EBITDA is far less affected — the margin story survives the adjustment even though the growth story does not.*

**Risk 2 — 🔴 Metallurgy fails to turn around by FY29.** **The number-one business risk.** If the aerospace-casting pivot produces no meaningful revenue in FY27 or FY28, the segment needs a write-down — **a 25-30% de-rating trigger.** *Q1FY27's EBIT crossing zero is encouraging but at a 3.7% EBITDA margin it proves very little.*

**Risk 3 — 🔴 Valuation.** 130x trailing, 8.9x book, on 6.62% ROE, after +74% in a year. **The base case loses money.**

**Risk 4 — 🟡 Aerospace margin compression.** FY26 Aerospace EBITDA margin **fell from 26.0% to 22.9% despite +27.6% revenue growth.** **If that trend resumes, the entire Aerospace-story valuation base collapses.** *Q1FY27's 23.8% is a recovery, not yet a reversal.*

**Risk 5 — 🟡 UK Hydraulics restructuring never really finishes.** *"Final stages"* was said for four quarters. **Q1FY27's 12.5% margin is one quarter, and the deck still describes product-line rationalisation between Bangalore and Swindon as ongoing rather than complete.**

**Risk 6 — 🟡 Debt servicing.** Coverage has improved to 2.4x **entirely on earnings**, with **net debt still rising in absolute terms**. **Any EBITDA slip pushes coverage back toward 1.5x, at which point banks start asking questions and possibly asking for equity.**

**Risk 7 — 🟡 Founder-transition risk.** Dr. Malhoutra is 40+ years into his tenure and is the strategic decision-maker. **The five-year reappointment reduces near-term uncertainty but does not address succession, which is not publicly discussed.**

**Risk 8 — 🟡 No earnings call since February 2024.** **The presentation is excellent; the absence of a forum to question it is the gap** — and the growth problem is precisely what nobody can ask about.

**Risk 9 — 🟡 Capital allocation history.** For a founder-run company of 40+ years, the record is **only okay** — multiple businesses acquired over decades and not rationalised. **If the current restructuring is another false start, it fits a pattern.**

**Risk 10 — ⚪ German recession or Eurozone contraction** widens Metallurgy losses and could require a Germany-restructuring provision.

**Risk 11 — ⚪ Airbus or Boeing production cuts** on the A320 or A220 hit Aerospace revenue immediately.

**Risk 12 — ⚪ Indian tractor cycle downturn** softens Hydraulics.

**Risk 14 — ⚪ Currency, the other way.** Significant EUR, GBP and USD exposure means **a 5% rupee appreciation compresses margins by 100-150bps** — the mirror image of Risk 1.

**Risk 13 — ⚪ Illiquidity.** **25,288 shareholders** at an ₹11,188 share price makes the stock structurally volatile.
