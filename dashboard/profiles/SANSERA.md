## 1. Business in Plain English

Sansera makes the **small, highly-stressed metal parts that sit inside things that must not fail.** Connecting rods, crankshafts, rocker arms, gear-shifter forks — the components that take the beating in an engine. It forges them, machines them, heat-treats them and finishes them, mostly under one roof, which is the unusual part: **most Indian component makers do one of those steps and buy the rest.**

**The useful way to hold it is three businesses stacked on one shop floor:**

1. **Auto-ICE** — the legacy engine business. **65.4% of Q1FY27 sales** *(deck slide 31)*, down from **83.4% in FY21.** Still growing 20.8% YoY. This is the cash engine.
2. **Auto Tech-Agnostic & xEV** — suspension, braking, chassis and EV-specific parts that do not care what powers the vehicle. **13.7% of sales**, +22.2% YoY, and within it *"the xEV business growing at nearly twice the pace of the tech-agnostic business"* *(earnings release, 12-Aug-2026)*.
3. **ADS — Aerospace, Defence & Semiconductor.** **14.2% of Q1FY27 sales**, up from **3.8% in FY21 and 9.6% in FY26.** Grew **more than 3x year-on-year.** This is the entire re-rating.

**The analogy that actually fits.** Sansera is a **precision job shop that has been slowly moving up the difficulty curve for forty years** — from a connecting rod for a Bajaj motorcycle, to a structural part for an Airbus fuselage, to an ultra-precision machined assembly for the equipment that makes semiconductors. **The machines are broadly the same. What changes is the tolerance, the certification burden, and the price you can charge for both.**

**The 30-second snapshot:**
- **Bengaluru** · founded **1981** · IPO **Sep-2021** · **87 customers** · 16+ plants in India, one in **Sweden**
- **FY26 revenue ₹3,498 cr (+16.0%)** · EBITDA **₹632 cr (18.1%)** · PAT **₹327 cr (+50.7%)**
- **Q1FY27 revenue ₹1,021 cr (+33.3%)** — first quarter ever above ₹1,000cr · EBITDA **19.2%** (+200bps) · **normalised PAT ₹100 cr (+59%)**
- **🟢 ADS unexecuted order backlog ₹5,700 cr, executable by FY31** — up from ₹3,800cr in Feb-2026
- **🟢 Top-5 customer concentration 59.2% (FY21) → 44.5% (FY26)**
- **🔴 Promoter holding 36.02% (FY22) → 29.21% (Jun-2026)** — most of it a QIP, but **the last 0.89pp is unexplained**
- **63.9x earnings · 7.7x book · ROCE 14.1% · ROE 11.5% · dividend yield 0.09%**
- **Stock +203% in one year**, and **5.6% below its all-time high**

**Big picture in one sentence.** Sansera is **a genuinely well-run auto component maker executing a real and verifiable pivot into aerospace and semiconductor precision machining** — and the entire question is whether **63.9 times earnings on an 11.5% return on equity** already contains the whole of that pivot, plus the part that has not happened yet.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — OEMs are outsourcing the hard parts.** Management's framing on the Q1FY27 call is the clearest statement of the thesis: *"our OEM customers across the board are talking about hyper-growth, high vehicle volumes, and continued consolidation of supply chain, better capitalized, more capable suppliers."* **Consolidation is the operative word: fewer, larger suppliers take a bigger share of a growing pie.** Sansera's specific version — two-wheeler OEMs outsourcing crankshaft manufacture, historically done in-house — is what the Pantnagar expansion is for.

**Force 2 — Aerospace has a decade-long backlog and India is where the capacity is being added.** Hari Krishnan, on the Q1FY27 call: *"The ultimate two customers are Boeing and Airbus… The aircraft order backlogs are increasing as we are talking month-on-month."* Sansera does not sell to Boeing or Airbus directly — it sells to their tier-1 suppliers and engine makers.

**Force 3 — 🟢 The semiconductor equipment cycle, and this is the one carrying the numbers.** The customers here are **semicon equipment manufacturers**, not chip fabs — the companies that build the tools. Management's read of their order books:
> *"the current boom is largely significantly driven by the AI demand. AI demand, data centers resulting in a massive surge in demand for chips, which in turn translates into demand for the equipment suppliers, which is our primary market… it looks like they are very, very busy or they see themselves not able to meet demand until FY29, FY '30."* *(Hari Krishnan, Q1FY27 call)*

**Force 4 — Powertrain-agnostic content is a hedge, not a bet.** Suspension, chassis and braking parts are sold into ICE, hybrid and EV platforms alike. **This is why the EV transition is not the existential risk for Sansera that it is for a pure engine-parts maker** — but note that Auto-ICE is still 65% of revenue, so the hedge is partial.

**Force 5 — Defence indigenisation and offset requirements.** Currently the smallest leg: *"We supply to small batch quantities to ISRO, HAL. We do a bit of exports to companies in Israel. But now we want to up our game to the next level."*

**Where SANSERA sits.** ₹23,807cr, the **second-largest name in Sector 3** and the sector's clearest aerospace-adjacency story. **AZAD is the pure-play comparison** (₹18,248cr at 131x); **HAPPY FORGINGS the pure-play auto forging comparison** (₹20,916cr at 63.9x).

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — 🟢 Vertical integration is the actual moat, and it is why ADS was possible at all.** Forging, machining, heat treatment, and now **surface treatment in-house.** Preetham on why the new surface-treatment line matters: *"Surface treatment is a defining capability that allows us to complete the production within the single facility… as we enter larger and more complex structural paths in this segment, having this process in-house will give us better speed, quality control, and reduced external dependency."* **In aerospace, every external process step is a separate NADCAP-certified vendor and a separate schedule risk.**

**Moat 2 — 🟢 The customer concentration has genuinely fallen, and that is rare.** *(Deck slide 32.)*

| | FY21 | FY23 | FY25 | **FY26** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer 1 | 20.7% | 14.3% | 13.7% | **13.0%** |
| **Top 5 combined** | **59.2%** | **50.8%** | **46.2%** | **44.5%** |

**Most component makers' concentration gets worse as they scale. Sansera's improved by 15 points in five years while revenue more than doubled.**

**Moat 3 — Qualification lead times cut both ways, and Sansera is on the right side now.** An ADS part takes years to qualify. That was the cost of entry; it is now the barrier behind it. Management: *"we are not today in production of anything where a significant portion is going to come to end of life in the ADS business."*

**Moat 4 — Capability the peer group does not have.** **5-axis machining in roughly ten configurations; a special-process facility that takes components up to 4 metres; a turbine blisk programme with first samples due ~Sep-2026; and machining of monocrystalline components** — *"This is something that we have never done before because this is one single crystal."*

**Moat 5 — Sweden gives a European manufacturing address.** Grew 11.6% YoY in Q1FY27. Modest, but it is why *"exports to Europe grew by 32.1% YoY, while excluding Sweden operations it grew by 48.8%."*

**Moat 6 — 🟢 Zero promoter pledge.** The Reg 31(4) annual disclosure filed 2-Apr-2026, signed by S Sekhar Vasan for all promoters, declares that they *"have not made any encumbrance over the shares held by us, directly or indirectly, during the financial year ended March 31, 2026."* **Nil.** *(Worth stating explicitly because it is the opposite of what NRBBEARING's equivalent filing disclosed.)*

**Moat 7 — Incremental asset turns are disclosed, and ADS is the better one.** **Automotive 1.25–1.3x; ADS 2.0–2.2x** *(deck slide 10, verified by rendering the page).* **The mix shift is therefore capital-efficiency-accretive as well as margin-accretive.**

**Moat 8 — Segment margin is disclosed, if imprecisely.** Preetham: *"our exports business and ADS business definitely comes with a higher margin profile… we are targeting or we are operating between 25% to 30% EBITDA margins in both these sectors."* **Against a 19.2% blended margin, that implies the auto domestic base earns something closer to 15–16%.** *(That last figure is my arithmetic, not management's — they declined to give it when asked directly.)*

### Where the moat is weaker

- **🔴 Returns do not yet reflect the story.** **ROCE 14.1%, ROE 11.5%** — below Happy Forgings (18.0% / 15.0%), Sundram Fasteners (17.6% / 14.9%) and Endurance (17.8% / 14.9%), at an identical or higher multiple. **Five-year and three-year ROE are both 12%. This has never been a high-return business.**
- **🔴 Two consecutive years of negative free cash flow.** FY25 **−₹219cr**, FY26 **−₹123cr** *(Screener)*. CFO/OP has slipped 91% → 85% → **77%.** The growth is being funded, not self-funded.
- **⚠️ Working capital is heavier than it was.** Inventory days **147 → 169**; payable days **107 → 136**. **The payables stretch is what is holding the cash conversion cycle at 98 days.**
- **⚠️ Borrowings turned back up.** ₹891cr (FY24) → ₹407cr (FY25, post-QIP repayment) → **₹591cr (FY26, +45%).**
- **⚠️ The ADS backlog is concentrated.** A single semicon customer will be **~USD 75m of annual business** once the new order ramps. Management named it because it was large; the corollary is that it is large.
- **⚠️ ADS is not yet where the capex is going.** Of Q1FY27's plant-and-machinery spend, **Non-Auto took 13%** against Auto PV/CV 45% and Auto 2W 18% *(deck slide 10)*. The ADS buildings are being built; the machines are still mostly going into auto.
- **🔴 It is not a promoter-controlled company any more.** **29.21%** against DII 31.04% and FII 21.52%.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — The mix shift is real, verifiable, and the reason the multiple exists.** **ADS: 3.8% of revenue in FY21 → 9.6% in FY26 → 14.2% in Q1FY27**, with a backlog that went **₹3,800cr → ₹5,700cr in two quarters.** *This is not a narrative; it is a table.*

**Thread B — But the auto business is what actually delivered this quarter.** **Auto-ICE grew 20.8% on a high base**, with record quarters in passenger vehicles (+38.2%), commercial vehicles (+18.5%) and scooters, and 2W +14.6%. **ADS added ₹106cr of YoY revenue (₹394mn → ₹1,454mn); Auto-ICE added ₹108cr (₹5,194mn → ₹6,275mn).** ***The quarter that re-rated the ADS story was, in rupees, an equal-parts auto quarter.***

**Thread C — 🟡 And the auto strength has a stated shelf life.** Management's own visibility is *"at least next one and a half quarters"*, running through a festive season *"distributed between quarter 2 and quarter 3."* **Festive demand is pulled forward, not created.** The Q4FY27 comparison is where this gets tested.

**Thread D — 🟡 The margin beat has three components and only one of them is durable.** Management's own decomposition: **mix (durable), operating leverage (durable), and currency plus a ~40% international revenue share (not durable)** — and against those sits an unrecovered ₹126mn tariff provision and *"material inflation, yet to materialize"* in pass-throughs. **Management guided to 19%, not 19.2%, and told you why.**

**Thread E — 🔴 The capital is going in before the revenue comes out, and that is visible in the cash flow.** **Two years of negative FCF; CFO/OP 91% → 85% → 77%; inventory days 147 → 169; borrowings back up 45%.** **₹1,101cr of capex across FY25–26, with ADS revenue of ₹336cr to show for it so far** — the returns are genuinely ahead, in the hangar, the defence plant and the build-to-suit. ***The thesis requires believing the 2.0–2.2x ADS asset turn. It has not yet been demonstrated at scale.***

**Thread F — 🔴 The ADS order book concentrated in the same quarter it grew.** **The backlog fell during Q1FY27** and then rose 29% on a single post-quarter order from a single existing semicon customer who will represent **~USD 75m of annual business.** **Diversification at the segment level; concentration at the customer level within the segment.**

**Thread G — And the semicon cycle underneath it is an AI capex cycle.** Management's visibility is *"until FY29, FY '30"* and comes from *"the immediate feedback from the industry."* **That is a genuine and honest answer. It is also a cyclical industry, and the peak revenue from this order lands in calendar 2029 — exactly where the visibility ends.**

**Thread H — The governance layer thinned in the same month the story got bigger.** **Audit Committee chair retired, CRO superannuated, three of four IDs new in 2026**, and **a 0.89pp promoter decline nobody has explained or asked about.** ***None of these is an allegation. Together they are a reason to want the next two quarters' disclosures rather than to extrapolate.***

**How they interact.** ***A vertically-integrated component maker with a genuinely verifiable pivot into aerospace and semiconductor precision — beating its own ADS guidance, expanding margin, de-concentrating its customers, and building the capacity for a ₹3,500cr ADS business by FY31 — is being funded by borrowing and a prior QIP rather than by its own cash flow, at a multiple that already assumes the pivot works.*** **The business has got better. The question §6 has to answer is whether it has got better faster than the share price.**

### What the multiple is discounting, and whether it should

***The case for it.*** **A 33% revenue quarter, 200bps of margin expansion, an ADS backlog up 50% in six months, top-5 concentration down 15 points in five years, a delivery record with eight of eleven commitments met, D/E at 0.19x, zero pledge, and a management team that guides low and raises.** **Against Azad's 131x, this is the cheaper way to own the same theme with a real cash business underneath it.**

***The case against it, and it is the one I weight more heavily.***
1. **The returns are not there and have not been for five years.** 5-year ROE 12%, 3-year 12%, last year 12%. **ADS at 25–30% segment EBITDA has to get much larger before the group ROE moves, and the capital going in ahead of it pushes the denominator up first.**
2. **Free cash flow has been negative two years running.** **The pivot is being financed, not funded.** A further equity raise is explicitly not ruled out, at a promoter stake of 29.21%.
3. **The stock is 5.6% below its all-time high after tripling in a year**, with retail holders up 36% in fifteen months. **There is no valuation cushion in this price and the marginal holder is new.**
4. **Three separate one-offs and unexplained items in a single quarter** — the settlement, the tariff provision, the promoter decline — at a company that has just lost its Audit Committee chair and its CRO.

### Weighted return framework (2-year, to FY28)

*Base: FY26 revenue ₹3,498cr, EBITDA 18.1%, PAT ₹327cr. Current mcap ₹23,807cr.*

**Base case — 55%.** **Management's guidance is met, roughly.** FY27 revenue +19% to **~₹4,160cr**; FY28 +17% to **~₹4,870cr**. **ADS reaches ~₹580cr in FY27 and ~₹900cr in FY28**, lifting blended EBITDA margin to **~19.0%** → EBITDA **~₹925cr**. **Bull case — 25%.** **The ADS ramp beats and the mix does the work.** The build-to-suit and hangar commission on time; the semicon order ramps into calendar 2027 ahead of plan; **ADS reaches ~₹1,100cr by FY28** and Auto-ICE holds mid-teens on the outsourcing wave. FY28 revenue **~₹5,400cr** at **20.5% EBITDA** → **₹1,107cr**; **PAT ~₹610cr** at **60x** *(the market keeps paying an aerospace multiple because the aerospace share keeps rising)* → **₹36,600cr. ≈ +54%.**

**Bear case — 20%.** **The auto cycle normalises after the festive pull-forward and the semicon capex cycle rolls over sooner than FY29.** Pass-throughs stay unrecovered, the ₹126mn tariff provision proves not recoverable and repeats, ADS ramp slips on validation and NADCAP timelines, and the working capital and negative FCF force an equity raise into a falling multiple. FY28 revenue **~₹4,300cr** at **17.5%** → EBITDA ₹753cr, **PAT ~₹360cr**, **de-rated to 33x** *(between M M Forgings and Sundram)* → **₹11,880cr. ≈ −50%.**

**Weighted 2-year expected return ≈ +5%** *(0.55 × 2.9 + 0.25 × 54 + 0.20 × −50)* — roughly **2.5% CAGR**, plus a 0.09% dividend yield that rounds to nothing.

The operating improvement was real and it was also already paid for.*** **And per §0A rule 7: the distribution is wide and the left tail is a −50%. That is the finding, not a caveat on it.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — 🔴 The multiple is the risk.** **63.9x on 11.5% ROE.** A de-rate to Sundram/Endurance's 42x on unchanged FY28 earnings of ₹490cr gives **₹20,580cr, −13.5%, with nothing having gone wrong operationally.**

**Risk 2 — 🔴 The semiconductor cycle.** Management's visibility runs *"until FY29, FY '30"* on customer feedback about an AI-driven capex boom. **The ₹1,250cr order peaks in calendar 2029.** **If AI capex normalises earlier, the ADS ramp lands into a falling market with the capacity already built.**

**Risk 3 — 🔴 Free cash flow and the funding of the pivot.** Two negative years. **A fundraise is explicitly not ruled out** — *"take an appropriate decision if at all we need to look at a fundraise"* — and the CFO said flatly *"inorganic plans are there."* **At 29.21% promoter holding, further equity issuance changes the control picture.**

**Risk 4 — 🔴 The auto pull-forward.** Growth visibility is *"at least next one and a half quarters"* through a festive season straddling Q2 and Q3. **Q4FY27 is where a pulled-forward festive shows up as a miss.**

**Risk 5 — 🟡 Margin is partly currency and mix, and the pass-throughs have not landed.** ~40% international revenue in a quarter management itself called *"slightly more elevated"*; **steel and aluminium pass-throughs *"currently in process, not yet translated."***
**⚠️ ACCUMULATING:** the ₹126mn tariff provision is a cost already taken against a recovery that has not happened.

**Risk 6 — 🟡 ADS customer concentration inside a de-concentrating group.** One semicon customer at **~USD 75m annually**. **Top-5 concentration falling at group level while a single ADS customer becomes systemically important to the growth leg.**

**Risk 7 — 🟡 Execution and certification timelines.** NADCAP for the surface-coating line is *"underway"*; the hangar has already slipped once; the build-to-suit needs 10 months from a discussion that was still open in August. **Machine lead times are *"seven, eight, sometimes up to nine months."***

**Risk 8 — 🟡 The governance layer just thinned.** **Audit Committee chair and Stakeholders Relationship chair both retired 27-Jul; CRO superannuated 31-Jul with no announced successor; three of four IDs joined in 2026.** *No allegation attaches to any of this. It is a statement about how much institutional memory sits in the oversight functions right now.*

**Risk 9 — 🟡 The unexplained promoter decline.** See §3.5 and the trigger table. **This is Risk 8's practical test case: an unexplained 0.89pp move went unasked on an hour-long call.**

**Risk 10 — 🟡 Retail concentration at the top.** **1,04,350 shareholders, up 36% in fifteen months, in a stock 5.6% off its all-time high after +203%.** **A crowded holder base is not a valuation input, but it is a volatility input.**

**Risk 11 — 🟢 TRACKING WELL, stated for completeness.** v1's Risk 5 was *"Nichidai JV integration issues."* **The JV is incorporated, funded, and 40% allotted to Nichidai on 23-Jul-2026 with board composition settled 4:3 in Sansera's favour. On schedule; nothing to flag yet.**

**Risk 12 — 🆕 The MMRFIC investment is undisclosed in size and unexplained in fit.** Sansera holds a stake in a radar deep-tech company. **The Q1FY27 deck devotes a full slide to it but discloses neither the amount nor the stake.** The sharpest question about it is a ValuePickr one, not an analyst one: *"Sansera's competency is forging, machining of parts, while MMRFIC products are more fabrication led which has no overlap with Sansera's core expertise; so Sansera's value add is not clear."* **A fair question that has not been answered on any call in the file.**

**⚠️ v1 risks re-assessed:** *"ADS growth deceleration"* — **not materialised; ADS accelerated to 3.7x YoY.** *"Third dilution round"* — **not materialised as an issuance, but the 0.89pp Jun-2026 decline is a live and separate question.** *"Global aerospace order book normalisation"* — **not materialised; backlogs are rising.** *"Currency exposure"* — **materialised in the company's favour this quarter, which is the same risk pointing the other way.**
