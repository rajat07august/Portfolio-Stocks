## 1. Business in Plain English

Carysil makes **the sink in your kitchen** — and, increasingly, the tap above it and the appliances around it.

Its core product is the **quartz composite sink**: crushed quartz bound in acrylic resin, moulded into a single piece. It is harder than steel, quieter, heat-resistant to 280°C, and it looks like stone. Carysil is one of only a handful of manufacturers worldwide with the process capability to make them at scale — and it does it from Bhavnagar, Gujarat, at Indian cost for European and American brands.

**The useful analogy is a contract manufacturer that quietly became a brand.** Two-thirds of revenue is still exports made for other people's labels — **IKEA, Lowe's, Howdens** — while the Indian business increasingly sells under Carysil's own name into a modular-kitchen market that barely existed a decade ago.

**Four product lines, in order of maturity:**

| Line | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Quartz sinks** | The original franchise; global top-tier capability | ~80% utilised; **+1.25mn units by Mar-2027** |
| **Stainless steel sinks** | Newer, volume-led | **+70,000 units already added and filled to 94%**; another **150,000 by Mar-2027** |
| **Faucets** | Adjacency into the same customer | Scaling; **100k capacity guided end-FY27** |
| **Appliances** | Hobs, chimneys, ovens — the modular-kitchen attach | Scaling; **100k guided end-FY27** |

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — The Indian modular kitchen barely existed fifteen years ago and now compounds double-digit.** Rising incomes, nuclear households and apartment living turn the kitchen from a utility room into a designed space. **Carysil's domestic mix has gone 28% → 33% in a year.**

**Force 2 — Quartz composite is displacing stainless steel at the premium end globally.** Better acoustics, better heat tolerance, better aesthetics. **The process barrier is real** — very few manufacturers worldwide can do it at scale and consistent quality.

**Force 3 — Global brands want an Indian manufacturing base.** IKEA, Lowe's and Howdens do not want to own sink factories. **Carysil is the qualified vendor at Indian cost — and qualification into a global retailer's supply chain takes years.**

**Force 4 — The attach rate.** A customer buying a sink is buying a kitchen. **Faucets and appliances sell into a relationship that already exists**, which is why the adjacency economics are better than a standing start.

**Force 5 — Channel shift to e-commerce.** Management expects **e-commerce sales to grow 3x this year**, on breakthroughs with **Amazon Alpha and Flipkart**.

**Where Carysil sits.** ₹3,286cr, in **Sector 18 (Home Building Products — Kitchen & Bath)**, a category created for it in July 2026. **The natural Indian comparables — Astral, Cera, Kajaria, Somany, Hindware — are all adjacent rather than direct**; no listed Indian pure-play in quartz sinks exists.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — Process capability in quartz composite that very few manufacturers globally possess.** This is the foundation and it is genuinely narrow.

**Moat 2 — Qualification inside global retail supply chains.** **IKEA, Lowe's, Howdens UK, Amazon** — relationships measured in years, deepened by *"adding new models and colors."* **Being displaced requires the customer to requalify a new vendor, which nobody does casually.**

**Moat 3 — 🆕 Demand is currently ahead of capacity, and management says so plainly.** Chirag Parekh, Q1FY27: *"the kind of order booking what we have right now, **my factory has to literally run now 7 days a week. So we are in a serious stress.** We 100% have to see, that how fast can we expand our capacity."* **A supply-constrained manufacturer is a good problem, and it is the reason the ₹80–90cr capex matters more than the P&L does this year.**

**Moat 4 — The stainless-steel expansion filled instantly.** **+70,000 units added and immediately at 94% utilisation.** *That is the cleanest available evidence that the capacity thesis is demand-led rather than hope-led.*

**Moat 5 — A widening product range sold into the same customers.** Sinks → faucets → appliances. **80% of the FY27 capex still goes into the core sink business**, so the adjacencies are being added without diluting focus.

**Moat 6 — Domestic mix is rising, which de-risks the export dependence.** 28% → 33% in a year, with e-commerce guided to 3x.

**Moat 7 — Margin has expanded through a tariff shock.** FY25 OPM 17% → FY26 19% → **Q1FY27 20%**, while absorbing and then recovering US tariff-driven discounts.

**Where the moat is weaker.**
- **🔴 Three of four expansions commission in the same March-2027 window.** Only stainless steel has actually landed.
- **🔴 ~67% of revenue is still export**, and management named *"geopolitical adversities are very, very strong"* as the reason it would not raise guidance.
- **🔴 Free cash flow is structurally thin.** FY26 FCF **−₹2cr**; FY24 −₹38cr. **CFO/OP has averaged ~68% over five years**, and management now guides *"bare minimum ₹50 to ₹60 crores capex every year."*
- **⚠️ Promoter holding is 41.34%** — low for a founder-run business, and down from 43.84% in late 2023.
- **⚠️ Q1 quartz growth was single-digit** on container delays — a reminder that this is a physical-logistics business.
- **⚠️ UK is *"going through a bit of a tight phase."***
- **⚠️ 30.5x and 5.4x book leaves no room for a commissioning slip.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**🔴 Risk 1 — Commissioning concentration.** Three of four expansions commission in the same **March-2027** window; only stainless steel has landed. **FY28 is the year the entire capital programme converts to revenue.** Nothing has slipped and Parekh reaffirmed the date — **but nothing has arrived either.** **Signal to watch: any date drift at Q2 or Q3FY27.**

**🔴 Risk 2 — The 15% volume guide against a 6% Q1.** Management guides **15% value and 15% volume** for FY27; Q1 quartz volume grew **6%**. The explanation is container delays, not demand — *"we had a very strong order booking… we could not dispatch a lot of things."* **An analyst began to press exactly this and was cut off by the moderator for time.** **Signal to watch: Q2 volumes. This single number decides FY27.**

**🔴 Risk 3 — ~67% of revenue is export, into an environment management will not forecast.** Q4FY26, on why guidance would not be raised: *"The **geopolitical adversities are very, very strong. I cannot even say the future what it is.** But we stick to our guidance."* **Freight and container disruption already cost a quarter of quartz volume.** **Signal to watch: export growth in absolute terms; container/freight commentary.**

**🔴 Risk 4 — Free cash flow has been ~nil for five years and a capex floor is now guided.** FCF: **−21, +13, −38, +13, −2**. FY27 capex **₹80–90cr**, and *"bare minimum ₹50 to ₹60 crores every year"* thereafter on a ₹109cr CFO base. **At 5.4x book with a 0.21% yield, nothing is being returned and nothing is scheduled to be.** **Signal to watch: CFO/OP holding at 80%+, and whether FY27 FCF turns positive.**

**⚠️ Risk 5 — MMA (methyl methacrylate) input cost.** Q4FY26: *"MMA prices are on the rise… **increased by about 30%, 35%… in the last 2 months**."* Pass-through happens with a lag. **A sustained spike compresses margin.** **Signal to watch: gross margin against the 18–20% band.**

**⚠️ Risk 6 — Customer concentration in global retail.** **IKEA, Lowe's, Howdens, Home Depot, Amazon.** These wins are the growth engine and **no concentration percentage is disclosed.** A US home-improvement downcycle would hit directly. **Signal to watch: any customer-concentration disclosure; US retail comps.**

**⚠️ Risk 7 — The UK is the soft spot.** Management's own read: *"The U.K. market expects to remain **modest**, we remain cautiously optimistic and see strong opportunities to gain market share through distribution channels."* Growth is being sought through share gain, not market growth. Discussions in flight with **wren, Farrell, Huws Grey, ROK and Landmark**; new **Manchester showroom**. **Signal to watch: UK subsidiary growth turning.**

**⚠️ Risk 8 — India ramp disappointing.** Parekh has admitted India is *"the first time we are shifting our gears"* after historic underperformance. The build-out is real — **~180 stores over two years, funded 90-day festive plans September–November, an eight-city celebrity roadshow, e-commerce guided 3x** — and it carries an ad-spend drag if revenue does not scale. **Signal to watch: domestic growth holding near 39.8%.**

**⚠️ Risk 9 — Promoter holding at 41.34%.** Low for a founder-run business, down from 43.84% in late 2023, flat for five quarters since. **Mitigating: DII absorbed almost all of it (7.08% → 11.91%) and keeps adding.** **Signal to watch: any further step-down.**

**⚠️ Risk 10 — Appliances and faucets execution.** Both are guided to double by end-FY27 off small bases. **In-house content at 63% and 67% is rising**, which is the margin story — **but these are new manufacturing disciplines** (glass cutting, forming, colour coating). **Signal to watch: in-house content percentages, and whether appliance volumes accelerate from +12%.**

**⚠️ Risk 11 — Rupee appreciation.** ~67% exports; a reverse currency move hurts.
