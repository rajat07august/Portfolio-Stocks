## 1. Business in Plain English

NRB makes the small, unglamorous steel components that stop other components from destroying each other.

Founded in **1965**, NRB pioneered the **needle roller bearing** in India. A needle bearing uses long, thin rollers instead of balls, which lets it carry a heavy load in a very small radial space — exactly what you need inside a gearbox, a transmission, a steering column or a rocker arm, where there is no room for a fat conventional bearing.

**More than 90% of the vehicles on Indian roads run on NRB bearings.**

**The analogy: NRB is a bespoke joint-replacement surgeon, not a pharmacy.**

A pharmacy stocks standard product and sells it to whoever walks in. NRB does something different: it sits with a customer three-to-five years *before* a vehicle platform launches and co-designs the bearing into the gearbox. Management call this a **"mass customization mindset"**:

> *"Bearings are co-designed at the platform level, with the application data coming in from our customers. **We sit on the drawing board**, we brainstorm future technologies that do not exist... which makes them all our customers deeply, deeply embedded and **makes us difficult to replace**."* — Q4FY26 call

The company makes **~4,000 distinct products**. It is not a volume commodity manufacturer — it competes on engineering, not price:

> *"We do not work on commodity pricing and short-term solutions."*

**Who buys:** BMW (1, 2, 3 through 7 Series, X1, X2 — transmissions), Mercedes-Benz (A-class through Maybach; EQC and EQA e-drive), Ford (F-series pickups), Hyundai, Kia, Jeep, Foton, Daimler, Paccar, Stellantis, Renault, Magna, Siemens, JCB. Revenue splits roughly **70% domestic / 30% international**.

**Scale:** ₹1,395cr TTM revenue, ₹4,695cr market cap.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists — the durable forces

**1. Friction is a permanent tax on every machine.** Anything that rotates needs a bearing. This demand does not go away with a change of powertrain, fuel or regulation.

**2. Precision bearings are an oligopoly with brutal entry barriers.** Global supply is concentrated in SKF, Schaeffler, Timken, NSK, NTN and JTEKT. Qualification cycles run years, and a bearing failure destroys an engine — so OEMs almost never switch a proven supplier to save a few rupees.

**3. Import substitution is a live, policy-backed opportunity.** Management's stated strategy targets exactly this:

> *"In the domestic automotive segment, we are adding path-breaking and high-entry-barrier products, **which are currently being imported**... While many of the products that are included in the portfolio of multinational bearing suppliers who are present in India and show in their sales, **these are, in fact, imported**."* — Q2FY26

**4. Electrification changes bearing *specification*, not bearing *need*.** EVs spin faster, run quieter, and suffer electrical erosion through the bearing — which creates demand for new, higher-value solutions rather than destroying demand.

**5. Precision friction management is spreading beyond vehicles.** Robotics, humanoids, AGVs, drones, industrial gearboxes, data-centre cooling, renewable drives — all need micro-precision bearings, and all are growing far faster than autos.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different — the moat

**1. "EV-agnostic" — a term the MD coined, and it holds up.** This is NRB's single best structural feature. Rather than betting on ICE or EV, NRB deliberately sits in applications **common to all three powertrains**:

> *"We are in those applications predominantly that will be common in the future for EV, ICE, and hybrid. So we focus on chassis systems... we focus on transmissions that will have a commonality when they're connected into the steering column... **it doesn't matter what the bearing offtake is in pure EV because we are majorly, 70%, in those applications which are going to grow**."* — Q1FY27

The worked example is concrete and checkable: *"the BMW i steering that we are going to launch in 2027 — that BMW i steering is through ICE, hybrid, and the e-line of BMW."* One part, three powertrains.

Management further state that pricing is set so that **content per vehicle is value-neutral across technologies** — EV-specific bearings are *"priced in a manner to maintain our existing margin profile."*

**2. Extreme share-of-business inside its accounts.** Not merely a supplier — often the only supplier:

> *"For several products, we are 100% or near 100% suppliers to international customers... our situation on share of business is extremely high and at most companies for the products that we are leaders of, **it is even 60% to 70% with certain products being 100%**."* — Q4FY26

**3. The lifetime-nominated order book — and it is compounding fast.** NRB discloses "lifetime nominated business": orders won for the entire production life of a vehicle platform.

| Disclosed at | Lifetime nominated business |
|---|---|
| Q2FY26 (Nov-2025) | **₹600cr+** |
| Q4FY26 (May-2026) | **₹800cr** |
| **Q1FY27 (Aug-2026)** | **₹1,100cr** |

**+83% in nine months.** This is the most encouraging single number in the file, and it is forward-looking in a way that quarterly revenue is not.

**4. Genuine R&D depth, not a slide.** Proprietary and copyrighted computational analysis software, simulation, kinematic motion studies, dynamic light-weighting, noise reduction, and in-house domain expertise in material science, sealing and lubrication.

**5. Aerospace certification bought rather than built — a legitimately smart move.** NRB acquired **Mahant Tool Room (MTR)**, Bengaluru, completing **17-Jul-2026**, bringing **AS9100D** certification. The logic was stated with unusual clarity:

> *"Many people have asked us why are we acquiring a relatively small aerospace company where our plans for this space are extremely ambitious... The answer is simple. **We bought access, not capacity.**... What we needed was an operational runway that bypassed the multi-year gestation periods and lead times... **we stepped directly onto the market, day one.**"* — Q1FY27

Early wins are real and hard to fake: a **Sukhoi-30 plain spherical bearing** order (*"there are a handful of companies in the world, not even five, that can make this type of bearing"*), plus confidential platforms at **HAL** and **National Aerospace Laboratories**.

**6. A Make-in-USA footprint that just opened a door.** NRB's Columbia, South Carolina facility won **high-precision planet pins for the General Motors Corvette** programme — *"this program is only open to US bearing manufacturers"*, 300,000 peak volume. In a tariff-fragmenting world, local manufacturing is a moat, not an overhead.

**7. Margin built on structural actions, not price.** FY26 EBITDA margin expanded to 19.5% from 18.3% via solar power, automation, yield improvement and renegotiated vendor costing — all repeatable, none cyclical.

### Where the moat is weaker — stated plainly

- **🔴 Growth has been poor for a decade. 10-year revenue CAGR is 7%.** That is the number the bull case has to overcome, and no amount of vision slides changes it. Management attribute much of FY22-FY25 to a **fire at the Waluj plant** (see §3.5) — a real and verifiable explanation — but the pre-fire record is not much better.
- **🔴 Inventory is enormous.** 302 days (improved from 364, still extreme); cash conversion cycle 281 days. Management defend it as strategic positioning near customers in Germany and the US. It is still ~₹550cr+ of capital sitting in warehouses.
- **🟡 SNL Bearings, a listed subsidiary, competes with the parent.** Management conceded: *"it does cannibalize our products on and off."* No merger planned.
- **🟡 The six growth vectors are, today, mostly aspiration.** Data centres are *"in the research and development stage"*; robotics/humanoid orders are *"250,000 each"* (i.e. ~₹2cr); aerospace order book is ₹50cr against a ₹300cr-by-2031 target.
- **🟡 Scale disadvantage.** NRB's entire market cap is a rounding error against SKF or Schaeffler globally — one ValuePickr member noted *"some of the giants have their R&D spending equal to NRB Bearings market capitalisation."*

### 🔴 The promoter settlement — the defining event

NRB's history was reshaped in **February 2025** by a settlement between two branches of the founding Sahney family. Management addressed it by posing the question to herself, unprompted:

> *"Under the agreement effect this February, the Harshbeena Zaveri family, my family, and the promoters who are very closely associated with me, have total control and ownership of over 50% of NRB Bearings Limited. **Just me and my immediate family have over 50%**, and the other promoters... we together own 51.2% of NRB."* — Q2FY26

The other branch — **Devesh Singh Sahney and/or his daughter** — exited to **NRB Industrial Bearings Ltd (NIBL)**, a separate listed company. Cross-holdings were severed completely: *"my holding is down to zero in NIBL. And that family has zero holdings as well in NRB Bearings Limited."*

The settlement also resolved the **NRB brand**: all names and logos belong to NRB Bearings Ltd; NIBL may use its specific red logo only while Devesh Sahney or his daughter control it, reverting on any change of ownership. NIBL handed back the portion of the heritage headquarters it occupied.

**NRB paid ₹55cr for this**, in Q4FY25 — and that single fact explains the most misleading number on the screen today (see §4).

**Promoter holding fell 51.20% → 44.73% — a 6.47 percentage-point decline — in the June 2026 quarter. FII holding rose 4.23pp and DII 3.79pp in the same quarter.**

**Verification (§0A rule 1 — cross-checked against a second, independent source):**
- **Screener** reports 44.73% at Jun-2026.
- **NSE's own filed shareholding-pattern master** independently reports public shareholding of **55.27%** at 30-Jun-2026 (submitted 15-Jul-2026), against **48.80%** for every one of the preceding eight quarters. 100% − 55.27% = **44.73%**. **The two sources agree exactly.**

**What I could NOT find, having looked:**
- **No Reg 29(2) disclosure of a promoter sale.** The only Reg 29(2) filed in the quarter (25-Jun-2026, Harshbeena Sahney Zaveri) is a **pledge release**, and it explicitly records **"Shares sold — nil"**.
- **No promoter-group reclassification announcement.**
- **No mention on the Q1FY27 earnings call**, held 10-Aug-2026 — six weeks after quarter-end and three weeks after the shareholding pattern was filed. **Not one analyst asked.** The only adjacent remark was an analyst's aside that *"the transition in the ownership control happened, and the pledging has finally been reduced"* — which management neither corrected nor expanded.

**I am stating a verified fact and an unresolved cause — not an allegation.** A 6.47pp move could be a sale below individual disclosure thresholds spread across promoter entities, a reclassification not yet announced, an invocation, or a filing revision. **I do not know which, and neither the filings nor the call say.** It directly contradicts the "over 50%, total control" framing given nine months earlier, and it is the single most important thing to resolve on this stock.

### 🔴 The pledge — large, under-discussed, and only now unwinding

From NRB's own Reg 31(4) filing (17-Jun-2026):

> *"As on **March 31, 2026**, the total pledged shares of the promoters were **3,85,76,384 (39.80% of the total share capital** of the company)."*
> *"As on April 7, 2026 the total pledged shares of the promoters were 3,66,79,709 (**37.84%**)."*

**Against a promoter holding of 51.20%, that means roughly 78% of the entire promoter stake was pledged.** The Reg 29(2) filing shows Harshbeena Zaveri alone held 30.06% unencumbered plus **11.42% encumbered = 41.48% total**.

Pledges were then released in June: **37,84,427 shares (3.90%) on 23-Jun-2026 and 7,84,000 (0.81%) on 25-Jun-2026 — 4.71% in total.** That is real, verified deleveraging of promoter debt and it is a genuine positive.

But the level was extraordinarily high, it went almost entirely undiscussed across four earnings calls, and **the coincidence of a large pledge release and a 6.47pp holding decline inside the same fortnight is a question that deserves an answer.**

### 🟡 Three disclosure inconsistencies worth recording

**1. The MTR order book was quoted three different ways in ~5 weeks.** An analyst put this to management directly on the Q1FY27 call: **₹50cr on the Q4FY26 call, ₹70cr in a media interview, ₹25cr on the Q1FY27 call.** Management reconciled it — ₹30cr sits in MTR (aerospace defence) and ₹20cr in NRB's own non-aerospace defence, ₹50cr combined — and the reconciliation is coherent. But a ValuePickr member independently flagged the same thing (post #53, 11-Aug-2026): *"Everything was great in the concall except MTR order book disclosure... Was surprised with this response but it is what it is."* **Two independent observers caught the same wobble.**

**2. The "typographical error" revision.** The only substantive change between the original and revised Q1FY27 transcripts is a single sentence:

- **Filed 13:04:** *"NRB's trailing 12-month profit growth is the highest **in the group of competitors we generally benchmark ourselves against**."*
- **Revised 18:19:** *"NRB's trailing 12-months track record for profit growth is **the highest**."*

The revision **removed the peer-benchmark qualifier**. Recorded as a fact; I am not going to speculate about why, and it may simply be a transcriber's insertion being corrected. But a same-day revision of a comparative performance claim is worth noting.

**3. Management explicitly prefer annual to quarterly disclosure** — on segment mix, exports, order book. *"If I give it on a quarterly basis, then it unnecessarily causes this kind of confusion."* Defensible, but it means several key metrics cannot be tracked quarter to quarter.

**A genuinely benign item, stated so it is not mistaken for a red flag:** NSE sought a clarification on the Q4FY26 results (19-May-2026). NRB's reply (29-May-2026) explains it was an **XBRL filing error — "Audited" was selected instead of "Unaudited"** — and a revised XML was uploaded. **This is administrative, not an earnings-quality issue.**

**One-line verdict on management:** *Exceptional technical and strategic operator — the EV-agnostic positioning, the "buy access not capacity" aerospace logic and the customer embedment are genuinely first-class thinking, and the FY26 margin and inventory delivery were real. But the disclosure culture is thin (one voice, annual-only metrics, three versions of one order-book number, a same-day transcript revision), and an unexplained 6.47pp fall in promoter holding sits unaddressed on top of a pledge that ran to 78% of the promoter stake. Back the operator; do not yet trust the disclosure.*

### 🟡 The consolidated/standalone divergence in Q1FY27

Management's own framing:

> *"Revenue... stood at INR370 crores in Q1 compared to INR310 crores the year before, reflecting a growth of 19.2% on a consolidated basis. Profit after tax increased to INR38 crores... an increase of **15%** on a consolidated basis. **Standalone results showed extremely healthy growth with sales rising 14.7% Y-o-Y and PAT rising 31.7%** year-on-year."*

**Read that carefully. Standalone PAT +31.7%; consolidated PAT +15%.** The parent grew profit at more than twice the group rate — which means **the subsidiaries collectively diluted profit in Q1FY27.** Management did not explain the gap and no analyst asked. Given MTR was consolidated from mid-July (i.e. after quarter-end) and the Unitec JV is pre-revenue, the likely drags are NRB USA's ramp-up and step-down subsidiary costs — **but that is my inference, and it is unverified.**

Note too that **consolidated revenue was flat QoQ (₹372cr → ₹370cr) and OPM slipped 18% → 17%**, while depreciation rose 38% YoY as the capex lands. The +19.2% is against a soft Q1FY26 base.

Other expenses jumped ~₹10cr QoQ; management attributed it to *"cost escalation in terms of electricity... logistics... and obviously petroleum product related"*, addressed by value engineering and price increases.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**The core insight: NRB has spent a decade unable to grow, for reasons that were mostly identifiable and are now mostly resolved.**

Three things held it down:
1. **The Waluj fire.** It destroyed production lines and, critically, the stores holding two-wheeler work-in-progress — *"the two-wheeler industry grew substantially during those two years, and we were unable to supply them."* Insurance covered it (which is why odd exceptional gains appear in the P&L). Full capacity returned only in the last quarter of 2024.
2. **The family dispute**, which consumed management attention and ended in a ₹55cr payment in Q4FY25.
3. **A 78%-pledged promoter stake**, which constrains everything a promoter can do.

**All three are now behind the company.** The fire is repaired, the settlement is signed, cross-holdings are severed, and the pledge is being released. FY26 is the first clean year in five — and it produced 19.5% EBITDA margins, ₹241cr of operating cash flow and ₹136cr of free cash flow.

**What the company is doing with that clean slate is the actual investment case:** taking a proven, deeply-embedded automotive friction franchise and pointing it at six adjacent markets — aerospace, automotive adjacencies, electrification beyond EVs, mobility beyond vehicles (robotics/AGVs/drones), heavy off-highway, and mission-critical industrial. Management insist this is not a leap:

> *"While this is a fresh start, **it's not any speculative leap into unknown territory**. This strategic direction is built entirely on the corporate strengths we systematically developed over the last decade. And the target markets are **adjacent spaces where customers seek the same strengths** that our traditional automotive customers rely on us for."*

**That claim is largely credible** — a spherical bearing for a Sukhoi-30 and a needle bearing for a BMW transmission draw on the same material science, sealing and precision machining. It is genuine adjacency, not diworsification.

**Where the dots do NOT yet connect:**
- Industrial is **14%** of revenue against a 20-25% goal.
- Aerospace has a **₹50cr** order book against a **₹300cr-by-2031** target — and that target implies a **30% PAT margin** (₹90cr profit on ₹300cr), which would be extraordinary and is entirely unproven.
- Robotics/humanoid orders are ~₹2cr each.
- Data centres are at R&D stage with *"nothing concrete... to announce."*

**So: an excellent, freshly-unencumbered franchise with a credible strategy, priced at 31.4x for outcomes that are today at 5-15% of their stated targets.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

1. **🔴 The unexplained 6.47pp promoter holding decline.** Verified against two independent sources; explained by neither the filings nor the earnings call. If it turns out to be a promoter sale, the "total control, over 50%" narrative is broken and the multiple should compress. **The single most important unknown on this stock.**
2. **🔴 Residual pledge.** Down from 39.80% of share capital, but ~35% likely remains. Pledged promoter stakes amplify every other risk.
3. **🔴 The 7% decade.** If the fire and the family dispute were excuses rather than causes, NRB reverts to a single-digit grower and 31.4x is far too rich.
4. **🔴 The screen is flattering.** Headline FY26 PAT +77% is a base effect; the underlying number is ~+15%. Anyone screening on profit growth is being misled until FY27 laps it.
5. **🟡 Inventory at 302 days.** If it rebuilds, FY26's ₹241cr CFO was a one-off working-capital release, not a new run-rate.
6. **🟡 The subsidiaries are diluting profit.** Standalone PAT +31.7% vs consolidated +15% in Q1FY27, unexplained.
7. **🟡 Aerospace targets imply a 30% PAT margin** (₹90cr on ₹300cr by 2031) against a ₹50cr order book today. Extraordinary claims, thin evidence so far.
8. **🟡 Export exposure is a two-way risk.** International is ~30% of revenue and grew only 4% in FY26 amid tariffs, Middle East disruption and a gas shortage.
9. **🟡 Key-man risk is acute.** One executive runs the calls, the strategy and the narrative.
10. **🟡 SNL Bearings cannibalises the parent** by management's own admission, with no merger planned.
11. **🟢 Disclosure culture.** Annual-only metrics, three versions of the MTR order book, a same-day transcript revision. Individually minor; collectively a pattern to watch.
