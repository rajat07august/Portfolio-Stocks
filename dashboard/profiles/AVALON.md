## 1. Business in Plain English

Avalon Technologies builds electronics for other companies — printed circuit board assemblies, and increasingly complete finished systems — for industrial equipment, rail, aerospace, clean energy, medical devices and semiconductor tooling. It is **India's most US-facing EMS company**, running **15 manufacturing units across India and the United States.**

**The analogy is a contract kitchen with two addresses.** A restaurant group can have its dishes cooked in the expensive kitchen next door to its customers, or the cheap one overseas, or start in one and move to the other — same recipes, same chef, same quality standard. **Avalon's whole proposition is that the customer does not have to choose up front.**

**And management is unusually blunt about how that actually works.** Chairman Kunhamed Bicha, Q1FY27 call:

> *"Our plan to reel in the customers, make the customers comfortable and then as it ramps up, **transition it to India manufacturing. So it is our beachhead** from that perspective."*
>
> *"it is **a treadmill. I feel bad for the U.S. guys.** But any time there's a good number coming in there, **it's always more profitable to move to India.** So as a group, we tend to do that."*

**This is the single most clarifying thing said about Avalon on any call, and it reframes the US losses entirely.** The US operation is not a struggling division awaiting a turnaround — it is a **deliberate customer-acquisition beachhead** whose successful work is systematically migrated to India. It loses money partly by design. *(It is also, one notes, a candid thing to say out loud about one's own employees.)*

The 30-second snapshot:

- **HQ Chennai, Tamil Nadu** · **15 manufacturing units** across India and the US
- Founder **Kunhamed Bicha** (Chairman & MD), with a professional C-suite built out over the last three years
- **FY26:** revenue **₹1,603cr (+46%)** · EBITDA **₹173cr (10.8%)** · PAT **₹113cr (+78%)**
- **Q1FY27:** revenue **₹484cr (+49.8% YoY, +0.9% QoQ)** · EBITDA **₹58cr (12.0%)** · PAT **₹35cr (+145% YoY, −15.3% QoQ)**
- **Geography, Q1FY27:** India **41%** of revenue, exports **59%** — and **India grew 53% against exports at 48%**, a reversal of the FY26 pattern
- **Verticals, Q1FY27:** Industrial **32%** (+52%) · Mobility **25%** (Rail ~15% +37%, Aerospace 8% +47%) · Clean Energy **29%**
- **🆕 Box build reached 60% of revenue**, from 44.5% — full system integration rather than bare board assembly
- **Order book ₹2,208cr** at 30-Jun-2026 (+23.4% YoY), executable over ~14 months, plus **₹1,256cr of long-term contracts**
- **Net debt ₹24cr** (debt ₹196cr less cash ₹171cr); net debt/equity **0.03**
- **🆕 FY27 revenue growth guidance RAISED to 26–30%** from 24–27%, on the 5-Aug-2026 call

**Big picture in one sentence:** the only listed Indian EMS company with a real US manufacturing footprint, using it as a funnel to win Western customers and then move the work to India — compounding at 46% with the mix shifting toward higher-value box build, **priced at 109x earnings and 20x book.**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Electronics content is rising in everything.** Rail signalling, industrial automation, clean-energy inverters, medical devices, semiconductor capital equipment. Each of Avalon's verticals is adding electronics per unit faster than unit volumes are growing.

**Force 2 — China+1, and Avalon is unusually placed for it.** Most Indian EMS names offer "not China." **Avalon offers "not China, and you can start in Ohio and move to Chennai when you're ready."** That is a different and stickier proposition.

**Force 3 — Indian policy support.** PLI schemes, the **Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme**, and **India Semiconductor Mission 2.0** are all pushing capital and demand toward domestic electronics manufacturing.

**Force 4 — Tariffs, which have helped rather than hurt.** Bicha on the Q1FY27 call: *"that has helped us through the tariff situation… **It has given a lot of solace to our customers that we are in the U.S.** And if they want to launch there, they're more than welcome to launch there at a higher cost."* Avalon recovered **99% of tariffs from customers** during the 50% tariff period, and **the US tariff has since normalised from 50% to 18%.**

**Force 5 — 🆕 Two new geographies opening.** Bicha, Q1FY27: *"we are also opening up two geographies, right, which we are very excited about, okay, which are **Europe and Southeast Asia**, which is a very small percentage of exports. The next two years, we see that **hopefully growing in a very, very meaningful way**… that is a foray, which I believe will take us to the next level."* **Currently immaterial; explicitly flagged as the next leg.**

**Where Avalon sits.** Peers: **Syrma SGS** (broad domestic EMS), **Kaynes Technology**, **Dixon** (consumer volume), **Cyient DLM**, **Rossell Techsys** (aerospace niche). **Avalon is deliberately not a volume player** — high-mix, flexible-volume, critical industrial applications. That explains both the better margins and the lumpier revenue.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — The India + US hybrid, used as a funnel rather than a footprint.** The only listed Indian EMS with material US manufacturing. **And per §1, it is explicitly a beachhead:** win the customer in the US, migrate the work to India. **That is a customer-acquisition mechanism, not just a factory location** — and it is why a loss-making US operation can be strategically correct.

**Moat 2 — Box build at 60% of revenue, from 44.5%.** Full system integration rather than bare PCB assembly: higher value per unit, deeper OEM integration, harder to switch away from. **This is the mix shift the entire margin thesis depends on**, and it moved 15 percentage points in a year.

**Moat 3 — Vertical integration.** Bicha: *"we are vertically integrated. So that's why you see our box-build move from 44% to 60%."* Avalon can assemble **up to 32-layer double-sided PCBs**, with POP/BGA/QFN packaging down to **01005** component sizes — genuinely high-end capability, not commodity assembly.

**Moat 4 — Vertical diversification with no single dominant exposure.** Industrial 32%, Clean Energy 29%, Mobility 25% (rail + aerospace), plus medical, semiconductor equipment and communications. **No vertical above a third.**

**Moat 5 — Order book at 1.4x trailing revenue.** ₹2,208cr executable over ~14 months, plus ₹1,256cr of long-term contracts (14–36 months). **Total visible business ~₹3,464cr against TTM revenue of ₹1,764cr** — the FY26 equivalent was **₹3,441cr against ₹1,603cr, or 2.1x.**

**Moat 6 — Capex-light economics.** Asset turns **9.9x, up from 7.5x in FY25**, ROCE 19.5%, net debt of ₹24cr against ₹14,609cr of market capitalisation. **Growth has not required leverage.**

**Moat 7 — Eight consecutive quarters of sequential revenue growth**, and 46% average revenue growth across those eight quarters.

**Moat 8 — Tariff pass-through discipline.** 99% recovery during the 50% tariff regime, on the strength of long-standing customer relationships.

**Where the moat is weaker — and the valuation makes every one of these matter more.**

- **🔴 109x earnings and 20x book.** **The highest P/B on this watchlist.** The stock is at a fresh 52-week high after a +23% move in the fifteen days since the last review.
- **🔴 Cash conversion has never been good.** CFO/operating profit by year: **5% (FY23) · 54% (FY24) · 39% (FY25) · 53% (FY26).** For a working-capital-hungry EMS business, **this is the thing that eventually bites.** *(Q1FY27 is the first genuinely encouraging datapoint — see §4.)*
- **🔴 The sequential run-rate has flattened.** Q1FY27 revenue **+0.9% QoQ** and PAT **−15.3% QoQ**. The YoY optics are extraordinary because Q1FY26 was the weakest quarter of the last eight.
- **🟡 A 6-percentage-point promoter sell-down in the June-2025 quarter**, 50.57% → 44.60%. Stable for four quarters since, and DIIs absorbed it — but promoter holding is now **below 45%.**
- **🟡 US manufacturing is being deliberately capped.** Bicha: *"our goal is to keep it U.S. manufacturing around 20%."* It ran at ~28% in Q1FY27. **The US is a tool, not a growth engine, and management says so.**
- **🟡 Gross margin fell 85bps YoY** (35.5% → 34.7%) even as EBITDA margin rose 272bps — operating leverage is doing the work, not pricing.
- **🟡 High-mix, flexible-volume means lumpy revenue.** Management is explicit that a single large program slipping from February to April moves a year.

**⚠️ Read the two columns against each other.** The +49.8% revenue and +145.3% PAT are real, but they are measured against **Q1FY26, the weakest quarter of the last eight** (₹323cr revenue, ₹14cr PAT). Against the immediately preceding quarter, **revenue rose 0.9% and PAT fell 15.3%.**

The deck describes this as the *"eighth consecutive quarter of sequential"* growth. On revenue that is technically true — but at **+0.9% it is the thinnest of the eight**, and profit went backwards. **My read: the YoY comparison flatters, the QoQ comparison is the honest run-rate signal, and Avalon has plateaued around ₹480cr a quarter for two quarters.** Not a break in the thesis; a pause worth naming. *Management's own framing of the quarter is more modest than the deck's: "Q1 revenues are **marginally ahead** of Q4 FY26, a positive start to FY27."*

> **🟢 One adjustment materially improves the margin read, and it comes only from the call.** CFO Suresh V.R.: *"**Adjusting for the tariff pass-through impact, our EBITDA margin would have been approximately 0.9% higher.**"* **Tariff pass-through is billed through at zero margin, so it inflates revenue and mechanically dilutes the percentage.** On that basis Q1FY27 EBITDA margin is **~12.9%, not 12.0%** — and the +272bps YoY improvement is **understated, not flattered.** *This appears in no deck or press release.*

**What genuinely improved:**
- **Order book ₹2,208cr at 30-Jun-2026, +23.4% YoY**, executable over ~14 months. Long-term contracts ₹1,256cr.
- **Net working capital 142 days (Jun-25) → 117 days (Jun-26)** — a 25-day improvement, against a guided range of 120–130. **Inventory days 104 → 94; receivable days 87 → 74; payable days 49 → 51.**
- **🆕 Box build reached 60% of revenue** from 44.5% — the mix shift the whole margin thesis depends on.
- **India manufacturing delivered 16.7% EBITDA and 11.1% PAT margins in Q1** — the domestic operation is already at healthy profitability.
- **🆕 India is now the faster-growing side.** Q1FY27: India **41%** of revenue growing **53%**; exports **59%** growing **48%**. **In FY26 the pattern was the reverse** (India +29%, US +59%). *The FY26 framing that "the US business is the growth engine" no longer holds.*

**And two things that did not:**
- **🟡 Net working capital rose sequentially, 112 days (Mar-26) → 117 days (Jun-26).** The 25-day YoY improvement is real; the sequential direction is not. Management attributes it to *"building inventory for new programs"* — plausible, and consistent with the guidance raise, but it is the same mechanism that has always consumed Avalon's cash.
- **🟡 Gross margin fell 85bps YoY** even as EBITDA margin rose 272bps. **Operating leverage is doing the work, not pricing.**

> **Read that carefully, because it inverts the obvious conclusion.** The US is not a business being fixed. **It is a permanent, deliberately sub-scale sales channel whose economic purpose is to make the Indian margins possible.** Its losses are a customer-acquisition cost, and its share of revenue rising to 28% is treated by management as an overshoot to be corrected, not progress.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — The US beachhead wins the customer; India earns the margin.** This is now management's stated model, not an inference. It explains the US losses, the 20% cap, and why India manufacturing already runs at 16.7% EBITDA margins while the group runs at 12.0%.

**Thread B — Box build is the margin lever, and it is moving fast.** 49% → 54% → **60%** of revenue. Full system integration is stickier and better priced than bare board assembly, and vertical integration is what makes it possible.

**Thread C — Vertical diversification means no single cycle can break it.** Industrial 32%, Clean Energy 29%, Mobility 25%, plus medical, semiconductor equipment and communications.

**Thread D — Operating leverage is real and is doing the work.** EBITDA margin 9.2% → 12.0% YoY (**~12.9% adjusting for tariff pass-through**) while gross margin *fell* 85bps. **The improvement is scale, not pricing** — which is durable as long as revenue keeps growing and fragile if it stops.

**Thread E — 🆕 Europe and Southeast Asia are the next leg**, currently immaterial, explicitly flagged by the founder as *"the next level"* over two years.

**Thread F — Capex-light, near-debt-free, self-funded.** Net debt ₹24cr, asset turns 9.9x, ROCE 19.5%. Growth has not required leverage or dilution *(the promoter sell-down was a secondary sale, not an issue of new shares)*.

**🎯 Where the threads meet — and the single place they are all exposed.** Threads A–D describe a genuinely differentiated business compounding at 46% with an improving mix, and Thread F says it has funded that without leverage. **The exposure is that Thread D — operating leverage — is the entire margin story, and operating leverage only works while revenue is accelerating.** Q1FY27's sequential print was **+0.9% revenue and −15.3% PAT.** If ₹480cr a quarter is a plateau rather than a pause, **the margin expansion stops with it**, and there is no pricing improvement underneath to catch the fall.

**And that matters more here than almost anywhere in this library because of Thread G, which is not the company's doing: the price.** At **109x earnings and 20x book**, after a **+23% move in fifteen days to a fresh all-time high**, the multiple assumes the plateau is a pause. **The business is doing well. The question this profile keeps arriving at is not whether Avalon executes — it is what happens to a 109x multiple in the quarter it merely does fine.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — 🔴 The multiple.** 109x earnings, 20.2x book, a fresh all-time high, and no dividend. **A de-rating to 45x on unchanged earnings loses ~54%.** This is not a tail risk; it is the dominant term in the return distribution.

**Risk 2 — 🔴 The plateau.** Two consecutive quarters at ~₹480cr, with Q1FY27 sequential revenue **+0.9%** and PAT **−15.3%**. **Q2FY27 is the clean test.** A second flat quarter converts "pause" into "plateau" and takes operating leverage with it.

**Risk 3 — 🔴 Cash conversion.** CFO/operating profit of **5% · 54% · 39% · 53%** across FY23–FY26. **Never above 54%.** Q1FY27's ₹32cr is the first good datapoint, but **net working capital rose five days sequentially in the same quarter.** For a business that must fund inventory and receivables ahead of every new program, **this is the failure mode that would force a capital raise into a 20x book multiple.**

**Risk 4 — 🔴 Operating leverage is the whole margin story.** EBITDA margin went 9.2% → 12.0% while **gross margin fell 85bps.** There is no pricing improvement underneath. **If revenue growth stops, margin expansion does not just stop — it can reverse.**

**Risk 5 — 🟡 US tariff re-escalation.** Tariffs have helped competitively so far and 99% has been passed through, but **pass-through inflates revenue at zero margin** (worth ~0.9% of reported EBITDA margin in Q1FY27) and a policy reversal changes customer behaviour, not just pricing.

**Risk 6 — 🟡 Lumpy, program-driven revenue.** Management's own example: a large program *"may happen in February of next year or customer may delay to April"* — a two-month slip moves a fiscal year.

**Risk 7 — 🟡 Promoter holding below 45%**, after a 6pp single-quarter sale in Jun-2025. Stable for four quarters, but there is no controlling majority.

**Risk 8 — 🟡 US breakeven slipping.** Guided for *"the end of this fiscal year"* with no revenue threshold given. Losses have narrowed ₹9cr → ₹4cr, but **management has simultaneously said it wants to shrink US manufacturing from ~28% back to ~20%** — which makes breakeven harder, not easier.

**Risk 9 — 🟡 Execution cost arriving ahead of revenue.** Five or six new VP-level hires, two new geographies, and inventory built for programs that have not yet shipped. **All of it is cost now for revenue later.**

**Risk 10 — 🟡 Competition from better-capitalised peers.** Kaynes, Syrma, Dixon and Cyient DLM are all expanding, and several have raised equity into the same demand.

**Risk 11 — ⚪ One fewer independent credit view** after the India Ratings withdrawal (§5.5(b)). Benign in itself — affirmed at A-/Positive before withdrawal, at the company's request — **but the cause has not been stated.**

**Risk 12 — ⚪ Retail-heavy register.** 72,969 shareholders, up 22% YoY. Retail joining at a 52-week high amplifies drawdowns.

**Risk 13 — ⚪ INR appreciation** would compress export margins on ~59% of revenue.

**Avalon is a young batsman who has just been promoted up the order and is scoring at nine an over — and the whole ground has priced him as if he will keep doing it.** India's only listed EMS with a real US manufacturing footprint, compounding revenue at **46% across eight quarters** to ₹1,603cr, with **box build up from 44.5% to 60% of revenue in a year**, ROCE near 20%, and **net debt of ₹24cr.**

**Thesis in a sentence:** a genuinely differentiated, well-run, fast-compounding EMS business whose entire investment question is the price rather than the company.

**What you're risking, and it is concentrated in one place: at 109x earnings and 20.2x book, after a 23% move in fifteen days to an all-time high, the base case — management delivering the middle of its own raised guidance — still returns roughly nothing over two years.** Underneath that: **two flat sequential quarters at ~₹480cr**; **cash conversion that has never cleared 54%** in four years; and a margin improvement built entirely on **operating leverage**, which reverses if growth stops — gross margin actually *fell* 85bps last quarter.

**Weighted 2-year return ≈ −13%, on a distribution from +40% to −54%. Conviction: Medium (Under Watch — valuation).** *A very good business. Not, at this price, a very good investment — and I would rather say that plainly than split the difference.*

**What to watch:** **⭐ Q2FY27 sequential revenue** — does ₹480cr break, or is it a plateau? Then **cash, not profit** — CFO/OP through H1FY27, after the encouraging ₹32cr in Q1; **US EBITDA breakeven by end-FY27** against a shrinking US footprint; **box build beyond 60% with gross margin holding**; and the first sign of **Europe or Southeast Asia** revenue.
