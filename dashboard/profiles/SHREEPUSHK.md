## 1. Business in Plain English

Shree Pushkar runs two businesses that share one factory's plumbing.

**The first is textile colour chemistry.** It makes **dye intermediates** — H-acid, K-acid and vinyl sulphone, the building blocks from which reactive dyes are made — and it makes some of those **reactive dyes** itself. The buyers are dye makers and textile processors, overwhelmingly outside India: *"majority of our sales is for the export business not for the domestic business"* (CMD, Q2FY26 call), with Bangladesh the single largest market.

**The second is fertiliser.** It makes **Single Super Phosphate (SSP)** — the cheap, low-analysis phosphate fertiliser Indian farmers use as a substitute when DAP is scarce — plus soil conditioners, water-soluble fertilisers and, from the new Unit 6, **NPK complex fertilisers**. These are sold through **800+ dealers** in Maharashtra, MP, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka and Haryana, under the government's Nutrient-Based Subsidy regime.

**What binds them is sulphuric acid.** The company burns imported sulphur to make sulphuric acid, oleum, chlorosulphonic acid and thionyl chloride. About **70-75% of that acid is consumed in-house** (CMD, Q3FY26 call) — it goes into the dye intermediates on one side, and into SSP on the other (*"to produce 1 ton of SSP you require 0.35 times of sulphuric acid"*). Gypsum and other by-products cross between the two. Management's own shorthand for the model is **"zero-waste, integrated"**.

**The analogy: a butcher who also runs a leather tannery.** One animal, two customer bases, and the by-product of one trade is the raw material of the other. The integration is real and it lowers cost — but it also means that when the price of the animal triples, both shops are hit at once. **That is exactly what happened in March 2026, when the Hormuz supply shock took sulphur from ~$250-300/t to ~$1,100/t** *(CMD, Q1FY27 call)*.

**Scale, in the company's own numbers (Q1FY27 deck):** fertiliser capacity **5,90,200 MTPA** (10,40,200 post-expansion), chemicals capacity **1,27,460 MTPA** (1,99,460 post-expansion), 9 manufacturing units, 3 solar plants (20.6 MW DC once Nanded is capitalised). FY26 consolidated revenue **₹976.6cr** — chemicals **₹531.8cr (54.4%)**, fertilisers **₹444.8cr (45.6%)**.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**1. India imports most of its phosphate and is trying hard to stop.** DAP is imported or made from imported rock and acid, and it costs the exchequer a fortune in subsidy. SSP is the domestic substitute the government actively encourages — the CMD, Q1FY26: *"the government is discouraging DAP, government is encouraging now other available phosphatic fertilizers, the substitute of DAP is SSP only."* When DAP is short or expensive, SSP volumes rise.

**2. The subsidy regime makes this a policy business, not a pure market.** NBS subsidy rates are reset by the government (rates were raised on 1-Feb and 1-Apr-2026), with retail prices constrained. When raw materials spike faster than subsidies move, margins are squeezed and **the rational response is to sell less** — which is precisely what Shree Pushkar did in Q3FY26 and Q4FY26.

**3. China used to supply India's dye intermediates; the flow has reversed.** The CMD, Q3FY26: *"five-six years ago, H-Acid, Vinyl Sulphone used to be imported in India… from last June, July till today at least 7 to 8 thousand tons of H-Acid has been exported."* China's withdrawal of VAT export rebates and India's BIS quality-control orders on K-acid and vinyl sulphone both push in the same direction. *(The export tonnage is management's figure and is not independently verified here.)*

**4. Textile manufacturing is migrating to Bangladesh, Vietnam, Turkey and Egypt** — which is why the dye-intermediate business is export-led and why a political disturbance in Dhaka shows up in this company's order book. Management sized Bangladesh at **"hardly 4%"** of revenue on the Q4FY25 call and at **"₹50-70cr… around 7% or 8%"** on the Q3FY26 call — two different figures nine months apart, both from the CMD.

**5. Sulphur is the swing input for both industries.** It is a refinery by-product, so its price follows crude and Gulf shipping. **Any thesis on this company is partly a thesis on sulphur.**

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**1. Integration across two industries that normally don't meet.** Most SSP makers buy acid; most dye-intermediate makers buy acid. Shree Pushkar makes its own and routes it both ways. When acid was ₹9-10/kg and rose to ~₹18/kg (CMD, Q3FY26), captive acid was worth real money.

**2. It will stop selling rather than sell at a loss — and says so.** Dispatches were halted in mid-March 2026; acid plants were put on low load in Q1FY27 because *"sulphur requires advance payment, while acid is sold entirely on credit. If I am earning two cents, I will do it. When I am not earning those two cents, why should I block working capital?"* **It is disciplined behaviour; it also makes volumes lumpy and forecasts unreliable.**

**3. A cleaner balance sheet than the capex programme suggests.** Consolidated borrowings ₹131cr against ₹140.7cr of non-lien deposits at March 2026 — **net debt/equity −0.01x** (CFO, Q4FY26). Finance cost ₹4cr on ₹977cr of revenue. **ICRA A+/Stable**, reaffirmed July 2026.

**4. A named position in niche intermediates.** The CMD's claim, Q1FY27: *"we are the largest manufacturer of K-acid in India. No one else is in K-acid."* **This is management's assertion and is not verified.** The BIS quality-control order on K-acid (deferred to May 2026) is a real regulatory barrier to Chinese imports if enforced.

**5. Renewable power as a cost lever.** 10.6 MW DC operating (Ratnagiri + Hisar), 10 MW at Nanded completed — 20.6 MW DC total. Each MW ~1.4 million units/year (CMD, Q2FY26).

**6. Promoters add capital rather than extract it.** Three preferential issues to the promoter family; the latest is **7,36,196 warrants to Joint MD Gautam Makharia at ₹407.50** (₹30cr, 25% paid, allotted 27-Dec-2025). **Promoter holding has not fallen in any filed quarter since Dec-2022** (§3.5). Promoters also lent the company **₹26.55cr interest-free** during FY26.

### Where the moat is weaker — honestly

- **Neither product is differentiated to the end customer.** SSP is a commodity sold into a price-controlled market; H-acid and vinyl sulphone are commodity intermediates priced off Chinese competition. The integration is a **cost** advantage, not a **pricing** one.
- **The integration cuts both ways.** A sulphur spike hits both segments simultaneously. There is no internal hedge.
- **Nameplate utilisation is low.** FY26 fertiliser sales of 2,52,777 MT against 5,90,200 MTPA of capacity is **~43% of nameplate** *(my calculation from deck capacity and call volumes)*. Management quotes 55-70% utilisation, presumably on a different base; **it is adding 4,50,000 MTPA more.**
- **The dyes business is not growing in volume** — see §4.
- **Management declines to disclose segment profitability**: *"there is no proper system of what is the profitability of the chemical and what is the profitability of the fertilizer"* (CMD, Q1FY26). The Annual Report treats the company as a single segment under Ind AS 108. **An investor cannot tell which half earns the money.**

### 🔴 Where the "chemical volume growth" actually came from

The FY26 message on every call and in the chairman's letter: **chemicals revenue +25.2% on +27.9% volume.** The standalone MD&A of the same Annual Report shows the standalone chemicals business — the dyes and intermediates units at Ratnagiri:

|---|---|---|---|
| Volume | 47,814 MT | **45,853 MT** | **−4.1%** |
| Value | ₹414.19cr | ₹449.24cr | +8.5% |

| Reconciliation *(my arithmetic, consolidated − standalone)* | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidated chemicals volume | 56,626 MT | 72,423 MT |

**About two-thirds of the ₹107cr of chemical revenue growth, and all of the volume growth, came from the fertiliser subsidiaries' chemical sales — at roughly ₹31/kg, i.e. predominantly acid.** The dyes-and-intermediates business grew value 8.5% on falling volume. **This is my reconciliation, not a company disclosure** — intercompany eliminations could move it by a few crore — but the direction is unambiguous. It also explains the question management could not answer on the Q4FY26 call: *"our chemical volumes are up more than 30%, but our revenues are flat from last year quarter 4 to this year quarter 4. ## 5. Connecting the Dots

**The integration thesis is sound, and the current environment is its stress test.** A sulphur shock is exactly the event a captive-acid producer should navigate better than peers who buy acid — and Q1FY27's 11.4% EBITDA margin on lower volumes suggests it did. **The model is protecting margin by shrinking volume.** That works for a quarter or two. It does not grow a company.

**The growth plan is almost entirely fertiliser.** Of +4,50,000 MTPA fertiliser and +72,000 MTPA chemical capacity being added, most of the chemical addition is acid feeding NPK. **The strategic weight is moving from textile chemistry toward complex fertilisers** — a policy-driven, subsidy-dependent market in which the company is a new entrant against Coromandel, Paradeep and Madhya Bharat Agro, all building capacity in the same central-India geography (a ValuePickr member made exactly this point in Nov-2025).

**And the dyes half — the one with export customers and pricing optionality from China's retreat — is not growing in tonnage.** The FY26 "chemicals growth" is substantially the subsidiaries selling acid. Unit 5's dye capacity, which would change that, is finished but not switched on.

**So the chain is: project delays → capex in CWIP without revenue → negative free cash → reliance on deposits and promoter money → a return profile stuck at 12-13%.** Each link resolves the moment Units 5 and 6 run. **The investment question is simply whether they now start, and at what utilisation in a market where the input has tripled.** Management has given a date for that seven times.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**1. 🔴 Units 5 and 6 do not start — or start and run at low load.** Seven timetables in 21 months, three different stated causes. At ₹100/kg sulphur and ₹100/kg ammonia, **a new NPK plant is a working-capital sink** — the CMD's own words: *"once it's started, if I stop later this will involve lot of working capital… at the three times price."*

**2. 🔴 Sulphur and ammonia stay at crisis prices and the finished-product market does not reprice.** Both segments are hit simultaneously. The integration does not hedge; it concentrates.

> 📈 **Factor check 19-Sep-2026:** Sulphur is not tracked by any available source; crude, its driver, is +30.1% over three months, and rock phosphate +11.5%. National fertiliser output is −8.0% YoY. *(Numbers and sources: Input & factor check, at the top.)*

**3. 🔴 Profit-margin guidance is not reliable.** FY26 was guided at 8.25%, 8.5-9%, 7.5-8% and 8% across four calls and delivered 7.1%. **FY27's 8.5-9% should be read through that record.**

**4. 🔴 Negative free cash through FY28.** ₹303cr of capex outstanding on top of five years of cumulative −₹45cr FCF. Funding is stated to be deposits + warrants + accruals, with term debt undecided. **A delay in Unit 6 revenue arriving collides directly with the Unit 8 spend.**

**5. 🔴 The dyes business is not growing in volume.** Standalone chemical tonnage fell 4.1% in FY26 while the consolidated headline said +27.9%. **If Unit 5 does not change that, the "chemical company" half of the thesis is a price story, not a growth story.**

**6. 🟡 Policy risk in fertilisers.** NBS subsidy rates, the 10% cap the CMD cited, and any change in the government's DAP-vs-SSP stance directly set realisations. **Central-India SSP/NPK capacity is being added simultaneously by Coromandel, Madhya Bharat Agro and Krishna Phoschem.**

**7. 🟡 Bangladesh and export-textile exposure.** Stated as 4% (May-25) and 7-8% (Feb-26) of revenue by the same CMD. Either way, the dyes customer base is concentrated in politically unstable textile economies.

**8. 🟡 No segment profitability disclosed.** The Annual Report reports one segment. The CMD says a split *"won't be the right presentation."* **An investor cannot see whether fertilisers or chemicals earn the 10% EBITDA.**

**9. 🟡 Spoken figures drift from printed ones.** ROCE 15.3% (call) vs 13.2% (AR); "higher sales volume across both businesses" in a quarter where both fell. Not accounting issues — **a pattern in how results are narrated.**

**10. 🟡 Key-man risk and communication style.** The CMD answers essentially every question, often from the plant without data, and responds to hard questions with challenge (*"Then why are you asking me this question when you aren't confident yourself?"*, Q1FY27). **Institutions have not come; the register is 99.5% retail and promoter.**

**11. 🟡 Family related-party flows** — ₹2.2cr annual rent to the CMD's mother, family salaries, interest-free promoter loans. Individually small and disclosed; collectively a reason to watch the RPT note each year.

## 8. 30-Second Memory Hook

**Shree Pushkar burns sulphur into acid and sends it two ways** — into H-acid and vinyl sulphone for Bangladesh's textile dye makers, and into SSP and NPK for Indian farmers — a zero-waste, A+-rated, net-cash family business that hit its revenue guidance three years running, halted dispatches rather than sell at a loss when Hormuz tripled sulphur, and has promoters who lend it money interest-free and buy warrants rather than sell shares. It is also a company whose two new Ratnagiri plants have had seven start dates, whose profit-margin guidance missed every time in FY26, whose "27.9% chemical volume growth" turns out to be subsidiaries selling acid while the dyes business shrank, and which has produced −₹45cr of free cash from ₹259cr of profit over five years.

**The cricketer is Cheteshwar Pujara.** Utterly dependable at what he does, occupies the crease, never gives his wicket away cheaply — and scores slowly. You never lose the match with him in, but you rarely win it quickly either, and the scoreboard pressure builds while he waits for the right ball. **Shree Pushkar's discipline is real: it would rather not bat than play a bad shot.** The problem is that the run rate — a 13% ROCE, a plant a year late — is what the price is being judged against.

**Thesis in a sentence:** *A well-financed, integrated dye-intermediate and phosphate-fertiliser maker at 22.7x that has built a doubling of capacity it has not yet switched on — cheap enough if Units 5 and 6 finally run in FY27, and a patient hold with a −50% tail if the sulphur shock keeps them idle.*
