## 1. Business in Plain English

**Shree Refrigerations keeps warships cool.**

A naval vessel is a sealed steel box full of people, electronics and machinery, operating in tropical seas. **The air-conditioning and refrigeration plant is not a comfort system — it is what keeps the sonar, the combat management system and the crew alive.** It has to survive shock, vibration, salt, roll and battle damage, and it has to be certified to do so.

**Shree makes those systems: AC plants, HVAC systems, marinised split units, refrigeration plants — 5 TR to 300 TR — plus the control panels that run them.** It also does retrofits and aftermarket service on ships already in the water.

**Three facts define the company, and all three came from the FY26 earnings call:**

1. **It is the only Indian firm to have indigenised an entire submarine HVAC system** — the **Scorpene** air-conditioning plant.
2. **It holds what management calls "three out of three" naval registrations**, and is *"the only company in the HVAC segment in India which can manufacture or which are allowed to manufacture and supply HVAC system, AC and Ref plant, as well as our own control panel."* **Competitors hold one or two and must buy the control panel from someone else.**
3. **It introduced the first oil-free magnetic-bearing compressor chiller to the Indian Navy** — a technology that, by coincidence or design, is exactly what hyperscale data centres are moving to.

**The history matters.** Founded around 1990, it spent most of its life **making test equipment for other refrigeration companies.** It **entered defence in 2016-17**, and listed on BSE-SME in 2025. **~350 staff, eight offices concentrated on the coastline, manufacturing at Karad, Maharashtra with a second unit newly inaugurated.**

**Management's own line, delivered twice on the call:** *"we don't manufacture products, we manufacture pride."*

**The 30-second snapshot:**
- **Pune / Karad, Maharashtra** · **CMD Ravalnath Gopinath Shende** · **WTD Cmde. Sunil Kaushik, NM, VSM (Retd.)** — a retired Navy Commodore on the board

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — 🟢 The Indian Navy is expanding, and the number is large.** From the company's own deck: **the Navy is expected to reach ~230 ships by 2035, with government approvals already received for projects worth ~₹2.35 lakh crore**, and order inflows expected over a two-year horizon. **AC/HVAC systems are ~1% of project value** — so about **₹2,350cr of addressable defence-marine HVAC.**

**Force 2 — Merchant marine adds a second layer.** **~437 vessels, ~₹1.9 lakh crore being tendered, HVAC at ~0.5% of project value** — roughly **₹950cr.** *Together the two give the ₹3,000–3,500cr TAM management quoted on the call, which they were careful to say is **marine only**.*

**Force 3 — 🟢 Indigenisation is policy, and it is the whole reason this company exists in defence.** *A submarine air-conditioning plant that was previously imported and is now made in Maharashtra is exactly what the positive-indigenisation lists are designed to produce.*

**Force 4 — 🟢 Qualification is the barrier, and it is measured in years.** The deck's own framing: **long qualification cycles, high reliability requirements, strong aftermarket and service opportunities.** *Once a system is certified onto a class of ship, it stays there for the life of the ship — and the refits come back to you.*

**Force 5 — 🆕 Data-centre cooling, which is the optionality.** **Cooling is ~12–13% of a data centre's total project cost**, and the technology is moving toward **oil-free magnetic-bearing systems and liquid cooling** — *which is the technology Shree already builds for the Navy.* **A capability transfer rather than a new capability.**
> ⚠️ **The deck's own data-centre sizing does not add up.** It states Indian data-centre capacity going from **~₹9,600cr (2024) to ~₹13,000cr (2033)** *and* a **~10.5% CAGR**. **Those two are inconsistent — ₹9,600cr compounding at 10.5% for nine years is roughly ₹23,600cr, not ₹13,000cr.** *One of the figures is wrong and I am not going to guess which. The directional case for data-centre cooling is sound; this particular slide should not be relied on.*

**Where SHREEREFR sits.** ₹1,469cr in **Sector 1 (Aerospace & Defence)** alongside **PARAS, AZAD, AXISCADES, DYNAMATIC, ROSSELL and UNIMECH.** *It is the smallest name in the sector and the only one selling into naval auxiliary machinery rather than airframes, engines or electronics.*

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — 🟢🟢 The "three out of three" registration, and it is the reason to own this at all.** **Shree is, on its own account, the only Indian HVAC company permitted to supply the complete package — HVAC system, AC and refrigeration plant, and its own control panel.** *Competitors must buy the panel from a third party, which means they cannot own the interface, the fault diagnostics or the retrofit.* ***In a business where the customer is a single navy and the qualification takes years, being the only complete-scope supplier is close to a structural monopoly on new scope.***

**Moat 2 — 🟢 The submarine.** **The only Indian firm to have indigenised the whole Scorpene HVAC system.** *A submarine's cooling plant is the hardest version of this problem — noise, vibration and acoustic signature all matter in a way they do not on a surface ship. Having done it is a permanent reference.*

**Moat 3 — 🟢 An installed base that generates refits.** **Named programmes include INS Dhruv (Project VC 11184) and the Fleet Support Ships.** *Every ship fitted is a service and retrofit annuity for twenty-five years.*

**Moat 4 — 🟢 Oil-free magnetic-bearing technology, introduced to the Navy first.** Lower energy consumption, less maintenance, better lifecycle economics, lower vibration and noise. **The 29-Aug-2026 Navy order is specifically for a *"Magnetic Bearing Compressor Based AC Plant."*** ***And it is the same technology data centres are converting to — which is why the data-centre entry is a genuine adjacency rather than a diversification.***

**Moat 5 — A board with the customer's own experience on it.** **Cmde. Sunil Kaushik, NM, VSM (Retd.), is Whole-Time Director.** *In defence procurement, knowing how the customer specifies, tests and accepts is not a soft advantage.*

**Moat 6 — 🟢 In-house test facilities and a new plant.** Performance validation done internally at **Karad**, with **Unit 2 newly inaugurated** and **Phase 1 capex of ₹25cr at Hanbarwadi** substantially complete.

### Where the moat is weaker

- **🔴 The margin went the wrong way in the year revenue grew 55%.** **EBITDA +21.8% on revenue +55.5% means margin fell from ~27% to 21.4%.** *Management guided 20–24% forward — which is guiding to the compressed level, not back to the old one.*
- **🔴 68.2x earnings and 6.8x book on ₹21.4cr of profit.** *The multiple assumes the data-centre story works.*
- **🔴 Working capital is enormous even after a large improvement.** **~370 days, down from ~570.** *Receivable days around 250, inventory around 230.*
- **⚠️ The orders are small and lumpy.** **₹9.75cr (Kongsberg), ₹2.55cr (Mazagon Dock), ₹3.93cr (Indian Navy)** are the three disclosed since May. *A ₹270cr book built from ₹3–10cr tickets is a lot of individual execution.*
- **⚠️ Customer concentration is close to absolute.** *The Indian Navy, its shipyards and its primes are effectively the entire business.*
- **⚠️ Half-yearly reporting and a ~3-call history.** *There is very little track record to score.*
- **⚠️ The data-centre business does not exist yet.** **Management confirmed FY27 guidance contains no data-centre revenue; they expect it in FY28.**

## 4. Numbers Decoded

> **⚠️ BASIS NOTE. Shree reports half-yearly.** Screener's "quarters" table is half-years (Sep/Mar). **FY26 figures below are from the company's own August-2026 investor deck, cross-checked against Screener.** *Note the deck gives FY26 EPS as **₹6.47** while Screener shows **₹6.04** — an unreconciled difference, both stated.*

### FY26 — the year in full

|---|---|---|---|

| **EBITDA** | ₹26.9cr | **₹32.8cr** | **+21.8%** |

**🔴 The margin line is the one to sit with.** **Revenue grew 55.5% and EBITDA grew 21.8%.** *In a business with high fixed costs and a new plant coming on, you would expect the opposite — operating leverage should have expanded the margin, not compressed it by six points.* **Management's forward guidance of 20–24% accepts the new, lower level rather than promising a return to 27%.** ***That is honest guidance. It is also an admission that FY26's growth was bought at a lower price point.***

**🟢 Against that, the two things that got materially better are the two that usually kill small industrials: operating cash flow turned positive, and the working-capital cycle came down 200 days.** *From ~570 days, though — and 370 days is still more than a year of revenue tied up.*

### The half-yearly shape

| ₹cr | H1FY25 | H2FY25 | H1FY26 | **H2FY26** |

| **Revenue** | 51 | 48 | 50 | **103** |

***Read that row and the volatility of this business is obvious.*** **H1FY26 did ₹50cr at an 11% margin; H2FY26 did ₹103cr at 26%.** *Half of the full year's revenue and most of its profit landed in the second half. **Any read of a single half will mislead, and the H1FY27 print due in November will look weak against H2FY26 for structural reasons that have nothing to do with the business deteriorating.***

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — 🟢 The moat is narrow, deep and genuinely rare.** **The only Indian company allowed to supply the complete naval cooling package including its own control panel; the only one to have indigenised a submarine's HVAC.** *In a market with one customer and multi-year qualification cycles, that is close to structural.*

**Thread B — 🟢 And it has just been validated in revenue.** **₹10cr to ₹154cr in five years**, operating cash flow positive for the first time, working capital down 200 days, Phase 1 capex delivered on time.

**Thread C — 🔴 But the growth was bought at a lower margin.** **Revenue +55.5%, EBITDA +21.8%, margin ~27% → 21.4%** — and guidance accepts the new level rather than promising the old one. *Operating leverage should work the other way in a business like this. It did not.*

**Thread D — 🔴 The working capital is still the dominant fact of the balance sheet.** **370 days after a 200-day improvement.** *More than a year of revenue tied up in receivables and inventory, in a business selling to government shipyards.*

**Thread E — 🟡 The order book is a large number made of small pieces.** **₹270cr from ₹2.5–9.75cr tickets.** *Robust to any single loss, demanding to execute, and largely invisible in Reg-30 announcements.*

**Thread F — 🟢 The data-centre adjacency is technologically genuine, not a bolt-on.** **Oil-free magnetic-bearing chillers, first proven on warships, are what hyperscale data centres are converting to.** **A wholly-owned subsidiary was approved for it on 31 August.** ***But management explicitly excluded it from FY27 guidance and put the revenue in FY28 — so nothing in the current numbers reflects it, and everything in the multiple might.***

**Thread G — 🔴 And the price already assumes a lot.** **68.2x earnings and 6.8x book on ₹21.4cr of profit, 4.9% below the 52-week high, at a company with three earnings calls of history and half-yearly reporting.**

**Thread H — 🟡 The communication is not yet reliable.** **Revenue growth stated as 150% when it was 55%; forward guidance given as both 20% and 40% CAGR in consecutive answers; a six-point margin decline unmentioned in the opening remarks.** *None of it is concealment — the correct numbers are all in the same deck. But it is a company still learning to be listed.*

**How they interact.** ***A rare and defensible defence niche, executed well enough to triple revenue in three years and finally generate cash, is being priced at sixty-eight times earnings on the strength of a data-centre business that does not yet exist — by a market that has three earnings calls and one full year of audited listed history to go on.*** **The moat justifies attention. The multiple prices the option, not the business.**

### Weighted return framework (2-year, to FY28)

*Anchored on ₹1,469cr and ~3.58cr shares. **FY26 base: revenue ₹153.5cr, EBITDA ₹32.8cr at 21.4%, PAT ₹21.4cr.***

**Base case — 50%.** **Growth lands between the CFO's 20% and the CMD's 40% — call it 30% — and margin holds inside the guided 20–24% band at 22%.** FY27 revenue **~₹200cr**, FY28 **~₹270cr** → EBITDA ₹59cr; depreciation ₹9cr, interest ₹4cr, other income ₹2cr → PBT ₹48cr, tax 22% → **PAT ~₹37cr, EPS ~₹10.5.** **Multiple de-rates to 40x** — still a defence-sector premium, discounted for SME status and the fact that data-centre revenue has not appeared → **₹1,480cr. ≈ +1%.**

**Bull case — 25%.** **The 40% CAGR is the right number, the ₹2.35 lakh crore of naval approvals convert into HVAC orders over the two-year horizon management describes, and Aquilon Deep Chill wins its first data-centre work in FY28.** FY28 revenue **~₹380cr at 24%** → EBITDA ₹91cr, **PAT ~₹60cr**, and the market pays **55x** for a defence-plus-data-centre story → **₹3,300cr. ≈ +125%.**

**Bear case — 25%.** **The margin keeps sliding as it has for two years — 31% → 27% → 21% → mid-teens — because winning larger naval orders means competing on price.** Working capital stays near 370 days, growth comes in at the CFO's 20% rather than the CMD's 40%, the data-centre entry is subscale or late, and an SME multiple de-rates hard on the first disappointing half. FY28 revenue **~₹210cr at 16%** → EBITDA ₹34cr, **PAT ~₹18cr**, multiple **25x** → **₹450cr. ≈ −69%.**

**Weighted 2-year expected return ≈ +14%** *(0.50 × 1 + 0.25 × 125 + 0.25 × −69)* — roughly **7% CAGR**, no dividend.

> ***The base case is essentially flat, and that is the honest arithmetic of buying a good business at 68x.*** **All of the expected return sits in the bull tail, and the bull tail requires a business that does not yet exist to start earning within eighteen months.** *With a −69% bear and a +125% bull, this should be sized as an option, not as a position.*

**Why not higher.** **68.2x earnings and 6.8x book on ₹21.4cr of profit, at 4.9% below the 52-week high, on a margin that has fallen in each of the last two years and guidance that accepts the fall.** **The company has three earnings calls of history, reports half-yearly, and gave two different growth numbers and one wrong growth number on the only call I could read in full.** ***A new position at this multiple with this little track record earns Medium-Low, and the next two prints will settle whether it should be higher.***

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — 🔴 The margin trend is the wrong way and it has been for two years.** **OPM 31% (FY24) → 27% (FY25) → 21.4% (FY26)**, with FY26 growing revenue 55% and EBITDA only 22%. **Guidance of 20–24% accepts the compressed level.** *If winning larger naval orders means bidding harder, the mix shift that drove growth is also what is killing the margin — and that trade continues.*
**⚠️ MATERIALISED, twice.**

**Risk 2 — 🔴 68.2x earnings, 6.8x book, on ₹21.4cr of profit.** *A de-rate to 40x on unchanged FY26 earnings is **₹856cr, −42%, with nothing going wrong.***

**Risk 3 — 🔴 Working capital at ~370 days.** *Even after a 200-day improvement, more than a year of revenue is tied up. **Government shipyard receivables do not shorten because you would like them to**, and the FY25 position of ~570 days shows how bad it can get.*

**Risk 4 — 🔴 One customer, effectively.** **The Indian Navy, its shipyards (Mazagon Dock) and its primes (Kongsberg) are the business.** *There is no commercial diversification to fall back on if a procurement cycle pauses — and the entire ₹2.35 lakh crore of naval approvals sits with one buyer whose timetable is political.*

**Risk 5 — 🔴 The data-centre business is entirely prospective.** **Management explicitly excluded it from FY27 guidance; revenue is expected in FY28; the subsidiary was incorporated on 31 August 2026 and no purpose was even stated in the filing.** ***A large part of a 68x multiple is being paid for a business with no customer, no order and no disclosed plan.***

**Risk 6 — 🟡 Half-yearly reporting, three calls, one full listed year.** *There is no base rate. The H1FY27 print in November will be the first datapoint against which any guidance can be scored — and it will look weak against H2FY26 for structural seasonal reasons.*

**Risk 7 — 🟡 Communication reliability.** **Revenue growth stated as "150%" when it was 55.5%; forward guidance given as both "20% CAGR" and "CAGR 40%" in consecutive answers; a six-point margin decline absent from the opening remarks.** *Not concealment — the correct figures are in the same documents — but it is a management still learning what listed disclosure requires.*

**Risk 8 — 🟡 Order tickets are small and mostly invisible.** **₹2.55cr, ₹3.93cr, ₹9.75cr are the three disclosed since May.** *Most of the ₹270cr book sits below the Reg-30 threshold, so an outsider cannot track momentum between half-yearly prints.*

**Risk 9 — 🟡 BSE-SME liquidity.** **4,461 shareholders, no NSE listing.** *The stock is +155% off its 52-week low; the same mechanism works in reverse, and exit liquidity in a drawdown is genuinely poor.*

**Risk 10 — 🟢 What would genuinely surprise on the upside.** **The "three out of three" registration is a scope monopoly, and scope monopolies eventually become pricing power.** *If the Navy's ₹2.35 lakh crore of approvals convert on the two-year horizon management describes, Shree is bidding for that HVAC content with no complete-scope domestic competitor — and the margin could go back up rather than down.*

## 8. 30-Second Memory Hook

> **Shree Refrigerations keeps the Indian Navy cool, and it is the only company in India allowed to supply the whole package.**
>
> **A warship is a sealed steel box in a tropical sea, full of people and electronics that stop working if the air-conditioning does. Getting certified to build that plant takes a decade. Shree holds all three naval registrations — the HVAC system, the AC and refrigeration plant, and its own control panel — and every competitor holds one or two and has to buy the panel from somebody else. It is also the only Indian firm to have indigenised an entire submarine's air-conditioning, on the Scorpene, and the first to put an oil-free magnetic-bearing chiller on an Indian warship.**
>
> **That last one matters twice, because oil-free magnetic-bearing cooling is exactly what hyperscale data centres are converting to. On the thirty-first of August the board incorporated a subsidiary called Aquilon Deep Chill to go after it.**
>
> **Revenue has gone from ten crore to a hundred and fifty-four in five years. Last year it grew fifty-five percent, operating cash flow turned positive for the first time, and the working-capital cycle came down by two hundred days.**
>
> **But EBITDA grew only twenty-two percent while revenue grew fifty-five, so the margin fell from twenty-seven to twenty-one — and management's guidance accepts the lower number rather than promising the old one back. Working capital is still three hundred and seventy days. The order book is two hundred and seventy crore assembled from tickets of two to ten crore. And the data-centre business that a lot of this price is paying for has no customer, no order, and management's own statement that it contributes nothing before FY28.**
>
> **What you're buying:** a rare, registration-protected monopoly on complete naval cooling scope, run by a team that includes a retired Navy Commodore, at four to five percent share of a three-thousand-crore addressable market. **What you're risking:** sixty-eight times earnings and nearly seven times book, on a margin that has fallen two years running, at a BSE-SME with three earnings calls of history that reports twice a year.
>
> **Weighted 2-year return ≈ +14%** — base **+1%**, bull **+125%**, bear **−69%**. ***Almost all of the expected return is in the bull tail. Size it as an option.***
>
> **MEDIUM-LOW.** **What to watch: the H1FY27 print in November — revenue above ₹75cr at a 22%+ margin, and whether the working-capital cycle keeps falling.**
