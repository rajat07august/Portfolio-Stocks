## 💰 The balance sheet — where this refresh actually changes the thesis

**v1 said, in full: "Net cash position."** Two words, no number. The number is the thesis.

**Treasury, audited consolidated balance sheet at 31-Mar-2026** *(₹ lakh → cr, from the 13-May-2026 results filing)*

| Line | ₹ cr |
|---|---|
| Investments (non-current) | 66.3 |
| Other financial assets (non-current — long-term deposits) | 231.0 |
| Investments (current) | 20.5 |
| Cash and cash equivalents | 42.6 |
| Bank balances other than cash and equivalents | 86.4 |
| Other financial assets (current — deposits) | 471.7 |
| **Total treasury** | **≈ ₹918.5 cr** |
| Borrowings (lease liabilities only) | (26.2) |
| **Net cash** | **≈ ₹892 cr** |

**Cross-checks that this is real cash and not receivables in disguise:** (a) trade receivables are stated **separately** at ₹35.0cr — consistent with Screener's 27 debtor days on ₹473cr revenue; (b) the FY26 cash-flow statement shows **₹649.8cr placed into fixed deposits** during the year under investing activities; (c) FY26 interest income of **₹48.4cr** on an average balance of roughly ₹800cr = 6.1%, a plausible deposit yield; (d) Screener's own grouping (Investments ₹87cr + Other Assets ₹890cr) reconciles.

**Total assets are ₹1,104.7cr. Treasury is ₹918.5cr. 83% of this company's balance sheet is a deposit account.**

### 🔴 The consequence — the core business is MORE expensive than the screen, not less

The reflex on a cash-rich company is "strip the cash, it's cheaper." Here that reflex is wrong, because you must strip the **income the cash generates** at the same time.

| Step | ₹ cr |
|---|---|
| Market cap | 5,078 |
| Less net cash | (892) |
| **Enterprise value** | **4,186** |
| FY26 consolidated PBT | 235.4 |
| Less total other income (interest ₹48.4 + unallocable ₹6.4) | (54.8) |
| **Core operating PBT** | **180.5** |
| At FY26 effective tax rate 26.2% → **core operating PAT** | **≈ 133** |
| **EV / core operating PAT** | **≈ 31.4x** |

*(Same exercise on TTM: EV ₹4,186cr ÷ core TTM PAT ≈ ₹138cr = **30.3x**.)*

**Headline P/E 28.6x. Core business ~31x.** v1's central claim — *"The most attractive **value** + quality name in Indian financial services professional-owned space"* — does not survive this. The quality claim survives easily. The value claim does not.

### 🟢 The same ₹918cr, read the other way — the business is BETTER than 26% ROCE suggests

Reported ROCE is 26.3% and ROE 19.7%. Both are computed over capital employed that is **83% idle deposits**. The FY26 segment disclosure shows the ratings segment earning **₹231.6cr of segment result**; the genuine operating capital behind it is roughly ₹120-150cr (net fixed assets ₹81cr + right-of-use ₹23cr + receivables ₹35cr, less payables). **The underlying rating franchise earns well north of 100% on operating capital.** It is a near-zero-capital regulatory annuity.

**So the cash cuts both ways, and this is the honest summary:** the *business* is far better than the reported ratios imply, and the *stock* is more expensive than the reported multiple implies. Both facts have the same cause. Which one dominates depends entirely on **what management does with the money** — and on that, four years of evidence say: nothing.

## 🔴 Governance & regulatory — three items, none of them ever spoken about

**This is the section v1 does not have, and the reason conviction moves.** All three items below were **properly filed with the exchanges** — there is no disclosure violation. But **none appears in the 14-May-2026 annual earnings call, and none appears in any investor deck.** Verified by keyword search across the full Q4FY26 transcript and the Q1FY27 deck: `Operus` 0 hits, `Vestian` 0, `GCC` 0, `warning` 0, `inspection` 0. For a company that speaks to investors **once a year**, an investor relying on the call and the decks would not know about any of them.

### 1. 🔴 SEBI administrative warning — received 24-Mar-2026

Verbatim from the Annexure to CARE's own 25-Mar-2026 filing:

> *"SEBI had conducted an inspection of the Company as a Credit Rating Agency, for the period May 1, 2024 to March 31, 2025. As an outcome of the inspection, SEBI has issued an administrative warning to the Company."*
>
> *Details of the violation(s): "Administrative warning has been issued for **inconsistencies observed with respect to disclosure of applicable criteria in Press Releases and Rating Notes**."*

**Proportionate reading — I want to be fair here.** An administrative warning is the **mildest** rung of SEBI action: no monetary penalty, no adjudication, no business restriction, no restraint. The company states there is no material financial or operational impact and I have no reason to dispute that. Most large CRAs have received one at some point.

**But it should be in the profile, for two reasons.** First, the finding goes to the **core product** — the disclosure of which rating criteria were applied, in the press releases and rating notes that *are* the deliverable. This is not a filing-clerical matter. Second, it sits uncomfortably beside the emphasis of the annual call held **seven weeks later**, in which Mehul Pandya said *"our stability rates in the investment grade category ... are consistently at or above industry averages"* and described analytical rigour as *"a non-negotiable aspect for us"* — without once mentioning that SEBI had just warned the company on criteria disclosure. **That is my inference about the framing, not a statement of fact about intent.**

*Related, and separate:* **BSE + NSE issued a cautionary letter on 17-Feb-2026** for *"inadvertently reporting incorrect ISIN of one of the clients ... in credit rating disclosure on System Driven Disclosure platform."* Genuinely minor and I treat it as such — but it is the second regulatory correspondence in five weeks.

### 2. 🆕 Operus Limited — the ₹22.5cr JV that fails management's own filter

Approved by the Board's Strategy and Investment Committee on **17-Mar-2026**. From the SEBI-mandated annexure:

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Entity | **"Operus Limited"** (proposed name) |
| Partner | **Vestian Global Workplace Services Private Limited** |
| Structure | **50% CARE Ratings / 50% Vestian** |
| CARE's investment | **≈ ₹22.5 cr**, at face value ₹10/share |
| Industry | **Global Capability Centre (GCC) enablement services** |
| Scope | *"design, establish, and operate Global Capability Centres (GCCs) in India, delivering end-to-end solutions across strategy, operating model design, **talent acquisition, workspace setup**, and regulatory compliance"* |
| Approvals | Requires **NOC from SEBI** |
| Target | Incorporation *"tentatively completed by June 30, 2026"* |
| Related party | **Yes** — will be a related party of the Company |

**Three things about this.**

**(a) It is the largest single capital commitment CARE has made in years** — ₹22.5cr, against ₹30cr for the entire CareEdge Global IFSC build.

**(b) It fails the filter management stated on the record two months later.** On 14-May-2026, pressed by Priyank Chheda (Vallum Capital) on why no acquisition had happened in four years, Mehul Pandya set out the test:

> *"We are looking for three things simultaneously: strategic fit with our core domain, **which is credit risk, analytics and consulting services**. A target that is either in an adjacent product area we cannot build quickly enough organically or a geography where a foothold matters, and a valuation that reflects intrinsic value..."*
>
> *"...a forced acquisition at the wrong price or of the wrong asset would destroy more value than it creates."*

**Talent acquisition and workspace setup for global capability centres is not credit risk, analytics or consulting services.** It is real-estate and HR services — Vestian's business, not CARE's. The JV had been Board-approved for **two months** when that answer was given, in a Q&A **specifically about capital allocation and inorganic strategy**, and it was not mentioned. *This is my judgement on strategic fit, not a claim that management said the two were inconsistent.*

**(c) It was not in the Q1FY27 deck either** — five months on, with a stated incorporation target of 30-Jun-2026 now passed and no completion filed. **Status unknown. Watch item for Q2FY27.**

**Counter-argument, stated fairly:** GCC enablement is a large, genuinely growing India opportunity; CARE brings regulatory-compliance and industry-research capability that a pure workplace-services firm lacks; the ₹22.5cr is 0.4% of market cap and 2.5% of the treasury — a cheap option, not a bet-the-company move. If it works it is a smart use of idle cash. **My concern is not the ₹22.5cr. It is that a related-party JV outside the core domain was approved and then never discussed at the only forum where investors can ask about it.**

### 3. 🟢 Madras High Court injunction — vacated 3-Jun-2026

From CARE's 4-Jun-2026 filing:

> *"...the Hon'ble Madras High Court order dated February 1, 2023, had issued an **injunction restraining the alienation of assets by the Company**, until the provision of adequate security by the Company. This is to disclose that the said injunction has been **vacated by the Court vide order dated June 3, 2026**, in view of Company having complied with the order dated February 1, 2023 (which was complied without prejudice to the rights of the Company in its appeals)."*

**Clear positive — and a reminder of what v1 didn't surface.** For **three years and four months**, CARE operated under a court order restraining it from alienating assets. That is a meaningful constraint on a company sitting on ₹918cr and talking about acquisitions, and it may be part of why nothing happened. v1's profile — written 3-Jul-2026, a month *after* the vacation — mentions neither the injunction nor its removal.

**Not fully resolved:** the underlying **appeals remain pending** (compliance was *"without prejudice to the rights of the Company in its appeals"*). The injunction is gone; the litigation is not. Quantum is not disclosed in any filing I read. **Stated gap.**

Stated by management on the Q4FY25 call (14-May-2025): *"For FY'25, the Ratings to non-Ratings business mix stood at 89.5% to 10.5%. **Our long-term target remains to transition towards an 80-20 mix.**"*

| | FY25 | FY26 | Q1FY27 |

| Non-ratings share | 10.5% | **10.6%** | **11.6%** |

That is a reasonable defence — the denominator grew. **But a target with no timeline and 1.1ppt of movement in five quarters is 🔴 on a delivery scorecard, and I score it as such.** The Q1FY27 move to 11.6% is the first real progress; treat it as one data point.

**Weighted 2-year return: (0.55 × 23) + (0.25 × 70) + (0.20 × −20) = +26.2%** ≈ **12.3% CAGR**, plus ~1.3% dividend yield ≈ **~13.6% p.a.**

**How this differs from v1 — the honest comparison.** The expected return barely moved (+28% → +26%). **Two things did.** First, the **bear case widened from −9% to −20%**, because v1's bear assumed only "bond issuance decline persists" while holding a 25x multiple; a cash-heavy, no-promoter financial growing below its listed peers can de-rate to 22x, and the −29.3% Q1FY27 bond print shows the bear scenario is already partly live. Second, **the reason to own it changed**. v1 bought "value + quality." At ~31x on the core, the value leg is gone; what remains is **quality at a full price, plus an option on capital allocation.**

**🎯 The option is the whole story.** ₹918cr is 18% of the market cap earning ~6% pre-tax inside a business earning >100% on operating capital. A buyback of ~10% of the equity at ₹1,700 would cost ₹510cr — **56% of the treasury, comfortably affordable** — and would accrete EPS ~11% immediately. Management has instead pointed at an acquisition pipeline for four consecutive years without closing anything, while payout fell to 39%. **A large part of the gap between the base and bull cases is a single board decision.** Note that until 3-Jun-2026 a court injunction restrained the alienation of assets — a plausible partial explanation for the inaction, and one that has now been removed. **FY27 is the year with no excuse left.**

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — Corporate bond issuances continue declining.**
> ⚠️ **MATERIALIZED, and worse than v1 framed it.** Corporate bond issuance fell **3.2% in FY26** and then **29.3% YoY in Q1FY27** (₹2.5tn vs ₹3.5tn). Bank credit meanwhile accelerated to **+18.6%**, and CARE's growth is now riding bank-loan ratings. That matters because management confirmed the yield differential — Revati Kasture, Q4FY26: *"the yields in the bond market as far as rating fees are concerned are better than in the bank loan market."* **The mix is shifting toward the cheaper, more commoditised product.** Offsetting it: this is an annuity book, so surveillance revenue on the existing 5,200+ rated entities cushions the swing.
**Risk 2 — CRISIL competitive pressure intensifies.**
**Risk 3 — ICRA + India Ratings MNC pricing power.**
**Risk 4 — Middle East + geopolitical impact on macro.**
**Risk 5 — International expansion delays.**
**Risk 6 — AI platform monetisation slower than expected.**
**Risk 7 — Regulatory changes (new entrants).**
> ⚠️ **RE-FRAMED — v1 pointed the regulatory risk in the wrong direction.** The live regulatory risk is not new entrants (the barrier is as high as ever); it is **SEBI supervision of CARE itself**. SEBI inspected the company as a CRA for 1-May-2024 to 31-Mar-2025 and issued an **administrative warning on 24-Mar-2026** for *"inconsistencies observed with respect to disclosure of applicable criteria in Press Releases and Rating Notes."* Mildest rung of action, no penalty, no restriction — but it is on the **core product**. Separately, BSE and NSE issued a **cautionary letter on 17-Feb-2026** over an incorrect ISIN on the System Driven Disclosure platform. Two pieces of regulatory correspondence in five weeks, **neither mentioned in any call or deck.**
**Risk 8 — Corporate governance risk (no promoter).**
> ⚠️ **ACCUMULATING — and v1 had the mechanism wrong.** "No promoter" is not itself the risk; professional ownership is arguably a strength, and DIIs have accumulated for 12 straight quarters. **The real governance texture is disclosure.** Three material items — the SEBI warning, the ₹22.5cr related-party Operus/Vestian GCC JV outside the stated core domain, and a three-year Madras HC injunction on alienation of assets — were **properly filed and never once mentioned** in the annual call or any investor deck (verified: `Operus` 0 hits, `Vestian` 0, `GCC` 0, `warning` 0, `inspection` 0 across the full Q4FY26 transcript and Q1FY27 deck). Compounding it: **CARE holds one earnings call a year.** Filing compliance is met; investor communication is not.
**Risk 9 — Talent attrition.**
**Risk 10 — DII exit accelerates.**
> ✅ **TRACKING WELL — the opposite happened.** DII holding rose **29.38% (Mar-25) → 31.58% (Jun-26)**, extending an unbroken 12-quarter accumulation from 23.72%. No sign of exit.

**🆕 Risk 11 — Capital allocation drift.** ₹918cr — 18% of market cap, 83% of assets — earning ~6% pre-tax inside a business earning >100% on operating capital. Four years of "actively evaluating" with nothing closed; payout down to 39% from 100% in FY19; no buyback offered while analysts asked in both the FY25 and FY26 calls. **The first actual deployment was ₹22.5cr into GCC enablement services** — talent acquisition and workspace setup — which fails management's own stated filter of *"strategic fit with our core domain, which is credit risk, analytics and consulting services."* Every year the cash sits idle mechanically dilutes ROE.

**🆕 Risk 12 — Losing relative growth to listed peers.** Q1FY27 revenue **+19.1%** against CRISIL **+27.5%** and ICRA **+31.5%**, versus management's explicit on-record commitment to *"continue to outpace the overall industry growth."* Consolidated mixes are not comparable, so this is directional evidence rather than proof of share loss — but two consecutive quarters of lagging both would be a genuine thesis problem.

**🆕 Risk 13 — Madras High Court appeals remain pending.** The injunction restraining alienation of assets was vacated 3-Jun-2026, but compliance was *"without prejudice to the rights of the Company in its appeals"* and **the underlying litigation continues.** Quantum is not disclosed in any filing I read — **stated gap, not a reassurance.**

**🆕 Risk 14 — Extreme seasonality invites mispricing.** Q2 and Q4 carry the year; Q1 and Q3 look weak by comparison. Management explicitly asks that performance be *"evaluated on an annual basis, rather than on a QoQ or sequential basis."* Any sequential read of this company will be wrong — a trap already visible in the ValuePickr thread.
