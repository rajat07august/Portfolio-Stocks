## 1. Business in Plain English

Imagine you're a global agri-chemical giant — Syngenta, Corteva, Sumitomo — and you've patented a new molecule that kills a specific weed in soybean fields. Making that molecule requires 12 sequential chemical reactions, each of which needs pristine process control. Doing this in-house would mean building a dedicated plant, training chemists, and locking up capital for a molecule that will lose patent protection in eight years. So you outsource it — to a *custom synthesis* partner in India who signs a **10-year exclusive supply contract** with you, invests in the plant, does the chemistry, and ships you the molecule at an agreed price. Anupam Rasayan is one of India's few big **custom synthesis** partners in this game.

Think of it as a **bespoke tailor for chemical molecules**. Off-the-shelf specialty chem companies (like Aarti Industries) make standard products you'd find in any catalogue. Custom synthesis players (Anupam, PI Industries, Divi's) make one-of-a-kind molecules to a customer's exact spec, under an exclusive contract, using multi-step chemistry that most competitors can't replicate.

Anupam started in **1984 in Surat, Gujarat** as an agrochemical intermediates maker. Today it makes molecules for four end-markets — **agrochemicals (55% of FY26 revenue), pharma (20%), performance materials / polymers (18%), and personal care (7%)**. The end-use spans pesticide actives, pharmaceutical Key Starting Materials (KSMs), EV battery chemicals, semiconductor precursors, and personal care ingredients.

The business model has three unusual features worth remembering:

1. **10-year LOI structure.** Anupam signs Letters of Intent with multinational customers that lock in a molecule for 4–10 years at agreed price and volume floors. Order book as of FY26 stood at **INR 14,646 crores** — meaning committed revenue for years ahead.
2. **Advance from customer for inventory.** Because the chemistry is exclusive, customers front some of the working capital as advances — which is unusual and useful for a company whose inventory days can otherwise stretch.
3. **Backward integration play.** In 2022 Anupam took controlling stake in **Tanfac Industries** (listed), a hydrofluoric acid and potassium fluoride maker — securing raw material supply for fluorine chemistry, which is where the highest margins in specialty chem sit.

FY26 was pivotal: standalone revenue jumped 60–70% YoY, consolidated revenue crossed INR 2,384 crores, and Anupam simultaneously **closed Jayhawk Fine Chemicals** (US$76 million US-based specialty chem) and **signed a definitive agreement for Bliss GVS Pharma** (a listed pharma formulations player, INR 927 cr revenue). The four-entity pro forma platform now delivers approximately **INR 4,000+ crores** of revenue and **INR 834 cr** of EBITDA — larger than any single-entity Indian specialty chem company outside PI Industries and SRF.

The 30-second version: *Anupam is transitioning from a single-vertical Indian custom synthesis player into a globally integrated chemistry + pharma platform. Whether that transition delivers or fractures is the next 24 months.*

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

Global specialty chemistry sits at the intersection of three durable forces:

**Force 1 — China+1 outsourcing.** Multinationals discovered during Covid that concentrating chemical intermediates supply in China (which had grown to 40–50% share by 2019) was a fragility, not an efficiency. Every large agri, pharma, and materials MNC has since committed to diversifying — India is the natural second home because of cost, English-language process chemists, and IP-friendly courts. India's specialty chem exports have grown ~15% CAGR through the last decade, and the target from MNCs is to move 20–30% of Chinese supply to India by 2030.

**Force 2 — Molecular complexity keeps rising.** A new agri active molecule today needs 8–15 sequential reactions vs 4–6 a decade ago. Same for pharma KSMs. Only a handful of Indian companies have the process chemistry, reactor infrastructure, and multi-step handling capability to make these. This is a structural entry barrier — you can't build 8-step synthesis capability with capex alone; it takes 15–20 years of chemist training.

**Force 3 — Long-cycle contracts.** Because MNC customers spend 3–4 years co-developing a molecule with a supplier before commercial launch, they don't switch. Once a molecule is validated, the supplier has quasi-monopoly for the patent life (7–10 years). That's why order book visibility is unusually long in this industry.

There is a fourth, more recent force: **electric vehicle and semiconductor chemistry.** EV battery electrolytes need fluorinated compounds; semiconductor manufacturing needs ultra-pure specialty chemicals. Both categories were niche in 2019, are becoming meaningful by 2026, and could be enormous by 2030. Anupam has signed LOIs in both categories (notably Elementium Materials for EV, disclosed Q4FY25) — early days, but positioning is real.

**Where Anupam sits on the industry map.** It's a **pure-play custom synthesis** company (not a commodity chem player), with a **diversified end-market mix** (four verticals), an **Indian manufacturing base with US onshore capability** (post-Jayhawk), and **backward integration to fluorine feedstock** (via Tanfac). Peers with similar structural positioning: PI Industries, Navin Fluorine, SRF (specialty division), Aarti Pharma. Anupam is smaller than all four but has a longer contract order book relative to its size — INR 14,646 cr on a ~INR 1,700 cr standalone revenue base = 8x forward revenue coverage, which is unusual.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

There are ~15 mid-cap specialty chem companies in India. What separates Anupam from the pack:

**Moat 1 — The 10-year contract book.** INR 14,646 crores of committed orders across 4–10 year contract lengths. Vishal Thakkar (Deputy CFO) said in Q4FY26: *"If I take a 7-year average… we are talking about INR 2,200 crores of additional incremental revenue that can come on an annual basis from this pipeline."* On a current standalone revenue base of INR 1,676 cr, that's 130%+ committed growth already visible. Most peers report order book on a 1–3 year basis; Anupam's is structurally longer.

**Moat 2 — Multi-step process chemistry across four verticals.** Q4FY25 concall: Anupam has **65+ molecules in R&D and pilot stages**, including **30+ in pharma alone**. The R&D bench is deep enough that even without the acquisitions, organic growth has a 3–5 year runway. Q3FY26: Polymer segment grew 245% YoY, Pharma grew 85% YoY — both driven by molecules that were in the pipeline 18 months earlier and are now commercial.

**Moat 3 — Fluorine backward integration via Tanfac.** In 2022, Anupam acquired controlling stake in Tanfac Industries — the only Indian producer of hydrofluoric acid and potassium fluoride at commercial scale. This matters because fluorinated molecules command 30–40% higher margins than non-fluorinated equivalents, and China dominates HF supply globally. Every fluorine-containing molecule Anupam makes now uses Tanfac feedstock — a supply security no listed Indian peer has.

**Moat 4 — US onshore capacity via Jayhawk.** Jayhawk Fine Chemicals — closed in FY26 — is a Kansas-based specialty chem maker with US$76 million revenue and access to US Department of Defense, semiconductor, and pharma customers who prefer American-made intermediates. This isn't just "Make in America" branding. It's a physical footprint that most Indian peers cannot replicate (Divi's, Aarti, PI Industries have no US manufacturing). Post-tariff environment, this becomes a real advantage.

**Moat 5 — Japanese customer trust.** Q4FY26: *"Japan contributed 17% of sales… we expect it to be over 20%."* Japanese multinationals (Sumitomo, Kureha, Mitsui) are notoriously picky about supplier quality and process discipline. Once you're in, they don't leave. Anupam is one of only 3–4 Indian specialty chem companies with a scaled Japanese book — this is a durable relationship moat, not a contract you can lose.

**Moat 6 — Vertical stack coming together.** Q4FY26 concall (Vishal Thakkar): *"If you look at the whole value chain starting from Tanfac to Anupam, Jayhawk and Bliss — all will be able to contribute to this platform as we go further."* Post-Bliss, the group covers KSM → API intermediate → API → finished dosage form. No other Indian specialty chem player has this full vertical stack. If integration delivers, the pricing power at each stage of the value chain compounds.

**Where the moat is weaker.** Not every molecule Anupam makes is exclusive; some are competitive intermediates where 3–4 Indian players can supply. Not every LOI converts to commercial revenue — some slip 6–12 months. And the moat depends on Anand Desai's ability to keep signing new 10-year LOIs faster than existing molecules go off-patent. There's no guarantee that pipeline replenishment stays at current pace.

**How to read the scorecard.** The **volume, geography, product-mix, and cash-flow guidance were all delivered.** The **margin guidance was missed by a meaningful 300–500 bps.** This gap has two possible explanations that matter for the thesis:

1. **Mix effect (transitory).** New pharma and polymer molecules ramp with lower initial margins; as they hit steady-state utilization, margins normalize. Management believes this in Q4FY26: *"There will be upward bias to that. I agree with you."* If true, FY27–28 could see 25–26% EBITDA margin.
2. **Structural pricing pressure (permanent).** Agri intermediates have been under pressure from Chinese oversupply through FY24–25 and pricing has structurally reset. If FY26 is the new margin reality, then the 26–28% number is history and the stock's valuation needs to reset.

*My read:* Given Anupam's specific mix (55% agri, still in cyclical ramp-back), the answer is probably "60% transitory, 40% structural." Base-case FY27 EBITDA margin: 23–24.5%. This still won't match the 26–28% guided, but it's better than FY26 delivered.

**Capital allocation aggressiveness — worth watching.** Three acquisitions in four years (Tanfac 2022, Jayhawk 2026, Bliss GVS 2026 pending) is a lot. Bliss is being funded via INR 300 cr NCD + quasi-equity — no major shareholder dilution, but debt goes up. Management's argument: *"Anupam took 4 years to reach where we are today in terms of Tanfac. We believe and we endeavor that we can do it faster than what we did for Tanfac."* Confidence is high; execution risk is real.

**Verdict on management:** Founder-driven, technically credible, ambitious. Track record of delivering top-line growth is strong. Track record on margin discipline is weaker. Bet on Anand Desai + Vishal Thakkar to execute the platform vision, but with clear eyes on integration risk.

## 5. Connecting the Dots

Anupam's story is a **vertical stacking** story. Watch these threads compound:

**Thread A — Chemistry backbone.** Fluorination + bromination + multi-step organic synthesis. This capability was built over 25 years; you can't buy it. Every new molecule the industry needs — pharma KSM, EV electrolyte, semiconductor precursor — leverages the same chemistry backbone. The R&D pipeline (65+ molecules) is the *asset* here, not the plants.

**Thread B — Raw material integration.** Tanfac gives Anupam access to HF (hydrofluoric acid) and KF (potassium fluoride) — the two most important feedstocks for fluorine chemistry. Every fluorinated molecule Anupam quotes to a customer, it can now price aggressively because it controls the feedstock. This is the *margin lever*.

**Thread C — Geographic footprint.** India (main manufacturing base) → Japan (highest-quality customer base) → US (Jayhawk onshore, plus direct LOIs) → EU (rising). Four geographies means resilience to any single-country tariff or regulatory event. This is the *risk-mitigation lever*.

**Thread D — End-market diversification.** Agri (55%, the historical anchor, cyclical) → Pharma (20%, growing 85% YoY, non-cyclical) → Performance materials (18%, EV/semi exposure, structural) → Personal care (7%, small but stable). Diversification working, agri dependence falling. This is the *stability lever*.

**Thread E — Vertical stacking via M&A.** Tanfac (raw material) → Anupam (KSMs and intermediates) → Bliss GVS (formulations). If this works, Anupam becomes an *end-to-end pharma platform* — from HF acid to finished dosage form. No listed Indian specialty chem player has this stack. This is the *platform lever* — and the most speculative one.

**Where the threads reinforce each other:** Japanese customers (Thread C) want fluorine chemistry (Thread A + B) for their EV programs (Thread D). This is why Anupam signed a large LOI with a Japanese conglomerate in Q1FY26 covering 5–6 polymer molecules — the threads compound.

**Where they might not.** Bliss GVS integration (Thread E) requires pharma domain expertise that Anupam doesn't have in-house. If they can't manage a formulations business alongside a KSM business, Thread E backfires. And the pharma platform thesis requires Bliss to move from 30% utilization to 60–70% — which requires new regulatory approvals in US and EU that Bliss doesn't yet have. That's 18–24 months of execution risk.

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — Bliss GVS integration disaster.** Anupam has never operated a formulations business. Bliss has 30% utilization, complex product-mix across therapy areas, presence in emerging markets that Anupam doesn't understand. If the Bliss integration goes badly — either operationally (utilization stays at 30%) or culturally (Bliss management leaves) — the INR 300 cr NCD debt sits on Anupam's balance sheet with no revenue offset. Signal to watch: quarterly Bliss capacity utilization + Bliss management retention. Watch also for any news of the Bliss CEO (Kamath, took over Jan 2026) leaving.

**Risk 2 — Jayhawk doesn't deliver US$76m.** Jayhawk was consolidated for 1 month + 2 days in FY26. FY27 will be the first full year. If revenue falls short (say, to US$60m) it signals demand assumptions were wrong. Also worth watching: US tariff environment. Trump-era 25% tariffs on India-origin chemicals are currently *fluid* (per Q1FY26 concall) — if reinstated permanently, US customers might prefer Jayhawk over Anupam India (which is fine), but if tariffs also hit inbound intermediates that Jayhawk imports from India, margins get hit.

**Risk 3 — Agri cycle stays weak.** FY24 and FY25 were tough on Anupam because agri destocking hit hard. FY26 recovery is meaningful, but if China agri exports remain aggressive, Anupam's 55% agri book stays margin-compressed. This is the reason the 26–28% margin guidance is missing.

**Risk 4 — EBITDA margin never recovers to 26–28%.** Base case in this profile assumes 23–24.5% FY27, moving to 25–26% by FY29 as pharma and polymer mix rises. If pharma and polymer mix rises but doesn't bring margins up (because Anupam is competing against Aarti Pharma and other bigger pharma-KSM players who have scale advantages), the "expanding margin" thesis fails. Then Anupam is a 22–23% EBITDA margin company perpetually — and 85x P/E becomes hard to justify.

**Risk 5 — Working capital never fully fixes.** Standalone WC days are ~240; pro forma ~215–220. Guidance is for further improvement. If specialty chem industry stays inventory-heavy (which it structurally is), and if pharma/polymer scale-up doesn't reduce WC as much as expected, cash flows stay strained and debt stays elevated.

**Risk 6 — Warrant / equity dilution.** Promoter holding dropped from 61.19% to 59.07% in Sep 2025 (warrant conversion). If more capital-raising is needed for Bliss or future acquisitions, further dilution possible. Watch promoter holding trend at every quarter.

**Risk 7 — Founder / succession.** Anand Desai has run this for 42 years; he's now in his 60s. No formal succession plan is disclosed. Family second generation is present in the business but role/tenure isn't clear. Long-term investors need visibility on succession — a 10-year thesis requires knowing who runs it in year 5.

**Risk 8 — Customer concentration.** Q1FY26 concall: top 10 customers = 79% of revenue. This is typical for custom synthesis, but a customer loss (say, a big pharma innovator exits a therapy area) can crater a molecule. The order book protects some of this, but not all.

**Risk 9 — Regulatory / environmental.** Specialty chem plants globally face rising ESG scrutiny — effluent, air emissions, worker safety. Any major regulatory event at the Surat plants could halt production. Anupam's disclosures on this front are adequate but not best-in-class.

**Bear case scenario (30% probability):** Bliss integration goes slow (18–24 months to hit 45% utilization), Jayhawk delivers US$60m not US$76m, agri cycle stays weak, margins stay at 22–23%. FY29 revenue INR 5,500 cr, PAT INR 450 cr. Market cap re-rates to 25x = INR 11,250 cr. That's 25% downside from CMP.

**Base case (50% probability):** All three integrations work but slowly. FY29 revenue INR 6,500 cr, PAT INR 650 cr, market cap INR 20–22,000 cr. ~50% upside over 3 years.

**Bull case (20% probability):** Platform thesis delivers cleanly. FY29 revenue INR 8,000 cr+, PAT INR 900+ cr, market cap INR 30,000+ cr. 100%+ upside.

Weighted expected return over 3 years: ~30–40% total, or ~9–12% CAGR before dividends. Not a scream buy at these levels, but not overvalued either — position size should reflect the wide outcome band.

On paper, the responsibilities compound rather than distract — each new role makes the others stronger.

Some of that is the pitch (agri chemistry cycle down); some of that might be that he's spread thin.
