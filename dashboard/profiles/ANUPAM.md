## 1. Business in Plain English

Anupam Rasayan makes **molecules other companies cannot make**, under contract, to their specification.

This is **custom synthesis and CDMO** — a customer (a global agrochemical, pharma or materials company) has a compound it needs at scale but does not want to manufacture. Anupam takes the chemistry, engineers a process, builds the plant, and supplies under a multi-year contract. **The moat is process know-how plus the switching cost of re-validating a supplier for a regulated molecule.**

**What has changed is that it is no longer one company.** Management's own framing of the platform it has assembled:

> *"**Tanfac** gives us critical raw material and **fluorination integration**, **Anupam** brings deep chemistry and **custom synthesis** capabilities, **Jayhawk** gives us a **U.S. manufacturing and CDMO platform**, and **Bliss** adds a differentiated **pharmaceutical and formulation** platform."*

| Entity | What it contributes | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Anupam Rasayan** | Custom synthesis, complex fluorination, flow chemistry | Core |
| **Tanfac** | Fluorine raw-material integration | Consolidated, separately listed |
| **Jayhawk** | US manufacturing + CDMO presence | **FY27 is the first full year of consolidation** |
| **Bliss GVS Pharma** | Formulations, regulated manufacturing, global market access | **Open offer concluded 10-Aug-2026; closing** |

**The useful analogy is a private-equity roll-up run by chemists.** *Each piece is a real business; the thesis is that owning all four is worth more than owning any one — and the bill for assembling them is on the balance sheet.*

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Western customers want their difficult chemistry made somewhere other than China.** Custom synthesis and CDMO demand has structurally relocated toward India, and complex fluorination is among the hardest sub-segments to replicate.

**Force 2 — Fluorine chemistry is where the value is.** Fluorinated intermediates run through agrochemicals, pharmaceuticals, electronics, semiconductors and batteries. **Tanfac gives Anupam the raw material; ETFA proves the process capability.**

**Force 3 — 🆕 Flow chemistry is a genuine technical frontier.** Deputy CFO Vishal Thakkar: *"flow chemistry per se is a very, very **cutting-edge technology**, which very limited number of people across the globe would have been using it on a commercial [scale]… it improves the environmental footprint… you are able to do at a higher scale with a **lesser land requirement**… these reactors are classically **tabletop reactors**."* **On ETFA specifically: *"to our knowledge, we are the only ones who are able to do"* it in flow** — with applications *"across pharma, agro and electronics and semiconductor."*

**Force 4 — Regulated-market pharma is a higher-barrier, higher-margin destination** than agrochemical intermediates, and it is where the Bliss acquisition is aimed: *"taking us from key starting materials and intermediates towards **formulations, regulated manufacturing and global market access**."*

**Force 5 — 🆕 Next-generation energy materials are becoming addressable.** The **BASQUEVOLT** LoI — a solid-state battery developer — is *"approximately **USD300 million spread over a period of 10 years**"*, with the technology *"fairly with us"* already.

**Where Anupam sits.** ₹14,322cr in **Sector 4 (Chemicals — Diversified & Specialty)** alongside DCMSHRIRAM and ASTEC. ***It is priced as a platform compounder and currently earns like a capital-intensive contract manufacturer.***

## 3. What Makes This Company Different

**Moat 1 — 🆕 A world-first in flow chemistry, commercialised.** **ETFA (ethyl trifluoroacetate) via flow** — *"the first company globally"* — with cross-industry applications in pharma, agro, electronics and semiconductors. *This is a genuine technical credential, not a marketing claim, and it was announced through a Reg 30 filing.*

**Moat 2 — Vertical integration into fluorine.** Tanfac supplies the raw material step that most Indian custom-synthesis players buy in.

**Moat 3 — A four-part platform with real geographic and regulatory reach.** India custom synthesis + US CDMO (Jayhawk) + fluorine feedstock (Tanfac) + regulated formulations (Bliss). **Few Indian mid-caps can offer a customer that span.**

**Moat 4 — Contracted, multi-year revenue.** Custom synthesis contracts carry switching costs measured in re-validation cycles, not price.

**Moat 5 — A deep and specific pipeline.** **62 molecules over the next two years, 48+ targeted at regulated markets**; 65+ pharma and polymer molecules in R&D and pilot; **10+ commercialised in this segment over the last two years.**

**Moat 6 — 🆕 A $300m ten-year LoI in next-generation batteries**, with commercialisation starting FY27 and the technology already in-house.

**Moat 7 — 🟢 The capex cycle is finished.** *"All our major planned projects have been commissioned and are operational."* **After five years of heavy building, the denominator stops growing — which is the only mechanical route to a better ROCE.**

**Moat 8 — Acquisitions are run at arm's length.** *"Anupam's approach towards any acquisition that we have done till date will be that we will continue to **keep them as independent as they can be**… the investment we have done is primarily with a view that **they have a strong management team**."* **Tanfac and Jayhawk are run this way and Bliss will be too.**

**Where the moat is weaker — and this is the longer list.**
- **🔴 ROCE is 7.36% and ROE 5.50%.** *A platform is only worth assembling if it eventually earns more than its cost of capital.*
- **🔴 Free cash flow has been negative in four of five years** — **−318, +232, −644, −440, −220** — roughly **₹1,390cr cumulative.**
- **🔴 Borrowings have gone ₹836cr → ₹1,867cr in four years**, and **₹300cr more is coming for Bliss**, plus *"an **equity-linked instrument**"* for the balance.
- **🔴 Below-EBITDA costs are eating the P&L.** Q1FY27: interest ₹49cr and depreciation ₹56cr against ₹162cr of operating profit. **Revenue +35% produced PAT +6%.**
- **⚠️ Margin has compressed as the platform grew** — OPM 28% (FY25) → 22% (FY26) → 22% TTM.
- **⚠️ Other liabilities exploded ₹1,045cr → ₹2,811cr** in FY26; total liabilities ₹5,269cr → ₹8,013cr.
- **⚠️ Domestic institutions have left** — DII 2.24% → **0.38%** in four quarters.
- **⚠️ Promoter holding fell 61.19% → 59.07%** in the Sep-2025 quarter.
- **⚠️ 81.9x earnings** with a **0.06% dividend yield.**

## 5. Connecting the Dots

**Thread A — The platform is the thesis, and it is now four-legged.** Tanfac (fluorine feedstock) → Anupam (custom synthesis) → Jayhawk (US CDMO) → Bliss (regulated formulations). **Management's claim is that the combination lets it *"participate across a much broader part of the value chain"* than any leg alone.**

**Thread B — Fluorine chemistry is the technical spine.** Tanfac supplies it, ETFA proves the process capability, and it runs into pharma, agro, electronics, semiconductors and batteries. **The world-first in flow chemistry is the credential that makes the rest credible.**

**Thread C — The mix is deliberately migrating up-value.** Agri is stable but shrinking as a share; pharma went 15% → 20%; polymer/performance materials guided to 30–35% consolidated. **BASQUEVOLT is the furthest extension of this — a battery-materials customer reached through fluorine chemistry.**

**Thread D — 🔴 Every leg was bought or built with capital, and the cost is in the P&L.** **Interest plus depreciation now consumes 65% of operating profit.** **Threads A–C are real; Thread D is why they have not yet reached shareholders.**

**Thread E — 🟢 The capex cycle ending is the mechanical route out.** *"All our major planned projects have been commissioned and are operational."* **Depreciation stops compounding, the denominator stops growing, and ROCE can only improve from here if revenue does.**

**Thread F — 🔴 But the acquisition cycle has not ended.** **₹300cr of debt plus an equity-linked instrument for Bliss, and an API plant *"still on the exploratory stage."*** *Thread E's benefit is being spent before it arrives.*

**How they interact.** **This is a genuine platform being assembled by people who understand the chemistry, financed in a way that has so far produced 7.36% ROCE and roughly ₹1,390cr of cumulative negative free cash flow.** ***The strategy and the arithmetic are pulling in opposite directions, and the resolution — if it comes — is Bliss consolidating, capex staying finished, and the 62-molecule pipeline commercialising into a fixed asset base.***

### The arithmetic that decides this

**81.9x earnings on a 7.36% ROCE and a 5.50% ROE.** ***There is no way to make those three numbers comfortable together.*** A 7.36% return on capital is below almost any reasonable cost of capital; an 81.9x multiple prices a business that compounds far above it. **One of the two has to move.**

**The bull framing, stated fairly:** trailing earnings are depressed by a depreciation and interest load created by assets that are only now commissioned and a Bliss acquisition not yet consolidated. **On that reading the E in the P/E is artificially small, and the platform's earning power is ahead of it.** *That is a legitimate argument and it is the reason this is not rated lower.*

**The problem with it:** **the same argument was available a year ago, and ROCE has not moved.** **Revenue is up 65% and PAT is flat against FY23.** **And the capital base is still growing — ₹300cr of new debt plus an equity-linked instrument for Bliss, with an API plant under exploration.**

### Weighted return framework (2-year, to FY28)

*Modelled on roughly 15.8cr shares — the current ~14.6cr plus dilution from the equity-linked instrument for Bliss. **The instrument's terms have not been disclosed, so the share count is my estimate and the single largest source of error in this framework.***

**Base case — 50%.** Bliss consolidates from H2FY27 and contributes; the capex cycle stays finished; the 62-molecule pipeline commercialises steadily; consolidated EBITDA holds at the guided 23%. **FY28 revenue ~₹4,000cr, EBITDA ₹920cr, interest ~₹200cr, depreciation ~₹250cr → PBT ₹470cr, PAT ~₹376cr. EPS ~₹23.8.** **At 50x — still a very full multiple for a business at ~10% ROCE — → ₹1,190. ≈ −5%.**

**Bull case — 25%.** Bliss transforms the mix as intended; BASQUEVOLT ramps toward its $30m-a-year run-rate; ETFA and flow chemistry open the electronics and semiconductor markets at premium margins; ROCE climbs into double digits and the market keeps paying for a platform. **FY28 EPS ~₹28 at 62x → ₹1,736. ≈ +38%.**

**Bear case — 25%.** Integration of a fourth business strains a stretched balance sheet; the equity-linked instrument dilutes more than assumed; agri pricing stays structurally reset; interest and depreciation continue to absorb two-thirds of operating profit; **and the market stops paying platform multiples for a serial acquirer earning 7% on capital.** **FY28 EPS ~₹13 at 35x → ₹455. ≈ −64%.**

**🔴 Weighted 2-year expected return ≈ −9%** *(0.50 × −5 + 0.25 × 38 + 0.25 × −64)*, plus a negligible dividend.

***Say it plainly, per the standing quality bar: at ₹1,258 this framework produces a negative expected return, and the base case is slightly negative on its own.*** **That is a statement about the price and the capital structure, not about the chemistry** — which is genuinely good, and which §3 credits at length.

### Conviction: **MEDIUM → MEDIUM-LOW**

**Why the downgrade.** The 10-Jul re-rate to Medium rested on *"~9-12% CAGR + 85x P/E on 7.4% ROCE + missed margin guide + Bliss integration risk."* **Every one of those still holds, and three things have got worse since:**

**Why not lower.** **The chemistry is real and improving** — an ETFA world-first, a $300m ten-year LoI in battery materials, a 62-molecule pipeline with 48+ for regulated markets, and a coherent four-part platform. **The capex cycle is genuinely finished**, which is the one mechanical route to a better ROCE. **The Bliss open offer closed inside its guided window** — the delivery record on process is good. **And FY26 CFO of ₹334cr at 150% of PAT shows the cash can come when the building stops.**
