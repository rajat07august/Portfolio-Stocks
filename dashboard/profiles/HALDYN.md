## 1. Business in Plain English

**Haldyn Glass makes the bottle, not what goes in it.**

It is a **container-glass manufacturer** — it melts sand, soda ash and cullet in a furnace and forms it into **bottles and vials for pharmaceuticals, food and beverages, liquor and cosmetics.** The plant is in **Gujarat (Vadodara district)**.

**Three things define the economics of this business, and all three matter more than the product:**

1. **The furnace never stops.** A glass furnace runs continuously for eight years or so and cannot be throttled — you either fill it or you waste fuel. **Utilisation is therefore the single biggest driver of margin**, which is why the FY24 capacity expansion and its subsequent filling is the whole story of the last three years.
2. **Energy is the largest variable cost.** *Which is exactly why the company has just bought into a captive renewable power vehicle — see §4.*
3. **It is heavy and low-value per unit, so freight caps the sales radius.** Container glass is a regional business by physics.

**And there is a fourth thing, which is unusual and which v1 missed entirely:**

> **Haldyn owns 56.80% of Haldyn Heinz Fine Glass Private Limited — a majority stake — and accounts for it as an *associate*, not a subsidiary.** *The annual report explains why, and the explanation is legitimate. But it means a profitable glass business that Haldyn majority-owns contributes a single equity-accounted line rather than appearing in revenue at all.* **§3.5 works through it.**

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

**Force 1 — Glass is the default primary packaging for anything that must not react with its container.** Injectables, syrups, liquor, sauces. *Regulatory and shelf-life requirements make substitution slow.*

**Force 2 — 🟢 Pharmaceutical packaging is the quality end, and India is the pharmacy of the world.** *Vial and bottle demand tracks formulation volumes, and the qualification burden on a pharma glass supplier is what keeps the customer list stable.*

**Force 3 — Furnace economics create a natural barrier.** *A container-glass line is a large, indivisible, eight-year commitment. You cannot enter at small scale, and you cannot exit quickly.*

**Force 4 — Freight caps the radius, which regionalises competition.** *A Gujarat furnace serves western and northern India economically and not much beyond.*

**Force 5 — 🟢 And the newest one: energy cost is becoming a competitive variable rather than a shared input.** **Haldyn is buying captive renewable generation and has installed an oxyfuel firing system to cut fuel consumption.** *In a business where energy is the largest variable cost, the plants that decarbonise cheapest win on margin, not just on ESG.*

**Where HALDYN sits.** ₹772cr in **Sector 11 (Packaging & Materials)** alongside **GARWARE, EPL and MAYUR.** ***Its direct listed comparable is AGI Greenpac — larger, higher-returning, and trading at half the multiple. §6.***

**Moat 5 — 🟢 A profitable associate that does not show in revenue.** **Haldyn Heinz Fine Glass earned ₹10.25cr in FY26, +11.2%**, and Haldyn's 56.80% share flows straight to consolidated profit. **Carrying value ₹41.75cr against a ₹772cr market cap.**

## 3.5. Management Track Record & Promoter Background

### The group structure — and the judgement inside it

| Entity | Relationship | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **Haldyn Glass Limited** | listed parent | — |
| **Haldyn Glass USA Inc.** | **Wholly Owned Subsidiary** | **100%** |
| **Haldyn Heinz Fine Glass Private Limited (HHFGPL)** | **Associate / Joint Venture** | **56.80%** |

**🔴 Haldyn owns 56.80% of HHFGPL — a clear majority — and does not consolidate it.** The FY26 Annual Report says so directly, and states the reason:

> *"The shareholding of the Company in HHFGPL is 56.80% as on March 31, 2026. **Though this has resulted in HHFGPL becoming a subsidiary of the Company based on percentage holding, however, the Company will exercise rights and control in accordance with the terms of the agreements entered with joint venture partners. As the Company's substantive rights would remain restricted, HHFGPL will continue to be an Associate/Joint Venture of the Company.**"*

> ***My assessment, and I want to be fair about it: this is a legitimate and standard position, not a red flag.*** **Ind AS 110 defines control by substantive rights, not by ownership percentage — if a shareholders' agreement with the JV partner restricts Haldyn's ability to direct the relevant activities, then equity accounting is the correct treatment and consolidating would be wrong.** **The company discloses the fact, the percentage and the reasoning in plain language in the Directors' Report, which is more than many do.**
>
> ***What it means practically, and this is the part an investor must hold:*** **a glass business that Haldyn majority-owns contributes one equity-accounted line, not revenue.** **HHFGPL earned ₹10.25cr in FY26 (+11.2%), of which Haldyn's share flows to consolidated profit — roughly 25% of consolidated PAT arrives this way.** *Carrying value ₹41.75cr for 1,04,37,500 shares of ₹10 each; associated since 24-Jun-2015.* **Any revenue-based multiple on Haldyn understates the group; any profit-based one does not.**

**Management's own description of it, from the Directors' Report:** *"we continue to be excited and optimistic about our joint venture, which has been **accretive to our profitability** in our endeavour to build out our capabilities and global presence."*

## 7. What Could Prove Us Wrong

**Risk 1 — 🔴 The multiple against the peer.** **24.9x versus AGI Greenpac's 13.2x, on an 11.4% ROE against 15.8%.** *A convergence to 16x on unchanged FY26 earnings is **₹400cr, −48%, with nothing going wrong operationally.***

**Risk 2 — 🔴 Energy and soda ash set the margin, and neither is controllable.** *Glass melting is one of the most energy-intensive processes in manufacturing. **The oxyfuel system and the captive renewable stake are the mitigations, and both are small and early.***

**Risk 3 — 🔴 Interest still consumes a third of profit.** **₹14cr against ₹25cr of PAT, on ₹116cr of borrowings.** *Deleveraging is the biggest earnings lever left — but it also means the equity is more cyclical than the operating business.*

**Risk 4 — 🔴 Three-year profit CAGR is −1%.** *Revenue grew 45% from FY23 to FY26 and profit went ₹27cr → ₹25cr, because the expansion's depreciation and interest ate it. **The bull case requires that never to happen again — but the next furnace rebuild is a known, cyclical event.***

**Risk 5 — 🔴 No concall, no guidance, permanently.** *Every forward number in §6 is my construction from filings. There is no mechanism to test the inflection, no management commentary on utilisation, order flow, pricing or the capex cycle, and no one to ask why other income is a fifth of pre-tax profit.*
**⚠️ STRUCTURAL — this does not improve with time.**

**Risk 6 — 🟡 A quarter of consolidated profit comes from an entity that reports no revenue here.** **HHFGPL, 56.80%-owned, equity-accounted on a disclosed substantive-rights argument.** *The treatment is legitimate. **Risk 10 — 🟢 What would genuinely surprise on the upside.** **The two energy levers are real and cumulative.** *If oxyfuel firing plus captive renewable power take two or three points out of the cost base in a business running at a 14–16% operating margin, that is a 15–20% uplift to operating profit for a few crore of investment — and it compounds with the volume already arriving.*
