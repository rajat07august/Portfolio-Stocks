## The one-paragraph version

**Revenue grew 5%. That is the number that matters, against 20% guided for the year.** Q1FY27 revenue **₹1,736cr (+5% YoY, −7% QoQ)**, EBITDA ₹203cr, **PBT ₹144cr — down 2%** — and **PAT ₹108cr (+3%) only because the tax rate fell from 28.6% to 25%**. Management attributes the miss to supply-chain disruption (*"diesel, LDO, and certain amount of delays… for procurement"*), points out that **Q1FY25 also grew 5% and the year finished at +29.5%**, and has **maintained every FY27 guidance number: 20% revenue growth, 11%+ EBITDA margin, ₹10,000cr+ order intake.** The margin guidance is being beaten already — **11.7% against 11% guided.** But the quarter also brought a **₹1,034cr order intake against a ₹10,000cr annual target**, an order book that **shrank from ₹16,361cr to ₹16,035cr**, and **net debt that nearly tripled from ₹174cr to ₹466cr** (net debt/EBITDA 0.33x → 0.67x, working capital days 81 → 85) on *"delayed collections."* Of v1's four overhangs: the **margin one has resolved favourably**; the **QIP has crystallised into a concrete ₹600cr enabling resolution** (~9.6% dilution at today's price, for *"long-term working capital"*); the **DII exit continues** (8.14% → 6.51%); and the **order-inflow question is unresolved and now more acute.** Against that: **India Ratings upgraded to AA-**, so the company now holds AA- from both agencies across ~₹7,500cr of limits; **Butibori tower plant commissioned**; **Gactel acquired** for cooling-tower EPC; **first Australian order** takes the footprint to six continents; a **500kV HVDC order** won; and an **interim dividend of ₹3/share**. **Conviction HELD at Medium-High, moved to Under Watch** with a specific Q2FY27 trigger. **The thesis is not broken; it is one quarter behind, and the second half has to carry an unusually heavy load.**

Narang: *"we maintain our 11% guidance for the year, which is one of the best in the industry."* v1 treated the FY26→FY27 margin guide-down (from ~11.9% to ~11%) as an overhang; one quarter in, **the company is running ahead of its own conservative number.**

### Why revenue grew only 5% — management's answer, and the historical precedent

> **Kartikay Agrawal (Equitree Capital):** *"can you elaborate a bit on why the quarter didn't see the top line growth as we have been guiding on?"*
>
> **Randeep Narang:** *"growth happens based on the order execution plan, the engineering readiness, and the start-up… **There were supply chain disruptions in our factory. As you know, diesel, LDO, and certain amount of delays have happened for procurement.** And with the global disruptions which happened in the first quarter, we still have maintained and grown…"*
>
> *"We maintain our guidance of 20% revenue growth for this year. And as you know, the Q1 and H1 is slow for EPC industry… **In fact, in the year '25 first quarter, we grew by 5%. On the annual basis, we grew by 29.5%.** So, this is a trend which we will catch up… **which we will catch up in Q2 for sure.**"*

**That precedent is real and checkable, and it is the strongest thing in the bull case.** Q1FY25 revenue was ₹1,088cr on Q1FY24's ~₹1,036cr — and FY25 finished at ₹5,308cr, +30%. **This company has done exactly this before.**

**But the arithmetic is demanding.** 20% on FY26's ₹6,880cr is **₹8,256cr**. Q1 delivered ₹1,736cr, so the remaining nine months need **₹6,520cr — about ₹2,173cr per quarter, 25% above the Q1 run-rate.** An analyst put precisely that to management:

> **Sunil Bhojwani:** *"with your 20% guidance, our FY27 turnover comes around INR8,300 crores… which brings us to the run rate of about INR2,200 crores to INR2,300 crores per quarter, so do you think we can execute at that rate?"*
>
> **Randeep Narang:** *"**Yes, very much**, because our H2 normally is much higher, and Q2 also, we are very buoyant… Our tower capacity has gone up, so our supply chain for our projects will be improved in this quarter and next quarter. **So the capacity to produce and deliver to execute is very much in place.**"*

**Management has staked FY27 on H2 with full specificity. That is the trigger this refresh is built around.**

## 🟡 The ₹600cr QIP — v1's overhang, now specified

> **Deepak Khandelwal:** *"Board has approved a proposal to raise **up to INR600 crores through a qualified institutional placement, QIP, or other eligible securities**, subject to the necessary shareholders and regulatory approvals."*
>
> **Randeep Narang:** *"the fund raising is **predominantly an enabling resolution** right now. We have a subcommittee evaluating various market conditions and opportunities, and this will also help us in our **cash flow management**… In the next call, we can definitely tell you the plan going forward."*
>
> Asked by **Nihal Shah (Prudent Corporate Advisory)** why a company with good operating cash flow and a stable balance sheet needs capital — **Khandelwal:** *"we are raising for **long-term working capital requirement** and other things… it is just enabling provision, so let the approvals come and then we will plan further."*

**Sizing it:** ₹600cr at the current ₹467 is roughly **1.28cr new shares on 13.43cr outstanding — ~9.6% dilution.** A postal ballot (02/2026-27) was issued 10-Aug-2026, presumably carrying the enabling resolution.

**The honest read.** Raising equity into a stock that is **41% down over a year and 3.8% above its 52-week low** is expensive capital, and the stated purpose — long-term working capital — is exactly the pressure the net-debt line just showed. **It is an enabling resolution, not a committed raise**, and management explicitly deferred the plan to the next call. **But an investor should assume it happens and price ~10% dilution, rather than treat it as optional.**

**Separately, a buyback was raised and ruled out.** Viraj Mahadevia (Moneygrow India) asked whether the promoter group had considered a buyback *"to take advantage of the undervaluation in the share price and underperformance since IPO."* Narang: *"I think the question is a little complicated. **We have no such intentions as of now.**"* **Reasonable given a QIP is in flight — you cannot buy back and issue simultaneously — but the two together tell you the direction of share count.**

## Conviction: **HELD at Medium-High — Under Watch**

**Not a downgrade, and deliberately so.** A single seasonally-weak quarter, with an explicit and checkable precedent (Q1FY25 also +5%, FY25 finished +29.5%), against maintained guidance, a beaten margin, a ratings upgrade, commissioned capacity and a 2.3x order book at 15x earnings — that is not a thesis break. **Nor is it confirmation.** The order book has stopped growing, working capital has moved the wrong way, and a ₹600cr raise is queued into a stock near its 52-week low.

**Under Watch trigger — Q2FY27 (results ~Nov-2026). Downgrade to Medium if ANY of:**
1. **Q2FY27 revenue below ₹2,000cr** — management guided a ₹2,173-2,300cr run-rate and said *"very much"* achievable;
2. **H1FY27 order intake below ₹3,500cr**, i.e. the ₹10,000cr annual guide becomes arithmetically out of reach;
3. **Net debt/EBITDA above 0.67x** at September, having promised normalisation *"in Q2"*;
4. **The Burberry ₹80cr is not repaid by September 2026** as contracted.

**Upgrade back to clean Medium-High** on Q2 revenue ≥ ₹2,150cr with order intake ≥ ₹3,000cr in the quarter.

## 1. Business in Plain English

Transrail builds the high-voltage skeleton of the power grid — and, unusually, it *manufactures the bones itself*. It's a turnkey EPC (engineering, procurement, construction) contractor that wins contracts to erect transmission lines up to **765 kV and 800 kV HVDC**, and it runs its own factories making the lattice towers, monopoles and aluminium conductors those lines are built from. Most EPC contractors buy towers and conductors on the open market and live on thin single-digit margins; Transrail captures the *manufacturing* margin and the *construction* margin on the same project. Roughly 60–65% of project inputs are backward-integrated in-house, factories run ~95% utilised.

The cleanest analogy: a **bespoke bridge-builder that also owns the steel mill**. When a utility hands it a job, it forges the steel members, spins the cable, engineers the route, and strings the line — one throat to choke, better cost control, faster delivery.

Four verticals: **Power T&D** (~90% of revenue — the engine), plus **poles & lighting** (the "Lighting" in the name — galvanised poles, streetlight masts), **railways** (electrification, OHE, bullet-train masts), and **civil** (bridges — incl. a 10.2km Kosi road bridge, cooling towers). It also has a nascent **solar EPC** foray (international only). Genuinely global: footprint across **63 countries**, active in 20+, ~50:50 domestic:international revenue. Africa is ~20% of the order book (28 African countries, multilateral-funded), and the Bangladesh Rooppur river-crossing was a flagship (now winding down).

**History — founder-led, 4 decades:** Founded by **Digambar Bagde in 1984** as Transrail Engineering Company; first 400 kV line 1987, tower factory Baroda 1993, conductor factory Silvassa 2007, first 800 kV HVDC 2012. In **2016 it acquired Gammon India's T&D business via the NCLT/insolvency route** — an add-on to a company Bagde had already built, not a reverse-merger. IPO'd **December 2024** at ₹432/share. So the *listing* is young (~1.5 years) but the operating business is a 40-year-old, founder-run franchise.

## 2. Why This Industry Exists

Five durable forces underwrite the T&D EPC opportunity, all reiterated across the FY26 calls and deck:

1. **Renewable-evacuation build-out.** India's National Electricity Plan targets **191,000 CKM of new transmission lines by 2032** (~38% of the grid) and **₹9.15 lakh crore of transmission investment** — chiefly to move renewable power (500 GW non-fossil by 2030) from generation clusters to demand. Transmission is the bottleneck the entire energy transition runs through.
2. **Rising base demand + grid modernisation** — data centres, EVs, green hydrogen, AI load; UHV (up to 1200 kV) integration; re-conductoring (HTLS).
3. **HVDC / higher-voltage complexity** shrinks the pre-qualified (PQ) contractor pool. Transrail has executed 765 kV and 800 kV HVDC — a PQ moat newcomers can't shortcut.
4. **International electrification** — Africa's "Mission 300" (World Bank/AfDB-funded), SAARC, GCC. Multilateral funding de-risks payment; India-heritage EPC is cost-competitive vs Chinese/local bidders.
5. **Backward-integration economics.** Towers + conductors are ~70% of a T&D contract's value; owning that supply chain is the gap between a 6% EPC margin and Transrail's ~12%.

## 3. What Makes This Company Different (the moat)

1. **True backward integration** — in-house towers (172,400 MTPA, doubled in FY26), conductors, poles; ~95% utilisation; tower testing up to 1200 kV (NABL-accredited). "Most of our new competition doesn't have" own conductors (Narang, Q4FY26).
2. **Best-in-class margins** — ~12% EBITDA, which management repeatedly frames as "3–5% ahead of peers." Structurally above pure-construction contractors.
3. **PQ depth** — 765 kV and 800 kV HVDC executed; global PQs across 63 countries. In a business where you can't bid what you can't pre-qualify for, this compounds.
4. **Focus, not diversification** — ~90% T&D. Explicitly contrasts itself with diversified infra peers: "we are steadfast and focused on T&D."
5. **Selective, risk-first bidding** — "5 out of 10 jobs we don't bid"; international only with multilateral funding; margin-and-risk selection over revenue-chasing.
6. **Geographic diversification** — ~50:50 domestic:international revenue insulates against any single tender cycle or payment shock.
7. **Credit-rating tailwind** — CRISIL **AA-/Stable** (upgraded Aug-2025 from A+, reaffirmed Mar-2026); IND A+/Positive; short-term A1+.
8. **Marquee client roster** — POWERGRID (20+ live jobs), Adani (Khavda), ReNew, Reliance; internationally World Bank/AfDB/KfW/BOAD-funded.

**Where the moat is weaker (honest section):**
- **It's a contractor, not a product company** — every rupee is re-won through competitive tenders; no annuity, no pricing power beyond price-variation clauses (~35% of the book).
- **Backward integration ≠ commodity immunity** — steel, zinc, aluminium still swing the P&L; ~65% of contracts carry input risk on fixed prices.
- **T&D concentration cuts both ways** — a domestic tender slowdown or an international payment shock hits ~90% of the business.
- **Working-capital-heavy** — 81 WC days, ₹1,200cr+ LC, ₹2,643cr contract assets (retention), and analysts twice flagged **CFO-to-EBITDA conversion below peers** on the calls.
