Weinstein Live — Stage Analysis

Every stock is in one of four stages, read from its weekly price against a 30-week moving average. This screen re-runs on demand, compares against the previous run, and reports what changed — because in stage analysis the transition is the signal.

The pipeline · four stages, in order

STAGE 0 · THE MARKET

Ten gauges, majority verdict. If the market is in Stage 4, no buy may be issued at all — regardless of what any individual chart shows.

STAGE 1 · THE GROUP

The sector's own stage, read from NSE's own indices. A stock in a Stage 3 or 4 group cannot be a buy — “don't try to be a whiz kid and find the one winner in a sick group.”

STAGE 2 · THE STOCK

Its own stage, then three filters: volume ≥ 2×, rising relative strength, and little overhead resistance.

STAGE 3 · THE RISK

Where the stop sits, before any order. More than 15% away and it is not a buy, however good the chart.

Stage 0 — the market gate · nothing below matters until this is settled

“Forest to the trees”: market → group → stock, never the reverse. “If it's negative, you'll want to do very little if any buying, even if you see some stocks breaking out.”

Weinstein does not gate on one indicator — “any technical tool, no matter how good its record, will throw you a curve at some point. If you lean too heavily on just a few indicators, your system will sooner or later take you out into left field.” Every gauge below is recomputed on each refresh. What changed since the last run · the live part

Weinstein's rules fire on transitions, not on states: a stock entering Stage 4 is a sell, a stock breaking into Stage 2 on volume is a buy candidate, a close below the trailing stop is an exit. Each run diffs against the last and lists only what moved.

How to read the charts

Click any row in the table below to open its annotated chart. Each one is labelled the way Weinstein teaches it:

  • Shaded bands, named — every stage the stock has passed through: STAGE 1 base STAGE 2 advance STAGE 3 top STAGE 4 decline. Runs shorter than 8 weeks are absorbed into their neighbours, because a stage is a phase of months, not a weekly flip.
  • The red line is the 30-week MA — the single most important object on the chart. “Stocks trading beneath their 30-week MAs should never be considered for purchase, especially if the MA is declining.”
  • The green dashed line is the ceiling of the most recent base, with an arrow at the point price broke through it — where Stage 2 begins.
  • The purple dotted line is the protective sell-stop, placed below the correction low or the MA (whichever is lower) and tucked just under a round number, with the risk from today's price.
  • Volume panel — the dashed red line is 2× the 4-week average, Weinstein's breakout threshold; bars that clear it are red. “Never trust a breakout that isn't accompanied by a significant increase in volume.”
  • A written read-out accompanies every chart — how Weinstein would read it in words, followed by the specific price levels at which the stage would change. Naming those levels is his instruction: “write down the price that each would need to break out.”
  • RS panel — relative strength against the Nifty as a 0–99 rank. Above 50 the stock is leading the index, below 50 it is lagging. “As long as this line is in a downtrend, don't consider buying the stock even if it breaks out.”

Look up any stock · not just these 58

Give it a name or symbol and it runs the whole screen on that stock the way Weinstein would work through a new idea — market first, then the stock's own stage, then each filter with the sentence from the book behind it, then the risk — and ends with a verdict.

cd "Investor Frameworks/WEINSTEIN" && ../../.venv-charts/bin/python tools/lookup.py RELIANCE

Accepts a symbol or a name — lookup.py INFY, lookup.py "tata motors". Add --json for machine-readable output. Why this is a command and not a box on this page: the lookup has to fetch live weekly data, and Screener sends no Access-Control-Allow-Origin header — a browser cannot call it from this domain. Doing it here would need a serverless function, which means changing the Vercel config that serves the main dashboard. That is not a change worth risking untested, so it is a follow-up rather than a shortcut taken now.

Keeping it live

The screen is a script, not a snapshot. To refresh:

cd "Investor Frameworks/WEINSTEIN" && ../../.venv-charts/bin/python tools/refresh.py

  • Re-fetches weekly price and volume for all 58 names plus the Nifty, with back-off on rate limits and a fall back to the cached file if a fetch fails — a failed fetch never silently becomes an abstention.
  • Re-stages everything on the frozen criteria, re-computes stops and RS ranks.
  • Diffs against the previous run and writes changes.json: stage transitions, verdict changes, stop breaches, and volume expansions through the 2× threshold.
  • Appends a row to history.json so the distribution can be tracked over time.
  • --no-fetch recomputes from cache without touching the network.

Stop breaches fire on the transition, not the state — a stock that is already below its stop is not re-reported every run. That bug was caught by running the engine twice and demanding zero changes the second time; it is now a standing check.

What this screen does not claim

  • The market gate rests on more than one gauge but not on all of them. Index stage analysis, breadth (% above the 30-week MA), the cumulative A–D line, the momentum index and world markets are built; the A–D line's true peak sits outside a 751-session window and world markets are ETF proxies, three of four American.
  • Group stages use NSE's own indices — 81 of the 85 broad-market, sectoral and thematic indices on NSE's live-indices page are staged here on the identical criteria. The gaps, named rather than rounded away: three (Smallcap 500, REITs & Realty, Cement) are listed by the data source but it publishes no price series for them; Nifty500 Multicap 50:25:25 is not carried at all; India VIX is excluded deliberately — it is a volatility gauge derived from option prices, not a trend. Constituent lists are public for 66 of 81, so for the remaining 15 the index is staged but membership is unknown and no watchlist name can be placed in it.
  • Volume is tested loosely — the correct test is volume on the breakout week; most names are not at a fresh breakout, so what is shown is the best week in twelve.
  • Blinding was imperfect — the Fisher and Lynch results and the holdings were known, and two names were charted before the criteria were frozen.
  • Stage boundaries are a slope threshold, not a fact. ±2% over ten weeks separates “rising” from “flat”; names near that line can flip between runs, which is why transitions are reported rather than treated as certainties.
